PART I: WORLD FOUNDATIONS
1. WORLD CONCEPT & IDENTITY
1.1 The Setting
The campaign takes place in an original fantasy world built using the rules, mythology,
creatures, ancestries, gods, magic, and established lore of Pathfinder First Edition.

It is not Golarion, nor is it a direct adaptation of another fantasy setting. It is an original world
incorporating Pathfinder's established fantasy elements while developing its own geography,
kingdoms, histories, cultures, political conflicts, and legends.

The primary creative inspiration is A Song of Ice and Fire / Game of Thrones, particularly:

   ·   Medieval political intrigue.
   ·   Noble houses and dynastic struggles.
   ·   Inheritance and succession disputes.
   ·   Feudal obligations and competing loyalties.
   ·   Warfare and military strategy.
   ·   Competing kingdoms and political alliances.
   ·   Family rivalries, ambition, betrayal, and shifting power.
   ·   A world with a long and complicated history.

These inspirations establish the general tone and political structure, not the actual geography,
characters, factions, or history.

The setting must develop its own identity. Existing names, histories, locations, and events from
other fantasy settings should not be copied directly.

1.2 Core Identity
The world should feel:
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 2 / 40>
   ·   Old, with histories extending far beyond the current generation.
   ·   Politically complicated, with competing noble houses and shifting alliances.
   ·   Medieval, with feudal relationships, castles, armies, kingdoms, and hereditary nobility.
   ·   Dangerous, with threats beyond the protections of established settlements.
   ·   Socially divided, with wealth, ancestry, religion, and birth influencing people's
       opportunities.
   ·   Lived-in, with believable cultures, institutions, traditions, and conflicts.
   ·   Mysterious, with ancient histories and supernatural events that are not always understood.

Civilization survives behind walls.

Beyond the protections of established settlements lie dangerous wilderness, forgotten ruins,
ancient mysteries, and creatures that ordinary people would rather not encounter.

The supernatural exists, but its nature and history are frequently misunderstood.

2. RULES & SETTING PHILOSOPHY
Pathfinder First Edition is the mechanical foundation of the campaign.

Whenever possible, existing Pathfinder rules and established lore will be used rather than
creating unnecessary homebrew mechanics.

This includes:

   ·   Classes and archetypes.
   ·   Feats and skills.
   ·   Ancestries and racial abilities.
   ·   Spells and magical traditions.
   ·   Gods, domains, and divine powers.
   ·   Monsters and supernatural creatures.
   ·   Planes and cosmology.
   ·   Equipment, weapons, and armor.
   ·   Combat and character advancement.

The setting may reinterpret how its inhabitants understand certain things without changing the
underlying Pathfinder mechanics.

For example, magic may be rare, feared, misunderstood, or socially forbidden without changing
how a player character's spells function.

Homebrew mechanics should only be introduced when there is a compelling reason and the
decision is explicitly agreed upon.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 3 / 40>
The world should adapt Pathfinder lore to fit its identity rather than forcing the world to resemble
Golarion.

3. TECHNOLOGY & CIVILIZATION
The baseline civilization operates at a high-medieval technological level, comparable to the
societies depicted in A Song of Ice and Fire.

Society is built around hereditary nobility, fortified settlements, agriculture, trade routes, military
obligations, and feudal relationships.

Common features include:

   ·   Massive stone castles and fortified keeps.
   ·   Noble houses and hereditary titles.
   ·   Kings, queens, lords, ladies, knights, and sworn swords.
   ·   Squires, retainers, household guards, and professional soldiers.
   ·   Chainmail, plate armor, shields, and medieval weapons.
   ·   Swords, spears, axes, crossbows, and longbows.
   ·   Siege engines and conventional medieval warfare.
   ·   Walled cities, market towns, villages, and agricultural settlements.
   ·   Taverns, guilds, merchants, craftsmen, and established trade routes.
   ·   Horse-drawn transportation and sailing vessels.
   ·   Traditional blacksmithing, masonry, carpentry, and other medieval crafts.

There is no established industrial or steampunk technological culture.

Alchemy exists within the Pathfinder framework, but it does not imply industrialization or
advanced machinery. It is a specialized field of knowledge and craft.

Technological development should remain consistent with the medieval character of the setting.


PART II: POLITICS & CIVILIZATION
4. POLITICAL STRUCTURE
The continent contains numerous political entities.

Not every region is ruled by a king, and not every territory belongs to a unified kingdom.

Political entities may include:

   ·   Major kingdoms.
   ·   Independent territories.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 4 / 40>
   ·   Principalities.
   ·   Duchies and counties.
   ·   Powerful noble houses.
   ·   Independent city-states.
   ·   Religious territories.
   ·   Merchant-controlled territories.
   ·   Remote regions with weak or nonexistent central authority.

Major noble houses retain considerable independence and influence.

4.1 Current Political Climate
The continent is experiencing a period of uneasy peace.

There is no established continent-wide war at the beginning of the campaign, but the political
order is fragile.

Some rulers are capable and respected. Others are incompetent, unpopular, or struggling to
maintain legitimacy.

Major houses are positioning themselves for greater influence.

Territorial disputes remain unresolved, alliances shift, and declining houses may attempt to
reclaim lost power.

The political situation should feel active and independent of the player characters.

The characters are not automatically important to the world's political events. They may become
influential through their own decisions and actions.

4.2 The Six Great Houses
Six major houses dominate the continent's political landscape.

These houses were responsible for the destruction of House Targaryen and represent some of the
most powerful political and military forces in the setting.

Their individual names, histories, territories, heraldry, and political relationships are still being
developed.

Several ancestry associations and house concepts have been established.

The Elven Great House

One of the major houses is an elven dynasty loosely inspired by the political and economic role
of House Lannister.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 5 / 40>
The Lannister name and identity will not be used directly.

The house will have its own history, culture, ambitions, territory, and political identity.

The Dwarven Great House

The Baratheon-inspired great house is dwarven and led by a dwarven ruler.

It was the principal mastermind behind the Great Rebellion against House Targaryen.

It developed the specialized dragon-slaying ballistas and claimed Dragonstone following the
rebellion.

Its final identity, history, territory, and relationship with the other great houses remain to be
developed.

The Northern Great House

An ancient and powerful human noble house dominates the northern territories.

It maintains the Ancient Barrier and possesses substantial military independence.

Its established characteristics are detailed in the Northern Wilderness section.

House Edarth

House Edarth is established as one of the six great houses that rebelled against House Targaryen.

Its ancestry, territory, ruling family, history, and specific role in the rebellion remain to be
developed.

The remaining great houses and their ancestry associations will be developed separately.


PART III: RELIGION, MAGIC & THE
SUPERNATURAL
5. GODS & RELIGION
5.1 The Central Mystery
The existence of gods is genuinely uncertain in this world.

Nobody knows with absolute certainty whether the gods exist.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 6 / 40>
Pathfinder's established gods, portfolios, alignments, domains, religious traditions, and
mythology provide the foundation for the setting's religions.

However, their actual existence is not established as an objective fact within the world's known
history.

Religious institutions exist, and their influence can be enormous.

Faith is a powerful force in society, regardless of whether the beliefs behind it are correct.

5.2 Religion and Society
Religious institutions may:

   ·   Influence kings and noble houses.
   ·   Control land and wealth.
   ·   Maintain military or religious orders.
   ·   Spread doctrine and religious teachings.
   ·   Influence laws and social expectations.
   ·   Justify political authority.
   ·   Declare individuals or groups heretical.
   ·   Engage in religious persecution.
   ·   Compete with other religious institutions.

Different cultures may interpret the same Pathfinder deity in dramatically different ways.

Religious institutions may possess conflicting scriptures, histories, doctrines, and interpretations
of supposed divine signs.

Religious fanatics and zealots are prominent features of the setting.

Faith may be sincere, corrupt, compassionate, political, coercive, or contradictory.

5.3 Divine Magic
Divine magic exists within the Pathfinder mechanical framework.

Its ultimate source and relationship to the supposed gods remain part of the setting's larger
mystery.

The existence of divine magic does not automatically settle the question of whether gods exist.

This uncertainty should remain a defining characteristic of the setting.

No original gods have been established.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 7 / 40>
6. MAGIC & THE SUPERNATURAL
Magic is uncommon in everyday civilization.

Arcane magic is particularly rare.

Much of its knowledge has been lost, forgotten, suppressed, or misunderstood.

Wizards may be regarded as:

   ·   Dangerous scholars.
   ·   Heretics.
   ·   Legends.
   ·   Remnants of an ancient age.
   ·   Individuals possessing knowledge that ordinary people cannot comprehend.

Possible features of the world's magical history include:

   ·   Ancient spellbooks and magical texts are extremely valuable.
   ·   Magical academies may have disappeared.
   ·   Arcane knowledge may survive in isolated individuals.
   ·   Hidden collections and ancient ruins may preserve forgotten knowledge.
   ·   Certain forms of arcane practice may be forbidden or heavily restricted.
   ·   Alchemists may be mistaken for practitioners of other forms of magic.

The average person may live their entire life without witnessing a spell being cast.

The scarcity of magic is a feature of society, population, institutions, and history.

It does not arbitrarily weaken player character spellcasting or change Pathfinder mechanics.

The reason arcane knowledge became rare has not been established.

6.1 Supernatural Threats
Monsters, undead, vampires, cults, and other supernatural dangers are real possibilities.

However, they are uncommon in settled civilization.

Most ordinary people know about these threats through stories, rumors, religious teachings, and
occasional encounters.

The danger increases in remote areas.

Examples include:

   ·   Ancient forests.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 8 / 40>
   ·   Abandoned settlements.
   ·   Forgotten ruins.
   ·   Old battlefields.
   ·   Remote wilderness.
   ·   Forbidden territories.
   ·   Places associated with lost civilizations.

Major cities are generally less affected by supernatural threats, although they are not immune.

Small agricultural settlements may occasionally experience supernatural dangers.

The world should preserve uncertainty.

Not every rumor is true, and not every strange event needs an immediate explanation.


PART IV: ANCESTRIES &
CIVILIZATIONS
7. ANCESTRIES AND SOCIETIES
Humans are the dominant population across much of the continent.

Other Pathfinder ancestries maintain their own territories, cultures, communities, and political
influence.

However, they are generally minorities within a larger human-dominated political landscape.

Ancestry does not automatically determine an individual's culture, beliefs, or loyalties.

Members of the same ancestry may have very different customs and identities depending on
where they live.

Intermarriage, adoption, inheritance, and political alliances between different ancestries are
possible.

7.1 Humans
Humans are the dominant population across much of the continent.

They control many of the major kingdoms and territories and form the majority of the continent's
population.

Human cultures, political systems, and social traditions vary considerably by region.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 9 / 40>
Humanity is not a single unified political or cultural group.

7.2 Dwarves
Dwarven civilization once possessed a much larger empire.

That empire no longer exists in its former unified form.

The current dwarven political landscape consists of:

   ·   One surviving dwarven kingdom.
   ·   Several independent territories descended from the former dwarven empire.

The relationships between these territories, their histories, and their political ambitions remain to
be developed.

The Baratheon-inspired great house is dwarven and led by a dwarven ruler.

7.3 Elves
Elves possess an important place in the continent's political and cultural landscape.

One of the six great houses is an elven dynasty inspired by the political and economic archetype
of House Lannister.

The wider elven population, its history, culture, territories, and relationship with human
kingdoms remain to be developed.

Elves should not automatically be treated as a unified civilization.

7.4 Ratfolk Civilization
Origins and Identity
Ratfolk are naturally subterranean inhabitants whose ancestors developed underground societies
long before the rise of many current human kingdoms.

They are not a displaced people or the remnants of a fallen surface empire. Their existence
underground is an established part of their history and identity. The surface world is simply
where other peoples live.

Ratfolk recognize a shared ancestry and a broader cultural identity, but this does not create
universal loyalty between communities. Family, settlement, clan, organization, and personal
alliances generally take precedence over ancestry alone.

There is no unified ratfolk kingdom, empire, or central government.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 10 / 40>
The Underground Network
Ratfolk settlements are connected through an extensive, irregular network of underground
passages and routes.

These include:

   ·   Natural cave systems.
   ·   Constructed tunnels and passageways.
   ·   Underground waterways.
   ·   Abandoned mines and excavations.
   ·   Hidden routes between established settlements.
   ·   Larger subterranean roads and trade passages.

The network is not completely connected. Geological barriers, collapsed passages, dangerous
territories, hostile creatures, and unexplored regions separate some communities from one
another.

Ratfolk possess considerably more knowledge of the underground world than most surface
civilizations realize. However, no single community has complete knowledge of the entire
network.

Political Organization
Ratfolk settlements govern themselves independently.

Political structures vary significantly between communities and may include:

   ·   Councils of elders.
   ·   Merchant or trade councils.
   ·   Clan-based leadership.
   ·   Tribal hierarchies.
   ·   Elected representatives.
   ·   Authoritarian rulers.
   ·   Religious authorities.

There is no universal system of government or recognized authority over all ratfolk.

Relations between settlements vary. Some maintain strong trade relationships and mutual
agreements, while others compete over resources, territory, access to passages, or political
influence. Rivalries and conflicts can exist between ratfolk communities just as they do between
surface kingdoms.

Settlements and Communities
Ratfolk civilization is not uniform.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 11 / 40>
Their settlements range from sophisticated underground cities with substantial populations and
developed infrastructure to smaller established communities, isolated warrens, and hidden
settlements.

Differences between communities may include:

   ·   Population and wealth.
   ·   Political organization.
   ·   Access to resources.
   ·   Architectural traditions.
   ·   Trade relationships.
   ·   Technological development.
   ·   Cultural customs.

Some settlements are prosperous centers of commerce and specialized knowledge. Others are
comparatively poor, isolated, or dependent on traditional methods of survival.

Technology and Specialized Knowledge
Ratfolk civilization generally operates at a technological level comparable to the high-medieval
civilizations of the surface world.

However, ratfolk communities have developed particular strengths in fields suited to
subterranean life and their specialized needs.

These areas include:

   ·   Alchemy and chemical knowledge.
   ·   Medicine and practical healing.
   ·   Engineering and construction.
   ·   Mining and excavation.
   ·   Metallurgy and material processing.
   ·   Subterranean agriculture.
   ·   The cultivation and use of fungi and other underground resources.

This does not mean every ratfolk settlement possesses advanced laboratories, sophisticated
equipment, or exceptional technical knowledge.

Development is uneven. Some communities contain skilled specialists, established workshops,
laboratories, and complex infrastructure. Others rely on traditional practices, limited resources,
and knowledge passed between generations.

Specialized knowledge may be concentrated within particular settlements, families, guilds, or
organizations.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 12 / 40>
Ratfolk are not universally gifted in alchemy or engineering. Their civilization simply contains
established traditions and areas of expertise in these fields.

Economy and Trade
Ratfolk communities make use of the resources available throughout the underground world.

Important resources may include:

   ·   Metals and minerals.
   ·   Fungi and subterranean crops.
   ·   Medicinal plants and materials.
   ·   Alchemical ingredients.
   ·   Stone and construction materials.
   ·   Gems and other valuable resources.
   ·   Specialized tools and manufactured goods.

Trade exists between underground settlements and, in some regions, between ratfolk
communities and surface civilizations.

Contact with surface peoples varies. Some settlements maintain established trading relationships,
while others rely on intermediaries, informal arrangements, or limited contact.

Family and Social Structure
Family and close personal relationships are important parts of ratfolk society.

Some communities organize themselves around extended families or clans, while others place
greater importance on settlement membership, professional organizations, or political
affiliations.

There is no universal ratfolk system of inheritance, marriage, family authority, or social
hierarchy.

Customs differ between communities and reflect their individual histories and circumstances.

Relations with Surface Civilizations
Ratfolk relations with surface peoples are mixed and vary considerably by region.

Prejudice against ratfolk is widespread in some surface societies and generally comes from a
combination of historical superstition, cultural misunderstanding, and deliberate political
exploitation.

Common stereotypes associate ratfolk with:

   ·   Disease and filth.
   ·   Theft and dishonesty.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 13 / 40>
   ·   Criminal activity.
   ·   Untrustworthiness.
   ·   Unwanted underground activity.

These beliefs are not inherent truths about ratfolk. They are prejudices that have developed and
persisted among certain surface populations.

Political and economic interests can also contribute to their mistreatment. Ratfolk may be
exploited for labor, denied certain rights, restricted from particular settlements, or blamed for
problems they did not cause.

Treatment varies by region. Some surface communities trade peacefully with ratfolk or accept
them as neighbors. Others discriminate against them, segregate them, exploit them, or openly
persecute them.

Ratfolk are not inherently criminal, evil, or hostile toward surface civilizations.

Secretive Organizations and Sects
Secretive organizations and sects exist within ratfolk society.

Some may operate within individual settlements, while others may maintain connections across
multiple communities.

Their identities, leadership, beliefs, practices, origins, and objectives have not yet been
established.

They should not be assumed to share a single purpose or ideology, nor should all secretive
organizations automatically be treated as malicious.

Historical Knowledge
Ratfolk civilizations predate many of the surface kingdoms that currently dominate the continent.

Their communities preserve their histories through different combinations of written records,
oral traditions, family accounts, and local archives.

Historical knowledge varies between settlements. Records may contradict one another, and some
communities may preserve information that has been lost elsewhere.

The earliest origins of ratfolk civilization, the complete history of the underground network, and
the development of their oldest settlements remain open areas for future worldbuilding.

Established Ratfolk Principles
The following principles define ratfolk civilization:
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 14 / 40>
   ·   Ratfolk are naturally subterranean inhabitants, not exiles from a fallen surface
       civilization.
   ·   Their communities are connected through an extensive but incomplete underground
       network.
   ·   No unified ratfolk government or central authority exists.
   ·   Individual settlements maintain their own political systems.
   ·   Ratfolk share an ancestry and cultural identity but do not possess automatic loyalty to one
       another.
   ·   Ratfolk settlements vary significantly in size, wealth, development, and customs.
   ·   Their civilization is generally high-medieval in technology, with specialized strengths in
       particular fields.
   ·   Technological and specialized knowledge is unevenly distributed.
   ·   Relations with surface civilizations vary, and prejudice is not universal.
   ·   Secretive organizations and sects exist, but their specific identities and purposes remain
       unresolved.

7.5 Other Ancestries
Halflings, gnomes, and other Pathfinder ancestries remain available for future development.

Their cultures, territories, political influence, and social standing have not yet been established.


PART V: DRAGONS & THE FALL OF
HOUSE TARGARYEN
8. HOUSE TARGARYEN
8.1 The Dragon Dynasty
House Targaryen was once the most powerful ruling dynasty on the continent.

For approximately 400 years, the Targaryens occupied the supreme royal throne known as the
Imperial Seat.

Their authority extended across much of the continent through a combination of direct rule,
subordinate kingdoms, political alliances, and military power.

The Targaryens were more than an ordinary royal family. They were the ruling dynasty of a
continent-spanning political order.

Their power rested on three foundations:

   ·   Their exclusive bloodline connection to dragons.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 15 / 40>
   ·   Their control of the Imperial Seat.
   ·   Their ability to command the loyalty, obedience, or submission of other kingdoms and
       noble houses.

The Targaryens possessed a substantial royal dynasty with multiple branches, descendants, and
collateral family lines.

They were considered superior in status to the other great houses.

Their family identity was built around ambition, pride, bloodline, and the preservation of their
power. They believed their lineage was exceptional and that their authority was justified by their
unique relationship with dragons.

However, Targaryen rulers varied considerably. Some were capable and respected monarchs,
while others were cruel, unstable, or destructive. The dynasty should not be remembered as
uniformly evil or uniformly benevolent.

8.2 Heraldry and House Words
Heraldry: A three-headed dragon, using black and red as its primary colors.

House Words:

"Blood, Fire, and Dominion."

The three-headed dragon represents the dynasty's identity, its royal bloodline, and its historic
dominion over the continent.

8.3 The Imperial Seat
The Imperial Seat was the supreme royal throne of the continent.

It was a physical seat of political authority, not merely a ceremonial title or crown.

For approximately 400 years, the Targaryen monarchs occupied it as the highest ruling authority
in the continent's political order.

The Imperial Seat was located in a separate royal capital, distinct from Dragonstone.

Following the Great Rebellion, the Targaryens lost control of the Imperial Seat. It remains
occupied by a different ruling dynasty under the changed political arrangement.

The current dynasty occupying the Imperial Seat has not yet been named.

The city surrounding the Imperial Seat has not yet been named.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 16 / 40>
8.4 Dragonstone
Dragonstone served three major purposes for House Targaryen:

   ·   The ancestral homeland of the dynasty.
   ·   The primary royal residence and seat of the family.
   ·   The principal location associated with dragon breeding and keeping.

Although the Imperial Seat represented supreme political authority, the Targaryen monarch spent
much of their time at Dragonstone.

The monarch traveled to the royal capital when political affairs required their presence.

The existence of dragons made travel between Dragonstone and the capital considerably easier.

Dragonstone was therefore the familial, political, and military heart of the Targaryen dynasty.

Following the Great Rebellion, House Baratheon claimed Dragonstone and its symbolic
importance.

8.5 Targaryen Government and Authority
The Targaryens did not govern the entire continent through a single uniform administrative
system.

Their authority operated through a combination of direct and indirect control.

In territories directly ruled by the dynasty, Targaryen-appointed governors and administrators
exercised royal authority.

Other kingdoms retained their own rulers, crowns, and internal political structures while
acknowledging the supremacy of the Imperial Seat.

Methods of maintaining authority included:

   ·   Sworn allegiance and tribute.
   ·   Political marriages.
   ·   Hostages and royal wards.
   ·   Military garrisons.
   ·   Royal governors and administrators.
   ·   Subordinate kings who retained their own crowns.
   ·   Military intervention when a kingdom challenged royal authority.

This arrangement allowed the Targaryens to control a large portion of the continent directly
while maintaining influence over almost every other kingdom.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 17 / 40>
Over time, the dynasty increasingly sought to centralize authority and reduce the independence
of the great houses.

This growing centralization contributed directly to the resentment that eventually produced the
Great Rebellion.

9. THE TARGARYEN DRAGON BLOODLINE
9.1 The Ability to Bond
The ability to bond with dragons was exclusive to individuals possessing the proper Targaryen
blood.

It was not an ability that could be learned, purchased, or obtained through ordinary magical
training.

The dragon-riding bloodline was restricted to the original Targaryen family lineage.

For the bloodline to remain eligible, reproduction had to occur between members of the eligible
Targaryen bloodline.

If an eligible Targaryen reproduced with someone outside that bloodline, their descendants
would not retain the ability to bond with dragons.

Consequently, Targaryen marriages were heavily influenced by the preservation of the dragon-
riding bloodline.

This contributed to generations of close-bloodline marriages within the dynasty.

9.2 The Dragon Bond
Each rider could bond with only one dragon.

The bond created a telepathic connection between rider and dragon.

Through this connection, the rider could communicate with and influence the dragon.

The bond was an exclusive supernatural relationship.

The exact limits of the bond, how it developed, and whether it could be established under
unusual circumstances remain to be developed.

9.3 Dragon Ownership
At the height of Targaryen power, the dynasty possessed exactly twelve dragons.

Dragon ownership was tightly controlled.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 18 / 40>
Only the reigning monarch and individuals specifically designated as heirs or successors were
normally permitted to claim dragons.

Other members of the royal family could possess the necessary blood without being granted
permission to claim a dragon.

This restriction helped preserve the monarch's control over the dynasty's greatest military
advantage.

10. THE NATURE OF DRAGONS
Dragons in this setting are enormous flying reptilian predators.

They possess tremendous physical strength, devastating attacks, and the ability to breathe fire.

They are not traditional Pathfinder dragons.

They are not:

   ·   Spellcasters.
   ·   Ancient magical scholars.
   ·   Sophisticated talking creatures.
   ·   Civilization-building intelligent beings.

Their danger comes from their physical capabilities, size, speed, flight, and fire.

They were once among the most powerful military assets in the known world.

Their existence fundamentally changed warfare and political authority.

The Targaryen bond is supernatural, but the dragons themselves are not traditional spellcasting
creatures.

Their eventual Pathfinder 1e statistics will be developed to reflect this interpretation while
remaining mechanically compatible with the system.

11. THE GREAT REBELLION
11.1 The Six Great Houses
Six great houses united against House Targaryen.

Their rebellion was driven by two closely connected concerns:

 1.    The Targaryens had become too powerful, and the other great houses feared permanent
       subordination.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 19 / 40>
 2.    The Targaryens increasingly sought to centralize political authority, threatening the
       independence and privileges of the great houses.

The rebellion was not simply a dispute over succession.

It was a coordinated attempt to destroy the existing Targaryen political order and eliminate the
dynasty's ability to command dragons.

House Edarth has been established as one of the six rebellious great houses.

The identities and histories of the remaining rebellious houses will be developed separately.

The current count remains six rebellious great houses in total. House Edarth is one of those six.

11.2 House Baratheon and the Rebellion
House Baratheon was the principal leader and mastermind behind the rebellion.

The house developed a specialized weapon designed to kill dragons: an enormous dragon-slaying
ballista.

The development of this weapon was a major strategic advantage.

House Baratheon wanted exclusive control over the weapon's design and production.

The ballistas were designed to penetrate dragon scales reliably, giving ordinary military forces a
means of killing creatures that had previously dominated the battlefield.

11.3 The Dragon-Slaying Ballistas
The dragon-slaying ballistas were developed and manufactured exclusively by House Baratheon.

Their manufacturing methods were closely guarded.

The weapons were devastating but had important limitations.

Strengths:

   ·   Capable of penetrating dragon scales.
   ·   Extremely dangerous against large targets.
   ·   Effective in castle defense.
   ·   Effective against cavalry charges.
   ·   Capable of threatening otherwise overwhelming military forces.

Weaknesses:

   ·   Heavy construction.
   ·   Slow aiming and repositioning.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 20 / 40>
   ·   Poor effectiveness against fast-moving targets.
   ·   Difficult to deploy effectively outside prepared defensive positions.

The weapons survived the rebellion.

Some remain in military service, primarily as defensive siege weapons protecting castles and
important fortifications.

Their continued existence represents an important part of House Baratheon's military legacy.

With dragons believed extinct, their primary purpose has shifted toward conventional defensive
warfare.

11.4 The War
The Great Rebellion lasted only a few years but was devastating.

The six houses understood that defeating the Targaryens required eliminating their dragon riders.

Their strategy focused on killing eligible Targaryen members first, thereby breaking the bonds
between riders and dragons.

Once the riders were killed, the dragons became separated from the people with whom they had
bonded.

Most dragons were killed during the conflict.

Those that survived and were not captured or killed fled after losing their riders.

Their ultimate fate remains unknown.

No one knows whether any surviving dragons still exist.

11.5 The Fall of the Dynasty
The final battles resulted in the destruction of House Targaryen's ruling family.

Nearly the entire royal family was killed.

The Imperial Seat fell, ending approximately 400 years of Targaryen rule.

The surviving Targaryen dragon-riding bloodline was believed to have been eliminated.

The six victorious houses believe the dynasty is extinct.

They do not currently fear the return of a Targaryen claimant.

House Baratheon claimed Dragonstone after the rebellion.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 21 / 40>
The Imperial Seat passed into the hands of a different ruling dynasty.

The exact political settlement following the rebellion, including the current relationships among
the six houses and the new ruling dynasty, remains to be developed.

The following family history was created for the campaign and is established working canon

Their children included:

   ·   Aerovar III Targaryen.
   ·   Prince Vaedric Targaryen.
   ·   Princess Maereth Targaryen.

Otheryn II and Vaelora both died before the Great Rebellion.

Prince Daevor Targaryen

12.2 King Aerovar III Targaryen
Age at death: 46
Length of reign: 22 years
Wife: Queen Seralyne Targaryen

Aerovar III was the last Targaryen monarch to occupy the Imperial Seat.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 22 / 40>
He was intelligent, disciplined, and politically capable. He could be ruthless when necessary, but
he was not inherently cruel or tyrannical.

He believed the continent required stronger central authority and that the kingdoms should be
more accountable to the Imperial Seat.

His reign included efforts to:

   ·   Maintain and improve royal roads.
   ·   Strengthen the royal administration.
   ·   Improve taxation and royal revenue.
   ·   Reduce excessive noble privileges.
   ·   Increase the authority of the Imperial government.

His policies were intended to strengthen the realm, but he underestimated how threatened the
great houses felt by his attempts at centralization.

He was a strict father who prepared his eldest son for kingship.

He loved his family but rarely behaved like an ordinary father.

Aerovar died during the fall of the Imperial Seat.

The exact circumstances of his death remain undecided.

12.3 Queen Seralyne Targaryen
Age at death: 40
Parents: Prince Daevor Targaryen and Princess Maerith Targaryen

Seralyne was Aerovar's first cousin.

She was politically intelligent and more diplomatic than her husband.

She understood that the great houses needed to retain a degree of independence if the dynasty
was to survive.

She attempted to moderate Aerovar's centralizing policies and preserve relationships with the
nobility.

She was particularly protective of her children.

Seralyne and her three younger children were at Dragonstone during the siege.

All four were killed during the fall of Dragonstone.

12.4 The Four Children of Aerovar III and Seralyne
The Crown Prince

Age at the beginning of the rebellion: 9

The eldest child of Aerovar III and Seralyne.

He was the unquestioned heir to the Imperial Seat and was being prepared to become the next
Targaryen monarch.

He survived the destruction of Dragonstone and escaped the rebellion.

His personal name remains reserved for the player.

His dragon-bonding process had begun but was not complete. He was still a child and had not
fully established the bond.

When he was separated from his dragon during the fall of the dynasty, the dragon fled.

The dragon's ultimate fate remains unknown.

Prince Caedran Targaryen

Age at death: 7

Energetic and adventurous.

He admired his older brother and was close to him.

Killed during the siege of Dragonstone.

Princess Elyra Targaryen

Age at death: 5

Quiet, observant, and curious about history.
