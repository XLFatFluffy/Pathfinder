# Canon Register

This file tracks major setting decisions and their status. It does not replace detailed records in the Campaign Bible.

## Status definitions

- **PROPOSED:** Under consideration; not canon.
- **APPROVED:** Accepted direction; details may remain incomplete.
- **ESTABLISHED:** Confirmed setting fact.
- **REJECTED:** Considered and declined.
- **REVISED:** Previously established material deliberately changed.
- **UNRESOLVED:** An open question that must remain unanswered.

## Continuity rules

1. **Originality:** The imported Bible retains names and historical scaffolding from Game of Thrones. New material must use original identities and substantially rework the underlying histories, not merely rename copied material.
2. **Gods and Pathfinder cosmology:** The Pathfinder gods are real. Their deeper lore and the details of divine magic remain open for development.
3. **Map status:** The uploaded atlas is a working visual reference only. Its names, borders, terrain, and exact geography are not final canon.
4. **Technology boundary:** High-medieval technology is the approved baseline. Industrial and steampunk technology are excluded unless the GM explicitly revises this decision.

## Stage 5 — Political Atlas: Approved Regional Framework (2026-11-04)

**Status: ESTABLISHED.** The GM approved the Political Atlas package, including broad territorial spheres, principal seats, internal regions, named settlements, the Southern Reaches framework, minor-house categories, principal trade routes, and Great House economic dependencies. Exact surveyed borders, coordinates, travel times, and some local jurisdictions remain unresolved.

- **House Veyr — Northern Marches:** controls the majority of the northern frontier. Principal seat: **Frosthold**. Regions: Frostlands, Northern Holds, Pine Marches, Southern Marches. Settlements: Wintermere, Greywatch, Pinecross. Maintains the Northern Watch, Marcher Companies, and Household Guard.
- **House Vaelorian — Western Highlands:** controls western highlands, mountain valleys, forests, mineral-rich lands, and important western trade routes. Principal seat: **Aurelith**. Regions: High Peaks, Green Valleys, Western Passes. Settlements: Silverfall, Thornvale, Goldmere.
- **House Edarth — Western Heartlands:** controls western-central plains, agricultural valleys, inland roads, and defensible approaches toward the central heartlands. Principal seat: **Stoneguard**. Regions: Golden Fields, Riverlands, Western Borderlands, Southern Estates. Settlements: Harveston, Riverrun, Westfield.
- **House Calderan — Central Crownlands:** principal Crown territory is inland in the central river basin, near major confluences and central routes. Capital and principal seat: **Valedorn**, an old fortified river city and location of the Imperial Seat. Its districts are Imperial Quarter, Council District, Old City, Merchant Quarter, and Outer City. Crown subdivisions: Royal Basin, King's Reach, Southern Crownlands. Settlements: Kingsbridge, Highmere, Dunwall.
- **House Kharadorn — Inland Sea and Dragonstone:** controls Dragonstone, the central inland sea, and surrounding territory. Khar-Dum remains its separate northeastern ancestral homeland. Dragonstone's historic fabric includes Ancient Foundation, Royal Remains, and Dwarven Occupation layers. Settlements: Ironharbor, Stonewake, Deepwatch. Dwarven military authorities maintain sea control while surface communities and merchant guilds conduct much everyday commerce. The underground access to Dragonstone remains a GM-only secret.
- **House Merrowen — Eastern Coast:** controls an extended eastern maritime territory, without automatically controlling every eastern port. Principal seat: **Tidemark**. Regions: Northern Coast, Eastern Shore, Great Delta, Southern Coast. Settlements: Eastwatch, Saltmere, Deltaport, Stormhaven.
- **Southern Reaches:** remain politically fragmented. Southwestern Marches contain arid highlands, dry valleys, plateaus, and oases; Southern Principalities contain fertile lowlands, forests, rivers, small hereditary kingdoms, and independent cities; Southern Islands contain island chains, sheltered harbors, fishing settlements, and maritime principalities. Established settlement names: Sablecross, Brightwater, Marrowgate, Duskport.
- **Minor-house categories:** Banner Houses (lesser families sworn to a Great House), Independent Lords (minor houses outside direct Great House rule), and Crown Vassals (lesser nobles owing allegiance directly to the monarch). These are descriptive administrative categories, not necessarily formal titles. Minor houses remain locally important but continentally insignificant.
- **Principal trade routes:** Northern Road, Western Mountain Road, King's Road, Inland Sea Route, Eastern Coastal Road, Southern Trade Road. Their exact courses remain to be mapped.
- **Economic dependencies:** Veyr supplies northern resources and frontier security but needs grain and support; Vaelorian supplies metals, minerals, financing, and trade connections but needs food, labor, and maritime access; Edarth supplies grain, livestock, manpower, and military strength but needs metals and manufactured goods; Calderan supplies royal administration, legal authority, and recognition but needs revenue and cooperation; Kharadorn supplies metalwork, weapons, engineering, and inland-sea control but needs food and surface commerce; Merrowen supplies shipping, naval power, and maritime trade but needs raw materials, food, and inland markets.

**Valedorn and the Imperial Seat:** Valedorn is established as the current royal capital. The Imperial Seat is both a physical throne and a political institution and symbol representing royal authority, dynastic legitimacy, and the post-Rebellion settlement. The palace developed around an older royal fortress and contains architectural layers from successive dynasties.

**Still unresolved:** precise borders and coordinates; exact courses of rivers and roads; map scale calibration; population and travel times; individual minor-house names and holdings; the jurisdiction of every port, island, and coastal settlement; the precise appearance and construction history of the Imperial Seat; and detailed military/legal arrangements between territories.


## Stage 6 — Imperial Court and Government (2026-11-04)

**Status: ESTABLISHED.** The GM approved the proposed structure of the Crown, Imperial Council, royal household, court factions, and current political climate.

- **Royal offices:** Royal Chancellor (administration and decrees); Master of Coin (revenue, taxation, debts, treasury); Master of Laws (justice and Crown law); Master of War (royal military and defense); Royal Spymaster (intelligence and covert information-gathering); Lord Chamberlain (royal household, ceremonies, access to the monarch, palace administration). These officers serve the Crown, not the Great Houses. Powerful houses and factions seek influence over appointments.
- **Royal forces:** The Crown maintains a permanent household guard and a distinct royal military force. They can protect Valedorn and support ordinary royal authority but are not presumed capable of defeating all six Great Houses together.
- **Imperial Council:** Six permanent seats, one appointed by each Great House. The representative need not be the house ruler. The monarch presides but holds no additional Great House seat. The Council addresses inter-house disputes, extraordinary taxation, major military levies and wars, significant treaties, royal succession recognition, Crown-dwarven relations, and laws affecting Great House privileges. It is an aristocratic negotiating institution, not a modern parliament. Exact procedures and legal powers remain unresolved.
- **Court factions:** Royal Court (stability and compromise); Traditionalists (Great House rights and independence); Royal Expansionists (stronger Crown); Merchant Interests (commerce and financial stability); Religious Establishment (institutional religious authority). These are overlapping political movements, not necessarily formal organizations. Membership is not determined by house affiliation.
- **Valedorn:** Established as the principal arena for Crown administration, Great House diplomacy, merchants, religious authorities, and factional competition. Its river port connects the inland capital to wider trade networks.
- **Current climate:** The monarch seeks to strengthen royal authority without provoking rebellion; Great Houses uphold the settlement publicly while competing privately; Kharadorn remains independent and distrustful; Vaelorian and Merrowen compete commercially; Edarth and Veyr resist centralization for different reasons; the Religious Establishment is gaining court influence; southern territories remain difficult for the Crown to control.

**Still unresolved:** Monarch and officeholders; royal force composition; Council voting, quorum, vetoes, and legal procedure; representatives; faction leadership, membership, and relative influence; specific religious institutions and doctrines.


## Stage 7 — Religion, Faith, and Religious Institutions (2026-11-04)

**Status: ESTABLISHED.** The GM approved the Stage 7 framework for worship, religious institutions, the Religious Establishment, independent scholarship, divine spellcasters, Great House religious character, and religious tensions.

- The Pathfinder pantheon is broadly recognized across the continent. Worship varies by region, culture, community, and individual; no single god is established as universally dominant. Individuals may pray to different gods for different needs.
- There is no single supreme religious authority. Major faiths maintain independent hierarchies, institutions, traditions, and internal disputes.
- Temples, monasteries, shrines, cathedrals, religious orders, and clerical courts are recognized institutional forms, with variable presence and authority.
- Clergy and divine spellcasters are distinct. Priests may serve without magic; not every temple worker is a spellcaster. Divine spellcasters remain uncommon and devote themselves to a god. Their abilities are significant and may inspire respect, fear, or suspicion, but magical ability does not automatically confer senior religious office.
- Religious institutions may own land and wealth, provide charity and education, advise rulers, influence ceremonies and public expectations, and condemn rulers or teachings. They do not universally govern secular territories, command Great House armies, or automatically overrule secular law.
- The Religious Establishment is a politically influential but divided court faction/network. Senior religious authorities may advise the monarch and maintain a recognized court presence, but they have no automatic Imperial Council seat; its six seats represent the Great Houses.
- An independent scholarly tradition exists, institutionally distinct from religious hierarchies but able to cooperate with them. Scholars study fields including history, genealogy, mathematics, medicine, astronomy, law, languages, natural philosophy, theology, and religious history. Scholars need not be clergy or spellcasters.
- The Great Houses have broad religious tendencies but no exclusive patron deity or uniform doctrine: Vaelorian emphasizes traditions, temple patronage, and scholarship; Kharadorn maintains strong Torag and clan traditions; Edarth values traditional worship, public ceremonies, and oaths; Calderan cultivates religious legitimacy; Veyr's faith is shaped by hardship and frontier danger; Merrowen's customs reflect sailors, travel, trade, and maritime dangers.
- Religious tensions include religious versus secular authority, competing interpretations, institutional wealth, accusations of heresy, and questions surrounding divine power. Religious life is varied and is not uniformly fanatical or corrupt.

**Still unresolved:** How gods grant or mediate divine magic; major religious institutions and leaders; regional prominence of particular gods; the scholarly order's name, ranks, and governance; legal relationships between religious and secular courts; the exact influence of religion over marriage, inheritance, coronation, and secular law; and the history of major religious disputes.

## Table and Rules Philosophy (2026-11-04)

**Status: ESTABLISHED CAMPAIGN STANDARD.** Pathfinder First Edition is the mechanical baseline. The campaign will be strict and consistent with the published rules unless the GM explicitly approves a house rule.

- Player creativity and ambition should be supported within the rules. The GM should adjudicate unusual plans fairly rather than rejecting them merely because they are unconventional.
- Setting conditions may affect what is available: knowledge, materials, teachers, markets, legal permissions, time, and money can all matter. These are world circumstances, not undisclosed changes to character mechanics.
- Do not invent additional mechanical prerequisites, penalties, or restrictions for convenience. Any house rule must be discussed and explicitly approved.
- NPCs operate under the same mechanical framework as PCs. Their capabilities should have a plausible rules-based explanation.
- Consequences should arise from established rules, the setting, and the choices of the PCs—not arbitrary permission gates or plot protection.
