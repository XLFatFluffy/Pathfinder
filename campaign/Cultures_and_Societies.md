# Cultures and Societies Framework

**Status:** GM-approved worldbuilding expansion  
**Baseline:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC

This document establishes broad cultural patterns without turning them into rigid stereotypes. Individuals, families, cities, and social classes can differ from regional norms.

## 1. Continental cultural principles

Most people identify themselves through several overlapping loyalties:

- household and family;
- village, town, or city;
- local lord or charter;
- faith;
- occupation or guild;
- ancestry;
- Great House or Crown;
- region.

A person may therefore be loyal to a local lord while distrusting the Great House above that lord, worship one god while observing another god's festival, and identify as a merchant before identifying with a noble's political faction.

## 2. Crownlands and central river culture

The central Crownlands are comparatively administratively connected.

Common tendencies:

- respect for royal law and written charters;
- mixed populations drawn toward markets and roads;
- strong distinction between noble privilege and common civic rights;
- formal public ceremonies around royal events;
- practical religious pluralism;
- emphasis on contracts, tolls, roads, bridges, and reliable administration.

Valedorn's people are accustomed to seeing nobles, merchants, soldiers, clergy, scholars, and foreign travelers in the same city.

A common central proverb is:

**"A road is worth more than a promise if you can keep it open."**

## 3. Edarth heartland culture

The Western Heartlands emphasize land, harvest, duty, and military obligation.

Common tendencies:

- family farms and landed estates are central social institutions;
- oaths and personal reputation carry great weight;
- military service is respected;
- harvest celebrations are major community events;
- wasting food or failing to prepare for winter is socially condemned;
- disputes over land, pasture, and customary rights are taken seriously;
- noble households are expected to provide protection in exchange for obligations.

Edarth culture tends to admire competence more than courtly sophistication.

## 4. Veyr northern culture

Northern society is shaped by distance, winter, frontier defense, and the Ancient Barrier.

Common tendencies:

- preparedness is treated as a moral virtue;
- wasting food, fuel, or equipment is regarded as foolish;
- hospitality to travelers is important because isolation can be dangerous;
- military service is respected but not romanticized;
- families maintain practical records of stores, debts, and winter obligations;
- funerals emphasize remembrance and duty to the living;
- southern court politics are often viewed with suspicion.

Northern communities value people who can be relied upon when conditions become difficult.

## 5. Vaelorian highland and elven culture

Vaelorian society contains several overlapping traditions rather than one uniform elven culture.

Common tendencies:

- long memory and careful record keeping;
- emphasis on lineage, precedent, education, and craftsmanship;
- wealth is often displayed through patronage, architecture, art, scholarship, and carefully maintained estates rather than crude excess;
- long-lived elves may regard human political cycles as unusually rapid;
- commercial success is compatible with aristocratic identity;
- older elven families may value customary land use while newer commercial branches favor contracts and consolidation;
- public insults can be remembered for decades even when the immediate dispute appears trivial to outsiders.

Vaelorian elites often think in terms of decades rather than seasons.

## 6. Kharadorn dwarven culture

Dwarven society is organized around clan identity, ancestry, craft, oath, and reputation.

Common tendencies:

- clan history is treated as a living obligation;
- craftsmanship is both practical work and a source of honor;
- promises and contracts are taken seriously;
- a person's reputation is linked to the reliability of their work and word;
- ancestral records matter;
- disputes may be settled through formal testimony, precedent, compensation, or clan arbitration;
- surface dwarves are uncommon outside major trade or political centers;
- dwarves generally prefer durable construction and long-term planning.

The Kharadorn claim of descent from Torag is an important dynastic tradition, but dwarves do not universally interpret it as proof of literal divine ancestry.

## 7. Merrowen maritime culture

Coastal and maritime communities place high value on practical knowledge.

Common tendencies:

- sailors learn weather, tides, currents, navigation, and harbor customs;
- hospitality is especially important during storms;
- ship crews often develop strong internal hierarchies;
- merchants and nobles interact more frequently than in isolated inland communities;
- foreign languages and customs are less unusual in major ports;
- sailors may maintain private superstitions even when formally religious;
- harbor rights and pilotage traditions can be politically important.

Merrowen culture is commercially cosmopolitan without being culturally uniform.

## 8. Southern cultures

The Southern Reaches are deliberately fragmented.

### Southwestern Marches

Communities are shaped by dry conditions, wells, caravan movement, and fortified settlements.

Important social expectations include:

- respect for water rights;
- hospitality toward travelers who arrive in good faith;
- careful protection of wells and cisterns;
- strong local independence;
- negotiation between settled communities and caravan interests.

### Southern Principalities

The fertile principalities vary considerably.

Some are dominated by hereditary nobles, others by city councils, merchants, or strong local families.

Common features include:

- elaborate local customs;
- competitive courts;
- river commerce;
- strong city identities;
- shifting alliances;
- political independence from the Crown.

### Southern Islands

Island communities are shaped by maritime distance.

Common tendencies:

- ship ownership carries social prestige;
- harbor customs are closely guarded;
- local captains may possess influence disproportionate to their formal titles;
- islanders often distrust distant mainland authorities;
- fishing, pilotage, shipping, and harbor law shape daily life.

## 9. Ratfolk society

Ratfolk are not a single culture.

Established principles:

- ratfolk are naturally subterranean;
- communities are decentralized;
- different networks maintain different customs;
- surface prejudice is widespread but not universal;
- ratfolk are not inherently criminal, evil, diseased, or hostile;
- family, mutual aid, trade, survival knowledge, and local trust are important social foundations.

Ratfolk communities may maintain their own forms of:

- leadership;
- dispute resolution;
- religious practice;
- trade;
- craft specialization;
- storytelling;
- naming;
- kinship.

No single ratfolk organization speaks for all ratfolk.

## 10. Social classes

### Nobility

Nobles are expected to maintain:

- household reputation;
- hereditary legitimacy;
- obligations to lieges;
- obligations to dependents;
- military readiness appropriate to status;
- hospitality;
- marriage and inheritance arrangements.

A noble who fails these expectations may lose political support even when technically acting within the law.

### Merchants

Successful merchants are influential because they control:

- credit;
- goods;
- shipping;
- information;
- contracts;
- employment.

They do not automatically possess noble authority.

### Guilds and craftsmen

Guilds regulate training, reputation, shared facilities, and professional relationships where established.

Craft skill is socially respected, but guild membership varies by region.

### Rural communities

Farmers, laborers, shepherds, foresters, fishers, miners, and other rural workers form the economic foundation of most territories.

Local custom can be as important as formal law in determining their rights.

## 11. Hospitality and travel

A traveler who arrives peacefully and behaves respectfully is generally entitled to basic hospitality within settled communities, though the quality of that hospitality varies by wealth and region.

Noble hospitality is more formal.

Breaking the expected rules of hospitality can damage reputation and create political consequences.

This is a cultural expectation, not an absolute magical law.

## 12. Death and remembrance

Funerary practice varies by faith and region, but remembrance is broadly important.

Common customs include:

- naming ancestors;
- maintaining family records;
- grave or memorial visits;
- distributing possessions according to law and custom;
- preserving weapons, tools, jewelry, or written records associated with the dead.

Veyr and Kharadorn traditions place particularly strong emphasis on continuity and remembrance.

## 13. Festivals and calendar culture

The established calendar governs public life, agriculture, taxation, travel, and religious observances.

Major seasonal celebrations vary by region.

Harvestfall is particularly important in agricultural communities because it marks the beginning of the harvest season and the accounting of stored resources.

Urban celebrations may emphasize:

- markets;
- trade fairs;
- religious ceremonies;
- guild events;
- public games;
- noble hospitality.

## 14. Scholarly institution

The continent has an independent scholarly tradition known as the **Collegium of the Nine Lamps**.

The Collegium is a loose network of scholars, archives, tutors, translators, genealogists, mathematicians, physicians, legal historians, and natural philosophers.

It is not a single university controlling all scholarship.

### Principles

- preserve records;
- distinguish evidence from speculation;
- train competent scribes and scholars;
- maintain copies of important texts;
- provide expertise to rulers, merchants, temples, and wealthy households.

### Political position

The Collegium attempts to remain institutionally independent.

Great Houses patronize individual scholars and archives, which creates unavoidable political pressure.

The Collegium's scholars are not automatically neutral or correct. Their records can contain omissions, bias, lost sources, and competing interpretations.

## 15. Religious regional emphasis

The Pathfinder pantheon is broadly recognized, but regional emphasis varies.

Established broad tendencies:

- **Torag:** especially prominent among dwarves and craftspeople.
- **Erastil:** common among rural communities, hunters, farmers, and isolated settlements.
- **Abadar:** influential among merchants, administrators, judges, and urban institutions.
- **Pharasma:** widely respected in funerary traditions and communities concerned with death and the dead.
- **Gozreh:** especially relevant to sailors, farmers, weather, and coastal communities.
- **Iomedae:** respected among soldiers, knights, and people emphasizing duty and righteous conduct.
- **Desna:** common among travelers, sailors, wanderers, and people who place importance on freedom and dreams.
- **Sarenrae:** influential among healers, charitable communities, and people emphasizing mercy and redemption.
- **Calistria:** present in urban and social contexts, particularly among people concerned with independence, desire, revenge, and personal freedom.
- **Nethys:** respected among scholars and practitioners of magic, though arcane magic remains uncommon in ordinary life.

These are tendencies, not exclusive regional religions.

## 16. Cultural portrayal rule

When an NPC or institution acts, consider:

1. region;
2. ancestry;
3. social class;
4. occupation;
5. family tradition;
6. faith;
7. local custom;
8. political allegiance;
9. personal history.

Do not reduce a character's behavior to ancestry or regional stereotype. These factors are tools for informed GM judgment, not an algorithm for determining behavior.
