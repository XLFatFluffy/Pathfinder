# Current World State

**Status:** Current GM-approved baseline  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-03

This is a concise navigation and continuity document, not a replacement for the full Campaign Bible.

## Setting foundation

- Original fantasy setting using Pathfinder First Edition as its mechanical foundation.
- Not Golarion and not a direct adaptation of another setting.
- **High-medieval technological baseline.**
- No industrial or steampunk technological culture.
- Alchemy exists as specialized Pathfinder-based knowledge and craft; it does not imply industrialization or advanced machinery.
- Grounded medieval fantasy is the central identity. Supernatural horror exists as a supporting element, not the main theme.
- Political landscape shaped by kingdoms, noble houses, feudal obligations, warfare, and intrigue.
- Civilization survives behind walls; wilderness beyond settled areas is dangerous.
- The Pathfinder gods are real.
- Arcane spellcasters are rare; divine spellcasters are also uncommon and devote themselves to a god.
- The average person may live their entire life without witnessing a spell being cast.

## Ancestries and societies

- Humans are dominant across much of the continent.
- A dwarven empire has always existed and has never collapsed; it has never been especially massive in territorial scale. It consists of several independent clans united under the main branch, which is said to descend from Torag himself. Khar-Dum is the Kharadorns' ancestral homeland. Dragonstone was the ancestral stronghold of the fallen dragon-riding dynasty, captured by the dwarves through conquest during the Great Rebellion and retained after its destruction. Dwarves primarily inhabit mountains and underground settlements and rarely live in conventional surface stone-built settlements.
- Dwarven pride leads them to reproduce only with other dwarves as a cultural choice, not an established biological incompatibility.
- Elves possess a major political power. One of the six Great Houses is an influential elven dynasty loosely inspired by the wealthy, politically powerful noble-house archetype.
- Ratfolk are naturally subterranean, with an extensive but incomplete underground network and decentralized settlements.
- Prejudice against ratfolk is widespread across surface societies, although a few communities tolerate or openly accept them. Ratfolk are not inherently criminal, evil, or hostile.
- Other Pathfinder ancestries may exist; none are designated rare or uncommon by default. The continent remains human-dominated.

## Northern mystery

- A harsh, sparsely populated northern wilderness lies beyond the settled territories.
- The Frozen Expanse remains largely unexplored.
- The Ancient Barrier separates settled northern territories from the Frozen Expanse.
- Its builders and original purpose are unknown.
- The northern great house maintains surviving portions of the Barrier.

## Political and historical baseline

- A dragon-riding dynasty held the Imperial Seat for approximately 400 years and possessed exactly twelve dragons at its height.
- Eligible Targaryen blood was required to bond with a dragon; each rider could bond with only one dragon through a telepathic connection. Maintaining eligibility required reproduction within the eligible bloodline.
- Six Great Houses rebelled both to preserve their political independence against increasing centralization and to eliminate dragon-riding as the source of Targaryen supremacy. These were connected objectives: without dragons, the dynasty could no longer enforce its overwhelming power.
- The rebellion lasted a few years and occurred approximately 200–300 years before the present campaign; its exact calendar year remains unresolved. The dwarven Great House was its principal mastermind and developed specialized dragon-slaying ballistas. Most dragons were killed; surviving dragons fled after losing their riders, and their fate remains unknown.
- The Targaryen ruling family was nearly exterminated and the Imperial Seat fell. The dwarven leader and the Kharadorn house held power in the immediate aftermath. The same dwarven leader ruled for more than a century, until his death. His successor inherited his authority, but a succession crisis followed. The successor threatened war unless a human was placed on the Imperial Seat. The head of another of the original six rebel Great Houses was installed as a compromise candidate and became the current monarch, but he is a puppet. The dwarven successor reluctantly accepted the arrangement, returned to Dragonstone, and continued ruling the dwarven clans. The dwarven empire and human monarchy maintain an uneasy peace marked by mutual distrust. War would not favor the dwarves, but they are not afraid of that prospect. House Calderan is the established ruling house and supplied the compromise monarch. The monarch's controller or controllers remain unresolved. The victorious dwarves claimed Dragonstone, now occupied by the main branch of the dwarven clans, which is said to descend from Torag himself.
- One nine-year-old Crown Prince survived the destruction of Dragonstone. His survival is concealed; he is a player character.

The legacy labels House Targaryen and House Kharadorn remain historical working labels pending replacement. The approved Great Houses are Vaelorian (elven), Kharadorn (dwarven), Edarth (primarily human), Calderan (human ruling dynasty), Veyr (human northern dynasty), and Merrowen (primarily human maritime dynasty). Their words, sigils, identities, political aims, and relationships are recorded in the Campaign Bible. Khar-Dum is the Kharadorns' ancestral homeland; Dragonstone was the Targaryen dynasty's ancestral stronghold, taken by dwarven conquest during the Great Rebellion. The approved geographic foundations are: a vast continent substantially larger than Westeros; an enormous central inland sea; Dragonstone as an island connected to the mainland by a narrow natural land bridge; a secret dwarven underground tunnel system providing another route to Dragonstone; Khar-Dum in the northeastern mountain system; and substantial, clearly defined territories for each Great House. Exact dimensions, borders, house seats, and geographic names remain unresolved.

## Imperial Seat and Current Monarchy
The human monarch's family has a legitimate claim to the Imperial Seat. The monarch retains substantial authority, but the Great Houses can constrain him. A council or coalition of Great Houses influences him, while several competing factions pull him in different directions. The other rebel Great Houses regard the throne as an opportunity to manipulate imperial policy for their own interests. The identities and relative influence of these factions remain unresolved.


## Approved Great Houses

The six Great Houses and their core identities are established:
- **House Vaelorian:** Elven commercial and financial power; “Wealth Endures. Power Remembers.”
- **House Kharadorn:** Dwarven imperial dynasty associated with the main clan branch; “Stone Remembers. Blood Endures.” Khar-Dum is its ancestral homeland. Dragonstone was taken from the fallen dragon-riding dynasty by conquest.
- **House Edarth:** Primarily human, traditional landed and martial power; “Stand When Others Fall.”
- **House Calderan:** Human ruling dynasty with an older hereditary claim to the Imperial Seat; “By Right, We Remain.”
- **House Veyr:** Human northern dynasty responsible for maintaining the Ancient Barrier; “Beyond the Wall, We Stand.”
- **House Merrowen:** Primarily human maritime and naval power; “All Roads Reach the Sea.”

Their sigils, detailed identities, political goals, and relationship matrix are in the Campaign Bible. Their exact territories, seats, current heads, and local geography are not yet established.


**Ruling-house clarification:** House Calderan is the established human ruling dynasty and was one of the six original rebel Great Houses. The identity of the individuals and factions controlling or influencing its puppet monarch remains unresolved.


**Historical naming update:** House Kharadorn is the approved original name for the dwarven Great House formerly referenced by the legacy placeholder House Baratheon. Khar-Dum is the Kharadorns' ancestral homeland; Dragonstone is their conquered former Targaryen stronghold.


## Approved continental geography foundations (2026-11-03)
- The continent is vast and substantially larger than Westeros, following the larger scale implied by the provisional atlas. Exact dimensions and travel distances remain unmeasured.
- The enormous central inland sea is retained.
- Dragonstone, the provisional name of the fallen dragon-riding dynasty's ancestral stronghold, is an island within the inland sea. A narrow natural land bridge is the only known surface route onto or off the island without dragons.
- **GM-ONLY SECRET:** The dwarves have constructed an underground tunnel system providing another route to and from Dragonstone. The rest of the world does not know this system exists. Its route, entrances, extent, construction history, and who among the dwarves knows about it remain unresolved.
- Khar-Dum, the Kharadorns' ancestral homeland, lies in the northeastern mountain system. It is separate from Dragonstone.
- Each Great House controls a substantial, clearly defined territory. Smaller noble houses, independent settlements, and lesser political powers also exist across the continent.
- Exact dimensions, map scale, territorial borders, house seats, and final geographic names remain unresolved.

## Revised Continental Geography and Minor Houses — 2026-11-03

The continent is deliberately smaller than Westeros. Approved working dimensions for the revised map are approximately 1,700 miles north to south and 1,300 miles east to west at its widest point, subject to modest cartographic adjustment. Preserve the atlas's recognizable geographic identity: frozen northern wilderness; northern mountains and forests; western highlands and forests; central plains and river valleys; enormous central inland sea; northeastern mountains; eastern forests and river delta; southwestern arid lands; and southern peninsulas and island chains. The old scale bar is superseded, and the revised geography should be physically coherent.

The central inland sea supports substantial shipping, naval commerce, and important ports. The southern reaches remain politically fragmented, with room for lesser kingdoms, independent city-states, minor houses, and territories beyond the direct control of the six Great Houses. Khar-Dum remains in the northeastern mountains. The dwarven empire's visible surface territory should appear comparatively limited; its true reach is primarily subterranean and poorly understood by outsiders. Dragonstone remains a separate conquered island, with its narrow land bridge and secret dwarven underground route.

**Minor houses are an established category:** wealthy lords and ladies with relatively small territories and local holdings. They possess wealth and local standing but are nearly politically insignificant in continent-wide affairs. They do not rival the Great Houses or automatically hold Imperial Council seats. Their names, number, allegiances, and holdings remain undecided.

The previously suggested specific regional placements for the six Great Houses remain proposals until the revised map is reviewed.

### Regional details
- **Veyr:** Frostlands, Northern Holds, Pine Marches, Southern Marches. Settlements: Wintermere, Greywatch, Pinecross. Its permanent frontier force is the Northern Watch, supported by mobile Marcher Companies and the House Guard.
- **Vaelorian:** High Peaks, Green Valleys, Western Passes. Settlements: Silverfall, Thornvale, Goldmere. Its ruling family directly controls its most valuable mines, major commercial settlements, and important passes, while older elven families retain estates and privileges.
- **Edarth:** Golden Fields, Riverlands, Western Borderlands, Southern Estates. Settlements: Harveston, Riverrun, Westfield. Its decentralized landed structure relies on lesser families, agricultural production, heavy infantry, levies, and household cavalry.
- **Calderan:** Valedorn is the old fortified inland capital at a major river confluence and the location of the Imperial Seat. Its districts are Imperial Quarter, Council District, Old City, Merchant Quarter, and Outer City. Crown subdivisions: Royal Basin, King's Reach, Southern Crownlands. Other settlements: Kingsbridge, Highmere, Dunwall.
- **Kharadorn:** Dragonstone is the conquered former dragon-riding stronghold, distinct from Khar-Dum, the ancestral northeastern homeland. Dragonstone's surviving architecture contains Ancient Foundation, Royal Remains, and Dwarven Occupation layers. Other settlements: Ironharbor, Stonewake, Deepwatch. Dwarven military authorities maintain inland-sea control; surface communities and merchant guilds conduct much everyday commerce.
- **Merrowen:** Northern Coast, Eastern Shore, Great Delta, Southern Coast. Settlements: Eastwatch, Saltmere, Deltaport, Stormhaven. Tidemark is a major natural-harbor city and naval/commercial seat. Merrowen's extended eastern maritime territory does not imply control over every eastern port.

### Southern Reaches and minor nobility
The Southern Reaches remain politically fragmented into the Southwestern Marches, Southern Principalities, and Southern Islands. Small principalities, hereditary kingdoms, independent city-states, island powers, and minor houses exist beyond direct Great House control. Established settlement names include Sablecross, Brightwater, Marrowgate, and Duskport.

Minor-house descriptions include Banner Houses (sworn to a Great House), Independent Lords (outside direct Great House authority), and Crown Vassals (owing allegiance directly to the monarch). These are descriptive categories rather than necessarily formal titles. Minor houses have local wealth and standing but remain nearly insignificant in continent-wide politics.

### Routes and dependencies
Principal trade corridors are the Northern Road, Western Mountain Road, King's Road, Inland Sea Route, Eastern Coastal Road, and Southern Trade Road. Their precise courses remain uncharted.

Veyr depends on southern grain and support; Vaelorian on food, labor, and maritime access; Edarth on metals, manufactured goods, and commercial access; Calderan on taxes and political/military cooperation; Kharadorn on food and surface commerce; and Merrowen on raw materials, food, and inland markets. Their complementary strengths create practical interdependence as well as political leverage.

### Imperial capital
Valedorn is the established capital of the current monarchy. The Imperial Seat is both a physical throne and a political institution/symbol representing royal authority, dynastic legitimacy, and the post-Rebellion settlement. The palace grew around an older royal fortress and reflects successive dynastic building periods.

**Remaining open:** exact borders, coordinates, river and road courses, travel times, population, individual minor houses, local port jurisdictions, and the precise appearance/history of the Imperial Seat. The map's scale bar remains uncalibrated and should not be used to calculate distances.


## Stage 6 — Government and Court (Established, 2026-11-04)

The current monarchy governs through royal officers, a permanent household guard, and a distinct royal military force. The established royal offices are Royal Chancellor, Master of Coin, Master of Laws, Master of War, Royal Spymaster, and Lord Chamberlain. These officials serve the Crown, though Great Houses and court factions seek influence over appointments. The Crown's forces can protect Valedorn and uphold ordinary royal authority but are not presumed capable of defeating all six Great Houses acting together.

The Imperial Council has six permanent seats, one appointed by each Great House. A representative need not be the house ruler. The monarch presides but holds no additional Great House seat. The Council addresses inter-house disputes, extraordinary taxation, major military levies and wars, significant treaties, recognition of royal succession, relations with the dwarven empire, and laws affecting Great House privileges. It is an aristocratic negotiating body, not a modern parliament. Its precise procedures and legal powers remain open.

Five overlapping political movements operate within the court:
- **Royal Court:** stability and compromise.
- **Traditionalists:** preservation of Great House rights and independence.
- **Royal Expansionists:** stronger royal authority and reduced Great House autonomy.
- **Merchant Interests:** protection of commerce, trade privileges, and financial stability.
- **Religious Establishment:** preservation and expansion of organized religious authority.

Faction membership is not determined by Great House affiliation. Members of one house may support different movements, and individuals may cooperate across factions on particular issues.

Valedorn is the principal arena for Crown administration, Great House diplomacy, merchants, religious authorities, and factional competition. Its river port connects the inland capital to wider trade networks.

### Current political climate
The monarch seeks to strengthen royal authority without provoking another rebellion. The Great Houses publicly uphold the post-Rebellion settlement while privately competing for influence. Kharadorn remains independent and distrustful of the Crown. Vaelorian and Merrowen compete for commercial influence. Edarth and Veyr are wary of royal centralization for different reasons. The Religious Establishment is gaining influence in court affairs. The southern territories remain difficult for the Crown to control.

**Still unresolved:** identities of the monarch and royal officeholders; exact composition of the royal forces; Council voting and legal procedures; representatives; faction leaders and membership; and the religious institutions and doctrines represented by the Religious Establishment.
