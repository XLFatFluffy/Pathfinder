# Campaign Development Roadmap

**Current milestone:** Prepare and run Session 1 on Sunday, 2026-11-08.  
**Guiding principle:** Build what play requires first. Expand the wider world as the campaign creates reasons to explore it.

## Milestone 0 — Session 1 readiness (Nov 3–8, 2026)

### Nov 3 — Scope and continuity
- Confirm the opening premise and why the two PCs are together.
- Identify first-session-critical unknowns.
- Keep the continuity records current.
- The six Great Houses, their core identities, broad territorial arrangement, and principal seats are established. Do not reopen these decisions; continue with exact borders and cartographic details as needed.

### Nov 4 — Starting region and city
- Establish the city name, location, government, and immediate political situation.
- Define its districts and 3–5 usable locations.
- Establish the local economy, ordinary life, and visible social tensions.

### Nov 5 — Cast and local power
- Create the NPCs required for the opening session.
- For each important NPC, define public role, immediate goal, leverage, what they know, what they believe, and GM-only secret if needed.

### Nov 6 — Session adventure
- Prepare the opening scene and inciting problem.
- Prepare clues, choices, obstacles, possible encounters, and consequences.
- Include more than one viable player approach.
- Prepare a flexible outline rather than a fixed sequence that assumes player compliance.

### Nov 7 — Table readiness
- Verify Pathfinder First Edition stat blocks, encounter difficulty, treasure, and rules interactions.
- Prepare GM notes, player-facing handouts, maps, and names.
- Check PC hooks and backstory continuity.
- Make a short at-table reference sheet.
- Confirm a strong stopping point or next-session hook.

### Nov 8 — Session 1
- Run the session.
- Record actual events, dates, NPC changes, player discoveries, decisions, unresolved questions, and consequences.
- Do not treat unverified rumors as objective truth.

## Milestone 1 — Stabilize the local campaign (Sessions 1–3)
- Flesh out the starting city and surrounding region in response to player choices.
- Establish recurring NPCs, local factions, allies, rivals, services, and threats.
- Track the PCs' reputation, obligations, enemies, and discoveries.
- Define the immediate political context without requiring a full continent-wide political atlas.
- Establish a usable local calendar and timeline.

**Exit condition:** The starting region supports several sessions of meaningful player-directed activity and has reliable records.

## Milestone 2 — Regional and political framework (Sessions 3–6)
- Define neighboring territories and travel routes.
- Develop the local roles of established noble houses, rulers, succession claims, resources, and alliances as they become relevant.
- Establish how local events connect to wider politics.
- Build ratfolk communities and underground connections where play makes them relevant.
- Develop regional religions and institutions. The Pathfinder gods' existence is established; the means by which divine magic is granted remains unresolved.

**Exit condition:** The party can travel beyond the starting city and encounter a coherent regional power structure.

## Milestone 3 — Wider world and historical foundations (ongoing)
- Finalize detailed continent geography and political borders. Stage 5 geography foundations are approved: the continent is approximately 1,700 miles north to south and 1,300 miles east to west at its widest point, smaller than Westeros; the central inland sea remains; Dragonstone is an island connected by a narrow land bridge, with a GM-only secret dwarven underground route; Khar-Dum lies in the northeastern mountains; and each Great House controls a substantial, clearly defined territory.
- The six Great Houses are established: Vaelorian, Kharadorn, Edarth, Calderan, Veyr, and Merrowen. Their broad territories, principal seats, current rulers and heirs, Council representatives, Crown officers, and court-faction leaders are also established. Continue with exact borders, wider family branches, detailed histories, and local consequences as geography and play require.
- Develop the dwarven empire around Khar-Dum and its conquered possession of Dragonstone; do not treat the dwarves as fragmented successor territories.
- Develop the elven political and economic power centered on House Vaelorian.
- Establish the history of the fallen dragon-riding dynasty in layers: public history, disputed accounts, and GM truth.
- Develop the northern territories, Ancient Barrier, and Frozen Expanse.
- Expand cultures, trade, warfare, religions, and historical chronology.

**Exit condition:** No fixed deadline. Expand in response to campaign relevance.

## Milestone 4 — Deep mysteries (long-term)
- Develop the origins and limits of vampirism.
- Decide what happened to dragons, if and when the campaign needs the answer.
- Explore the origins and purpose of the Ancient Barrier.
- Explore the source and operation of divine magic. The Pathfinder gods are real; their deeper relationships with mortals and the means by which divine power is granted remain open.
- Develop the history of ratfolk marginalization and the purposes of secretive organizations.

**Rule:** These mysteries can remain unresolved. Their records should distinguish objective GM truth, competing theories, public beliefs, and player discoveries.

## Ongoing checkpoint cycle
At each meaningful worldbuilding checkpoint and after each session:
1. Review the current canon and relevant records.
2. Compare new material against established facts and open questions.
3. Flag contradictions rather than silently resolving them.
4. Record approved decisions and their rationale.
5. Update detailed records, indexes, timeline, and changelog.
6. Separate GM-only information from player-known information.
7. Prepare a concise current-state handoff for future work.

## Scope control
- Do not complete lore merely because a category exists in the repository.
- Prioritize anything the PCs can encounter, affect, or learn soon.
- Keep proposals distinct from approved canon.
- The simulator may suggest events; only GM approval makes them history.
- The high-medieval technology baseline remains in force unless explicitly revised.

## Ongoing system — Creature bestiary and regional ecology (started Nov 6, 2026)

### Campaign records
- The creature registry and reusable creature-record template are established in `campaign/creatures/`.
- Keep creature identity, regional occurrence, frequency, evidence, public knowledge, specialist knowledge, PC knowledge, and GM-only truth distinct.
- Register prepared encounters without treating them as established regional populations.
- After each session, update only what was observed, documented, or explicitly approved by the GM.
- Expand the catalogue in response to locations the PCs visit, creatures they encounter, and explicit setting decisions. Do not bulk-import generic Pathfinder monsters as assumed setting canon.

### Future app support
- Add creature records as a first-class GM-managed entity, with stable source keys and provenance.
- Support multiple regional occurrence records for one creature, plus separate sightings and encounter links.
- Support GM-only and player-shared knowledge at the field or section level where feasible; enforce access in database policies, not only in the UI.
- Add source references, evidence status, review history, and preview-first import/update behavior.
- The current campaign importer does not accept creature records. Extend its schema, validation, persistence, and permissions deliberately before preparing creature JSON for import. Do not force creature records into unrelated collections.

**Current blocker:** Creature records are documented in the repository but are not yet supported by the app's import template or importer. Until that changes, the Markdown registry remains authoritative.
