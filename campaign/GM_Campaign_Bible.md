**Ruleset:** Pathfinder First Edition

> This is the working GM-facing guide to the campaign's current world. It consolidates the setting's operating assumptions and points to the detailed records. It does not replace those records. When a detail conflicts, apply the canon hierarchy below.

## 1. Canon control and how to use this bible

### Canon hierarchy

1. Explicit GM decisions in conversation.
2. Latest approved stage, decision log, and current GM-approved change.
3. Latest dedicated lore or rules reference for the subject.
4. `campaign/Current_World_State.md`.
5. This consolidated guide and the older imported `Campaign_Bible.md`.
6. Unapproved proposals and brainstorming.

A later approved decision supersedes an earlier statement. The imported 40-page `Campaign_Bible.md` contains legacy wording and unresolved-topic lists that have since been superseded. It is preserved source material, not the sole authority. Do not reintroduce discarded names, old descriptions, or old claims that the setting is unrelated to Westeros.

### Status labels

- **ESTABLISHED:** Approved campaign fact.
- **APPROVED FRAMEWORK:** Direction and operating structure accepted; some details remain open.
- **PROVISIONAL:** Prepared for possible use, but not locked.
- **UNRESOLVED:** Do not fill in without evidence or a GM decision.
- **GM-ONLY:** Not player-facing unless deliberately revealed.

### Campaign premise

The setting is an original fantasy world built around the political and dynastic framework of Westeros / *Game of Thrones*, using Pathfinder First Edition for its rules, gods, ancestries, classes, monsters, spells, and equipment. It is not Golarion and is not a direct copy of Westeros. Tallowmere is the campaign's starting city, not the entire setting. The campaign is intended to support noble politics, feudal obligations, personal ambitions, exploration, trade, military conflict, and a living world that responds to player choices.

The central tone is grounded high-medieval fantasy. Supernatural horror and dangerous wilderness exist, but they support rather than replace the political and social campaign.

## 3. World foundations

### Technology and everyday life

The technological baseline is high medieval: fortified stone keeps, walled towns, hereditary nobility, knights, sworn retainers, household guards, levies, conventional armies, sailing vessels, horse transport, agriculture, guilds, and skilled craft production. There is no industrial or steampunk technological culture. Alchemy exists as specialized Pathfinder-based knowledge and craft; it does not imply industrialization.

Magic is real but uncommon in everyday life. Arcane spellcasters are rare, divine spellcasters are also uncommon, and the average person may live their entire life without seeing a spell cast. Magic item creation is rare because the required talent, knowledge, and materials are uncommon. Player-character spellcasting still follows Pathfinder rules; social rarity does not rewrite mechanics.

### Ancestries and societies

- Humans dominate much of the continent.
- Elves possess a major political power through House Vaelorian.
- Dwarves maintain an enduring, relatively compact empire composed of independent clans united under the main branch. It has never collapsed. The main branch is said to descend from Torag himself. Dwarves primarily inhabit mountains and underground settlements; they rarely live in conventional surface stone-built settlements. Their cultural pride favors reproduction only with other dwarves, a cultural choice rather than biological incompatibility.
- Ratfolk are naturally subterranean and live in decentralized networks rather than one unified people or government. Prejudice against them is widespread across surface societies, but it is not universal. Ratfolk are not inherently criminal, evil, diseased, or hostile.
- Other Pathfinder ancestries may exist. None is automatically designated rare or uncommon by default.

### Society and ordinary life

The continent is feudal, agricultural, commercial, and socially unequal. Most people live through household labor, farming, animal care, craft, fishing, transport, service, or trade. Noble status brings rights and obligations, but wealth, office, guild privilege, religious standing, and commercial access can also create influence.

A person's life is shaped by household, land, work, family, faith, local custom, and the authority that claims jurisdiction over them. A tenant farmer, freeholder, guild apprentice, merchant factor, household servant, sailor, soldier, and noble heir do not experience the same opportunities or risks.

Literacy is valuable but not universal. People learn through households, apprenticeship, temples, noble tutors, commercial practice, military service, and specialist scholarship. Practical knowledge is not inferior to book learning: a farmer may understand soil and seasons better than a court scholar, while a merchant clerk may understand contracts and accounts better than a landed knight.

For detailed everyday patterns, see `world/Everyday_Life_and_Material_Culture.md`. Education and access to knowledge are covered in `world/Education_Literacy_and_Knowledge.md`.

### Religion

The Pathfinder gods are real. The continent broadly recognizes the Pathfinder pantheon, but worship varies by region, culture, institution, and individual. There is no single supreme religious authority over all faiths. Major faiths maintain independent hierarchies. Clergy and divine spellcasters are not interchangeable; a priest may conduct rites, education, charity, records, or mediation without casting spells.

The Religious Establishment has a recognized and growing presence at the royal court but no automatic Imperial Council seat. An independent scholarly tradition exists outside religious hierarchies, though it may cooperate with them. The exact mediation of divine magic, major institutional hierarchies, religious courts, and the legal role of religion in marriage, inheritance, coronation, and secular law remain open.

## 4. Geography and the wider world

### Continental scale and major features

The continent's approved working dimensions are approximately 1,700 miles north to south and 1,300 miles east to west at its widest point, making it smaller than Westeros. It contains a central inland sea that shapes trade, naval power, and the strategic importance of Dragonstone.

Established geographic foundations:
- A large central inland sea.
- Dragonstone, an island stronghold connected to the mainland by a narrow natural land bridge.
- A GM-only dwarven underground route to Dragonstone. It is not known to ordinary actors and must not be inferred by player-facing map content.
- Khar-Dum, the Kharadorns' ancestral homeland in the northeastern mountains.
- Broad Great House territorial regions and the major seats listed below.
- A harsh, sparsely populated northern wilderness beyond settled territories.
- The Frozen Expanse beyond the Ancient Barrier. Its builders and original purpose are unknown; House Veyr maintains surviving portions.

**Map discipline:** Do not invent exact borders, coordinates, route alignments, travel times, or political polygons. The authoritative artwork and calibrated atlas layers govern visual placement. Regional descriptions are not surveyed geometry. Political regions should not overlap unless a specific condition such as occupation or war justifies it. General geographic regions may overlap.

### Broad regional character

These descriptions are geographic and cultural orientation, not surveyed borders or exact climate classifications.

- **Northern Wilderness and Frozen Expanse:** severe, exposed frontier country beyond the settled northern territories. The Ancient Barrier divides the known frontier from the largely unexplored Frozen Expanse. Its builders, original purpose, and any threat beyond it remain unresolved.
- **Northern Holds and Pine Marches:** cold uplands, forests, valleys, and sparse settlements associated with House Veyr. Timber, livestock, frontier service, and imported grain are important. Supply lines matter as much as military courage.
- **Western Highlands and High Peaks:** mountain and forest country associated with Vaelorian influence, mines, passes, and old estates. Elevation creates local variation; Vaelorian does not control every route through the ranges.
- **Green Valleys and Western Forests:** sheltered valleys, woodland, managed estates, and resource communities. Customary woodland and estate rights may overlap without being identical.
- **Western Heartlands, Golden Fields, and Riverlands:** productive plains and river valleys associated with Edarth's agricultural and martial strength. Grain, livestock, landed obligations, and inland roads shape the region.
- **Central Crownlands and Royal Basin:** the inland political and administrative heart of the realm. Valedorn combines royal government, courts, markets, river transport, military administration, and scholarship.
- **Central Inland Sea:** a major shipping and naval system under Kharadorn influence. Dragonstone's position gives the dwarven empire strategic control, but the sea is not a blanket grant of sovereignty over every shoreline or port.
- **Northeastern Mountains and Khar-Dum:** the ancestral homeland of House Kharadorn and the dwarven main branch. Surface settlements, mines, underground communities, and clan territories have distinct relationships.
- **Eastern Forests and Eastern Shore:** coastal and river country with woodland resources, ports, fishing communities, and maritime trade. Merrowen's influence is substantial but does not mean every port is directly governed by the Great House.
- **Great Delta:** branching waterways, wetlands, river traffic, fishing, and coastal trade. Channel conditions and local authority vary.
- **Southwestern Marches:** arid highlands, dry valleys, plateaus, and oases. Water access, wells, cisterns, and caravan movement are structural concerns.
- **Southern Principalities and Southern Islands:** fertile lowlands, forests, rivers, independent cities, hereditary rulers, and maritime principalities. The south is politically fragmented, not one unified kingdom.

Use the geographic and regional records for finer detail. Do not infer political borders from these broad descriptions.

### Established inter-house relationship baseline

- **Calderan and Kharadorn:** uneasy peace and mutual distrust after the post-Rebellion succession settlement. Both understand that war would be costly; Kharadorn is not afraid of war, but its position would not favor a prolonged conflict.
- **Vaelorian:** commercial and financial influence can shape the choices of other houses through credit, trade, expertise, and long-term relationships. This is influence, not automatic control over debtors.
- **Edarth:** agricultural production and landed military networks make the house important to the stability and defense of the realm. Its traditional authority and obligations can place it at odds with centralizing reforms.

- **Merrowen:** maritime reach and port relationships make the house important to trade and naval affairs. Its interests do not automatically align with Kharadorn's inland-sea authority or give it sovereignty over every coastal settlement.

These are structural relationships, not fixed alliances or active disputes. Individual rulers, heirs, council representatives, and factions may disagree with their house's broader interests.

- **Lady Meredyth Sorn:** Master of Coin; precise administrator, works with lenders but is not Vaelorian.
- **High Prelate Aveline Marr:** Most visible religious adviser at court; represents an influential religious movement, not all faiths.

Five overlapping court movements, not formal parties:
1. **Royal Court:** Queen Elowen Calderan; key associate Othmar Rusk. Stability, legitimacy, compromise, gradual institutional strengthening.
2. **Traditionalists:** Lord Garrick Edarth. Noble rights and resistance to excessive centralization.
3. **Royal Expansionists:** Princess Maerelle Calderan. Stronger Crown administration and military capability.
4. **Merchant Interests:** Lady Cassia Merrowen. Commercial stability, access, and influence of merchant networks.
5. **Religious Establishment:** High Prelate Aveline Marr. Institutional independence and religious influence at court.

Faction membership is fluid. A person may cooperate with one movement on one question and oppose it on another. Do not assume every member of a house shares the same faction.

### Constitutional balance in practice

The Council's approved operating framework establishes:
- Four of six representatives for an ordinary Council resolution.
- Five of six for protected settlement matters, including permanent changes to Great House hereditary privileges, extraordinary realm-wide levies, permanent expansion of military obligations, transfer of Great House territory or hereditary rights without consent or lawful forfeiture, fundamental alteration of the Council, or change to the succession settlement.
- A quorum of four representatives.
- The monarch may postpone a vote once for negotiation; a second postponement requires agreement from at least three representatives.
- The Council cannot command armies directly. Its leverage lies in political legitimacy, funding, cooperation, and the post-Rebellion settlement.
- The monarch retains authority for immediate defensive action in a direct attack. A prolonged offensive war against a recognized major power normally requires Council assent unless a demonstrable immediate threat makes delay unreasonable.

The Crown's officers serve the monarch rather than representing the Great Houses. The monarch appoints them where law permits, but powerful houses and court factions seek influence over appointments. Royal authority is substantial, but the Crown cannot simply administer Great House lands, command their armies at will, or impose extraordinary levies without political consequences.

For the full framework, consult `politics/Imperial_Governance_Operating_Framework.md` and `politics/Court_and_Council.md`.

### Political operating rules

Use `politics/Imperial_Governance_Operating_Framework.md` for Council procedure, Crown powers, protected settlement matters, deadlock, appointments, escalation, and vassal consequences. The Council is not a parliament; the Crown has real authority; the Great Houses have real leverage; none is omnipotent. Political disputes normally escalate through petitions, negotiation, legal challenges, withholding cooperation, sanctions, and only then force where circumstances justify it.

## 8. History

### Dragon-riding dynasty

A dragon-riding dynasty ruled from the Imperial Seat for approximately 400 years and possessed exactly twelve dragons at its height. Dragon-bond eligibility depended on the exclusive royal bloodline. Each rider could bond with one dragon through a telepathic connection. Maintaining eligibility required reproduction within the eligible bloodline.

The dragons were enormous flying reptilian predators with strength, flight, and fire. They were not traditional intelligent, spellcasting Pathfinder dragons.

### Great Rebellion

Approximately 200–300 years before the campaign, six Great Houses rebelled for two connected reasons: to preserve their independence against centralization and to destroy dragon-riding as the basis of the dynasty's overwhelming supremacy. The war lasted a few years. Kharadorn was the principal architect and developed specialized dragon-slaying ballistas. Most dragons were killed; surviving dragons fled after losing their riders and their fate is unknown. The ruling family was nearly exterminated and the Imperial Seat fell.

### Post-Rebellion settlement

The dwarven leader who directed the rebellion ruled for more than a century after the fall. After his death, his successor inherited authority and a succession crisis followed. The successor threatened war unless a human was placed on the Imperial Seat. The head of another original rebel Great House was installed as a compromise candidate and became the current Calderan monarch. The dwarven successor reluctantly accepted the arrangement, returned to Dragonstone, and continued ruling the clans. The dwarven empire and human monarchy maintain an uneasy peace and mutual distrust.

### Historical uncertainties

- The fallen dynasty's original name is unresolved.
- The former stronghold's final original name is unresolved; “Dragonstone” is a working label.
- Exact dates of the rebellion, the Crowning, and the postwar reigns are unresolved.
- Detailed campaign histories, individual house roles, and many named historical figures remain incomplete.
- No living claimant to the old dynasty is publicly recognized. One surviving Crown Prince is a GM-only truth; the earlier association of that survivor with a player character is provisional pending confirmation of the revised concepts.

Use `history/Historical_Accounts_and_Uncertainty.md` to separate public, noble, disputed, suppressed, and objective historical accounts.

### How to handle historical accounts

The public account, noble interpretation, scholarly record, suppressed testimony, and objective GM truth may differ. A person may know a version that is widely believed without knowing whether it is accurate. House histories often emphasize sacrifice, lawful inheritance, and the loyalty of ancestors; rival houses may emphasize broken oaths, self-interest, or unlawful ambition.

The GM should preserve the difference between what happened, what was recorded, what was remembered, and what is believed. The surviving Crown Prince's identity and survival remain protected. The old dynasty's original name and the final name of Dragonstone remain unresolved.


## 9. Economy, military, and law

The economy is medieval and regional: land rents, agricultural production, craft guilds, ports, river transport, tolls, charters, credit, and trade. Vaelorian finance and Kharadorn strategic maritime control are important structural influences, but neither controls all commerce. Merchant interests can shape policy through credit, supply, contracts, and access without becoming a sovereign government.

Military power is based on noble obligations, household forces, local garrisons, levies, professional soldiers, ships, fortifications, and conventional siegecraft. Exact Great House force totals and readiness are not established. Do not invent army sizes or naval tonnage. The royal military is distinct from household guards and Great House forces; its exact organization and strength remain unresolved.

Feudal law is governed by homage, land tenure, inheritance, jurisdiction, charters, customary rights, courts, and service obligations. Specific charters and local customs override broad assumptions. Use `politics/Feudal_Law_and_Landed_Obligations.md` for landholding and vassalage, and `world/Law_Justice_and_Crime.md` for ordinary justice, evidence, punishment, and enforcement.

Agricultural production, food supply, livestock, storage, and seasonal work are described in `world/Agriculture_Food_and_Seasonal_Work.md`. Everyday material life, medicine, education, literacy, and travel/communication are covered in the corresponding records under `world/`. These references supply practical context; they do not establish exact prices, yields, literacy rates, weather events, or travel times where those remain unresolved.



### Agriculture, food, and ordinary economy

The continent is primarily agricultural, feudal, maritime, and regional. Grain, livestock, dairy, wool, hides, orchard produce, timber, charcoal, stone, metalwork, and fish support households and trade. The Western Heartlands, Crownlands, and fertile southern lowlands are important food-producing regions. Veyr, Kharadorn, Merrowen, and major cities depend on reliable movement of food from productive regions.

Tallowmere is a regional port and River Vale trade hub, not a continent-scale breadbasket. Millhaven and the Northfields collect grain, flour, oats, and seasonal legumes. Nearby estates provide livestock, dairy, and orchard produce. Saltwick supplies fish; Brackenholt supplies charcoal, firewood, poles, and timber; Stonehollow supplies ordinary building stone. River and coastal shipping connect these producers to wider markets.

A poor harvest does not automatically create famine. Stores, imports, credit, alternative foods, transport, and political decisions shape the outcome. Exact crop varieties, yields, prices, tax totals, and market volumes remain selective and should be established only when relevant. See `world/Agriculture_Food_and_Seasonal_Work.md`, `economy/Continental_Economic_Network.md`,  `economy/Markets_Merchants_Guilds_and_Contracts.md` for how trade, contracts, markets, and commercial influence work, and `economy/Currency_Coinage_and_Moneychanging.md` for the campaign's currency baseline and practical money use.

### Military institutions and warfare

Military power is layered. The Crown maintains a permanent household guard and a distinct royal military force. Great Houses retain their own forces and hereditary military obligations. Local lords may maintain household guards and garrisons; towns maintain watches; vassals provide only the service required by their particular charter or custom.

Kharadorn's established strengths include heavy infantry, elite armored troops, engineers, siege specialists, clan contingents, underground fortifications, and naval forces controlling the Central Inland Sea. Veyr maintains the Northern Watch and mobile Marcher Companies. Vaelorian maintains a compact professional force, skilled archers, and selected mountain garrisons. Edarth's strength rests on a network of landed houses, fortifications, and military obligations. Merrowen's power includes shipping, naval tradition, port defenses, and maritime expertise.

Readiness is not mobilization. Commercial vessels are not automatically warships. A garrison cannot be counted as a free field army without exposing its post. Exact troop totals, fleet sizes, budgets, and readiness remain unresolved. See `military/Continental_Military_Institutions_and_Operational_Doctrine.md` for institutional structure and `military/Soldier_Life_and_Military_Service.md` for recruitment, training, garrison routine, campaigning, and the lived experience of service.

### Law, justice, and authority

The legal order is a patchwork of Crown authority, Great House privileges, local lordships, charters, guild rights, customary law, clan agreements, and religious jurisdiction. Law is consequential but not uniform. A charter, local custom, or recognized privilege may define rights that a neighboring jurisdiction does not share.

Local officials and magistrates handle ordinary matters; lordship courts address estate, tenant, and local disputes; liege authority may hear vassal and charter questions; Crown courts act within royal jurisdiction; the Imperial Council handles major inter-house and constitutional matters. Religious courts generally address internal religious matters and do not automatically control secular crime.

Status and wealth affect access to justice. Available remedies may include restitution, fines, forfeiture of privileges, detention, corporal punishment, or capital punishment where local law permits. Do not assume universal trial procedures, a standard penalty schedule, or automatic noble immunity. See `politics/Feudal_Law_and_Landed_Obligations.md` and `world/Law_Justice_and_Crime.md`.

### Medicine, education, and knowledge

Practical medicine is provided through household care, midwives, barber-surgeons, physicians, herbalists, and religious charity. These roles are not interchangeable. Magic is not routine medical infrastructure; clergy are not automatically spellcasters, and a temple cannot be assumed to provide magical healing on demand. Specific local practitioners, endemic diseases, and outbreaks remain open unless recorded.

Literacy is valuable but not universal. Household instruction, apprenticeship, noble tutors, temple learning, commercial numeracy, military training, and specialist scholarship coexist. The Collegium of the Nine Lamps is an established independent scholarly network, not a single university. Its internal ranks, training, governing body, and geographic reach remain unresolved.

See `world/Medicine_Disease_and_Healing.md`, `world/Education_Literacy_and_Knowledge.md`, and `religions/Religion_and_Faith.md`.

### Travel, communication, and information

People travel on foot, horseback, carts, wagons, caravans, rivers, coastal vessels, and inland-sea ships. Roads vary in condition and maintenance. The six major continental corridors are established, but exact courses and surveyed travel times remain subject to map calibration.

Letters, official messengers, merchants, ship crews, temples, scholars, public notices, and local conversation carry information. There is no established universal postal service, passport system, or instantaneous communication network. Rumors may travel faster than verified facts. NPCs know only what they could plausibly learn through their own contacts and circumstances.

See `world/Travel_Communication_and_Information.md` and the relevant regional geography records.

### Natural world

Tallowmere's established habitats extend from the settled Northfields, Brackenwood, River Vale, Greyback Ridges, Eastern Shore, Siltfen, and city outskirts into the less continuously worked frontier environments of the Deep Bracken, Upper Vale, Greyback Heights, and Outer Eastern Shore. The Siltfen is a defined wetland region, not simply a generic marsh. The frontier belt includes old woodland, headwaters, broken uplands, wetlands, and exposed coast. These are working and traveled landscapes, but protection, infrastructure, and reliable local knowledge diminish farther from the settled hinterland.

The regional wildlife and flora catalogues are substantial GM references but remain proposed species lists. The separate wilderness framework includes possible Pathfinder threats and ordinary resource opportunities; its lists do not establish every creature as a confirmed local population or place a lair at any specific site. Do not assign exact abundance, invent migrations, or create a monster population from a plausible habitat alone. No unique magical plant or supernatural ecology is established by the botanical catalogue.

See `creatures/Tallowmere_Regional_Ecology.md`, `creatures/Regional_Wildlife_Catalogue.md`, `creatures/Tallowmere_Flora_and_Fungi.md`, and `locations/starting_city/Wilderness_Beyond_Tallowmere.md`.

## 10. Running a responsive world through GM judgment

The setting exists independently of the player characters. People have duties, ambitions, fears, loyalties, families, work, and limited information. Events can continue while the PCs are elsewhere, but the GM decides what actually happens. There is no automated world simulator, required world-turn procedure, or universal schedule of consequences.

### A practical approach

When the PCs leave a place, ignore a problem, take an extended period of downtime, or change the balance of power:

1. Establish how much in-world time has actually passed.
2. Identify the people and institutions directly affected.
3. Consider what each could reasonably know, afford, attempt, and risk.
4. Consider ordinary constraints: distance, season, money, manpower, law, custom, weather, supply, and competing obligations.
5. Decide which developments are important enough to occur off-screen and which can wait until play returns to them.
6. Keep the result proportionate. Not every neglected issue becomes a crisis, and not every NPC takes decisive action.
7. Record consequential events, changed relationships, new obligations, discoveries, and unresolved questions in their appropriate subject records.

Years of downtime should be handled as a sequence of meaningful periods, not as a single automatic roll. The GM can summarize routine continuity, then focus on major changes that follow plausibly from decisions, resources, and circumstances. The passage of time does not require a catastrophe or a dramatic event.

### Established structural tensions

The following are durable political and economic conditions, not active crises or guaranteed future plots:

- Crown authority versus Great House autonomy.
- Kharadorn control of the Inland Sea versus Merrowen maritime interests.
- Vaelorian financial leverage and the political price of credit.
- Veyr frontier needs and southern support.
- Edarth defense of traditional noble rights.
- Fragmented Southern Reaches and their independent interests.

Use the dedicated Great House, minor-house, economic, and political records to understand these tensions. Do not assume that any one of them is escalating at the current date.

## 11. Calendar and campaign timeline

The dating system is **AC: After the Crowning** and **BC: Before the Crowning**. Year 1 AC is the first full year after the Crowning that established the post-Rebellion order and current monarchy.

- Twelve months of thirty days each, followed by five Yearturn days.
- Every fourth year includes one extra leap day.
- Months: Frostwane, Thawrise, Greenwake, Bloomtide, Suncrest, Highsun, Goldleaf, Harvestfall, Emberwane, Mistfall, Duskreach, Longnight.
- Week: Firstday, Secondday, Thirdday, Fourthday, Fifthday, Sixthday, Restday.
- Seasons are variable and not fixed to month names.
- The campaign begins on **1 Harvestfall, 243 AC**.

Widely recognized observances include Winter's End, First Planting, Day of the Crown, Highsummer Feast, Harvest Home, Night of Remembrance, and Yearturn. Exact festival dates and many regional customs remain open.
