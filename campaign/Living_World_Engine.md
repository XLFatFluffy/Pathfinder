# Living World Engine

**Status:** GM operating framework; initial baseline established 2026-11-06  
**Campaign start:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC  
**Authority:** The GM approves all consequential events and canon changes.

## 1. Purpose

The Living World Engine maintains a persistent simulation of political, economic, social, military, environmental, and personal developments beyond the player characters' immediate view. It answers:

- What are important people and institutions trying to achieve?
- What resources, leverage, and constraints do they have?
- What changes while the PCs are elsewhere?
- What happens when the PCs intervene, withdraw, ignore a problem, or change who holds power?
- What would different actors know, believe, fear, or misunderstand?

The engine is not a random-event table and does not force a plot. It models pressures and choices, then proposes plausible consequences.

## 2. Core principles

1. **Causality before randomness.** Events emerge from goals, resources, relationships, constraints, and prior events. Randomness may add uncertainty, not replace causes.
2. **Independent agency.** NPCs, houses, guilds, communities, and institutions pursue goals even when the PCs are absent.
3. **Consequences, not punishment.** Ignoring a problem allows its existing pressures to continue; it does not guarantee escalation or catastrophe.
4. **No plot armor or plot rails.** PC actions can permanently alter outcomes. The engine does not protect planned events.
5. **Information is local.** Actors act on what they know or believe, not omniscient campaign facts.
6. **Unequal power matters.** A minor house cannot casually command a Great House; a merchant guild cannot field an army, but may control credit, shipping, and supply.
7. **Institutions matter.** Titles, law, custom, legitimacy, obligations, geography, and logistics shape what is possible.
8. **Time has scale.** A day can change a street-level crisis; a month may change a local negotiation; major political realignments may take seasons or years.
9. **Canon is reviewed.** Generated developments remain proposals until the GM approves or records what actually happened.
10. **Player-facing knowledge is filtered.** The engine tracks objective state separately from rumors, reports, public knowledge, and PC discoveries.

## 3. Simulation layers

### A. Actors
Actors may be individuals, noble houses, guilds, councils, communities, military commands, religious institutions, criminal groups, or other organized interests.

Each substantial actor should have:
- Stable ID and name.
- Type and canon status.
- Leader or decision-making structure, if known.
- Public goals and private goals, if established.
- Interests, fears, values, and red lines.
- Resources: wealth, land, people, military force, ships, information, legitimacy, expertise, or access.
- Dependencies and vulnerabilities.
- Allies, rivals, obligations, vassals, patrons, and clients.
- Current stance toward relevant issues.
- What it knows, believes, suspects, and does not know.
- Current projects and active pressures.
- Available actions and constraints.
- Source records and last review.

Unknown values remain UNKNOWN. Do not assign invented numerical strength ratings to established houses.

### B. Issues and pressures
An issue is a continuing condition that may prompt action: a border claim, succession concern, debt, shortage, prejudice, unsafe warehouse, disputed berth allocation, damaged road, disease, military threat, or political reform.

Each issue records:
- What is happening and where.
- Actors involved and their distinct positions.
- Root causes and interests.
- Evidence and competing accounts.
- Current status and duration.
- What would worsen, improve, or resolve it.
- Thresholds for plausible developments.
- Consequences of delay or intervention.
- Who knows about it.

A pressure does not have to become a crisis. Some remain unresolved for years.

### C. Projects
Projects are deliberate efforts by actors: acquire a holding, secure a marriage, win a charter, raise troops, repair a fortification, build a trade route, suppress a scandal, gain market access, or change a law.

Track goal, sponsor, steps, resources required, progress, obstacles, opponents, dependencies, and plausible completion windows. Progress only when time passes and the actor has resources and opportunity.

### D. Events
An event is a dated occurrence that changes the state of the world. Every event should record:
- Event ID, date, location, status, and source.
- Triggering conditions and prior state.
- Actors involved and what each knew.
- Actions taken and why.
- Immediate outcome and lasting state changes.
- Public account, private truth, and disputed versions.
- PC involvement and knowledge.
- Follow-on pressures and open questions.

### E. World state
The current world state is a snapshot of approved facts: rulers, holdings, control, alliances, conflicts, resources when known, active projects, unresolved issues, and recent events. It is not a substitute for detailed source records.

## 4. Time advancement and update cadence

Advance the simulation when meaningful in-world time passes, not after every real-world chat message.

Suggested review cadence:
- **Hours:** immediate threats, patrols, travel, negotiations, combat, urgent messages.
- **Daily:** local commerce, deliveries, watch activity, rumors, weather impacts, short-term projects.
- **Weekly:** guild negotiations, minor-house decisions, market conditions, recruitment, investigations, repairs, local disputes.
- **Monthly:** harvest and supply outlook, taxes, credit, court politics, troop readiness, construction, regional trade.
- **Seasonal:** harvest results, campaigns, marriages, succession maneuvering, major trade shifts, migration, long projects.
- **Annual:** broad political, demographic, institutional, and dynastic review.

These are review intervals, not automatic event deadlines. An actor may act sooner if an opportunity or emergency arises.

At each update:
1. Establish elapsed in-world time and the GM-recorded regional season/conditions. Month names do not determine weather. Consult [Seasonal Conditions and Agricultural Cycles](world_engine/Seasonal_Conditions_and_Agricultural_Cycles.md); do not invent current weather or a seasonal event.
2. Review active issues and projects whose conditions could have changed.
3. Update relevant resources, constraints, relationships, and information.
4. Identify actions each actor could plausibly take.
5. Select actions consistent with goals, means, risks, and knowledge.
6. Resolve direct interactions and likely responses.
7. Record consequences, delays, failures, and new pressures.
8. Generate public rumors and reports only if information could plausibly travel.
9. Prepare a concise GM world-turn report.
10. Wait for GM approval before committing proposed events as canon.

## 5. Event resolution

For each possible development, assess:
- **Motivation:** Does the actor want this?
- **Capability:** Can it afford, organize, or enforce it?
- **Opportunity:** Are timing and conditions favorable?
- **Knowledge:** Does it know enough to act?
- **Opposition:** Who can resist, and at what cost?
- **Risk:** What might go wrong?
- **Alternatives:** Is there a cheaper or safer action?

Use qualitative assessments unless a specific mechanical subsystem is justified. Do not pretend to calculate precise probabilities from unsupported statistics.

Possible outcomes include success, partial success, delay, compromise, failure, escalation, exposure, defection, or unintended consequences. Failure should create plausible state changes, not simply erase the attempt.

## 6. Political change and holdings

When PCs acquire, inherit, conquer, marry into, or are granted a minor house or holding, evaluate the actual legal and political mechanism first.

Review:
- Legitimacy of the claim and recognition by liege, peers, Crown, and local institutions.
- Existing titleholder, heirs, household, sworn retainers, tenants, and obligations.
- Whether the holding is vacant, disputed, forfeited, conquered, or lawfully transferred.
- Vassalage, taxes, military service, charter rights, and higher obligations.
- Neighboring claims, boundary markers, access to roads, rivers, pasture, mines, woodland, and water.
- Local population, steward, reeve, clergy, guilds, merchants, and customary rights.
- Income, debts, repairs, harvest, stores, garrison, and ability to enforce decisions.
- Reactions from allies, rivals, liege, Crown, Great Houses, and affected communities.

Reactions should be differentiated. Some actors may welcome new rulers, some may wait, some may test them, and some may resist. Do not make every neighboring house hostile or every peasant loyal. Recognition and control are separate: a PC may hold a title without effective control, or control land without lawful recognition.

## 7. Border disputes and conflict

Use the [Continental Political Authority Register](maps/continental_political_authority_register.md) and [Boundary Junction Worklist](maps/continental_boundary_junction_worklist.md) when assessing holdings and borders. Broad territorial frameworks do not establish exact borders or pairwise adjacency. Do not invent border coordinates or convert an unmapped local dispute into overlapping territorial polygons.

Do not invent a live border dispute merely to make the world feel active. A dispute requires a claim, competing interest, location, and actors with something to gain or lose.

Potential causes include:
- Ambiguous inheritance or marriage settlements.
- Old charters, boundary stones, maps, and customary rights.
- Access to water, pasture, woodland, mines, roads, tolls, or ports.
- A river changing course or a bridge/ferry changing hands.
- Conflicting jurisdiction or taxation.
- A liege's grant overlapping another claim.
- Local violence or private retainers acting beyond authority.

Escalation is not automatic. Actors may negotiate, petition, arbitrate, intimidate, blockade, raid, mobilize, or seek a patron. War requires capacity, incentives, and a failure or rejection of alternatives. Record the evidence and each side's account.

## 8. Succession, assassination, and regime change

A monarch's death or assassination is never a random dramatic button. It requires a credible cause, opportunity, motive, actors, and consequences. If the GM chooses to introduce an assassination attempt or death, model:
- Who benefits and who is blamed.
- The succession rules and legitimacy of claimants.
- Where the heir is and who can reach or control them.
- The Crown's military and administrative response.
- Great House interests and Council positions.
- City, provincial, and military loyalties.
- Information control, investigation, rumors, and false accusations.
- Risks of civil war, regency, compromise, or rapid consolidation.

The engine must not assume the king is safe, doomed, secretly controlled, or destined to die. King Aldric Calderan's established authority and unresolved constraints remain the baseline.

## 9. Ignored issues and clocks

Each active issue may have a qualitative clock:
- **Dormant:** no immediate movement; underlying condition remains.
- **Active:** actors are taking steps or conditions are changing.
- **Pressing:** delay is becoming costly or an opportunity is closing.
- **Critical:** a decision or event is imminent.
- **Resolved:** outcome recorded.
- **Stalled:** actors lack means or incentive to proceed.
- **Transformed:** the issue has changed into a different problem.

Clocks advance only when causes justify progress. They can stall, reverse, split, or resolve through compromise. Ignoring an issue does not mean the worst possible outcome occurs.

## 10. Information and rumor

Use [Crown Intelligence and Information Operations](politics/Crown_Intelligence_and_Information_Operations.md) for Crown reporting, source evaluation, compartmentalization, and information-risk decisions. It does not give Nerys Vale or the Crown automatic access to Great House, clan, religious, merchant, or local information networks.

Maintain separate fields for:
- Objective GM truth.
- What each actor knows.
- What each actor believes or suspects.
- Publicly observable facts.
- Official statements.
- Private reports.
- Rumors and misinformation.
- What the PCs know and how they learned it.

Information travels through messengers, ships, merchants, ravens/couriers, travelers, institutions, and gossip at plausible speeds. Sensitive information may be delayed, distorted, suppressed, or never leave its source.

## 11. Player agency and consequence handling

When PCs act:
1. Record intent, method, resources, and known risks.
2. Identify affected actors and institutions.
3. Determine immediate mechanical and social outcomes.
4. Update relationships, reputation, obligations, and resources.
5. Let affected actors respond according to their own interests.
6. Recalculate active issues and projects.
7. Record what the PCs can actually observe or learn.

Do not punish players for failing to pursue every hook. Their choices establish priorities; unattended issues continue according to their own actors and conditions.

## 12. World-turn report format

Every meaningful time advance can produce a concise report:

- **Date advanced:** start → end.
- **Changes near the PCs:** what they may notice.
- **Regional developments:** relevant events and pressures.
- **Wider political developments:** only if plausible and relevant.
- **Projects advanced or stalled:** sponsor, progress, obstacle.
- **Issues changed:** clock movement and cause.
- **Rumors available:** source and reliability, if known.
- **GM-only developments:** hidden causes and decisions.
- **Decisions needed:** only where the GM must choose.
- **Database updates:** records that need amendment.

Avoid overwhelming the GM with every cart delivery or private conversation. Surface developments proportional to their impact and relevance.

## 13. Initial baseline: 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC

The following starting pressures are established in the Session One GM Readiness Packet:
- Harbor berth allocation dispute between established shipowners and smaller traders.
- Warehouse fire precautions after small fires and near-misses; cause unknown.
- Northfield harvest expected to be adequate but uneven.
- Ratfolk traders seeking regular market stalls and leases.
- House Denvor discussing succession, influence, and priorities without an open family crisis.
- House Kharadorn reviewing harbor defenses and charter obligations.
- Skilled labor and merchant credit under pressure, with fragmented supply and debt disputes.

These are independent issues, not a single conspiracy or mandatory plot. Their current positions, actors, evidence, and progress should be detailed before advancing them. No assassination, active border war, or continent-wide emergency is established at campaign start.

## 14. First implementation stages

1. Establish actor records for the Crown, six Great Houses, Tallowmere's governing house, relevant minor houses, guilds, and local institutions.
2. Convert the seven Tallowmere pressures into structured issue records with known actors, unknowns, and non-scripted paths.
3. Establish a simple holdings and vassalage register without inventing borders.
4. Establish project and event ledgers. **Completed:** local and continental project registers, event ledger, and world-turn ledger are now initialized.
5. **Completed:** Run WTR-001 as an opening snapshot on 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC without advancing the calendar. Later turns run only when in-world time advances or the GM requests a specific snapshot.
6. After several sessions, expand to neighboring territories and Great House-level projects.
7. Keep the engine manually reviewed until app support exists. The repository is authoritative; the app may later provide structured storage and interface.

## 15. Canon and implementation boundary

This document defines the intended operating model. It does not establish new political events, border disputes, assassinations, secret conspiracies, or numerical simulation values. Those require evidence, explicit GM decisions, or play. Suggested events must be labeled PROPOSED until approved.

Supporting operating references:
- [Continental Military Institutions and Operational Doctrine](military/Continental_Military_Institutions_and_Operational_Doctrine.md) distinguishes established force facts from proposed mobilization/logistics procedures. It does not establish force totals or active mobilizations.
- [Seasonal Conditions and Agricultural Cycles](world_engine/Seasonal_Conditions_and_Agricultural_Cycles.md) provides regional working tendencies and a weather/season recording method. It does not assign weather to calendar months or create current weather events.
- [Continental Political Authority Register](maps/continental_political_authority_register.md) and [Boundary Junction Worklist](maps/continental_boundary_junction_worklist.md) preserve the distinction between broad territorial frameworks and exact political borders.
