# Rules and Table Standards

**Status:** ESTABLISHED CAMPAIGN STANDARD

## Core Principle
**Strict rules, open possibilities.**

The campaign uses Pathfinder First Edition as its mechanical baseline. Players may attempt creative, ambitious, unusual, or unconventional plans. The GM adjudicates those plans using the published rules and the established setting rather than inventing arbitrary barriers.

## 1. Rules as Written
Published Pathfinder First Edition rules are the default authority for character abilities, combat, skills, spells, crafting, equipment, conditions, wealth, and advancement.

When a rule applies, use it consistently. Do not quietly alter DCs, costs, prerequisites, action types, durations, or other mechanics for convenience or plot.

If a rule is ambiguous, identify the ambiguity and make a consistent ruling. Record a lasting house rule explicitly.

## 2. House Rules Require Approval
A house rule is a deliberate campaign change, not an improvised obstacle. Discuss and approve it before treating it as part of the campaign's mechanics. Record approved house rules in this document with their scope and effective date.

No hidden house rules. No surprise mechanical penalties.

## 3. Setting Facts and Availability
The setting may establish what exists, who knows a technique, what services are available, what materials can be sourced, what laws apply, and what consequences follow from a choice.

These facts do not silently rewrite Pathfinder mechanics. For example, a city may have few qualified item crafters or limited access to a particular spell, but that does not add a universal prerequisite to item creation.

Any campaign-specific mechanical requirement must be explicitly identified and approved as a house rule.

## 4. Strict Rules, Open Possibilities
The rules define how an attempt works; they do not define which ambitions players are allowed to have. Players may pursue businesses, property, political influence, exploration, alliances, crafting, organizations, social change, or other goals.

The GM should look for a rules-supported way to adjudicate a plan before declaring it impossible. If a plan cannot work as described, explain the specific obstacle and allow the players to consider alternatives.

## 5. Consequences Are Not Prohibitions
The world reacts to actions. A successful business may attract competitors, taxes, scrutiny, customers, partners, or political attention. A political victory may create obligations. A dangerous expedition may carry risk.

Consequences should arise from established circumstances and the actions of independent NPCs, not be invented solely to negate player success. Success remains success even when it creates new complications.

## 6. NPC and PC Parity
NPCs use the same underlying rules as PCs. Their abilities, spells, crafting, wealth, and exceptional accomplishments should have plausible mechanical and setting explanations. Do not give NPCs special powers or waive prerequisites simply because the plot needs them.

## 7. Crafting and Magic Items
Magic item creation follows published Pathfinder First Edition rules, including relevant feats, prerequisites, caster-level requirements, spells, skill checks, costs, and crafting time.

Magic items are uncommon in the setting because specialized knowledge and talent are uncommon and necessary materials can be difficult to obtain. This scarcity is represented through the actual availability of practitioners, spells, services, materials, and markets. It does not impose an additional universal material prerequisite on every crafted item.

Do not add special mandatory ingredients, extra costs, extra checks, or arbitrary crafting restrictions unless they are required by published rules or explicitly approved as a house rule.

## 8. Transparency and Consistency
When a ruling materially affects a character or plan, explain the relevant rule or setting fact. Keep track of established rulings so similar situations receive similar treatment. If an earlier ruling is found to be wrong, correct it openly.

## 9. Player Agency and World Independence
The PCs are important protagonists, but the world does not exist solely for them. NPCs have their own goals and act within their means. Factions and events continue between sessions. Players may choose which opportunities to pursue and which to ignore.

The GM should neither force a predetermined story nor protect the PCs from every consequence. The campaign is an open world governed by consistent rules and a living setting.

## Approved House Rules

The following campaign-wide house rules were approved on 10 November 2026. They apply to PCs and NPCs consistently unless a rule's wording explicitly limits its scope.

### Combat Expertise prerequisite waiver
Combat Expertise is not required as a prerequisite for any feat that normally lists it. All other prerequisites for the feat still apply.

### Intelligence prerequisite waiver for combat maneuver feats
An Intelligence score of 13 is not required for combat maneuver feats. All other listed prerequisites still apply, including any other ability-score, feat, base-attack-bonus, or special requirements.

### Expanded ability score increases
At character levels 4, 8, 12, 16, and 20, a character receives two +1 ability score increases instead of the standard single +1 increase. The two increases must be assigned to different ability scores; they cannot both be applied to the same score at the same level.

This is a campaign-wide advancement adjustment intended to compensate for the setting's scarcity of magic items. It does not change the normal ability-score increase schedule or grant additional increases at other levels.
