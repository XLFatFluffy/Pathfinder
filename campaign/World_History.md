# World History

**Status:** ESTABLISHED HISTORICAL FOUNDATION / CHRONOLOGY INCOMPLETE  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-03

This record separates confirmed historical facts from dates, names, and political details that remain undecided. Legacy names such as House Targaryen, House Baratheon, and Dragonstone are working labels inherited from the source Campaign Bible; they are not necessarily the final names of the original setting.

## Established historical foundation

### The dragon-riding dynasty

- A dragon-riding dynasty ruled from the Imperial Seat for approximately **400 years**.
- The dynasty's power rested on its exclusive bloodline connection to dragons, its control of the Imperial Seat, and its ability to command the loyalty, obedience, or submission of other kingdoms and great houses.
- The dynasty maintained a substantial royal family with multiple branches and collateral lines.
- Its authority combined direct rule, subordinate kingdoms, political alliances, tribute, hostages, garrisons, governors, and military intervention.
- Over time, the dynasty increasingly sought to centralize authority and reduce the independence of the great houses.
- At the height of its power, the dynasty possessed exactly **twelve dragons**.
- The ability to bond with dragons was exclusive to eligible members of the Targaryen bloodline. Eligibility depended on preserving the bloodline through reproduction within that eligible lineage.
- Each rider could bond with only one dragon. The bond created a telepathic connection through which the rider could communicate with and influence the dragon.
- The dragons were enormous flying reptilian predators with great physical strength, flight, and fire-breath. They were not traditional Pathfinder dragons: they were not spellcasters, scholarly, or sophisticated talking creatures.

### The Great Rebellion

- **Six Great Houses** united against the dragon-riding dynasty.
- The rebellion had two connected objectives:
  1. Restore and preserve the Great Houses' political independence against a dynasty they feared would permanently subordinate them.
  2. Eliminate the dynasty's dragon-riding capability, because its control of dragons was the foundation that allowed it to maintain overwhelming political and military power.
- The rebellion was therefore both a struggle against centralization and a deliberate campaign to destroy the dragon-riding institution as a source of dynastic supremacy.
- The war lasted a few years and was devastating.
- The dwarven Great House associated with the main branch of the dwarven clans was the principal leader and mastermind behind the rebellion.
- That house developed enormous specialized dragon-slaying ballistas. Their design and manufacture were kept exclusive and closely guarded.
- The ballistas could reliably penetrate dragon scales, but were heavy, slow to aim and reposition, and most effective from prepared defensive positions.
- Rebel strategy prioritized killing eligible Targaryen dragon riders. When a rider died, the bond was broken and the dragon was separated from its rider.
- Most dragons were killed during the conflict. Dragons that survived and were not captured or killed fled after losing their riders.
- The ultimate fate of the surviving dragons remains unknown. No one knows whether any still exist.

### The fall and its aftermath

- The final battles destroyed the Targaryen ruling family. Nearly the entire royal family was killed.
- The dynasty lost the Imperial Seat, ending approximately 400 years of rule.
- The Great Rebellion took place approximately **200–300 years before the present campaign**. The exact year remains undecided.
- The Great Rebellion took place approximately **200–300 years before the present campaign**. The exact year remains undecided.
- The surviving dragon-riding bloodline was believed to have been eliminated. The six victorious houses believe the dynasty is extinct and do not currently fear a Targaryen claimant.
- The dwarven victors claimed Dragonstone, the dynasty's ancestral home and principal royal residence, after the rebellion.
- The main branch of the dwarven clans now occupies Dragonstone and is said to descend from Torag himself. This descent is an established dynastic claim or tradition, not a confirmed fact of divine ancestry.
- In the immediate aftermath of the rebellion, the dwarven leader and the Baratheon-inspired house held power. The dwarven leader's position was the first post-Targaryen ruling power.
- At a later point, another of the continent's major houses took the Imperial Seat. The current ruling house is therefore one of the major houses, but its name and the circumstances of its accession remain unresolved.
- One Targaryen Crown Prince survived the destruction of Dragonstone. He was nine years old at the beginning of the rebellion. His survival is concealed; his personal name belongs to the player character and is intentionally not recorded here.

## Chronology

The relative sequence is established. The Great Rebellion occurred approximately 200–300 years before the present campaign; the exact calendar year and the dates of the dynasty's rise and fall remain unresolved.

1. The dragon-riding dynasty establishes and maintains its continent-spanning political order.
2. Across generations, it increasingly centralizes authority, provoking resistance among the Great Houses.
3. Six Great Houses unite in rebellion, seeking both political independence and the destruction of Targaryen dragon supremacy.
4. The dwarven leader develops dragon-slaying ballistas and directs the rebellion.
5. The rebels target eligible dragon riders. Most dragons are killed; surviving unclaimed dragons flee or disappear.
6. The Imperial Seat falls and the Targaryen ruling family is nearly exterminated.
7. The dwarven clans' main branch claims and occupies Dragonstone.
8. The dwarven leader and the Baratheon-inspired house hold power in the immediate aftermath of the fall.
9. At a later point, another major house takes the Imperial Seat and becomes the current ruling dynasty.

## Continuity notes

- The dwarven empire did **not** fragment or collapse. It has always existed as a relatively compact union of independent clans under its main branch.
- Dragonstone's occupation by the dwarven main branch is consistent with the established dwarven imperial structure.
- The rebellion's two motives must remain linked: independence from centralization and removal of dragon-riding as the Targaryens' means of enforcing supremacy.
- Do not treat the surviving Crown Prince as publicly known. The victorious houses believe the bloodline was eliminated.
