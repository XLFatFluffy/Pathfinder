# Current Worldbuilding Direction

**Status:** APPROVED direction / details unresolved  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-03

This file records current GM-approved creative direction. It supplements the imported Campaign Bible; it does not silently rewrite preserved source material.

## Technology and atmosphere

**The setting is high-medieval. This is an explicit GM decision.**

- Use a high-medieval technological baseline: feudal societies, castles, fortified cities, conventional medieval crafts, arms, armor, siege engines, horse-drawn transport, and sailing vessels.
- There is no industrial or steampunk technological culture.
- Alchemy exists within Pathfinder First Edition's framework as a specialized field of knowledge and craft. It does not imply industrialization, mass production, or advanced machinery.
- The central identity is grounded medieval fantasy, with political and dynastic conflict at its heart.
- Supernatural horror, vampires, cults, and other supernatural threats exist, but supernatural horror is not the main theme.
- Do not carry forward the former gothic steampunk campaign concept; it was superseded when the campaign direction changed.
- Do not introduce industrial machinery, steam-powered infrastructure, or Dishonored-style technology as setting features unless the GM explicitly revises this decision.

## Originality

- Use broad fantasy archetypes and political structures as inspiration, not copied names, maps, house identities, histories, sigils, or plotlines.
- Rename and substantially rework legacy Game of Thrones scaffolding before treating it as final setting lore.
- The existing continent atlas is a provisional visual reference, not the final geography.

## Major ancestries and societies

- House Vaelorian is the approved elven Great House: a wealthy, politically influential dynasty with the words “Wealth Endures. Power Remembers.” and a golden sun rising behind a black tree on deep crimson. Its full identity and political role are recorded in the Campaign Bible.
- Prejudice against ratfolk is widespread across surface societies, although a few communities tolerate or openly accept them.
- Ratfolk have established subterranean societies and communities; they are not inherently criminal, evil, diseased, or hostile.
- Elves possess a major political power, and one of the six Great Houses is an influential elven dynasty.
- A dwarven empire has always existed and has never collapsed. It has never been especially massive; it consists of several independent clans united under the main branch, which is said to descend from Torag himself. Khar-Dum is the Kharadorns' ancestral homeland. Dragonstone was the ancestral stronghold of the fallen dragon-riding dynasty, captured by the dwarves through conquest during the Great Rebellion. Dwarves primarily live in mountains and underground, rarely in conventional surface stone-built settlements.
- Dwarven pride leads them to reproduce only with other dwarves as a cultural choice, not an established biological incompatibility.
- Other Pathfinder ancestries may exist without being designated rare or uncommon by default; the continent remains human-dominated.
- Ratfolk are not inherently criminal, evil, diseased, or hostile. Prejudice is a feature of the societies that practice it, not an objective truth about ratfolk.
- Ratfolk communities are decentralized and diverse; no single settlement or organization speaks for all ratfolk.

## Working principles

- The Pathfinder gods are real.
- Divine spellcasters are uncommon and devote themselves to a god; the deeper details of divine magic remain open.
- Arcane spellcasters are rare. Divine spellcasting is also uncommon, and an average person may never witness a spell cast.
- Civilization survives behind walls; wilderness is dangerous.
- Keep mysteries unresolved until deliberately answered.
- GM approval is required before proposed material becomes established canon.


## Approved Great House direction

The six Great Houses are established as follows: House Vaelorian (elven), House Kharadorn (dwarven), House Edarth (primarily human), House Calderan (human ruling dynasty), House Veyr (human northern dynasty), and House Merrowen (primarily human maritime dynasty). Their words, sigils, political identities, resources, goals, rebellion motives, and relationship matrix are established in the Campaign Bible.

The Kharadorns' ancestral homeland is Khar-Dum. Dragonstone was the ancestral stronghold of the fallen dragon-riding dynasty. The dwarves acquired Dragonstone through conquest during the Great Rebellion; it is a conquered possession, not the Kharadorns' ancestral homeland.

The political settlement includes House Calderan's legitimate hereditary claim to the Imperial Seat, a compromise monarch with substantial but constrained authority, an Imperial Council representing the six Great Houses, and competing political factions. The exact identities of council members and faction leaders remain unresolved.


**Naming update:** House Kharadorn is the approved original name of the dwarven Great House. The legacy placeholder House Baratheon is superseded. Khar-Dum is the ancestral homeland; Dragonstone is the conquered former Targaryen stronghold.
