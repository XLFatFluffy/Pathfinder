# Tallowmere Region — Wildlife and Ecology Baseline

**Status:** Habitat baseline established; species catalogue intentionally incomplete  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-06  
**Geographic authority:** Regional Geography and Points of Interest

## 1. Purpose

This record establishes the ecological framework for the starting region without turning every woodland, river, or abandoned quarry into a monster lair. It distinguishes ordinary wildlife, domesticated animals, pests, dangerous animals, and supernatural creatures.

The region is a settled, worked landscape. Farms, managed woodland, river traffic, quarries, roads, fishing coves, and human activity shape where animals live and how people encounter them.

## 2. Established habitats

### Northfields
Open farmland, pasture, orchards, hedgerows, drainage hollows, ditches, and small copses. Human land use is intensive near roads and settlements, becoming less regular farther from them.

### Brackenwood
Managed woodland west of Tallowmere and around Brackenholt. Coppice, mature stands, clearings, charcoal burns, gathering areas, and older unmanaged pockets coexist. Woodland rights and seasonal work shape access.

### River Vale and tributaries
A navigable lower river, slower pools, gravel bars, mill reaches, seasonal streams, flood meadows, and drainage channels. Barges, mills, ferries, fishing, and riverbank settlements create regular human activity.

### Greyback Ridges and quarry country
Low stony hills, exposed ridges, worked quarry faces, prospecting cuts, caves, and rough upland margins. The existence of caves and abandoned workings does not establish a lair, ruin, or monster population.

### Eastern Shore
Low bluffs, narrow beaches, shingle coves, small landing places, salt-working sites, fishing huts, and exposed water subject to sudden storms.

### Settled city and outskirts
Harbor, warehouses, streets, walls, refuse, cellars, gardens, granaries, stables, and the decentralized Underways. Human food stores and infrastructure influence pests and commensal animals.

## 3. Established ecological facts

- The Brackenwood supplies game and gathered plants as well as timber, fuel, and poles.
- The Northfields support farms, orchards, and pasture.
- The River Vale supports barge traffic, mills, local fishing, and wetland/flood-meadow habitats.
- The Eastern Shore supports fishing households and boat landings.
- The region experiences seasonal flooding, autumn rain, and sudden inland-sea storms.
- No specific dangerous animal population, monster lair, migration, or supernatural ecology has yet been established for the region beyond the provisional Session One troll encounter.

## 4. Catalogue status

Do not treat generic Pathfinder Bestiary entries as confirmed setting fauna. Add named species only when the GM approves them, when a reliable source establishes them, or when they are observed in play.

For ordinary background life, the GM may describe plausible common animals as low-impact scene dressing, but recurring or consequential species records should be marked PROPOSED until approved. Do not assign exact population sizes or regional frequency without evidence.

Suggested catalogue groups to develop when useful:
- Farm and household animals.
- Common woodland game and birds.
- River fish and waterfowl.
- Shoreline fish, shellfish, and seabirds.
- Pests and scavengers around settlements.
- Dangerous predators and vermin.
- Magical beasts and supernatural threats.

## 5. Encounter ecology

A creature's presence should make ecological and logistical sense:
- Food and water availability.
- Shelter, breeding, and seasonal behavior.
- Human settlement, hunting, land use, and livestock.
- Roads, rivers, cliffs, caves, and other movement corridors.
- Whether the creature is native, passing through, captive, escaped, summoned, or artificially introduced.

A creature encounter should leave evidence appropriate to the creature and circumstances. A single encounter does not establish a common population.

- Keep all supernatural origins and unexplained phenomena unresolved until supported by campaign decisions.
