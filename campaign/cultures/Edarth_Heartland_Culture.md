# Edarth Heartland Culture

**Status:** GM-approved worldbuilding expansion  
**Baseline:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC

## 1. Land and obligation

House Edarth's Western Heartlands are known for agricultural plains, valleys, landed estates, and important inland roads.

The region's wealth comes from productive land, livestock, labor, mills, storage, and the ability to move food to market.

The land is not uniformly fertile. Soil, water, tenure, labor obligations, and access to roads create substantial differences between neighboring communities.

## 2. Settlement and estate life

A typical rural settlement may include:

- a manor or administrative house;
- tenant farms or freehold holdings;
- barns and granaries;
- a mill;
- a smithy;
- a shrine or small temple;
- livestock enclosures;
- a market green;
- common pasture;
- woodland or hedgerows.

Larger towns support markets, specialized crafts, legal administration, and military mustering.

## 3. Farming and the year

Farmers plan around planting, weeding, harvest, threshing, storage, livestock breeding, and winter preparation.

A harvest is not secure until crops have been cut, processed, stored, and protected.

Mills, carts, roads, bridges, labor availability, and storage buildings can be as important as the weather.

## 4. Food

Common foods include grain breads, porridge, beans, peas, onions, cabbage, root vegetables, cheese, eggs, poultry, pork, and seasonal fruit.

Meat consumption varies by wealth and season. Preserving meat is essential for winter.

Feasts are associated with harvest, weddings, religious observances, noble hospitality, and successful completion of communal work.

## 5. Duty and reputation

Edarth culture places strong emphasis on keeping obligations.

A lord is expected to protect dependents, maintain roads and defenses, uphold customary rights, and provide leadership in crisis.

A tenant or vassal is expected to meet agreed obligations, pay rents or dues, provide service where required, and respect legitimate authority.

The system is unequal. Its defenders describe it as reciprocal duty; its critics point out that powerful landholders often decide what counts as reciprocal.

## 6. Military tradition

Military service is respected because the region's roads, estates, and food stores make it strategically important.

Household guards, levies, mounted retainers, professional soldiers, and local watches all have roles.

A soldier's reputation depends on discipline and reliability as well as courage. Protecting a granary or keeping a road open may matter more than winning a glorious duel.

## 7. Hospitality and community

Rural hospitality is shaped by resources. A prosperous manor may host lavishly. A poor household may offer bread, broth, and a place near the hearth.

Seasonal labor creates relationships between villages, estates, and towns. Disputes over wages, harvest shares, grazing, and access to woodland are common sources of friction.

## 8. Religion

Erastil is influential among farmers, hunters, and rural communities. Abadar may be important in market towns and administrative centers. Iomedae is respected among knights and soldiers. Pharasma's rites mark death and inheritance.

These traditions overlap. A family may attend a local Erastilian shrine while calling on Iomedae before a son's military service.

## 9. Internal tensions

- freeholders and tenants have different interests;
- noble duty can become an excuse for control;
- harvest taxes and requisitions can create resentment;
- merchants seek predictable access while lords defend local rights;
- younger nobles may favor courtly advancement over estate management;
- veterans may return to find their land or obligations changed.

## 10. GM portrayal

Do not reduce Edarth people to loyal farmers or honorable knights. The heartland contains ambitious landlords, indebted tenants, clever merchants, religious skeptics, deserters, idealists, and people who simply want a good harvest.

**Core cultural idea:** Land is wealth, but responsibility is what makes a landholder legitimate.
