# Merrowen Maritime Culture

**Status:** GM-approved worldbuilding expansion  
**Baseline:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC

## 1. A coast-facing society

House Merrowen's territories include extensive eastern coastline and maritime interests. Direct authority over coastal land and influence over shipping, ports, pilots, and sea routes are related but not identical.

Merrowen culture is not one uniform sailor culture. It includes port merchants, shipowners, fishers, shipwrights, coastal farmers, sailors, dock laborers, naval officers, and inland communities connected to maritime trade.

## 2. Ports and settlements

A working port is an industrial and social environment.

It contains some combination of:

- quays and landing stages;
- warehouses;
- fish markets;
- ropewalks;
- sailmakers;
- chandlers;
- shipwrights;
- customs offices;
- pilot stations;
- taverns and lodging;
- repair yards;
- shrines;
- counting houses;
- workers' housing.

The harbor's appearance changes with tide, weather, traffic, and season. A port can be prosperous while its laborers remain poor.

## 3. Ships and work

Ships require constant maintenance.

A voyage depends on hull condition, rigging, sails, provisions, water, crew health, navigation, weather, and a captain's judgment.

Maritime work includes:

- fishing;
- cargo handling;
- piloting;
- navigation;
- shipbuilding;
- caulking;
- rope making;
- sail repair;
- customs and inspection;
- provisioning;
- salvage;
- coastal patrol;
- naval service.

A shipowner may be wealthy without personally knowing how to sail. A skilled pilot may be socially important without owning a boat.

## 4. Food

Coastal diets include fish, shellfish, seaweed where locally used, breads, beans, onions, preserved vegetables, and traded goods.

Fresh fish is not automatically cheap everywhere. Catch, weather, preservation, distance from landing sites, and market control all affect price.

Salted and smoked fish are important because they can be transported inland.

## 5. Weather and knowledge

Maritime communities observe:

- wind direction;
- cloud forms;
- swell;
- tides;
- currents;
- visibility;
- seasonal storm patterns;
- bird behavior;
- harbor conditions.

Knowledge is often practical and locally specific. A sailor familiar with one coast may still be inexperienced elsewhere.

Sailors' sayings and superstitions coexist with technical skill. A captain can be highly competent and still refuse to sail on a day considered unlucky.

## 6. Hospitality and shipboard order

A ship is a workplace where poor discipline can kill everyone aboard.

Crew hierarchies are therefore important. The exact structure varies by vessel and purpose, but authority generally rests with the captain or master while specialists control particular duties.

Hospitality at sea is morally significant. A vessel may rescue people in distress, but rescue can create difficult questions about provisions, legal responsibility, salvage, and danger to the rescuing crew.

## 7. Trade and credit

Merrowen merchants depend on contracts, cargo records, insurance-like arrangements, loans, partnerships, and reliable port law.

A lost ship can ruin a household or make a creditor powerful. Cargo disputes may involve several ports and jurisdictions.

The value of a port lies not only in its harbor but in its warehouses, roads, customs administration, repair capacity, labor force, and trust.

## 8. Religion

Gozreh is important among sailors, fishers, and coastal communities. Desna is honored by travelers and navigators. Abadar is influential among merchants, administrators, and port authorities.

Pharasma's rites matter when the sea returns the dead, or does not.

Religious practice does not eliminate superstition. Many sailors keep private charms or rituals alongside formal worship.

## 9. Clothing

Clothing favors layers, wool, linen, leather, weatherproof outer garments, and footwear suited to wet surfaces.

Sailors often carry practical knives and tools as occupational equipment, not as a universal sign of criminality.

Wealthy merchants may wear imported cloth and jewelry, while dock workers wear patched clothing suited to hard labor.

## 10. Social tensions

- shipowners and crews disagree over wages and risk;
- customs officers and merchants dispute fees and inspections;
- local fishers may resent commercial fleets;
- coastal communities may distrust distant naval commands;
- port workers may resent wealthy counting houses;
- smugglers exploit complicated jurisdiction;
- inland nobles may underestimate the political importance of maritime expertise.

## 11. GM portrayal

Do not make every Merrowen character a sailor or every port a romantic tavern full of pirates.

Maritime society is built on labor, maintenance, risk, law, weather, and information. Its glamour is real, but so are debt, injury, wrecks, and unequal control of trade.

**Core cultural idea:** The sea offers opportunity, but it never signs the contract.
