# Currency, Coinage, and Moneychanging

**Snapshot:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC  
**Status:** GM reference; Pathfinder First Edition currency is the mechanical baseline  
**Visibility:** GM reference  
**Related records:** [Markets, Merchants, Guilds, and Contracts](Markets_Merchants_Guilds_and_Contracts.md), [Continental Economic Network](Continental_Economic_Network.md), [Everyday Life and Material Culture](../world/Everyday_Life_and_Material_Culture.md), [Tallowmere Shops and Stock](../locations/starting_city/Shopkeepers_and_Stock.md)

This record establishes how to handle money consistently at the table without inventing a detailed monetary history that the setting does not yet need.

The campaign uses Pathfinder First Edition's standard coin denominations and exchange values as its rules baseline. Local coin designs, issuing authorities, mints, historic currency reforms, and regional exchange practices remain open unless a source record establishes them.

## 1. Standard denominations

Use the standard Pathfinder First Edition relationships:

| Coin | Abbreviation | Relative value |
|---|---|---:|
| Copper piece | cp | 1 cp |
| Silver piece | sp | 10 cp |
| Gold piece | gp | 10 sp = 100 cp |
| Platinum piece | pp | 10 gp = 100 sp = 1,000 cp |

These are accounting relationships. A listed price in gp can be paid with equivalent combinations of smaller coins, subject to what the seller can reasonably make change for.

For play, one coin weighs approximately 1/50 of a pound, following the Pathfinder First Edition equipment rules. A large payment in physical coin is therefore heavy and conspicuous. Merchants and nobles may prefer account transfers, written obligations, deposits, or other arrangements for large sums.

## 2. What the denominations mean in daily life

Copper is useful for small purchases and minor services. Silver is practical for ordinary market transactions, modest wages, meals, and routine household expenses. Gold is significant purchasing power for most ordinary people and is more likely to appear in larger transactions, rents, contracts, equipment purchases, and payments between merchants. Platinum is high-value currency and should be uncommon in ordinary street commerce.

This is a practical portrayal guide, not a legal rule that forbids a poor person from possessing gold or requires a wealthy person to carry platinum. Coin distribution depends on occupation, wealth, savings, debt, and the immediate transaction.

A person may be wealthy in land, goods, livestock, or credit while having little coin immediately available. A shopkeeper may have a full day's sales in copper and silver but be unable to make change for a large gold coin late in the day.

## 3. Coin in circulation

The setting has not yet established a complete continental minting system. Do not assume:
- a single royal mint supplies every region;
- all coins bear the current monarch's image;
- every Great House issues sovereign currency;
- every coin is accepted at identical practical value in every port;
- a named coin series or historic reform exists without a source.

For ordinary play, coins are counted by their Pathfinder denomination. Differences in mint, weight, wear, age, or local trust may matter in a specific transaction, but should not be introduced as a surprise surcharge or arbitrary exchange penalty.

A coin can be worn and still accepted. A damaged, clipped, suspicious, counterfeit, or unfamiliar coin may be refused or referred to a moneychanger or official assay where such a service exists. No universal continental assay office or counterfeiting procedure is established.

## 4. Making change and paying large sums

Small retailers need working change. A buyer who offers a gold piece for a very cheap item may be asked for smaller coin or told to return when the seller has change. This is a practical cash-flow issue, not a refusal to honor the currency system.

Large payments may use:
- counted coin;
- a deposit followed by a balance on delivery;
- a written debt or payment order;
- a merchant's account entry;
- a witnessed contract;
- a transfer arranged through a trusted factor or moneychanger.

These arrangements require trust, records, and parties willing to honor them. A written instruction is not magical money and may be disputed, forged, delayed, or refused if the issuer lacks funds or authority.

## 5. Moneychangers and financial services

A moneychanger may count, assess, exchange, or provide change for coin. A merchant or lender may also keep accounts, extend credit, arrange deposits, or settle obligations between distant partners.

Do not assume every town has a formal bank. Financial services may be provided by moneychangers, wealthy merchants, lenders, temple treasuries where appropriate, or established trading houses.

A moneychanger's fee or exchange rate should be set for the actual service and location. Do not invent a universal percentage. A straightforward exchange between accepted Pathfinder denominations should normally follow the standard values unless a specific, established circumstance makes the coins suspect, unfamiliar, damaged, or difficult to use.

Vaelorian finance is an important continental power, but this does not mean Vaelorian institutions operate every moneychanging stall or control all credit.

## 6. Coin, credit, and wealth

Coin is only one form of wealth. Other forms include:
- land and rents;
- stored grain and livestock;
- tools, workshops, and buildings;
- cargo and inventory;
- ships, carts, and pack animals;
- debts owed to the holder;
- contracts and chartered rights;
- access to labor, transport, or markets.

These forms of wealth cannot always be converted quickly into spendable coin. A land-rich noble may struggle to pay an immediate bill. A merchant with valuable cargo may be unable to meet a debt before the ship arrives. A farmer may have grain but need coin for a tax or tool repair.

When a character attempts to pay a large sum, consider whether the character has liquid funds, whether the recipient can accept the payment, and whether the transaction is safe and lawful.

## 7. Prices and local variation

Use published Pathfinder First Edition prices as the mechanical baseline for standard equipment, goods, and services unless the campaign's local records intentionally establish a different price.

Local asking prices may vary with:
- quality and workmanship;
- region and distance from production;
- scarcity and season;
- transport costs and tolls;
- local rent and labor costs;
- market demand;
- urgency;
- reputation and credit;
- negotiation and quantity.

Tallowmere's named shops record their own stock and asking prices in [Shopkeepers and Stock](../locations/starting_city/Shopkeepers_and_Stock.md). Those local entries take precedence for those businesses. Commercial rents and guild dues are separately recorded in the city's property and guild references. Do not turn one shop's price into a universal continental price.

## 8. Fraud and disputes

Money-related disputes may involve:
- counterfeit coin;
- clipped or underweight coin;
- false accounts;
- forged payment orders;
- stolen purses;
- fraudulent debts;
- hidden fees;
- disputed exchange rates;
- refusal to honor a contract;
- a debtor who cannot pay when due.

The correct response depends on evidence, local law, custom, and the authority with jurisdiction. A suspicious coin is not proof that the person offering it knowingly committed fraud. A failed payment may reflect dishonesty, insolvency, delay, theft, or a genuine dispute.

## 9. Deliberately unresolved

The following remain open:
- the names, designs, and metal standards of regional coin issues;
- which rulers or authorities operate mints;
- historic debasements, reforms, and currency conflicts;
- local coin acceptance customs at specific ports;
- named moneychangers and financial houses outside established records;
- standard interest rates, exchange fees, and commercial commissions;
- formal banking, deposit, or payment-order institutions;
- counterfeit-detection procedures and legal penalties by jurisdiction.

Do not create a new currency system simply to decorate a purchase scene. Use the established Pathfinder denominations and add local monetary detail only when it has consequences worth playing.
