# Campaign Calendar

**Status:** ESTABLISHED WORKING CALENDAR  
**Established:** 2026-11-06  
**Campaign start:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC

This calendar provides a consistent way to date sessions, downtime, travel, historical events, festivals, and world-state changes. Month names are traditional names; they do not determine the actual weather or season.

## Dating Convention

- **AC** means **After the Crowning**.
- **BC** means **Before the Crowning**.
- Year **1 AC** is the first full year after the Crowning that established the post-Rebellion order and the current monarchy.
- The campaign begins in **243 AC**.
- The Great Rebellion remains dated approximately 200–300 years before the present campaign. Its exact starting year, ending year, and the exact date of the Crowning remain unknown.

## Year Structure

A standard year has **365 days**:

- Twelve months, each exactly 30 days: 360 days.
- Five additional **Yearturn days**, outside the months.
- Every fourth year adds one additional leap day after Yearturn, producing a 366-day year.

### Week

The seven-day week is:

1. Firstday
2. Secondday
3. Thirdday
4. Fourthday
5. Fifthday
6. Sixthday
7. Restday

The weekday sequence continues through the months and Yearturn. The leap day is also counted in the weekday sequence.

### Yearturn

Yearturn follows the 30th day of Longnight and consists of five days outside the regular months:

1. **Day of the Dead:** Remembering those who died during the year.
2. **Day of Debts:** Settling accounts and obligations where possible.
3. **Day of Oaths:** Renewing sworn promises, contracts, and duties.
4. **Day of Ancestors:** Honoring family and those who came before.
5. **New Year's Day:** Welcoming the year ahead.

In every fourth year, a sixth intercalary day follows Yearturn as the **Leap Day**. It is part of the calendar but outside the twelve months.

## Seasons

Seasons are not fixed to the calendar. They may last months or years, arrive early or late, and vary by region. A month name such as Highsun or Longnight is traditional and does not guarantee that the weather matches the name.

The GM records the actual seasonal condition for the campaign when it becomes relevant. Do not infer the current season from the month alone.

## Festivals and Observances

These are widely recognized observances. Their local customs may differ.

| Observance | Usual month | General character |
|---|---|---|
| Winter's End | Thawrise | A hope for the return of milder weather and renewed travel. |
| First Planting | Greenwake | Blessings and customs marking the start of planting. |
| Day of the Crown | Suncrest | A public observance of the monarchy and the post-Rebellion settlement. |
| Highsummer Feast | Highsun | A communal feast associated with the year's height and abundance. |
| Harvest Home | Harvestfall | Thanks and celebration for the harvest, with local variation. |
| Night of Remembrance | Duskreach | Remembering the dead and maintaining family and community memorials. |
| Yearturn | Final five days | Remembrance, settling debts, renewing oaths, honoring ancestors, and welcoming the new year. |

The observance names do not imply that every settlement celebrates identically or that a festival is compulsory.
