# Historical Accounts and Uncertainty Register

**Status:** GM-approved historical framework  
**Baseline:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC

This record separates public history, educated/noble accounts, disputed interpretations, suppressed information, and objective GM truth.

## 1. Public history

Most educated people agree that:

- A dragon-riding dynasty ruled the Imperial Seat for roughly four centuries.
- Its rulers possessed dragons and used them to dominate the continent.
- Six Great Houses rebelled against the dynasty.
- The rebellion ended the dragon-riding dynasty.
- Most dragons were killed.
- The victorious Great Houses established the current political settlement.
- House Kharadorn took Dragonstone after the rebellion.
- A later succession crisis led to a human monarch being installed on the Imperial Seat.
- House Calderan now rules from Valedorn.
- The dwarven empire remains independent.

Public accounts differ in their treatment of responsibility, heroism, betrayal, and the moral justification of the rebellion.

## 2. Noble and scholarly history

Educated histories generally recognize that the rebellion had two related purposes:

1. preserve Great House independence from increasing royal centralization;
2. eliminate dragon-riding as the military foundation of the dynasty's supremacy.

Most surviving records agree that Kharadorn developed specialized dragon-slaying ballistas and that the rebellion deliberately targeted dragon riders.

The precise contribution of each Great House remains disputed.

## 3. Competing interpretations

### The Crown's interpretation

The Crown emphasizes:

- the rebellion as a necessary end to dynastic tyranny;
- the legitimacy of the post-Rebellion settlement;
- the Calderan hereditary claim;
- the necessity of preserving peace between the Crown and Great Houses.

### Traditionalist Great House interpretation

Traditionalist nobles emphasize:

- the Great Houses' defense of hereditary rights;
- the danger of centralized monarchy;
- the sacrifices made by noble families;
- the legitimacy of local autonomy.

### Kharadorn interpretation

Kharadorn traditions emphasize:

- the necessity of destroying dragon supremacy;
- dwarven military ingenuity;
- clan sacrifice;
- the preservation of dwarven independence;
- the legitimacy of Kharadorn control over Dragonstone.

### Vaelorian interpretation

Vaelorian historians emphasize:

- the political danger of unchecked centralization;
- the importance of preserving commercial autonomy;
- the economic costs of dynastic war;
- the long-term value of negotiated political settlements.

### Veyr interpretation

Northern accounts emphasize:

- the costs of defending the realm;
- the danger of southern political complacency;
- the importance of maintaining strong frontier institutions.

### Merrowen interpretation

Maritime histories emphasize:

- the disruption of trade;
- the importance of keeping ports and sea lanes open;
- the role of fleets and shipping in sustaining the rebellion and its aftermath.

## 4. Suppressed or dangerous history

The following subjects are dangerous for political reasons:

- the precise identity of surviving members of the fallen dragon-riding bloodline;
- details of the final destruction of the dragon-riding dynasty;
- the identity and fate of surviving dragons;
- evidence of Great House betrayal during the rebellion;
- private agreements made during the post-Rebellion settlement;
- details of the dwarven succession crisis;
- evidence that could undermine the legitimacy of the current settlement.

Possession of such records does not automatically mean that the records are true.

## 5. Objective GM truth

The following are established as objective campaign truth:

- The dragon-riding dynasty possessed exactly twelve dragons at its height.
- Eligible Targaryen blood was required for dragon bonding.
- Each rider could bond with one dragon.
- The rebellion deliberately sought both Great House independence and the destruction of dragon-riding supremacy.
- Kharadorn was the principal mastermind of the rebellion and developed specialized dragon-slaying ballistas.
- Most dragons were killed.
- Surviving dragons fled after losing their riders; their ultimate fate is unknown.
- The ruling dragon-dynasty family was nearly exterminated.
- A nine-year-old Crown Prince survived the destruction of Dragonstone.
- His survival is concealed.
- His personal name is the player's character information and is not recorded in the campaign database.
- The six victorious houses believe the bloodline is extinct.
- The same dwarven leader ruled for more than a century after the rebellion.
- His successor inherited dwarven authority and later threatened war unless a human was installed on the Imperial Seat.
- A head of House Calderan was installed as the compromise monarch.
- House Calderan possesses a legitimate hereditary claim to the Imperial Seat.
- The monarch is not a puppet and is not secretly controlled by Kharadorn or another single power.
- The dwarven successor returned to Dragonstone and continued ruling the clans.
- The dwarven empire and human monarchy remain in an uneasy peace.
- The precise historical dates and detailed battle chronology remain unknown even to the GM unless separately established.

## 6. Historical recordkeeping rule

When a new historical source appears, classify it as:

- **confirmed:** independently supported campaign fact;
- **probable:** strong but incomplete evidence;
- **disputed:** credible competing interpretations exist;
- **suppressed:** politically dangerous or deliberately concealed;
- **false:** a deliberate or accidental historical error;
- **unknown:** insufficient evidence.

A document being old does not make it true.

## 7. Rebellion chronology framework

The campaign may eventually assign specific dates and named battles to the following sequence:

1. growing royal centralization;
2. formation of the rebel coalition;
3. early rebellion;
4. development of dragon-slaying weapons;
5. major dragon-rider defeats;
6. destruction of the royal stronghold;
7. fall of the Imperial Seat;
8. extermination campaign against the royal family;
9. Kharadorn occupation of Dragonstone;
10. postwar settlement;
11. more-than-century-long dwarven rule;
12. dwarven succession crisis;
13. compromise installation of House Calderan;
14. return of the dwarven successor to Dragonstone;
15. present uneasy settlement.

Exact dates and individual campaign names remain open.

## 8. Historical mystery rule

The campaign should not answer every historical mystery through a single exposition dump.

Players should encounter:

- contradictory records;
- old monuments;
- family traditions;
- damaged archives;
- surviving weapons;
- disputed genealogies;
- oral histories;
- religious interpretations;
- political propaganda;
- physical evidence.

The truth should emerge through accumulated evidence.
