# Holidays and Public Observances

**Status:** GM-approved worldbuilding expansion  
**Baseline:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC

The calendar establishes common observances. This record explains why people care about them and how celebrations differ by class, region, and faith.

## 1. Winter's End — Thawrise

### Meaning
Winter's End marks the point at which communities begin preparing for renewed travel, planting, trade, and outdoor work.

It is not a claim that winter literally ends on the same date everywhere.

### Origins
The observance predates the current monarchy and likely developed independently in farming communities.

### Common customs
- repairing tools;
- cleaning houses and barns;
- checking roads and bridges;
- communal meals;
- prayers for favorable weather;
- exchanging preserved foods.

### Poor households
The emphasis is practical: repairing what survived winter and preparing for work.

### Wealthier households
Nobles may host formal meals and sponsor repairs to roads, bridges, or village infrastructure.

## 2. First Planting — Greenwake

### Meaning
First Planting marks the beginning of the agricultural year in communities where the soil and weather permit planting.

### Religious associations
Erastil is commonly honored, though other gods may be invoked according to local tradition.

### Customs
- seed blessings;
- plowing ceremonies;
- communal planting;
- children's games;
- village meals;
- agricultural wagers and contests.

### Taboos
Mocking the first planting or deliberately wasting seed is considered deeply foolish.

## 3. Day of the Crown — Suncrest

### Meaning
This is the principal civil observance of the current monarchy and the post-Rebellion political settlement.

It celebrates continuity rather than claiming that everyone loves the Crown.

### Customs
- public readings of royal proclamations;
- military displays;
- market fairs;
- civic ceremonies;
- oaths of loyalty in some jurisdictions;
- charitable distributions;
- bells, music, and banners.

### Political meaning
Traditionalists emphasize the rights preserved by the settlement.

Royal Expansionists emphasize the unity and authority of the Crown.

Merchants emphasize stable law and trade.

Religious leaders may emphasize legitimate rule and social order.

Different people therefore celebrate the same holiday for different reasons.

## 4. Highsummer Feast — Highsun

### Meaning
Highsummer celebrates abundance, work completed so far, and the height of the growing season.

### Customs
- communal feasts;
- dancing;
- music;
- contests;
- markets;
- courtship;
- drinking;
- charitable meals.

### Noble version
Nobles may host multi-day feasts, tournaments, hunts, and diplomatic gatherings.

### Common version
Villages focus on food, music, games, and rest.

## 5. Harvest Home — Harvestfall

### Meaning
Harvest Home is among the most important observances for agricultural communities.

It marks the beginning or culmination of the harvest depending on local climate and crops.

### Why it matters
Harvest determines:

- whether families have enough food;
- whether taxes can be paid;
- whether animals can be fed;
- whether debts can be settled;
- whether merchants can buy grain;
- whether winter preparations succeed.

Harvest is therefore economic, religious, and political.

### Customs
- first-fruit offerings;
- communal harvest meals;
- market fairs;
- songs;
- blessings;
- settling seasonal accounts;
- hiring seasonal labor;
- repairing storage buildings.

### Noble obligations
A competent lord is expected to ensure that harvest infrastructure, roads, mills, and storage remain usable.

Failure can become a political grievance.

## 6. Night of Remembrance — Duskreach

### Meaning
The Night of Remembrance honors the dead.

### Customs
Families visit graves or memorials, tell stories, light lamps, preserve genealogies, and share meals.

Pharasma is commonly invoked, but local customs vary.

### Emotional character
Some communities are solemn.

Others tell funny stories about the dead.

Some families consider laughter a form of remembrance rather than disrespect.

## 7. Yearturn

Yearturn is the five-day transition between years.

### Day of the Dead
Families remember those who died during the year.

### Day of Debts
People attempt to settle debts, return borrowed property, and reconcile accounts.

Merchants take this seriously.

### Day of Oaths
Sworn obligations and contracts are renewed.

Nobles may use the day for formal declarations.

### Day of Ancestors
Families honor those who came before them.

Kharadorn dwarves and Veyr northerners place particular emphasis on this day, though their customs differ.

### New Year's Day
Families share meals, make predictions, exchange gifts where affordable, and begin the new year.

## 8. Leap Day

Leap Day occurs every fourth year.

It is not universally religious.

Common traditions include:

- unusual games;
- charitable acts;
- public performances;
- temporary markets;
- practical jokes;
- special contracts;
- marriages in cultures that consider the day auspicious.

Some communities consider Leap Day outside ordinary time and therefore unsuitable for important business. Others consider it especially fortunate.

## 9. Regional differences

The same observance can feel completely different in different places.

A northern village may celebrate Harvest Home with practical accounting and gratitude for survival.

A wealthy city may turn it into a huge market festival.

A Merrowen port may combine it with sailors' ceremonies.

A Kharadorn community may incorporate clan remembrance.

A Vaelorian estate may treat it as an opportunity for patronage, feasting, and formal recognition of workers.

The calendar is shared. Culture is not.

## 10. Religious festivals

Faith-specific festivals are maintained by temples and religious communities in addition to the civil calendar.

There is no single continent-wide religious calendar that overrides all local observance.

A temple may close its doors for a private holy day while the surrounding market operates normally.

Conversely, a local religious festival can become so popular that merchants voluntarily close for the day.

## 11. Political use of holidays

Holidays provide opportunities for rulers to demonstrate competence.

A lord who provides food during a shortage gains legitimacy.

A ruler who uses a festival to announce a tax increase may create resentment.

A Great House may sponsor games, roads, hospitals, temples, or public meals.

The same act can therefore be charity, propaganda, religious devotion, or political investment.

## 12. What players should notice

Festivals are useful because they reveal the society around the PCs.

The important question is not simply "what festival is today?"

It is:

**Who benefits from the festival, who pays for it, who is expected to participate, and what does the community believe it means?**
