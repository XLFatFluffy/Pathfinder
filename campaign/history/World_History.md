# World History

> Layered public, noble, disputed, suppressed, and objective historical accounts are maintained in `Historical_Accounts_and_Uncertainty.md`. Use that record when separating what characters may know from GM truth.

**Status:** ESTABLISHED HISTORICAL FOUNDATION / CHRONOLOGY INCOMPLETE  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-06

This record separates confirmed historical facts from dates, names, and political details that remain undecided. House Kharadorn is an approved original Great House name. The fallen dragon-riding dynasty's name and Dragonstone's name remain provisional working labels pending replacement. The Imperial Seat is an established institutional term, though its final in-world naming may still be refined.

## Established historical foundation

### The dragon-riding dynasty

- A dragon-riding dynasty ruled from the Imperial Seat for approximately **400 years**.
- The dynasty's power rested on its exclusive bloodline connection to dragons, its control of the Imperial Seat, and its ability to command the loyalty, obedience, or submission of other kingdoms and great houses.
- The dynasty maintained a substantial royal family with multiple branches and collateral lines.
- Its authority combined direct rule, subordinate kingdoms, political alliances, tribute, hostages, garrisons, governors, and military intervention.
- Over time, the dynasty increasingly sought to centralize authority and reduce the independence of the great houses.
- At the height of its power, the dynasty possessed exactly **twelve dragons**.
- The ability to bond with dragons was exclusive to eligible members of the Targaryen bloodline. Eligibility depended on preserving the bloodline through reproduction within that eligible lineage.
- Each rider could bond with only one dragon. The bond created a telepathic connection through which the rider could communicate with and influence the dragon.
- The dragons were enormous flying reptilian predators with great physical strength, flight, and fire-breath. They were not traditional Pathfinder dragons: they were not spellcasters, scholarly, or sophisticated talking creatures.

### The Great Rebellion

- **Six Great Houses** united against the dragon-riding dynasty.
- The rebellion had two connected objectives:
  1. Restore and preserve the Great Houses' political independence against a dynasty they feared would permanently subordinate them.
  2. Eliminate the dynasty's dragon-riding capability, because its control of dragons was the foundation that allowed it to maintain overwhelming political and military power.
- The rebellion was therefore both a struggle against centralization and a deliberate campaign to destroy the dragon-riding institution as a source of dynastic supremacy.
- The war lasted a few years and was devastating.
- The dwarven Great House associated with the main branch of the dwarven clans was the principal leader and mastermind behind the rebellion.
- That house developed enormous specialized dragon-slaying ballistas. Their design and manufacture were kept exclusive and closely guarded.
- The ballistas could reliably penetrate dragon scales, but were heavy, slow to aim and reposition, and most effective from prepared defensive positions.
- Rebel strategy prioritized killing eligible Targaryen dragon riders. When a rider died, the bond was broken and the dragon was separated from its rider.
- Most dragons were killed during the conflict. Dragons that survived and were not captured or killed fled after losing their riders.
- The ultimate fate of the surviving dragons remains unknown. No one knows whether any still exist.

### The fall and its aftermath

- The final battles destroyed the Targaryen ruling family. Nearly the entire royal family was killed.
- The dynasty lost the Imperial Seat, ending approximately 400 years of rule.
- The Great Rebellion took place approximately **200–300 years before the present campaign**. The exact year remains undecided.
- The surviving dragon-riding bloodline was believed to have been eliminated. The six victorious houses believe the dynasty is extinct and do not currently fear a Targaryen claimant.
- The dwarven victors claimed Dragonstone, the fallen dynasty's ancestral home and principal royal residence, through conquest after the rebellion. Dragonstone was not the dwarves' ancestral homeland; the Kharadorns' ancestral homeland is Khar-Dum.
- The main branch of the dwarven clans occupies the conquered Dragonstone stronghold and is said to descend from Torag himself. Khar-Dum remains the Kharadorns' ancestral homeland. This descent is an established dynastic claim or tradition, not a confirmed fact of divine ancestry.
- In the immediate aftermath of the rebellion, the dwarven leader and the Kharadorn house held power. The same dwarven leader ruled for more than a century and remained in power until his death.
- After his death, a succession crisis followed. His successor inherited the dwarven leader's authority, but the crisis led to a confrontation over the Imperial Seat.
- The dwarven successor threatened war unless a human was placed on the Imperial Seat. The head of another of the original six rebel Great Houses was installed as a compromise candidate and became the current monarch under a compromise settlement. The dwarven successor accepted this arrangement reluctantly, then returned to Dragonstone and continued ruling the dwarven clans from the conquered former Targaryen stronghold. The dwarven successor retained the authority inherited within the dwarven order, while the other house took the wider imperial throne. The dwarven empire and the human monarchy now maintain an uneasy peace marked by mutual distrust. War would not favor the dwarves, but they are not afraid of that prospect.
- House Calderan, one of the original six rebel Great Houses, now occupies the Imperial Seat. Its head was installed as the compromise monarch.
- The monarch's family has a legitimate claim to the Imperial Seat. He retains substantial authority, although the Great Houses can constrain him. A council or coalition of Great Houses influences him, while several competing factions pull him in different directions. The other rebel houses see the throne as an opportunity to manipulate it for their own interests. The specific identities and balance of these powers remain unresolved.
- One Targaryen Crown Prince survived the destruction of Dragonstone. He was nine years old at the beginning of the rebellion. His survival is concealed; his personal name belongs to the player character and is intentionally not recorded here.

## Chronology

The relative sequence is established. The Great Rebellion occurred approximately 200–300 years before the present campaign; the exact calendar year and the dates of the dynasty's rise and fall remain unresolved.

1. The dragon-riding dynasty establishes and maintains its continent-spanning political order.
2. Across generations, it increasingly centralizes authority, provoking resistance among the Great Houses.
3. Six Great Houses unite in rebellion, seeking both political independence and the destruction of Targaryen dragon supremacy.
4. The dwarven leader develops dragon-slaying ballistas and directs the rebellion.
5. The rebels target eligible dragon riders. Most dragons are killed; surviving unclaimed dragons flee or disappear.
6. The Imperial Seat falls and the Targaryen ruling family is nearly exterminated.
7. The dwarven clans' main branch claims and occupies Dragonstone, the former ancestral stronghold of the fallen dragon-riding dynasty. Khar-Dum remains the Kharadorns' ancestral homeland.
8. The dwarven leader and the Kharadorn house hold power in the immediate aftermath of the fall.
9. The same dwarven leader remains in power for more than a century, until his death.
10. His successor inherits his authority, but a succession crisis follows.
11. The dwarven successor threatens war unless a human is placed on the Imperial Seat; the head of another of the original six rebel Great Houses is installed as a compromise candidate and becomes the current monarch. Earlier summaries describe him as a puppet, but current canon establishes his legitimate hereditary claim and substantial formal authority; the practical constraints and sources of influence remain unresolved.
12. The dwarven successor reluctantly accepts the arrangement, returns to Dragonstone, and continues ruling the dwarven clans.
13. The dwarven empire and human monarchy maintain an uneasy peace marked by mutual distrust. War would not favor the dwarves, but they are not afraid of it.

## Continuity notes

- The dwarven empire did **not** fragment or collapse. It has always existed as a relatively compact union of independent clans under its main branch.
- Dragonstone's occupation by the dwarven main branch is consistent with the established dwarven imperial structure.
- The rebellion's two motives must remain linked: independence from centralization and removal of dragon-riding as the Targaryens' means of enforcing supremacy.
- The dwarven leader was the same individual for more than a century after the rebellion. After his death, his successor inherited dwarven authority; the successor's threat of war compelled the installation of the head of another rebel Great House as a human compromise monarch. The monarch has a legitimate hereditary claim and substantial formal authority, although the Great Houses and court factions constrain his practical freedom. Do not characterize him as a powerless puppet or assume a single controlling power. The dwarven successor reluctantly accepted the arrangement, returned to Dragonstone, and continued ruling the clans. The dwarven empire and human monarchy maintain an uneasy peace; war would not favor the dwarves, but they are not afraid of it.
- Do not treat the surviving Crown Prince as publicly known. The victorious houses believe the bloodline was eliminated.


## The Six Great Houses — approved identities

The six houses below are the established identities of the rebel Great Houses and the current major powers. Their detailed words, sigils, cultures, resources, ambitions, and relationships are recorded in the Campaign Bible.

- **House Vaelorian (elven):** A wealthy commercial and financial dynasty whose influence rests on trade, lending, mines, contracts, and political leverage. Words: “Wealth Endures. Power Remembers.”
- **House Kharadorn (dwarven):** The Great House associated with the main branch of the enduring dwarven empire and the principal mastermind of the Great Rebellion. Words: “Stone Remembers. Blood Endures.” Khar-Dum is its ancestral homeland. Dragonstone was the ancestral stronghold of the dragon-riding dynasty and was taken by dwarven conquest during the rebellion.
- **House Edarth (primarily human):** A traditional landed and martial power committed to preserving noble independence. Words: “Stand When Others Fall.”
- **House Calderan (human):** The current ruling dynasty, descended from an older royal bloodline that lost the throne during the dragon-riding dynasty's conquest but preserved a legitimate hereditary claim. Its head was installed as the post-rebellion compromise monarch. Words: “By Right, We Remain.”
- **House Veyr (human):** The northern dynasty responsible for maintaining the Ancient Barrier and defending the frontier. Words: “Beyond the Wall, We Stand.”
- **House Merrowen (primarily human):** A maritime and naval power whose wealth and influence rest on ports, shipping, fleets, and trade. Words: “All Roads Reach the Sea.”

The houses shared enemies during the rebellion but did not share a single vision for the political settlement that followed. Their approved political relationship matrix and differing rebellion motives are recorded in the Campaign Bible.
