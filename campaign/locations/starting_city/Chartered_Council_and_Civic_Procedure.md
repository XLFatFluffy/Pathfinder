# Tallowmere: Chartered Council and Civic Procedure

**Status:** ESTABLISHED LOCAL FRAMEWORK — 2026-11-04  
**Authority:** House Denvor hereditary charter, confirmed by House Kharadorn.

## Purpose and legal position

Tallowmere's Chartered Council is a recognized advisory and negotiating body. It exists to give the lordship's principal commercial, property, and civic interests a regular place to raise concerns and coordinate practical obligations.

It is not an elected parliament, sovereign city council, or independent legislature. Lord Oswin Denvor retains final local authority under the charter. The Council cannot depose House Denvor, appoint the lord's officers, overturn a lawful judgment, or compel the lord to accept its recommendation.

Its influence is substantial because the city depends on cooperation. A lord who ignores every recommendation may discover that ships are delayed, repairs are deferred, merchants withhold investment, and labor becomes harder to organize.

## Membership

The Council has seven recognized seats:

1. **Harbor Fellowship seat:** A representative chosen by the Fellowship's recognized members. Current holder: Captain-Owner Tavin Rusk, human, a cautious shipowner who favors predictable berth allocation and resists special treatment for any single fleet.
2. **Warehouse Guild seat:** A representative chosen by the Guild's recognized keepers. Current holder: Meret Sorn, human, the established Guild factor.
3. **Market Guild seat:** A representative chosen by established market members. Current holder: Beren Harlowe, human, general merchant and wholesaler. His appointment does not make him the voice of every independent trader.
4. **River Carters' and Bargemen's Company seat:** A representative chosen by active member operators. Current holder: Pellan Reed, halfling, the Company's established organizer.
5. **Crafts seat:** A joint nomination agreed by the Artificers' Hall and Shipwrights' Brotherhood. Current holder: Dagna Coppervein, dwarf, master of the Shipwrights' Brotherhood. The two bodies are expected to agree on a replacement when the seat becomes vacant.
6. **Property holders' seat:** A representative of recognized substantial urban property holders, selected by agreement among eligible holders and confirmed by the lord. Current holder: Master Edran Pellor, human, an established owner of rental and warehouse property. He is not related to every Pellor named in the city.
7. **Civic household seat:** A resident appointed by Lord Denvor to bring household, ward, and practical civic concerns before the Council. Current holder: Lady Anwen Denvor. Her presence is not an independent vote for the Denvor family.

The lord presides when present. The Town Reeve Marrec Holt keeps the record and issues notices. The Harbor Master, Chief Magistrate, Captain of the Watch, Castellan, and other officers attend when matters within their duties are discussed. They do not gain a Council seat merely by holding office.

Religious institutions have no automatic seat. Ratfolk neighborhoods have no separate guaranteed seat under the current charter. Ratfolk residents may petition, attend public portions where permitted, submit a written or witnessed statement through the Reeve, or seek sponsorship from a member. This is a real structural disadvantage and a live civic issue, not evidence that all Council members agree with exclusion.

## Appointment and tenure

Guild and company seats are filled according to each body's internal rules, subject to recognition by the lord. The property holders' representative is selected by eligible holders and confirmed by the lord. The civic household seat is held at the lord's pleasure.

There is no fixed universal term established for all seats. Guilds may replace a representative after internal disagreement, resignation, incapacity, or loss of recognized standing. A disputed appointment is referred to the Town Reeve and ultimately the lord.

A seat does not automatically pass to a representative's heir.

## Meetings and procedure

- The Council normally meets in the Reeve's Hall. The lord may summon a special meeting at Greywatch or another suitable civic location.
- Ordinary meetings are called by the Town Reeve after consultation with the lord's household and recognized members.
- A quorum is four of the seven recognized seats.
- Recommendations are recorded when a majority of those present support them. A tied or divided matter is recorded as unresolved, with principal objections noted.
- The lord may request a second discussion, accept a recommendation, reject it, or act independently within his lawful authority.
- The Council may submit a formal petition, request an accounting of a civic project, or ask the lord to delay a decision for negotiation. It has no general veto.
- Emergency meetings may be called during fire, flood, harbor closure, riot, or threat to the walls. Emergency advice does not transfer command from the lord or his appointed officers.
- The Town Reeve maintains minutes, attendance, petitions, and formal recommendations. Sensitive commercial matters may be summarized rather than publicly detailed.

The Council aims for consensus because its members must continue working together. A recorded majority is a political signal, not a statute.

## Public access and secrecy

Routine agenda notices are posted at Reeve's Hall and the principal market. Commercially sensitive disputes, security matters, private petitions, and personnel questions may be heard in closed session.

Residents may submit petitions through the Reeve's office. Petitioners do not have an automatic right to speak at every meeting. The presiding lord or a majority of present members may invite a petitioner to address a specific matter.

The absence of a formal ratfolk seat is increasingly criticized by some residents and charitable figures. A petition channel exists, but access depends on the Reeve's handling, sponsorship, and whether the issue reaches the agenda.

## Current divisions

- Tavin Rusk wants reliable harbor capacity and resists giving small operators priority that disrupts scheduled cargo.
- Meret Sorn supports fire-safety investment but wants costs shared by property owners, tenants, and the lordship according to responsibility.
- Beren Harlowe wants market rules applied consistently but is wary of expanding stall access in ways that reduce established members' advantage.
- Pellan Reed argues that river traffic and inland food supply receive too little attention.
- Dagna Coppervein wants skilled labor and material supply protected from unrealistic deadlines.
- Edran Pellor opposes extraordinary property levies and favors gradual improvements.
- Anwen Denvor listens closely to residents and merchants who rarely approach Greywatch, but she cannot independently commit her family to policy.

No two members agree on every issue. Their positions can change when circumstances or evidence change.

## Open details

The full charter text, original date of Council creation, exact guild election customs, property-holder eligibility, and historical precedents remain unresolved. The procedures above govern current play unless later amended by an explicit GM decision.
