# Tallowmere: City Layout and Defenses

**Status:** Working geographic framework. Relative layout is established; exact map geometry and measurements remain provisional.

## Setting
Tallowmere stands on the northwestern shore of the central inland sea, where a navigable river reaches the lake through a broad, sheltered estuary. The river approaches from the western uplands, bringing timber, stone, grain, livestock, and inland trade. The harbor opens east and southeast toward the inland sea.

The city occupies a defensible rise above the water and the lower river terraces. Older streets follow the contours of the hill; newer commercial quarters spread along the waterfront and river approaches.

## Overall Shape
The old walled city forms an irregular crescent around the natural harbor. Greywatch stands on the highest ground overlooking the harbor mouth and principal quays. The older wall follows the ridge and descends toward the waterfront. Later expansions have added outer defenses around the newer quays and working districts.

The walls are not a perfect continuous ring. The river, steep ground, harbor works, and later additions have produced different defensive lines and gates. Some outer neighborhoods lie beyond the oldest wall but within the wider defended perimeter.

## District Placement
- **Greywatch Rise:** Highest ground, overlooking the harbor and lower city. Greywatch occupies the dominant fortified position.
- **High Ward:** Below and southeast of Greywatch, on elevated streets with larger houses, legal offices, and established institutions.
- **Old Quays:** Along the older sheltered waterfront immediately below the central rise. Narrow streets, older warehouses, ship repair, chandlers, and taverns.
- **New Quays:** Extends along the more recently developed outer harbor frontage. Larger yards, bulk cargo storage, newer warehouses, and less uniform construction.
- **Market Ward:** Inland from the Old Quays and between the principal gate roads. It links harbor trade to the older city.
- **Rivergate:** Follows the lower river approach west and northwest of the market. Mills, bargemen, cart yards, ferries, and river warehouses.
- **Lantern Ward:** A quieter elevated quarter on the landward side of the High Ward, with temples, hospice grounds, burial spaces, and gardens.
- **The Underways:** Subterranean neighborhoods and routes beneath several districts. They are not a single contiguous level and do not map neatly to surface boundaries.

## Gates and Access
The principal approaches are:
- **West Gate:** Main road toward the western uplands and the Western Mountain Road.
- **North Gate:** Road toward farms, villages, and the Northern Road.
- **River Gate:** Controlled access for river traffic, barges, and the western riverbank approaches.
- **South Gate:** Road toward the southern shore settlements and Southern Trade Road.
- **Harbor Gates:** Controlled openings in the waterfront defenses, used for access between quays, yards, and the city proper.

Gate names are working names. Exact number, construction, and opening procedures remain to be mapped. Harbor traffic is also regulated through quay controls, booms or chains where appropriate, pilotage, and watch patrols.

## Harbor and River
The harbor has several functional zones:
- Older quays for established merchants, passenger traffic, and repair work.
- Newer wharves for bulk cargo and vessels requiring more room.
- Smaller landings and river stages for barges, ferries, and local craft.
- Warehouses and counting houses set back from the water.
- Shipyards and repair yards where timber, pitch, rope, and skilled labor are available.

The river is navigable by barges and suitable vessels for a substantial distance inland, but its depth and navigability vary with season and weather. The exact point at which larger ships must transfer cargo remains open.

## Roads
The main roads converge on Tallowmere but do not all pass through the same gate.
- Western Mountain Road: upland settlements, quarries, timber, and western trade.
- Northern Road: agricultural villages and the Northern Marches.
- King's Road: the principal overland route connecting the city toward the Central Crownlands and Valedorn.
- Eastern Coastal Road: ports, fishing settlements, and eastern maritime holdings.
- Southern Trade Road: fragmented southern territories and markets.
- Inland Sea Route: shipping connections to Dragonstone, Kharadorn territory, and other lake ports.

## Defenses
Tallowmere's defenses are designed to deter raids, contain disorder, and resist a limited siege. They are not sufficient to withstand a Great House's full army indefinitely.
- Greywatch commands the harbor approach and contains the lord's household guard.
- The walls have towers and gatehouses at major approaches.
- The garrison maintains stores, weapons, and basic siege equipment.
- The harbor can be closed or restricted in an emergency, though doing so damages trade.
- The watch handles ordinary order; the garrison reinforces the walls and gates when required.

The city is vulnerable to fire, disease, food disruption, river flooding, and blockade. A strong wall does not solve every problem.

## Subterranean Geography
The Underways are a patchwork beneath old foundations, cellars, drainage channels, abandoned wells, natural cavities, and constructed passages. Some passages connect neighborhoods; others terminate, collapse, or are deliberately sealed. Surface authorities do not possess a complete map. Ratfolk route knowledge is local and varied, not universal.

## Map Development Notes
This document establishes relative placement only. Do not treat it as a precise scale map. Before producing a definitive map, establish coastline shape, river bends, harbor depth, wall lines, gate locations, bridges, and the actual relationship between districts.
