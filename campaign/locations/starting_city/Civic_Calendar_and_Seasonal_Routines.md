# Tallowmere: Civic Calendar and Seasonal Routines

**Status:** ESTABLISHED LOCAL ROUTINE FRAMEWORK — reconciled 2026-11-09  
**Calendar dependency:** Uses the formal [Campaign Calendar](../../history/Calendar.md), established 2026-11-06. The AC dating system, year structure, month names, weekday names, Yearturn, leap day, and campaign start date are established. This document leaves local market schedules, bell times, gate hours, and exact dates of civic observances open.

## Daily rhythm

Tallowmere's working day follows daylight, tide, river conditions, and the needs of trade rather than a uniform clock.

### Before sunrise
- Bakers, mill workers, market porters, stable hands, and some quay crews begin work.
- Harbor watchmen check vessels that arrived overnight and note unsafe moorings.
- River pilots assess water level, weather, and current before upstream departures.
- Household shrines may receive brief morning offerings.

### Morning
- Bellmarket becomes active as food sellers, craftspeople, carriers, and customers arrive.
- The Harbor Office handles declarations, berth questions, and routine vessel business.
- Reeve's Hall receives petitions and civic payments during posted office hours.
- Workshops begin regular production; apprentices and hired labor report to their masters or foremen.

### Midday
- Cargo movement slows in the hottest part of warm-season days, though the quays rarely become still.
- Public meals and low-cost food stalls serve laborers.
- Some temples hold their principal public service or teaching during a predictable daylight interval. Each institution sets its own schedule.

### Afternoon
- Barges unload or depart according to river conditions.
- Merchants settle accounts, inspect goods, negotiate orders, and arrange transport.
- Civic officers conduct inspections, hearings, and maintenance rounds.
- Guild meetings and craft instruction are more likely when major work permits.

### Evening
- The market winds down; taverns, inns, shrines, and household courtyards become more active.
- Harbor crews secure lines, lamps, and cargo. Night unloading is possible when paid for and authorized, but is not the default for every vessel.
- The Watch concentrates patrols around gates, quays, market approaches, and known trouble spots.

### Night
- The gates follow posted closing procedures, with controlled entry for emergencies and authorized traffic.
- The Harbor Master may restrict vessel movement during dangerous weather or harbor incidents.
- The Watch maintains patrols; the city is not under a universal curfew.
- Underways movement continues according to local needs and access customs. Surface gate closure does not automatically seal every subterranean route.

## Bells, notices, and public information

Public bells and posted notices communicate major civic events:
- Market and harbor office openings.
- Fire, flood, storm, or security warnings.
- Public hearings and proclamations.
- Changes to gate or harbor procedures.
- Funerals or memorial gatherings of civic importance.

The bell system is practical, not a precise citywide clock. Bells may be used for religious observance, civic summons, and local warnings, but exact signal patterns, schedules, and meanings are not established. Do not assume a standardized citywide alarm code. See [Emergency Response and Civic Resilience](Emergency_Response_and_Civic_Resilience.md) for the limits of public warnings during an incident.

Routine agenda notices of the Chartered Council are posted at Reeve's Hall and the principal market. Other notices may be placed at the Weigh House or relevant guild and harbor offices when appropriate, but no comprehensive posting network or fixed posting schedule is established. Not every resident can read a notice; information may also be repeated orally.

## Market and trade cycles

Bellmarket operates on ordinary working days, with a larger gathering on recurring market days announced locally. The formal seven-day week is Firstday, Secondday, Thirdday, Fourthday, Fifthday, Sixthday, and Restday. The exact frequency and named day of Tallowmere's larger market gathering remain unresolved.

Seasonal trade affects what appears:
- Grain and orchard produce follow harvest and storage cycles.
- Fish, shellfish, and preserved food vary with weather and catch.
- Timber, charcoal, stone, and quarry shipments depend on roads, labor, and weather.
- Sea traffic rises and falls with sailing conditions.
- River cargo depends on water levels and safe passage.
- Imported luxuries arrive irregularly and may be delayed by distant events.

The Harbor Office and guilds publish practical notices when major arrivals, closures, or shortages affect trade. There is no single official citywide price board.

## Seasonal character

Residents speak of four broad seasonal phases in ordinary terms, but their arrival, duration, and regional character vary. The formal calendar does not fix seasons to particular months; a month name is not a weather forecast.

### Spring
- River levels rise with rain and upland melt.
- Mill operations and river transport may be disrupted by high water.
- Repairs to roads, bridges, drainage, and embankments become urgent.
- Farmers and estate workers prepare fields and livestock.
- Sailors watch for unsettled weather after winter.

### Summer
- Longer daylight supports construction, quarrying, travel, and major repairs.
- Harbor traffic and coastal fishing are generally more active when weather permits.
- Heat increases concern about stored grain, fire, sanitation, and water quality.
- Wealthier households host more outdoor gatherings and visiting merchants.

### Autumn
- Harvest and grain transport dominate regional commerce.
- Warehouses fill; disputes over storage, quality, and measurement become more common.
- Storms and heavy rain threaten the coast, river, and low quays.
- Households prepare fuel, food stores, roofs, and winter clothing.

### Winter
- Short daylight slows road travel and some outdoor work.
- River traffic may become unreliable during severe cold or high water.
- Harbor work continues when conditions allow, but storms and fog can delay vessels.
- Demand rises for fuel, repairs, lodging, and charitable assistance.
- The hospice and temples face greater pressure from illness, cold, and hardship.

These are broad tendencies, not guaranteed weather or harvest outcomes.

## Religious observances and civic gatherings

Tallowmere does not have one universal religious festival. Individual temples set their own services and observances, while some civic customs have become shared occasions regardless of faith. These local observances complement the wider calendar's [Holidays and Public Observances](../../history/Holidays_and_Observances.md); they do not replace them.

Established recurring customs include:
- **First Safe Passage:** At the opening of the main sailing season, sailors and families gather at the Tideward Shrine for prayers and remembrance. The date follows local conditions, not a fixed calendar day.
- **The Weighing of Measures:** A public inspection and reaffirmation of market weights and measures organized through the Reeve's office, with official weighing at the Weigh House. Merchants may sponsor food or small prizes; disputes over standards are heard separately.
- **The River Thanks:** After the main harvest movement, river carriers and mill communities make offerings and hold a shared meal. It is a custom rather than a compulsory religious rite.
- **Names of the Drowned:** A memorial service held by the Tideward Shrine when seasonal weather turns and families remember those lost on the water. Families may add names throughout the year.
- **The Common Table:** A charitable meal coordinated by Lantern Hospice and willing temples during the cold season. Its scale depends on donations and available stores.

The formal calendar is established, but no fixed dates have been assigned to these local observances. Their timing follows local conditions and institutional decisions. No festival requires every resident to participate.

## Public order and weekly routine

There is no universal curfew. Gates close according to local operating practice and seasonal daylight; controlled entry may be allowed for authorized travelers, urgent medical need, official business, and recognized emergencies. The exact gate hours and local exceptions remain unresolved and may vary by gate or season.

The Watch posts additional patrols during large market gatherings, major funerals, visiting noble events, and periods of heavy harbor traffic. These are staffing decisions, not proof that a riot or crime is expected.

The Council meets when summoned by the lord or through the Reeve's ordinary notice process. Guilds and temples maintain their own meeting schedules.

## Open details

The exact market-day frequency, bell times and signal patterns, gate hours, seasonal dates for local observances, and historical civic commemorations remain unresolved. Do not invent a fixed schedule unless the GM establishes one for play.
