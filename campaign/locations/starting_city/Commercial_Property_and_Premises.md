# Tallowmere: Commercial Property, Markets, and Premises

**Status:** Initial commercial property framework established. Named properties below are available opportunities, not reserved for the PCs. Availability can change through ordinary transactions and campaign events.

## Commercial Districts and Their Uses

### Market Ward
The city's principal retail and service district. Permanent shops, market stalls, moneychangers, scribes, food sellers, and guild offices cluster around the main market lanes. Foot traffic is strongest here, but rents are higher and established traders have influence over stall allocation.

### Old Quays
The older waterfront district. Suitable for chandlers, ship repair suppliers, taverns, warehouses, and businesses serving crews. Buildings are irregular, some are old timber construction, and storage space is valuable. Harbor access is an advantage; fire and flood exposure are disadvantages.

### New Quays
The expanded cargo district. Larger storage buildings, provisioning yards, cargo handling, and wholesale trade dominate. Premises tend to be larger and less polished. A business here benefits from deliveries and bulk trade more than casual pedestrian traffic.

### Rivergate
Workshops, mills, smiths, cart yards, bargemen, and river transport. Water access and practical workspace matter more than fashionable frontage. Noise, smoke, and heavy traffic are ordinary features.

### High Ward
Professional offices, established retailers, expensive residences, and services used by wealthy households. It offers prestige and access to affluent clients, but suitable commercial premises are scarce and rents are high.

### Greywatch Rise
The noble and garrison quarter. Commercial activity is limited and subject to the needs of House Denvor. Military suppliers and service providers may operate by contract or permission. It is not an ordinary open market.

### Lantern Ward
Temples, healers, charitable institutions, burial services, and quiet residential streets. Small premises may suit healers, apothecaries, scribes, and services that benefit from proximity to the hospice and temples. No single temple controls the whole district.

### The Underways
Subterranean spaces beneath the city are varied in ownership, condition, and access. Some are private cellars or storage spaces; others are used by ratfolk families and neighborhood groups. A surface lease does not automatically grant rights to underground passages, wells, or rooms. Any agreement involving Underways access must identify the actual space and the people with recognized authority over it.

## Typical Commercial Rents

These are current local asking rents for ordinary premises in serviceable condition. They are not universal Pathfinder prices and may vary with frontage, structural condition, location, lease length, security, and included facilities.

| Premises | Typical monthly rent | Typical features |
|---|---:|---|
| Open market stall | 2 gp | One stall space during market hours; no permanent storage |
| Covered market stall | 4 gp | Fixed covered pitch; limited lockable storage |
| Small street-front shop | 8 gp | One main room and small rear store |
| Established shop | 15 gp | Two rooms, secure door, modest storage |
| Workshop with workroom | 18 gp | Work area and small sales frontage; utilities vary |
| Workshop with yard | 30 gp | Workroom plus enclosed yard or loading space |
| Small warehouse | 25 gp | Dry storage for modest cargo volume |
| Large warehouse | 55 gp | Larger storage floor and loading access |
| Office or counting room | 6 gp | Small professional space |
| Upper-floor rooms above a shop | 3 gp | Two modest rooms; no separate street frontage |

**Deposits and terms:** Most landlords ask for one month's rent in advance and a deposit equal to one month's rent. Better-secured or longer leases may require references, a guarantor, or additional advance payment. These are customary terms, not a citywide law. Individual landlords may negotiate.

**Utilities and services:** Rent does not automatically include staff, guards, fuel, water delivery, waste removal, repairs, or business supplies. Tenants are responsible for ordinary upkeep specified in the lease. Structural repairs and major improvements should be allocated in writing.

**Purchase:** Commercial property is rarely sold at a simple fixed price. Owners may prefer a long lease, partnership, or sale to a known local buyer. Purchase prices require an appraisal of the actual building, title, condition, location, and rights conveyed. Do not infer a purchase price by multiplying rent by an arbitrary number.

## Market Stalls and Allocation

The Market Guild's clerk administers the regular stall plan under authority delegated by the town reeve. A trader may apply for:
- A daily pitch, paid by the day.
- A recurring weekly pitch.
- A covered permanent stall under a seasonal or annual agreement.

The current customary rates are 1 sp per day for an ordinary temporary pitch, 2 sp for a covered temporary pitch, and the monthly rents listed above for permanent spaces. A trader selling ordinary lawful goods does not need guild membership merely to make a lawful sale. Regular permanent stallholders are expected to follow market rules on weights, cleanliness, fire safety, and obstruction.

The Market Guild may recommend applicants and negotiate collective terms. It cannot invent a new tax or prohibit lawful independent trade solely because a trader declines membership.

## Workshop and Hazard Requirements

Businesses using flame, furnaces, volatile substances, corrosives, or large quantities of combustible stock may be required to use an approved work area, maintain safe storage, and comply with ordinary fire ordinances. These rules apply to comparable businesses consistently.

Miraq's fire-separated workshop lane exists because the city has learned the cost of careless storage. A new alchemist does not automatically need to copy her premises, but must meet the actual safety requirements applicable to the materials and scale of operation. A small sales room storing sealed stock is not treated identically to a production workshop.

## Lease and Ownership Checks

Before a PC pays for a premises, establish:
- Who owns the building and whether that person has authority to lease or sell it.
- The exact rooms, yard, cellar, access ways, and storage included.
- Lease duration, rent due date, deposit, notice period, and renewal terms.
- Who pays for structural repairs, ordinary maintenance, utilities, and security.
- Whether the intended use is permitted under existing ordinances and the building's condition.
- Whether any existing tenant, lien, inheritance claim, or guild contract affects possession.

A verbal agreement may be socially recognized in some circumstances, but written leases witnessed and recorded by a scrivener or magistrate are safer. No hidden encumbrance should be introduced after the PCs have reasonably checked title.

## Open Details

Exact parcel boundaries, named landlords, property sale listings, title records, and building plans remain to be developed when the PCs inspect or negotiate for a particular property.
