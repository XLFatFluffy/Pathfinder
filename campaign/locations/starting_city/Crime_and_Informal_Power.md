# Tallowmere: Crime and Informal Power

**Status:** Established framework; named organizations and leaders remain open.

## General Character
Tallowmere has crime because it is a busy port with valuable cargo, transient crews, debts, private rivalries, and uneven enforcement. It has no single criminal organization controlling the city. Its illicit economy is fragmented, opportunistic, and connected to legitimate commerce in ways that are not always obvious.

Most residents are not criminals. Petty theft, fraud, smuggling, bribery, debt enforcement, and occasional violence are more common than elaborate secret societies.

## Common Illicit Activities
- **Cargo theft:** Goods disappear from docks, carts, and warehouses; some theft is opportunistic, some organized through dishonest clerks or handlers.
- **Smuggling:** Traders evade fees or move prohibited, stolen, or undeclared goods through minor quays, river landings, or corrupt officials.
- **Fraud and forgery:** False cargo tallies, counterfeit seals, altered contracts, fake weights, and forged ownership papers cause financial harm.
- **Debt and coercion:** Lenders and private enforcers pressure debtors. Some practices are lawful but harsh; others cross into extortion.
- **Salvage disputes:** Wrecks and abandoned cargo attract competing claims, forged evidence, and violence.
- **Information trading:** Dockworkers, servants, clerks, sailors, and innkeepers hear things. Information is sold, traded, exaggerated, or weaponized.

## Informal Power
Power outside office belongs to people who can provide or withhold access to a quay, crew, warehouse key, loan, safe route, witness, document, or discreet introduction. Some are legitimate; others work in legal gray areas. A useful contact is not automatically a criminal, and a criminal may have respectable customers.

## Ratfolk and Stereotypes
Ratfolk are not inherently associated with theft, smuggling, or criminal networks. Individuals of any ancestry may participate in illicit trade. Surface prejudice may cause ratfolk to be blamed more readily or punished more harshly for comparable offenses. Ratfolk do not collectively control all tunnels or act as a citywide smuggling service.

## Enforcement and Corruption
The watch has honest officers, opportunists, and people who are overwhelmed. Corruption may involve overlooking a shipment, accepting a bribe, protecting a gambling room, or selective enforcement. It should be specific rather than universal. House Denvor wants reliable trade and contained violence. Arson, major cargo theft, open violence, or a challenge to harbor authority attracts attention.

## Sandbox Use
Crime can create investigations, bargains, rivalries, dangerous jobs, and opportunities to profit or intervene. The PCs can ignore it. Not every criminal is a villain, and not every lawbreaker is sympathetic. Consequences follow actual harm, evidence, law, and relationships.

## Open Details
Named criminal groups, operators, safehouses, corrupt officials, and the scale of illicit markets remain unresolved. Do not assign the entire underworld to one master villain.
