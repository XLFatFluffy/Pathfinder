# Tallowmere: Emergency Response and Civic Resilience

**Visibility:** GM-ONLY  
**Status:** Local response guide built from established civic offices, infrastructure, and current pressures. Practical response examples are GM portrayal guidance unless explicitly identified as established.  
**Baseline:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC

This reference explains how Tallowmere might respond when ordinary life is disrupted by fire, flood, harbor accident, structural damage, or sudden shortages. It is not a modern emergency-management plan. The city has no established professional fire brigade, ambulance service, public emergency-management department, universal evacuation scheme, or standardized alarm code.

The relevant established records are [Government and Law](Government_and_Law.md), [Water, Waste, and Street Cleanliness](Water_Waste_and_Street_Cleanliness.md), [Household Comfort, Fuel, and Winter Preparation](Household_Comfort_Fuel_and_Winter_Preparation.md), [Medical Care in Tallowmere](Medical_Care_in_Tallowmere.md), and [Current Situation and Rumors](Current_Situation_and_Rumors.md).

## 1. The city's capacity and limits

Tallowmere is a walled port of roughly 18,000 people, with a working harbor, river traffic, warehouses, workshops, stables, crowded older streets, lower waterfront areas, and a patchwork of inhabited Underways spaces. It has civic officers, a Watch, a garrison, harbor administration, charitable institutions, public wells and pumps, the Old Cistern, and emergency stores associated with city defense.

These resources do not form a single coordinated emergency service. Response depends on:
- where the incident occurs;
- how quickly someone notices and communicates it;
- which workers, officers, and equipment are nearby;
- whether streets, gates, quays, or passages are accessible;
- whether the incident threatens people, cargo, defenses, or several districts;
- whether the responsible institutions cooperate;
- how much water, labor, transport, shelter, and medical care are available.

The city can organize a meaningful response to a local emergency. It cannot guarantee that every incident is contained quickly or that all affected residents receive equal assistance.

The **Denvor Granary**, near the Rivergate approach, is an established storehouse maintained under the lordship's oversight for public provisioning and obligations under the local charter. The separate office of Master of Stores maintains grain reserves, emergency supplies, and materials held for city defense. The exact chain of responsibility between the office and the granary, current stock levels, grain quality, and release authority are not fully specified. The granary's existence does not mean unlimited reserves or automatic free distribution.

## 2. Fire: the most immediate urban danger

Fire is a practical concern in a port city with warehouses, workshops, stables, crowded buildings, hearths, braziers, candles, oil lamps, stored fuel, and combustible cargo. The danger varies by building, contents, spacing, weather, and available water. Do not assume every district is equally vulnerable or that every building is timber-framed.

### Established local pressure

On 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC, several small warehouse fires and near-misses have made fire precautions a public issue. No major warehouse has burned. The Warehouse Guild argues that improvements are overdue; some owners suspect the Guild is using safety concerns to pressure smaller competitors. The causes of the incidents are not established. The rumors that the fires were deliberate remain speculative.

This dispute concerns prevention, responsibility, cost-sharing, and trust. It does not establish a culprit, arson campaign, or wider conspiracy.

### Who becomes involved

- **Warehouse owners and workers** are closest to a fire in their premises and know what is stored there, who was working, and what was moved.
- **The Warehouse Guild and Meret Sorn** are involved in commercial standards, cargo security, fire precautions, labor, and the dispute over improvement costs.
- **The Watch under Captain Brinna Salk** handles ordinary public order and local response. Its staffing is limited and it cannot be everywhere at once.
- **The Reeve, Marrec Holt** may become involved where the incident creates civic petitions, public maintenance questions, costs, or a matter for the Chartered Council.
- **The Harbor Master, Ysella Vane** has a role where a fire affects harbor operations, vessels, quay access, cargo declarations, or safety around the waterfront.
- **The garrison under Castellan Hadrik Voss** may be needed if a fire threatens Greywatch, walls, gates, defensive stores, or another strategic position. It is not the ordinary street patrol.
- **The Master of Stores** maintains grain reserves, emergency supplies, and materials held for city defense. The exact inventory, release authority, and civilian relief arrangements are unresolved.

### How people might fight a fire

A small fire may first be addressed by workers, household members, neighbors, or whoever is nearby. People may raise an alarm, fetch water, move nearby goods, clear access, or call for the Watch. A larger fire can require coordination across several properties and institutions.

Buckets, water vessels, wet coverings, hand tools, and efforts to remove nearby fuel or cargo are plausible practical responses. Treat these as ordinary possibilities, not a documented citywide equipment list or standardized drill. No formal bucket company, dedicated fire engine, professional fire brigade, or fixed chain of command is established.

The Old Cistern is a covered public water source in the older city and is specifically useful during dry periods or fires. Its capacity, refill source, inspection routine, and allocation rules are unresolved. It is not a guarantee that sufficient water is available at every fire, and it does not supply every district or Underways chamber.

### Fire aftermath

A fire may leave more than burned property:
- wet or smoke-damaged cargo;
- lost wages and disrupted work;
- arguments over who was responsible for prevention;
- disputes over leases, insurance-like arrangements, debt, and compensation;
- temporary shelter needs;
- damaged water stores, drains, or neighboring property;
- pressure on the Watch and civic officers to identify a cause.

Do not assume a fire automatically causes a riot, epidemic, or citywide shortage. Determine the scale and consequences from what actually burned, who depended on it, and how quickly replacement goods or premises can be found.

## 3. Harbor and river accidents

Tallowmere's harbor and River Vale are working transport systems. Accidents may involve a vessel, landing, cargo, mooring, quay, barge, ferry, or worker. The response is shaped by water conditions, vessel location, weather, available crews, and whether the incident threatens other traffic.

The Harbor Master is the principal civic authority for vessel registration, anchorage, harbor safety, cargo declarations, quay assignments, and harbor fees. Ship crews, pilots, bargemen, quay workers, and nearby vessels may provide the first practical assistance because they are already present and understand the immediate conditions.

The Watch may become involved if an accident causes injury, public disorder, theft, or a criminal allegation. The garrison may be involved if a vessel or accident threatens defensive works or the harbor's strategic security. These responsibilities overlap in a serious incident but do not make the Harbor Master a general criminal magistrate or the garrison a harbor rescue service.

Possible consequences include:
- a closed or obstructed berth;
- delayed cargo and missed commercial commitments;
- damaged goods or vessels;
- missing or injured crew;
- disputes over negligence, salvage, ownership, or harbor fees;
- temporary danger to nearby workers and vessels.

No dedicated rescue flotilla, diving service, harbor ambulance, or guaranteed salvage procedure is established. Do not assume every crew can be recovered or every lost cargo can be raised.

## 4. Rain, flooding, and water disruption

Lower streets near Rivergate and the waterfront can become muddy or flood-prone after heavy rain. Drains may clog; cellars and storage spaces may take water; floodwater can carry silt, refuse, and animal waste into places that are normally dry.

The city's response is likely to begin locally. Residents protect household goods, move stored materials where possible, clear blocked drains, and seek help from neighbors or property owners. Civic officers may become involved where public routes, shared infrastructure, or multiple properties are affected. The Reeve's responsibilities include civic administration and maintenance matters; the exact division of responsibility for every drain, retaining structure, or embankment is unresolved.

The Old Cistern is useful in dry periods and fires, not a flood-control system. It does not prevent the River Vale from rising, drain lower streets, or guarantee safe drinking water after inundation.

A flood may create secondary problems:
- spoiled grain, food, fuel, or household stores;
- damaged tools, stock, and rented rooms;
- blocked roads and disrupted carts;
- unsafe wells or water storage;
- temporary displacement;
- increased demand for dry bedding, clean water, and practical care.

No current flood, named flood-defense project, citywide evacuation plan, or standing flood relief fund is established at the campaign opening.

## 5. Injury, illness, and temporary shelter

A sudden incident can overwhelm the small institutions that provide care. The Lantern Hospice has finite resources and is not a large hospital. Sister Alwen administers it; Brother Halen tracks stores and donations. Edda Moss provides independent midwifery and practical care, Fenna Wale provides herbal remedies and prepared supplies, Miraq Ashwhisk operates an alchemical business, and the House of the Dawn provides charitable and religious assistance. Their roles are not interchangeable.

The first people to help an injured person may be family, coworkers, neighbors, shipmates, or bystanders. A patient may be taken to a household, a nearby healer, the hospice, or the House of the Dawn according to the injury, resources, location, and the patient's relationships. Magical healing is not automatically available.

A large incident may create needs for:
- clean cloth and bandages;
- food and drink;
- dry bedding and temporary shelter;
- help finding missing relatives;
- transport for injured people;
- money for lost work or damaged tools;
- assistance for people whose homes or workplaces cannot be used.

No citywide ambulance, public relief department, or guaranteed compensation scheme is established.

## 6. Who coordinates a wider emergency?

There is no single permanent emergency commander for every kind of disaster. The responsible authority depends on the incident.

| Incident | Principal established responsibility | Other likely participants |
|---|---|---|
| Street fire or disturbance | Watch, under Captain Brinna Salk | Residents, property owners, Reeve where civic issues arise |
| Warehouse or quay fire | Watch for public order; Harbor Master for harbor operations | Warehouse Guild, workers, owners, Reeve, garrison if defenses are threatened |
| Vessel or harbor accident | Harbor Master Ysella Vane for harbor regulation and safety | Crews, pilots, bargemen, Watch if crime or public order is involved |
| Flooded public street or drain | Reeve's civic administration for public maintenance matters | Residents, property owners, workers, Watch if access or safety requires it |
| Threat to Greywatch, walls, or gates | Castellan Hadrik Voss and the garrison within defensive command | Lord Denvor, Watch, relevant civic officers |
| Large civilian relief need | No single established citywide relief authority | Lantern Hospice, House of the Dawn, neighborhood networks, Reeve, private donors |

Lord Oswin retains ultimate local authority. He may convene officers or the Chartered Council when cooperation and resources are needed. The Council can advise and negotiate; an emergency meeting does not transfer command from the lord or his appointed officers.

Kharadorn's charter rights concern strategic harbor and inland-sea defense. They do not automatically place ordinary fire, flood, medical, or relief work under dwarven administration.

## 7. Public warnings and evacuation

People may raise an alarm by shouting, sending a runner, using a bell or other local signal, or alerting an officer. The meanings of signals are local; no standardized citywide fire bell, alarm code, or emergency messenger rota is established.

Residents may leave a dangerous building or street, seek relatives, move goods, or gather at a place they believe is safe. Their choices depend on what they know, whether routes are open, whether they have dependents or animals, and whether they fear theft or loss of property if they leave.

No fixed citywide evacuation routes, designated muster grounds, shelter assignments, or compulsory evacuation rules are established. For a specific incident, choose safe routes based on the established city map and current conditions. Do not invent new streets, gates, or gathering places.

The Underways require particular care. Some passages are inhabited, some are used for work or storage, some are unsafe, and the network is not fully mapped. Surface officials do not know every route. A response in one chamber does not grant access to all others, and no single ratfolk contact can guarantee safe passage through the whole network.

## 8. Civic resilience and unequal recovery

Tallowmere's ability to recover depends on cooperation between households, workers, property owners, merchants, civic officers, charitable institutions, and the lordship. Wealthier households can replace supplies, pay for repairs, and find temporary accommodation more easily. Poorer residents may lose wages immediately, borrow at unfavorable terms, rely on neighbors, or seek limited charity.

The same emergency can therefore produce different experiences:
- A warehouse owner worries about cargo, contracts, and rebuilding costs.
- A dockworker worries about lost shifts, damaged tools, and rent.
- A household near the river worries about bedding, stored food, and safe water.
- A sailor's family may not know whether a missing crew member is injured, delayed, or lost.
- An Underways household may prioritize the safety of a particular passage or water chamber that surface authorities do not understand.
- A charitable institution may want to help but lack beds, food, clean cloth, or staff.

Recovery is not instantaneous. A building may reopen before all its workers are rehired; a quay may resume operation while claims remain disputed; a family may return home while repairs continue. These consequences are often more useful and believable than a citywide collapse.

## 9. Current opening-state position

On 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC:
- Tallowmere is not under martial law.
- No citywide fire, flood, epidemic, or water emergency is established.
- Several small warehouse fires and near-misses have made prevention a live public dispute; no major warehouse has burned and the cause is unknown.
- The Warehouse Guild supports improved precautions, while some owners dispute responsibility and cost.
- The Old Cistern is an established public resource useful during fires and dry periods, but its capacity and allocation remain unresolved.
- The city has a Watch, garrison, Harbor Master, Reeve, Chief Magistrate, Castellan, Master of Stores, hospice, and charitable religious house, but no dedicated all-purpose emergency service.

These facts describe the starting position, not a prediction that an emergency must occur.

## 10. Unresolved details

- Whether any neighborhood or guild maintains a regular fire-watch rota.
- What fire-fighting tools and water vessels are routinely stored at particular premises.
- The Old Cistern's capacity, refill source, inspection, and allocation rules.
- Any formal arrangement for calling the Watch or garrison during a large fire.
- The location and contents of the Master of Stores' emergency reserves and who can authorize civilian use.
- The exact division of responsibility for public drains, embankments, quay repairs, and flood cleanup.
- Any designated shelters, evacuation routes, or assembly places.
- Harbor rescue craft, salvage rules, and formal accident reporting.
- Any standing relief fund or compensation practice for displaced households.

Establish these details only when play needs them. A believable city has people who respond to emergencies; it does not need an invented modern bureaucracy to do so.
