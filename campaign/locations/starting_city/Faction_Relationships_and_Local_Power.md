# Tallowmere: Faction Relationships and Local Power

**Status:** Established GM-facing framework; individual private dealings and future developments remain subject to play.  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-04

This document describes how influence moves through Tallowmere. It is not a list of mandatory allies, enemies, or quest-givers. The factions and institutions below pursue their own interests, cooperate when useful, disagree when necessary, and change their positions as circumstances change.

## 1. How Power Works in Tallowmere

Tallowmere has no single organization that controls the city.

Formal authority rests with Lord Oswin Denvor under the hereditary charter confirmed by House Kharadorn. The Denvors appoint officers, enforce local law, collect established dues, command local defenses, and represent the lordship in dealings with Kharadorn. Their authority is real, but its exercise depends on cooperation from people who keep the city functioning.

Influence is distributed across several kinds of power:

- **Legal authority:** House Denvor, its courts, appointed officers, and chartered privileges.
- **Strategic leverage:** House Kharadorn's rights under the charter, especially harbor defense, tribute, and military support.
- **Commercial leverage:** Shipowners, warehouse holders, guilds, merchants, lenders, and river carriers can delay investment, redirect trade, or withhold cooperation.
- **Practical control:** Harbor officials, labor organizers, pilots, ferrymen, watch patrols, and people who maintain infrastructure can determine whether plans work in practice.
- **Community influence:** Ratfolk neighborhood contacts, religious and charitable institutions, household networks, and respected local figures can mobilize help, information, trust, or refusal.
- **Illicit leverage:** Smugglers, dishonest clerks, cargo thieves, private enforcers, and information brokers exploit gaps between law and enforcement. They are fragmented rather than centrally commanded.

No one category automatically defeats the others. The lord can issue an order that merchants comply with reluctantly; merchants can create a crisis without possessing lawful authority; Kharadorn can demand charter obligations without managing every street; and a neighborhood can refuse cooperation without being able to dictate city policy.

## 2. House Denvor and the Lordship

**Formal position:** The ruling house and final local authority.

Lord Oswin Denvor prioritizes stability, reliable revenue, functioning defenses, and the preservation of his family's authority. Lady Catrin is politically capable and well-connected. Ser Edwyn is interested in stronger defenses. Lady Anwen has ties to the commercial community and a good understanding of social currents.

The family is not politically uniform:
- Oswin is cautious about reforms that could unsettle established arrangements.
- Catrin is attentive to relationships, obligations, and the consequences of public positions.
- Edwyn is more willing to argue for defensive investment and visible preparedness.
- Anwen is better positioned to hear concerns among merchants and residents who do not routinely enter Greywatch.

These are differences of emphasis, not an established family schism.

**Sources of power:** The charter, land and demesne income, appointment of officers, courts, local armed force, control of public privileges, and the symbolic authority of Greywatch.

**Dependencies:** The city needs merchants to keep trade moving, laborers to handle cargo, guilds to maintain skilled work, officers to administer policy, and Kharadorn to remain satisfied with charter obligations.

**Current pressure:** The harbor allocation dispute, warehouse fire concerns, defense review, and the need to balance city improvements against tribute and household expenditure.

**Likely behavior:** Denvor policy favors negotiated settlements where possible. The family will act more forcefully when its authority, public order, harbor security, or charter obligations are directly challenged.

## 3. House Kharadorn's Charter Interest

**Formal position:** Overlord and strategic guarantor under Tallowmere's charter, not the city's day-to-day government.

Kharadorn's interest is primarily strategic: the harbor, inland-sea traffic, defensive readiness, tribute, and the ability to call upon ships or supplies under the charter. Its attention rises when these interests are threatened.

Kharadorn does not ordinarily decide market disputes, petty criminal cases, shop leases, or routine appointments. The Denvors retain local government and meaningful autonomy while honoring their obligations.

**Sources of power:** The charter, the dwarven empire's military and political weight, strategic rights, and the possibility of demanding performance of established obligations.

**Dependencies:** Kharadorn benefits from a stable and productive port. Excessive interference could damage the very trade and local cooperation that make Tallowmere useful.

**Current pressure:** A strategic review of harbor defenses and charter obligations is underway or expected. Its precise scope, personnel, timetable, and conclusions remain open.

**Relationship with House Denvor:** Necessary, formal, and occasionally uncomfortable. Kharadorn expects the charter to be honored; Denvor expects local autonomy to be respected. Neither side benefits automatically from provoking a crisis.

Do not assume that every dwarf in Tallowmere represents Kharadorn, that every Kharadorn official is hostile to the Denvors, or that Kharadorn intends to annex or directly administer the city.

## 4. The Chartered Council and Commercial Interests

The council is a negotiating and advisory body. Its influence comes from the economic and practical cooperation of its members, not from an independent right to overrule the lord.

### Harbor Fellowship

**Principal established contact:** Harbor Master Ysella Vane works with shipping interests through her office; the Fellowship itself represents shipowners, captains, pilots, chandlers, and agents.

The Fellowship wants safe and predictable harbor operations, fair berth allocation, manageable fees, and reliable access for its members. Its members disagree over whether large vessels and established shipowners should receive priority over smaller operators.

- **Works with:** Harbor Office, shipyards, chandlers, Warehouse Guild, and Denvor officials.
- **Disputes with:** Smaller traders, warehouse interests over responsibility and cost, and river carriers when sea traffic receives disproportionate attention.
- **Leverage:** Vessel schedules, shipping investment, crews, pilots, and the ability to redirect business to competing ports.
- **Internal division:** Large shipowners favor predictable priority and capacity for major cargoes; smaller operators want access that does not leave them permanently behind.

### Warehouse Guild

**Principal established contact:** Meret Sorn, guild factor.

The Guild wants secure cargo, reliable records, predictable storage rules, and fire prevention that does not impose ruinous costs on older premises.

- **Works with:** Harbor Fellowship, Market Guild, insurers or lenders where applicable, and civic officers.
- **Disputes with:** Shipowners over delays and liability; warehouse operators over the cost of improvements; merchants over storage fees and access.
- **Leverage:** Storage capacity, cargo records, bonded storage, warehouse keys, and the ability to make trade more or less convenient.
- **Current pressure:** Recent warehouse fires and near-misses have made safety standards a live issue. Their cause remains unknown.

### River Carters' and Bargemen's Company

**Principal established contact:** Pellan Reed.

The Company wants fair river tolls, safe landings, dependable access to the harbor, and recognition that inland food and materials are as important as seaborne luxury cargo.

- **Works with:** Millers, farmers, Rivergate businesses, ferrymen, Market Guild traders, and harbor officials.
- **Disputes with:** Sea-focused merchants who dominate attention and with officials over tolls, landing rights, and maintenance.
- **Leverage:** Inland transport, barge capacity, river pilots, cart teams, and knowledge of river conditions.
- **Internal pressure:** The Company is not a single fleet. Independent operators and larger carriers may disagree about rates, priorities, and collective action.

### Market Guild

**Principal established contacts:** Established shopkeepers and traders, including merchants such as Beren Harlowe; Ilyne Marris remains an independent broker rather than automatically speaking for the Guild.

The Guild wants consistent market rules, dependable measures, fair access to stalls, and protection against privileged exemptions that favor competitors.

- **Works with:** Town Reeve, moneychangers, retailers, wholesalers, and market officers.
- **Disputes with:** New traders seeking access, established sellers defending scarce locations, and other guilds when their privileges overlap.
- **Leverage:** Market relationships, stallholder coordination, commercial reputation, and the ability to influence practical acceptance of new businesses.
- **Current pressure:** Ratfolk traders seeking regular stalls and leases have made market access a visible question. Individual members hold different views.

### Artificers' Hall

**Principal established contacts:** Recognized craft masters, including metalworkers and other trades; Orsik Feld and related craftspeople interact with its wider trade environment.

The Hall protects workmanship, apprenticeships, standards, and the reputation of skilled trades. It is not a universal licensing authority and does not control every craftsperson.

- **Works with:** Denvor officials on public works, merchants on commissions, and the Shipwrights' Brotherhood where trades overlap.
- **Disputes with:** Customers demanding impossible deadlines, independent craftspeople over recognition, and merchants over price and quality.
- **Leverage:** Skilled labor, training, workshop reputation, and specialist referrals.
- **Important limit:** The Hall cannot add requirements to Pathfinder item-crafting rules or prohibit lawful crafting simply because a character is not a member.

### Shipwrights' Brotherhood

**Principal established contact:** Dagna Coppervein.

The Brotherhood wants skilled labor protected, repairs performed to sound standards, and enough timber and materials to meet demand without sacrificing quality.

- **Works with:** Harbor Fellowship, chandlers, Warehouse Guild, and the Denvor garrison when repairs are commissioned.
- **Disputes with:** Shipowners over deadlines and price, suppliers over timber and fittings, and other crafts over responsibility for complex jobs.
- **Leverage:** Skilled shipbuilding and repair labor, specialist knowledge, and the ability to prioritize urgent work.
- **Current pressure:** Demand for repairs competes with limited skilled capacity. The exact shipyard debts and contracts remain as recorded in the merchant dispute file.

## 5. Civic Offices and Public Institutions

These offices are not a unified faction. Their holders cooperate because the city requires it, but they have different responsibilities and constraints.

- **Town Reeve Marrec Holt:** Maintains civic records, dues, notices, and administrative coordination. His influence is procedural: a sound record can settle a dispute; a missing or delayed record can obstruct action. He values accuracy and Denvor confidence.
- **Harbor Master Ysella Vane:** Controls vessel registration, anchorage, berth assignments, declarations, and harbor safety under delegated authority. She is exposed to pressure from every commercial interest using the waterfront.
- **Chief Magistrate Odran Pell:** Handles legal proceedings delegated to his office. His credibility depends on evidence and procedure; he is not free to disregard the lord's authority or local custom.
- **Captain Brinna Salk:** Commands the watch. She wants sufficient staffing and consistent discipline, but has limited personnel and cannot patrol every district equally.
- **Castellan Hadrik Voss:** Maintains Greywatch and its defensive readiness. His professional loyalty is to his contract and duties, not to an assumed racial or political bloc.
- **Master of Stores:** The office exists within the government framework; its current holder and detailed network remain to be established.

The watch, garrison, harbor office, and magistracy may cooperate during emergencies, but their jurisdictions and priorities are not interchangeable.

## 6. Ratfolk Neighborhoods and Underways Contacts

Tallowmere's ratfolk are not one faction with one leader. Their influence is local, practical, and based on relationships, knowledge, work, and the ability to coordinate within particular neighborhoods.

### Neighborhood maintainers and mediators

**Known contact:** Nix-of-the-Cistern.

Nix values water access, safe routes, repairs, and agreements that protect families. Nix is willing to negotiate with surface authorities but is criticized by some younger residents for accepting limited concessions.

Nix can help resolve practical disputes and communicate neighborhood concerns. Nix cannot promise that every ratfolk household or passage will cooperate.

### Traders seeking open market access

**Known contact:** Tikka Vell.

Tikka wants ratfolk workshops and traders to sell openly rather than relying on intermediaries. Tikka's goals overlap with some Market Guild reformers and conflict with merchants who benefit from existing arrangements.

Tikka is commercially ambitious and may favor direct action where Nix would prefer gradual negotiation. Neither speaks for all ratfolk.

### Couriers and route guides

**Known contact:** Serren Coil.

Serren controls personal knowledge of selected routes and trusted introductions, not the entire Underways. Serren protects family shelters and unsafe or sensitive passages from casual access.

Route knowledge is a practical resource, but it is not a citywide intelligence service. Payment, trust, safety, and the purpose of a journey matter.

### Ratfolk businesses and working households

Miraq Ashwhisk, Tikka Vell, repairers, couriers, water maintainers, craftspeople, and households have overlapping interests but different livelihoods. Some want greater surface integration; others prefer autonomy and limited contact. Some may support the PC's ambitions, some may distrust them, and some may simply want reliable customers and safe streets.

**Political limit:** Ratfolk residents have no established formal seat on the chartered council. This does not mean they lack influence or that all surface officials reject their participation. The precise channels through which neighborhood representatives petition or negotiate remain open.

## 7. Religious and Charitable Institutions

The city's temples, hospice, shrines, and charitable networks possess social trust and practical reach, but are not a single political bloc.

- **Sister Alwen and Lantern Hospice:** The hospice provides care, shelter, and burial assistance within finite resources. Its need for donations creates relationships with merchants, households, and the Denvors. It may advocate for vulnerable residents without becoming a political organization.
- **Brother Caldus and the Sailors' Shrine:** The shrine serves sailors and their families, preserves names of the missing, and offers rites and counsel. It hears maritime news and grief, but does not automatically share confidential information with civic authorities.
- **Other temples and religious houses:** Their local clergy, patronage, doctrine, and political positions are not yet named. Do not treat them as one church or assign them a common agenda.

Religious institutions can shape public opinion, provide aid, mediate disputes, and criticize abuses. Their influence depends on trust and resources, not on an assumed power to command the city.

## 8. Informal and Illicit Power

Tallowmere's illicit economy is fragmented. It consists of opportunities and relationships rather than a single criminal syndicate.

People with informal leverage may include:
- Dockside handlers who know which cargoes are poorly guarded.
- Clerks who can alter or delay records.
- Carters and boatmen who know minor landings.
- Buyers willing to purchase undeclared or stolen goods.
- Private lenders and enforcers who pressure debtors.
- Information sellers who trade rumors, schedules, and personal details.

Some individuals operate entirely outside the law; others participate in both legitimate and illicit transactions. A person who knows a smuggler is not necessarily a smuggler. A person who breaks one law is not necessarily part of a wider organization.

No named citywide criminal boss, unified thieves' guild, or secret council is established. Named operators, safehouses, corrupt officials, and specific networks remain open.

## 10. Current Pressure Points

These are active conditions that can develop without PC involvement. They are not countdowns that must end in a particular way.

1. **Harbor allocation:** The Harbor Fellowship's established members disagree over priority and access. Ysella Vane must apply harbor rules while facing pressure from competing operators.
2. **Warehouse safety:** Fires and near-misses have increased demands for inspection and investment. Merchants disagree over who should pay and how much disruption repairs justify.
3. **Ratfolk market access:** Tikka and other traders want regular stalls or leases. Nix is concerned about practical protections and retaliation. Market Guild members are not unanimous.
4. **Kharadorn defense review:** The Denvors must demonstrate that harbor defenses and charter obligations are being taken seriously without diverting resources from the city's ordinary needs.
5. **River trade recognition:** River carriers want fair treatment and maintenance, particularly when sea merchants dominate civic discussion.
6. **Skilled labor pressure:** Ship repair, garrison work, and private commissions compete for a limited pool of skilled workers.
7. **Merchant credit and supply disputes:** Existing debts, late deliveries, quality concerns, and contracts create pressure between individual businesses. They are not evidence of a common scheme.

## 11. How the PCs Can Engage

The PCs are not required to choose a faction or declare a political identity. They can build relationships issue by issue.

Possible forms of engagement include:
- Renting premises, hiring workers, and establishing a lawful business.
- Negotiating supply agreements with merchants and craftspeople.
- Offering services or commissions to noble households, temples, shipowners, and private customers.
- Supporting or opposing changes to market access.
- Helping resolve a labor, cargo, legal, or neighborhood dispute.
- Working with civic offices, guilds, or independent contacts without becoming members.
- Exploring the Underways while respecting that route access is personal and locally controlled.
- Taking contracts from people whose interests conflict, with the resulting consequences made visible rather than sprung as arbitrary punishment.

No faction automatically trusts the PCs because of ancestry, class, wealth, or a shared interest. Trust is built through conduct and repeated dealings. No faction automatically opposes them because they are outsiders or competitors.

The surviving heir's identity and ambitions may create unusually significant political consequences if revealed, but no Tallowmere faction is assumed to know the PC's true identity at the campaign's outset. The ratfolk PC's goals may resonate with some local residents, but he is not automatically accepted as their representative.

## 12. GM Operating Rules

- Factions act on information they plausibly possess, not on GM omniscience.
- Do not make every dispute part of one conspiracy.
- Do not turn every merchant into a political operative.
- Do not assume all members of an ancestry, guild, neighborhood, or faith agree.
- A faction's leverage should be proportional to its actual resources and authority.
- Use established Pathfinder rules for mechanical outcomes. Social position may affect access, attitude, and consequences, but does not silently change character mechanics.
- When a faction changes policy or gains/loses influence, record the cause and update this document.
- Keep secrets, private motives, and uncertain allegations distinct from established facts.

## 13. Open Details

The following are deliberately unresolved:
- Exact membership, seats, and procedures of the Tallowmere chartered council.
- Whether any ratfolk neighborhood has a recognized formal petition channel or representative arrangement.
- Names and identities of Kharadorn envoys or inspectors assigned to the defense review.
- Individual leaders and membership of any informal criminal networks.
- Which merchants actively support or oppose regular ratfolk market access.
- The exact positions of local temples and their clergy.
- Which current commercial disputes will escalate, settle, or be overtaken by other events.
- The precise resources, manpower, and readiness of the Denvor garrison and city watch.

These details should be established when they become relevant, not invented retroactively to force a preferred plot.
