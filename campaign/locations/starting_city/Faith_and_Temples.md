# Tallowmere: Faith and Temples

**Status:** Established framework; local institutions and clergy established in Local_Religion_and_Institutions.md.

## Religious Character
The gods are real, but divine spellcasters are uncommon and most residents will not regularly witness divine magic. Religious life is expressed through worship, rites, festivals, burial customs, charity, vows, education, and community institutions.

Tallowmere has several visible faith communities. No single temple governs the city, and no religious authority holds an automatic seat on the Denvor council. Clergy can influence public opinion through charity, learning, moral authority, and relationships with powerful families.

## Lantern Ward
Lantern Ward contains visible religious institutions, charitable houses, healers, burial grounds, and hostels for pilgrims and travelers. It is not one temple precinct under a single authority. Its institutions provide food, care, burial assistance, instruction, mediation, and shelter. Capacity varies.

## Faith in Daily Life
Most residents are not theologians. They keep household shrines, observe holy days, seek blessings before voyages, make offerings after safe returns, or consult clergy during illness and bereavement. Maritime life encourages vows for safe passage and memorials for the drowned. Ratfolk may follow familiar surface faiths, distinct traditions, or a mixture; no single practice is assumed for all ratfolk.

## Institutions
- **A major public temple:** Regular services, recognized clergy, a burial ground, and charitable obligations. Visible but not a civic government.
- **A hospice and alms-house:** Shelter, food, and basic care, with limited resources dependent on donations.
- **A sailors' shrine:** Vows, offerings, and memorial records for those lost at sea.
- **A small scholarly or contemplative house:** Preserves religious texts and trains a limited number of clergy; not a center of widespread arcane magic.
- **Neighborhood shrines:** Modest spaces maintained by residents, including in working districts and the Underways.

## Divine Magic and Rules
Divine magic exists and the gods are real. Clerics, oracles, and other divine practitioners are uncommon in everyday life. A temple cannot provide every spell or cure on demand. Any divine spellcasting NPC must have a plausible Pathfinder First Edition rules basis, including class, level, spells known or prepared, and components or foci where relevant. Service availability and price depend on actual capability and circumstances.

## Faith and Politics
Religious leaders may disagree about poverty, prejudice, warfare, burial, charity, and rulers' obligations. They may advise House Denvor, criticize it, or avoid politics. Influence depends on congregation, reputation, resources, and service. Religious differences do not automatically map onto ancestry or class.
