# Tallowmere: Government and Law

**Status:** Established framework; detailed procedures remain expandable.\n\n**Related campaign-wide reference:** [Law, Justice, and Crime](../../world/Law_Justice_and_Crime.md) explains the wider legal structure and the limits of jurisdiction.

## Lordship and Charter
Tallowmere is governed by House Denvor under a hereditary charter confirmed by House Kharadorn after the Great Rebellion. Lord Oswin Denvor rules the walled city and modest surrounding territory. The charter requires annual tribute, maintenance of harbor defenses, and provision of ships, supplies, or transport when lawfully called upon. Kharadorn retains strategic rights concerning the harbor and inland-sea defense but does not administer ordinary city life.

The lord may appoint civic officers, enforce customary law, collect established dues, call the local levy, grant or confirm certain privileges, and preside over the lordship's courts or appoint judges. These powers are constrained by the charter, custom, obligations to Kharadorn, and the need for civic cooperation.

## The Lord's Officers
- **Town Reeve:** Coordinates civic administration, records, local dues, market ordinances, and communication with the chartered council.
- **Harbor Master:** Oversees vessel registration, anchorage, harbor safety, cargo declarations, quay assignments, and harbor fees.
- **Chief Magistrate:** Oversees hearings, warrants, fines, civil disputes, and criminal proceedings delegated by the lord.
- **Captain of the Watch:** Commands city patrols, supervises arrests, and coordinates with the garrison during emergencies.
- **Castellan of Greywatch:** Responsible for the noble seat, household guard, stores, and readiness of the lord's defensive force.
- **Master of Stores:** Maintains grain reserves, emergency supplies, and materials held for city defense.

These offices are established; individual holders and exact jurisdictions are recorded or developed in the civic cast. Officeholders are people with incentives and flaws, not automatic paragons or villains.

## The Chartered Council
The council is a negotiating and advisory body, not an elected parliament. It includes representatives of principal guilds, major shipowners, established property holders, and selected civic officers. Seats and procedures derive from charter, recognized privilege, and local custom.

It advises on market privileges, harbor works, taxation proposals, roads, walls, labor disputes, and matters affecting prosperity. It cannot lawfully depose the Denvors or simply overrule the lord. Its leverage is practical: merchants can delay investment, guilds can withhold cooperation, and shipowners can redirect trade.

The council is divided by interest. A shipowner seeking lower harbor fees may oppose warehouse holders seeking expensive fire protections. The lord can exploit these divisions but risks destroying cooperation.

## Courts and Customary Law
Local law combines the lordship charter, written ordinances, established custom, guild privileges, and feudal obligations. It is not a modern legal code, and status may affect access to justice.

Common matters include contracts and debts; property and inheritance; market weights and fraudulent sales; harbor accidents and salvage; assault, theft, arson, murder, and public disorder; and guild disputes. Minor civil matters may be heard by officials or guild arbiters. Serious crimes go to the chief magistrate or lord's court. The lord retains ultimate local authority but frequent intervention undermines ordinary justice.

Possible penalties include fines, restitution, forfeiture, public penalties, imprisonment, corporal punishment, or capital punishment where established law permits. Do not assume a universal sentence without checking the offense, evidence, status, harm, and local rule.

## Security
The city watch handles ordinary patrols, disturbances, market order, and warrants. The garrison protects Greywatch, walls, gates, and key defensive positions. Harbor officials regulate vessel movement and safety. Jurisdictions overlap during fires, riots, and attacks.

Tallowmere is not under permanent martial law. The watch is more visible near the market, gates, and quays than in poorer peripheral streets. Response quality varies with staffing, leadership, weather, and the importance of an incident. The Denvor garrison is sufficient for local security and basic defense, not independent war against a Great House.

## Taxes and Revenue
Revenue comes from customary dues and fees: harbor fees, market rents, warehouse and property dues, road or bridge tolls in the surrounding territory, court fines, and income from the Denvor demesne. Exact rates remain undecided. Do not invent arbitrary taxes solely to block a player business; established fees apply consistently to comparable enterprises.

## Kharadorn Oversight
Kharadorn's rights are strategic. Dwarven envoys may inspect obligations, negotiate shipping support, assess defenses, or intervene over a charter breach. They do not ordinarily adjudicate petty theft or market disputes. Denvor autonomy is real so long as the house honors its charter and does not threaten Kharadorn's strategic interests.

For a practical account of how complaints, Watch responses, hearings, guild mediation, and unequal access to justice work at street level, see [Law in Practice and Local Justice](Law_in_Practice_and_Local_Justice.md). It does not establish unresolved custody rules, fixed penalties, or new legal powers.

## Open Details
Exact charter text, council seat allocation, court procedure, tax schedule, watch strength, garrison numbers, and remaining officeholders are not yet fixed. No specific privilege or restriction is established until recorded.
