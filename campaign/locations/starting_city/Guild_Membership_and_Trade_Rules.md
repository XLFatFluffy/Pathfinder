# Tallowmere: Guild Membership, Privileges, and Civic Trade Rules

**Status:** Initial membership and trade framework established. Individual guild bylaws and privileges remain limited to what is written here; do not assume unrecorded powers.

## General Principle
Tallowmere recognizes trade associations and craft bodies because they coordinate training, standards, facilities, contracts, and representation. Guilds are influential, but they are not a single government and do not automatically possess a monopoly over every trade.

A person may work independently in a lawful trade unless a specific charter, safety ordinance, property covenant, or contract says otherwise. Guild membership can be valuable without being compulsory.

## Membership Categories
- **Apprentice:** Learner attached to a recognized master or workshop. Terms, duration, and instruction are set by agreement and custom.
- **Journeyman:** Trained worker entitled to practice the trade for wages or commissions, subject to local rules and work terms.
- **Master:** Recognized independent craftsperson or business owner with authority to take apprentices and certify work within the guild's standards.
- **Associate member:** Merchant, supplier, or related professional who pays dues for access to meetings, services, or representation without claiming craft mastery.
- **Independent trader:** Lawful businessperson who is not a member. May trade, rent premises, hire workers, and contract with customers, but lacks guild voting rights and member services.

Guilds may recognize foreign masters or experienced independents after examining their work. Recognition is not automatic and should have a stated basis.

## Current Guild Dues and Services
These are customary local dues, not Pathfinder rules. They are charged consistently and can be renegotiated by guilds through ordinary procedures.

| Body | Typical dues | Member services | Important limitation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Market Guild | 2 gp/year associate; 5 gp/year established stallholder | Market notices, dispute mediation, shared scales, representation on stall allocation | Does not control all lawful retail trade |
| Artificers' Hall | 5 gp/year journeyman; 12 gp/year master | Standards review, workshop referrals, apprentices, shared equipment by arrangement | Recognition does not grant exclusive rights to every craft |
| Shipwrights' Brotherhood | 8 gp/year working member; 15 gp/year master | Skilled labor referrals, yard access by agreement, repair standards | Membership does not guarantee work or materials |
| Warehouse Guild | 5 gp/year small keeper; 15 gp/year large keeper | Standard cargo records, fire guidance, bonded-storage referrals | Does not own members' warehouses |
| Harbor Fellowship | 10 gp/year individual trader; 25 gp/year shipowner | Harbor notices, collective negotiation, pilot and berth referrals | Harbor Master retains civic authority |
| River Carters' and Bargemen's Company | 4 gp/year individual operator; 10 gp/year fleet owner | Route notices, landing coordination, shared claims support | Does not own the river or prohibit independent transport |

Dues are normally paid annually. A new member may pay a prorated amount for the remaining season. Initiation, examination, or certification fees are separate only where a specific service is requested and the amount is agreed in advance.

## Membership and Recognition Process
An applicant speaks with the relevant clerk or master, identifies the work or trade they intend to conduct, and provides references or examples if seeking recognition as a craftsperson. The guild may inspect a workshop when safety or shared facilities are involved.

An applicant can be refused membership for stated reasons such as inadequate skill for a master designation, unpaid dues, serious prior misconduct in the trade, or lack of capacity in a training arrangement. Refusal of membership does not by itself prohibit lawful independent work.

A refusal should be recorded with its reason. Appeals may be brought to the guild's governing masters or, where a civic privilege or public right is implicated, to the town reeve or appropriate court.

## Specific Local Practice

### Market Guild
- Coordinates permanent stall allocation and publishes market rules.
- Maintains shared certified scales for a modest use fee.
- May mediate disputes between stallholders.
- Cannot prohibit an independent lawful shop from operating outside the market solely because the owner is not a member.
- Ratfolk applicants are subject to the same written application standards as other traders. Informal prejudice may still affect treatment by individual members; the guild's stated rules do not establish a blanket ban.

### Artificers' Hall
- Recognizes masters across several crafts and can refer skilled labor.
- Offers access to shared tools or specialist facilities by prior agreement and payment.
- May examine disputed workmanship when both parties agree or a magistrate requests an assessment.
- Does not impose extra Pathfinder crafting prerequisites, change item creation feats, or control who may use published crafting rules.

### Warehouse Guild
- Maintains common cargo-record standards and promotes fire precautions.
- Provides bonded-storage referrals where a merchant needs cargo held pending duties or delivery.
- Can discipline members for violations of its own agreements; civic fines remain the responsibility of lawful authorities.

### Harbor Fellowship
- Represents shipowners, captains, pilots, chandlers, and shipping agents.
- Negotiates over fees and access but does not assign berths; the Harbor Master does.
- Members may share information about vessel schedules, cargo demand, and weather disruptions.

### Shipwrights' Brotherhood
- Coordinates specialist repair labor and recognizes standards for hull work, caulking, rigging, and related crafts.
- Membership does not guarantee priority in every yard; contracts determine priority.
- Independent craftspeople may take lawful work if they meet applicable safety and contractual obligations.

### River Carters' and Bargemen's Company
- Coordinates landing schedules and shared transport arrangements.
- Independent boatmen may operate under ordinary river and landing rules.
- A company member's route agreement does not bind unrelated operators.

## Civic Trade Rules
1. Use honest weights and measures; false measures may lead to restitution, fines, or loss of market privileges.
2. Keep public lanes, quays, drains, and fire access clear.
3. Store flammable, corrosive, and volatile goods safely and disclose hazards to relevant inspectors when required.
4. Honor written contracts, leases, wages, and credit agreements.
5. Do not obstruct another tenant's access, water supply, loading rights, or lawful easements.
6. Pay established rents, harbor fees, and market dues applicable to the actual activity.
7. Do not claim a guild seal, master designation, or civic license that has not been granted.

Specific penalties depend on offense, harm, evidence, prior conduct, and applicable ordinance. No new tax, licensing fee, or monopoly should be invented at the table without recording it first.

## Employing Workers
Employers may hire independent workers, apprentices, or guild-referred staff. A guild may help find candidates but does not own the worker's labor. Wages, hours, duties, training, lodging, meals, and dismissal terms should be agreed clearly.

Use Pathfinder First Edition's published hireling costs as the baseline for mechanical hireling services. If an employee is a skilled craftsperson or manager whose role exceeds ordinary hireling service, agree on a reasonable wage and responsibilities before employment begins. Do not silently charge both a full hireling wage and an invented guild wage for the same service.

## Magical and Alchemical Businesses
- Published Pathfinder item creation requirements remain unchanged.
- The city does not impose a special alchemist license merely because alchemy is unusual.
- Ordinary fire and hazardous-material precautions apply to the actual scale and nature of the work.
- Access to buyers, premises, materials, spells, and skilled collaborators is a commercial question, not a new universal crafting prerequisite.
- A guild may review workmanship or offer referrals but cannot arbitrarily prevent a PC from crafting an item when the PC meets published rules and has necessary resources.

## Open Details
Guild leadership, internal voting rules, exact examination standards for each craft, shared-facility schedules, and specific appeal procedures remain open. Establish them when a PC seeks membership, recognition, or a particular service.
