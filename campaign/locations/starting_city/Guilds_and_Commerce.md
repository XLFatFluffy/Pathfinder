# Tallowmere: Guilds and Commerce

**Status:** Established commercial framework; individual privileges and membership rules remain expandable.

## Commercial Character
Tallowmere prospers by moving, storing, repairing, and exchanging goods. Its economy depends on a functioning harbor, reliable river traffic, safe roads, skilled labor, honest weights, and merchants willing to risk capital.

Guilds are associations of masters, recognized craftspeople, and privileged traders. They protect standards, negotiate rights, train apprentices, and defend members' interests. Some are chartered bodies; others are looser associations. Guild membership can provide training, contacts, shared facilities, and legal standing, but may also impose dues and obligations. Guilds do not automatically control everyone in a trade.

## Major Commercial Bodies
- **Harbor Fellowship:** Shipowners, captains, chandlers, pilots, and shipping agents. It bargains over harbor fees, quay access, pilotage, and navigational markers. Members disagree over priority for large vessels versus smaller traders.
- **Warehouse Guild:** Warehouse owners, bonded-store keepers, weighers, and cargo clerks. It values predictable rules, secure storage, fire prevention, and reliable records. It often disputes costs with shipowners.
- **River Carters' and Bargemen's Company:** Barge owners, carters, ferrymen, mill transporters, and river pilots. It connects the port to inland settlements and resents being treated as secondary to sea trade.
- **Market Guild:** Established stallholders, produce dealers, moneychangers, brokers, and shopkeepers. It seeks consistent market rules and limits on privileged exemptions.
- **Artificers' Hall:** Recognized masters in metalwork, carpentry, leatherwork, pottery, textiles, and related crafts. It influences civic works and quality disputes but does not monopolize every craft.
- **Shipwrights' Brotherhood:** Shipwrights, caulkers, rope-makers, sailmakers, and specialists. It protects standards and skilled labor; urgent repair demand can exceed available workers.

These bodies have competing interests and internal divisions. They are not a unified merchant faction.

## Independent Commerce
Family firms, partnerships, itinerant merchants, foreign factors, and independent traders operate alongside guilds. Wealthy merchant families may maintain warehouses, credit relationships, and long-term contracts without controlling the guilds. The Denvors depend on these interests but retain formal authority.

## Magical Craft and Commerce
Magic items are rare because specialized knowledge and talent are uncommon and necessary materials are difficult to obtain. Tallowmere has no mass market or large magical manufacturing quarter. A handful of people may identify, repair, commission, or occasionally create magical goods, but their abilities must be plausible under Pathfinder First Edition rules.

Possible routes for PCs include buyers among wealthy merchants, nobles, clergy, adventurers, and visiting factors; ordinary materials and occasional unusual imports; practitioners and scholars; appraisers and brokers; and merchant-backed commissions.

Do not impose additional universal crafting prerequisites beyond published rules. Scarcity is primarily expressed through availability of expertise, spells, services, materials, and buyers. Any campaign-specific mechanical restriction requires explicit GM approval.

## Commercial Tensions
Recurring pressures include harbor fees, guild privileges, labor wages, storage losses, fire safety, counterfeit goods, debt, salvage rights, and competition from rival ports. These are ongoing civic issues, not mandatory plot hooks.

## PC Opportunities
The city supports ordinary routes to a player-run business: renting or buying premises, hiring workers, negotiating supply contracts, finding customers, taking commissions, joining or bargaining with guilds, and establishing partnerships. The PCs may operate outside guild structures where lawful, accepting practical consequences. Success can create competitors, allies, debtors, and customers without automatically making every merchant hostile.
