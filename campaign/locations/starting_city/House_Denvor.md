# House Denvor

**Status:** ESTABLISHED MINOR HOUSE  
**Seat:** Greywatch, Tallowmere  
**Allegiance:** House Kharadorn, under a hereditary charter  
**Ancestry:** Human  
**Political scale:** Locally powerful; little continent-wide influence

## House Character

House Denvor is an old, respected family whose authority is rooted in hereditary rights, local custom, and its responsibility for keeping Tallowmere secure and prosperous. It is neither a Great House nor a disguised continental power. Its influence is substantial within its own city and modest territory, but it has little weight in the wider politics of the realm.

The Denvors prize continuity, public reputation, duty, and competent administration. They are traditional in matters of lineage and noble obligation, but not hostile to commerce. They understand that Tallowmere's wealth depends on merchants, shipowners, craftspeople, dockworkers, and the movement of goods. Their preferred style of rule is cautious, personal, and negotiated: preserve the family's authority, keep the harbor functioning, prevent disorder from threatening trade, and avoid drawing the attention of greater powers unnecessarily.

The house is not inherently benevolent or oppressive. Its members can be generous, self-interested, fair, prejudiced, farsighted, or foolish as individuals. Their actions should be driven by their personalities and circumstances, not by a preset role as villains or patrons.

## Arms and Words

**Arms:** A black heron standing in shallow silver water upon a deep green field.  
**Words:** *Where Waters Gather.*

The heron represents patience, vigilance, and the ability to thrive where land and water meet. The arms are recognizable on Greywatch banners, harbor seals, household livery, and official documents.

## The Current Family

The following immediate family is established for the current campaign era.

- **Lord Oswin Denvor:** Current lord of Tallowmere. In his early fifties, measured and observant, with a strong preference for negotiated solutions and predictable obligations. He takes the charter seriously and believes a lord's first duty is to leave the city more secure than he found it. His caution can preserve stability, but it can also make him slow to respond to problems that demand decisive action.
- **Lady Catrin Denvor:** Oswin's wife and a politically capable member of the household. She maintains relationships with the city's charitable institutions, established families, and visiting dignitaries. She is less patient than her husband with officials who mistake courtesy for weakness.
- **Ser Edwyn Denvor:** Their eldest child and recognized heir. In his mid-twenties, he has spent time inspecting the harbor, garrison, and surrounding estates. He believes Tallowmere needs more investment in its defenses and infrastructure, and he is impatient with arrangements that leave the city dependent on merchant goodwill. He is not a reckless warmonger, but he is more willing than Oswin to spend money and test boundaries.
- **Lady Anwen Denvor:** Their younger child, in her early twenties. She is perceptive, socially skilled, and more interested in the city's commercial and civic networks than in household ceremony. She has developed genuine relationships with people outside the nobility. Her influence is informal, and the extent of her ambitions is not yet publicly understood.

The family is not a simple united bloc. Oswin and Edwyn disagree about the pace and cost of reform; Catrin often acts as mediator but has her own priorities; Anwen sees opportunities and problems that the others overlook. These differences are not, by themselves, a succession crisis or an active conspiracy.

## The Lordship Charter

House Denvor's authority rests on a hereditary charter confirmed by House Kharadorn after the Great Rebellion. Under the charter, the Denvors govern Tallowmere and a modest surrounding territory in return for obligations to the Kharadorn rulers:

- Payment of an agreed annual tribute.
- Maintenance of harbor defenses and the city's capacity to support regional security.
- Provision of ships, supplies, or transport when lawfully called upon under the charter.
- Recognition of Kharadorn's strategic rights concerning the harbor and inland-sea defense.

The Denvors administer the city's daily affairs. Kharadorn does not directly appoint every local official or control routine civic decisions. The precise wording, historical negotiations, enforcement mechanisms, and any disputed clauses of the charter remain to be developed.

## Sources of Power and Vulnerability

House Denvor's strength comes from its hereditary legitimacy, Greywatch's defensible position, customary dues, surrounding land, household retainers, local garrison, and recognized authority over the harbor. It also benefits from the fact that most of Tallowmere's leading interests prefer a predictable local lord to direct administration by a distant Great House.

Its vulnerabilities are ordinary and consequential: the cost of maintaining walls and harbor defenses, dependence on trade, the need to meet Kharadorn obligations, family disagreements over policy, and the possibility that a poor decision damages the trust of merchants or residents. These are pressures, not predetermined weaknesses for the PCs to exploit.

## Role in the Campaign

House Denvor may become a source of patronage, a negotiating partner, a political rival, a source of employment, or an obstacle to particular plans. The PCs are not presumed to seek its title, wealth, or territory. They may build influence in Tallowmere without replacing the ruling family, and any major change in the lordship must arise from play and established rules of the world.
