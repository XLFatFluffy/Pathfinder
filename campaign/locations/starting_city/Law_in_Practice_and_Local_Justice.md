# Tallowmere: Law in Practice and Local Justice

**Visibility:** GM-ONLY  
**Status:** Local operating guide derived from established offices, charter, civic procedure, and campaign-wide legal principles.  
**Baseline:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC

This document explains how ordinary legal problems are likely to move through Tallowmere's institutions. It does not create a complete criminal code, fixed sentencing tariff, modern police procedure, or new court. The [Government and Law](Government_and_Law.md), [Chartered Council and Civic Procedure](Chartered_Council_and_Civic_Procedure.md), and campaign-wide [Law, Justice, and Crime](../../world/Law_Justice_and_Crime.md) records remain authoritative for established powers and limits.

## 1. The local legal order

Tallowmere is governed by House Denvor under its hereditary charter, confirmed by House Kharadorn after the Great Rebellion. Lord Oswin Denvor retains final local authority within the charter and established custom. The town is not governed by a sovereign elected council, and the Chartered Council is not a criminal court or independent legislature.

The city uses several overlapping sources of authority:
- House Denvor's chartered lordship authority and local customary law.
- Civic ordinances and the established duties of local officers.
- Guild and company rules within their recognized membership and privileges.
- Contracts, leases, debts, and private agreements.
- Kharadorn's strategic rights concerning the harbor and inland-sea defense.
- Religious rules within a temple or faith institution, not general secular jurisdiction.

A matter's location, parties, subject, and governing right determine who should address it. A warehouse dispute may involve a private contract, a guild rule, a fire-safety concern, and a civic ordinance at the same time. That does not give every involved institution authority to decide every part of the dispute.

## 2. Who does what

### Lord Oswin Denvor

The lord retains ultimate local authority under the charter. He may appoint civic officers, enforce customary law, collect established dues, call the local levy, grant or confirm certain privileges, and preside over the lordship's courts or appoint judges.

The lord is not expected to personally settle every debt, street quarrel, or market complaint. Routine administration depends on delegated officers. Frequent personal intervention can weaken those offices and turn ordinary disputes into political questions.

### Town Reeve Marrec Holt

The Reeve coordinates civic administration, records, local dues, market ordinances, and communication with the Chartered Council. His office is a natural starting point for petitions, administrative complaints, market access requests, civic notices, and matters that require a written record or referral.

Marrec does not automatically investigate every crime or know every private dispute. His records reflect matters brought to the office and the work the administration has capacity to perform.

### Chief Magistrate Odran Pell

The Chief Magistrate oversees hearings, warrants, fines, civil disputes, and criminal proceedings delegated by the lord. Odran values testimony that survives scrutiny and orderly hearings. He is concerned that wealth allows some litigants to delay proceedings while poorer residents struggle to press valid claims.

The office does not establish a modern professional prosecution service, a universal jury, standardized forensic practice, or an automatic appeal ladder. Odran must work within the evidence, authority, staff, and custom available in a particular case.

### Captain of the Watch Brinna Salk

The Watch handles ordinary patrols, disturbances, market order, arrests, and enforcement of lawful orders. Brinna commands its patrols and coordinates with the garrison during emergencies.

The Watch is expected to cover too much ground with limited personnel. It is more visible around the market, gates, and quays than in poorer peripheral streets. Response depends on staffing, leadership, weather, competing incidents, and the importance attached to the complaint. Do not treat the Watch as omnipresent or uniformly corrupt.

### Harbor Master Ysella Vane

The Harbor Master oversees vessel registration, anchorage, harbor safety, cargo declarations, quay assignments, and harbor fees. Ysella is the strongest official source for recorded harbor activity and practical shipping disruptions.

Her office can identify what a vessel declared, where it was assigned, or what was recorded about a berth. Such records do not by themselves prove theft, smuggling, fraud, or intent. Serious criminal allegations still require the appropriate civic or judicial authority.

### Castellan Hadrik Voss and the garrison

The Castellan is responsible for Greywatch, the household guard, stores, and defensive readiness. The garrison protects the noble seat, walls, gates, and key defensive positions. It is not the ordinary street patrol and does not replace the Watch in routine civic matters.

The Watch and garrison may coordinate during fires, riots, attacks, or threats to the walls. Their command responsibilities remain distinct. A military emergency does not automatically place the whole city under permanent martial law.

### Guilds, companies, and community mediators

Recognized guilds and companies may address internal standards, apprenticeship disagreements, member obligations, and commercial disputes within their privileges. They may mediate or arbitrate matters where the parties consent or recognized custom allows it.

They do not automatically possess general criminal jurisdiction. A guild may discipline a member or suspend a privilege; it cannot simply imprison an outsider or replace the Chief Magistrate in a serious assault or arson case.

Community mediators may help parties reach an agreement, especially where ongoing neighborhood relationships matter. Their influence depends on trust and acceptance, not an invented universal legal office.

### Kharadorn representatives

Kharadorn retains strategic rights concerning Tallowmere's harbor and inland-sea defense under the Denvor charter. Its representatives may inspect obligations, review defenses, negotiate shipping support, or respond to a serious charter breach.

They do not ordinarily adjudicate petty theft, household debts, market arguments, or routine local offenses. Strategic oversight is not day-to-day city government.

## 3. How a complaint enters the system

There is no single mandatory route for every complaint. A practical starting point is:

1. **Immediate danger:** The Watch is the ordinary civic response for street violence, disturbance, theft in progress, or threats to public order. Harbor incidents may also require the Harbor Master's staff. A fire or attack threatening defenses may require coordination with the garrison.
2. **Administrative or market issue:** A resident may approach the Reeve's office about market access, dues, civic maintenance, notices, or a petition requiring formal attention.
3. **Harbor matter:** Vessel movement, berth allocation, cargo declarations, harbor safety, and harbor fees begin with the Harbor Master or harbor administration.
4. **Private civil dispute:** Parties may negotiate directly, use a mutually accepted mediator, approach a guild or company where relevant, or seek the Chief Magistrate's hearing where the matter falls within delegated jurisdiction.
5. **Serious alleged crime:** The Watch may respond, preserve the immediate situation, identify witnesses, and make arrests where lawful. The matter proceeds to the Chief Magistrate or lord's court according to its seriousness and jurisdiction.
6. **Charter or strategic dispute:** A question about Denvor obligations, Kharadorn rights, or a conflict between charter authorities may require the lord and the appropriate superior authority. It is not an ordinary market-court matter.

These are practical pathways, not a rigid procedural code. A person may approach the wrong office first and be referred elsewhere. An urgent incident may require action before jurisdiction is fully settled.

## 4. Evidence and testimony

Local officials may rely on:
- eyewitness accounts and statements from people who heard or saw relevant events;
- physical traces, damaged property, stolen goods, and injuries;
- contracts, receipts, account books, harbor records, seals, and written petitions;
- testimony from workers, neighbors, servants, merchants, and recognized guild members;
- expert observations from craftspeople, healers, shipmasters, or other people with relevant practical knowledge;
- a person's opportunity, motive, conduct, and reputation, considered with caution.

A witness may be honest but mistaken. A document may be authentic but incomplete. A merchant's ledger may establish what was entered in the account, not necessarily what happened outside the counting room. A confession may be coerced, self-serving, or false.

Tallowmere has no established modern forensic service, universal evidence code, or professional detective bureau. The Chief Magistrate's preference for careful testimony does not mean every case receives exhaustive investigation. Available time, witnesses, records, social influence, and the seriousness of the alleged harm all matter.

## 5. Civil disputes: the ordinary workload

Much of local justice concerns practical obligations rather than dramatic crime:
- unpaid debts and broken contracts;
- disputed wages, shares, and cargo losses;
- tenancy, rent, inheritance, and property boundaries;
- market measures, quality, stall access, and fraudulent sales;
- harbor fees, berth assignments, salvage, and vessel damage;
- apprenticeship terms, workmanship, and guild privileges;
- grazing, woodland, fishing, water, quarry, road, and ferry rights.

The first response may be negotiation, witnesses, a written account, a surety, restitution, or mediation. Formal proceedings can be slow and costly. A merchant may prefer settlement to avoid exposing accounts or delaying cargo. A laborer may accept a poor settlement because they cannot afford to miss work or hire a scribe.

The Reeve's office can record a petition or administrative complaint. The Chief Magistrate can hear matters within delegated jurisdiction. A guild may address a member dispute within its recognized authority. None of these routes guarantees that a poor claimant will obtain a favorable result.

## 6. Arrest, detention, and judgment

The Watch handles arrests and enforcement of lawful orders. The Chief Magistrate oversees warrants, hearings, fines, civil disputes, and criminal proceedings delegated by the lord. The lord retains ultimate local authority.

The setting has not established the precise location or capacity of local holding cells, routine custody periods, bail or surety rules, rights of counsel, formal charging documents, or a fixed sequence of hearings. Do not improvise these as universal legal rights or procedures. Decide what is necessary for the specific scene and keep the ruling consistent thereafter.

Possible outcomes across the legal framework include warning, reprimand, apology, public correction, restitution, repayment, fines, forfeiture, guild discipline, restrictions on market or harbor access, temporary detention, imprisonment, corporal punishment, banishment, or capital punishment where the relevant law permits it.

No universal sentence tariff is established. Consider the offense, harm, evidence, status, local custom, jurisdiction, prior conduct, and the authority's ability to enforce the decision. A fine paid to the lord is not automatically compensation for the victim.

## 7. Unequal access and the city's social fault lines

The law is not socially neutral in practice. Wealth, ancestry, guild membership, reputation, literacy, patronage, and proximity to powerful households can affect who is heard and whose account is believed.

Ratfolk face widespread prejudice in surface society. This can affect how quickly a complaint is taken seriously, whether witnesses are willing to speak, and whether a trader can obtain guild or market support. It does not mean every official is hostile or that ratfolk have no recourse.

The Chartered Council has no guaranteed ratfolk seat. Ratfolk residents may petition, attend public portions where permitted, submit a written or witnessed statement through the Reeve, or seek sponsorship from a member. This is a real structural disadvantage. It does not mean the Council controls criminal justice or that every member supports exclusion.

A wealthy merchant may secure a scribe and witnesses. A laborer may rely on neighbors. A ratfolk household may use trusted mediators before approaching surface officials, especially if retaliation is feared. These differences should shape access and risk without predetermining every outcome.

## 8. Illustrative local cases

These examples show how established offices might interact. They do not create fixed rulings or new law.

### A disputed cargo tally

A merchant alleges that part of a shipment is missing. The Harbor Master can clarify the vessel's declaration, berth, and recorded harbor activity. The Warehouse Guild may have relevant storage accounts and worker testimony. The parties may negotiate or seek a civil hearing. A missing entry is a lead, not proof that a named clerk stole the goods.

### A fight at the Crooked Oar

The Watch responds to immediate disorder and injury. It may separate participants, identify witnesses, and determine whether an arrest or further referral is warranted. The tavern's reputation does not prove who started the fight. Serious injury or evidence of a wider crime may bring the matter before the Chief Magistrate.

### A ratfolk trader denied a market opportunity

The trader may raise the issue through the Reeve, seek support from Tikka Vell or other advocates, and use the petition process. The Market Guild's position and the Council's composition may shape the political response. This is not automatically a criminal matter, and the absence of a guaranteed Council seat is not itself a court judgment.

### A warehouse fire

The Watch and relevant civic officers address immediate public safety. The Warehouse Guild and owners may provide records, worker accounts, and evidence about precautions. The garrison may assist if the fire threatens defenses or major stores. The cause should not be declared arson without evidence; the fire-safety dispute does not establish who is responsible.

### A debt between merchants

The parties may rely on contracts, ledgers, witnesses, sureties, and commercial reputation. Ilyne Marris may be relevant as a moneychanger or broker if she handled the transaction, but her role does not make her a court officer. The Reeve or Chief Magistrate becomes relevant according to the nature of the complaint and the jurisdiction involved.

## 9. GM guidance

When a legal problem reaches the table, answer these questions before deciding the result:

1. What right, duty, contract, harm, or public interest is involved?
2. Where did it occur, and which authority has jurisdiction?
3. Who has the practical responsibility to respond?
4. What is directly known, what is recorded, and what is only alleged?
5. Who can provide credible evidence, and who might be reluctant?
6. Can the parties settle it without a formal hearing?
7. What remedy is available, and who can enforce it?
8. How do wealth, status, ancestry, reputation, and political pressure affect the process?

Do not turn every minor offense into a full courtroom scene. A warning, repayment, apology, guild reprimand, or quiet settlement may be more plausible and more useful to play. Conversely, do not let a powerful character's status erase consequences automatically.

## 10. Unresolved local procedure

The following remain open and should be decided only when play requires them:
- Full text and precise wording of the Denvor charter.
- Exact local court calendar, hearing sequence, and appeal procedure.
- Location, capacity, and staffing of any holding cells or prison.
- Custody periods, bail or surety practice, and formal charging procedure.
- Exact local penalties for named offenses.
- Watch staffing, patrol rota, equipment, and response times.
- Written ordinances beyond those already recorded.
- Whether specific guilds have recognized arbitration rules for particular disputes.
- Formal procedures for handling complaints against civic officers.

Do not fill these gaps with modern police or court assumptions. Tallowmere's justice system is personal, delegated, politically constrained, and dependent on the records and relationships that actually exist.
