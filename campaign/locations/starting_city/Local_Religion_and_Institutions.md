# Tallowmere: Local Religion and Institutions

**Status:** ESTABLISHED LOCAL FRAMEWORK — 2026-11-04  
**Ruleset:** Pathfinder First Edition  
**Parent canon:** `campaign/religions/Religion_and_Faith.md`

This document applies the established continental religious framework to Tallowmere. It does not create a separate pantheon, make one faith the city's official religion, or imply that every priest can cast spells.

## Religious character

Tallowmere's religious life reflects its port, river, mixed population, and practical concerns. Residents pray for safe voyages, honest contracts, healthy children, good harvests, fair judgment, recovery from illness, and proper remembrance of the dead. Household practice is often more common than attendance at formal services.

The Pathfinder pantheon is recognized. The institutions below are visible local presences, not the only faiths practiced in the city. Worshippers of other Pathfinder deities may maintain household shrines, small congregations, or private devotional customs without possessing a major public temple.

No deity is the city's official patron. House Denvor does not impose a single faith. Religious institutions may advise, criticize, bargain with, or cooperate with civic authorities, but none possesses a general right to overrule secular law.

## Principal institutions

### The Open Ledger — Abadar

**Location:** High Ward, near the legal and commercial offices.  
**Public role:** The city's most prominent formal temple.

The Open Ledger is dedicated to Abadar and emphasizes lawful trade, contracts, measured weights, civic order, roads, responsible stewardship, and the prosperity that follows predictable institutions. Its clergy bless new ventures, witness certain contracts when requested, maintain a public calendar of services, teach basic numeracy to a limited number of pupils, and mediate commercial disagreements when both parties consent.

The temple does not certify every contract, guarantee a debtor's honesty, or replace the Chief Magistrate. Its records are religious and institutional records, not a universal registry of property.

**Senior local figure:** Prelate Damas Orrel, human, a careful administrator who believes fair procedure protects the weak as well as the wealthy. He dislikes being treated as a civic official and resists attempts to turn the temple into a tool of House Denvor or the Market Guild.

**Staff and capacity:** A small resident clergy, lay record-keepers, attendants, and volunteers. Damas is not assumed to be a divine spellcaster. Any spellcasting service must be established individually under Pathfinder rules.

### The Quiet Gate — Pharasma

**Location:** Lantern Ward, adjoining the older burial ground.  
**Public role:** Funerary rites, burial records, care for the dying, and remembrance.

The Quiet Gate serves worshippers of Pharasma and those who seek proper rites regardless of wealth. Its clergy oversee burial customs, maintain records of interment where families request them, advise on mourning and inheritance customs, and provide rites for the unclaimed dead when resources permit.

**Senior local figure:** Keeper Lethia Sorn, human, a reserved and compassionate priest who insists that the dead be treated with dignity. She is suspicious of anyone who uses funerals to settle debts, conceal evidence, or exploit grieving families.

The Quiet Gate does not investigate every death. It cooperates with the magistrate when lawful process requires it, while protecting private pastoral conversations within the limits of local custom and law.

### The Tideward Shrine — Gozreh

**Location:** Old Quays, above the working waterfront.  
**Public role:** Sailors' vows, river blessings, weather observances, and memorials for those lost on the water.

The Tideward Shrine is a modest stone-and-timber sanctuary dedicated to Gozreh. It is maintained by sailors, fishers, riverfolk, and their families. Offerings are practical and varied: a portion of a first catch, a token from a safe voyage, or a name added to the memorial boards.

**Keeper:** Brother Caldus, half-orc, remains the established shrine keeper. His work includes rites, counsel, and maintaining the memorial records. He is not automatically a spellcaster or a representative of every sailor.

The shrine has no authority over harbor navigation or vessel safety. Harbor Master Ysella Vane remains responsible for those civic matters.

### The House of the Dawn — Sarenrae

**Location:** Lantern Ward, within walking distance of Lantern Hospice.  
**Public role:** Healing charity, refuge, reconciliation, and aid to the poor.

The House of the Dawn is a small Sarenite religious house. It provides meals, basic care, limited shelter, and assistance to people seeking to leave cycles of violence or exploitation. It coordinates with Lantern Hospice when needs exceed either institution's capacity.

**Senior local figure:** Dawn-Sister Vaela Dorn, human, leads the resident religious household. She is firm about mercy being active rather than merely spoken. She supports aid to ratfolk residents and dockworkers but opposes using charitable work as a public-relations shield for wealthy patrons.

The House of the Dawn is not a hospital with unlimited resources. Its attendants provide ordinary care; magical healing is available only when a capable practitioner is actually present and willing to provide it.

### Small shrines and household worship

- A modest shrine to Desna is maintained by several households near the Underways' better-known surface access points. It is used by travelers, couriers, and people who value luck and safe journeys. It does not speak for all ratfolk.
- Small household shrines to Erastil appear among families with rural ties, particularly merchants and workers from the Northfields.
- Individual sailors and travelers may carry tokens or make offerings to other gods without belonging to a formal congregation.
- Dwarven residents may observe Torag's rites privately or through visiting dwarven clergy. The city's dwarves are not all Kharadorn officials and do not all share the same local obligations.

No additional major temple is assumed solely because a deity appears in this list. Other faiths may be practiced privately or through small groups.

## Relationship with civic government

- The temples may petition the Town Reeve, seek charitable exemptions where lawful, request protection, and advise House Denvor.
- No temple holds an automatic seat on the chartered council.
- Religious clergy may witness marriages, funerals, oaths, and private agreements according to local custom. Their rites do not automatically determine legal inheritance or property ownership.
- The Chief Magistrate retains secular jurisdiction over crimes and civil disputes. Religious institutions may discipline their own members internally, but no local clerical court has been granted general criminal jurisdiction.
- The Denvors may request prayers or public ceremonies but cannot dictate doctrine or appoint a temple's senior clergy.
- Religious leaders disagree among themselves. They are not a single political bloc and do not all support the court faction called the Religious Establishment.

## Divine magic and services

Divine spellcasters remain uncommon. The Open Ledger, Quiet Gate, Tideward Shrine, and House of the Dawn do not each automatically possess a cleric capable of casting spells.

For every divine spellcasting NPC, the GM must establish a plausible Pathfinder First Edition class, level, spell access, preparation or spells known, and required components or foci. A temple may have no available caster on a given day. Healing, restoration, divination, and other services must be based on actual capability and availability, not assumed because a building is called a temple.

These limits do not change player-character spellcasting or add setting-specific restrictions to published rules.

## Current religious tensions

1. **Charity and market access:** Dawn-Sister Vaela supports fair access for ratfolk traders and worries that charitable relief is being used to compensate for avoidable exclusion.
2. **Commercial ethics:** Prelate Damas wants the Market Guild and merchants to treat contracts and measures consistently, but he will not endorse every guild demand as lawful.
3. **Burial and poverty:** Keeper Lethia is concerned about unclaimed dead and families unable to afford customary rites.
4. **Sailors' memorials:** Brother Caldus wants reliable records of missing crews, while shipowners sometimes prefer to declare a vessel lost and settle accounts quickly.
5. **Religious influence:** Some local clergy favor stronger public recognition of religious authority; others fear entanglement with House Denvor and commercial patrons.

These are current positions and pressures, not a coordinated conspiracy.

## Open details

The precise number of resident clergy, individual divine spellcasters, temple finances, landholdings, service schedules, and regional religious superiors remain open. No local institution is given an unrecorded spellcasting service, legal privilege, or political power.
