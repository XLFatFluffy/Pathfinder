# Medical Care in Tallowmere

**Status:** ESTABLISHED LOCAL SERVICE FRAMEWORK; practical portrayal guidance  
**Snapshot:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC  
**Visibility:** GM-ONLY  
**Scope:** Tallowmere and its immediate hinterland  
**Parent reference:** [Medicine, Disease, and Healing in the Realm](../../world/Medicine_Disease_and_Healing.md)

## 1. What medical care looks like here

Tallowmere has a real but limited network of ordinary care. It is not a city without healers, nor does it possess a modern hospital system hidden behind a temple door.

A resident may receive help from family, a neighbor, a midwife, an herbalist, a practical healer, the Lantern Hospice, a charitable religious house, or a trained alchemist. These services overlap, but they are not interchangeable. A midwife is not automatically a surgeon; an herbalist is not a physician; a hospice is not an infirmary with unlimited beds; and a temple is not a promise of magical healing.

The most important established local providers and institutions are:
- **Lantern Hospice:** charitable care and temporary assistance for sick, injured, poor, and traveling people in need, within finite means. Sister Alwen administers it.
- **Sister Alwen:** hospice administrator; practical, stubborn, and focused on food, shelter, care, burial assistance, and abandoned children. The hospice is chronically short of funds.
- **Brother Halen:** hospice stores clerk; tracks donations, food, blankets, and medicines. He is meticulous and overworked, and worries that opaque accounts will damage public trust.
- **Edda Moss:** independent midwife and practical healer in Lantern Ward. She works with the hospice when needed and serves poorer households. She is respected for competence and discretion; she is not a divine spellcaster.
- **Fenna Wale:** herbalist and trained healer who runs Fenna's Physic Garden near the hospice gardens. She sells mundane remedies, poultices, clean bandages, and limited specialist supplies. She is not a spellcaster.
- **Miraq Ashwhisk:** ratfolk alchemist and proprietor of Tallowmere Alchemical Supply in Market Ward. She is a specialist craftsperson, not a general physician or a free source of healing.

The House of the Dawn, dedicated to Sarenrae, provides meals, basic care, limited shelter, and refuge. It coordinates with Lantern Hospice when needs exceed either institution's capacity. The House of the Dawn is a religious house, not the hospice itself.

## 2. The Lantern Hospice

The Lantern Hospice is a charitable institution in Lantern Ward. It offers care and temporary assistance to sick and injured people, poor residents, and travelers who have nowhere else to turn. It also participates in burial assistance and seasonal charitable work.

Its limits matter:
- It has finite space, supplies, staff, and donations.
- It cannot accept every person who asks for help.
- It cannot provide continuous specialist supervision for every patient.
- It relies on donations and relationships with households, merchants, and civic patrons.
- Its stores must balance food, blankets, medicines, and other needs.
- It is not a substitute for a large hospital and has no established magical healing service.

The hospice should feel like a working charitable house. There are stores to count, bedding to wash, meals to prepare, visitors to assess, and difficult decisions about who can be helped. Its care is meaningful precisely because its resources are limited.

### Sister Alwen's role

Sister Alwen is an administrator and charitable worker, not automatically a physician or spellcaster. Her strength is practical judgment, persistence, and an unwillingness to treat suffering as a public performance. She must decide how to allocate limited resources while preserving dignity.

She is likely to prioritize immediate danger, vulnerability, lack of other support, and whether the hospice can realistically help. She will not promise a bed or treatment she cannot provide. She dislikes wealthy patrons who expect public gratitude or influence over who receives care.

### Brother Halen's role

Brother Halen maintains the hospice's stores and records. He tracks food, blankets, medicines, and donations. He is a useful contact for what the hospice actually has, what is running low, and whether a promised donation arrived. He is not the person to diagnose a patient.

His concern about transparent accounts is established. Do not turn that concern into proof of theft or corruption without play establishing it.

## 3. Midwifery and practical care

Edda Moss is the established independent midwife and practical healer. She works with the hospice when needed but maintains her own practice, including among poorer households.

Use her for:
- pregnancy support and ordinary birth preparation;
- practical assistance during labor;
- postnatal care and advice;
- infant care and common childhood complaints;
- wound cleaning and dressing within her skill;
- recognizing when a patient needs help beyond her abilities;
- discreet advice to households reluctant to discuss intimate matters publicly.

Her reputation rests on competence and discretion, not magic. Do not assume she can guarantee a safe birth or resolve every complication. Difficult labor, severe bleeding, infection, malpresentation, or a newborn in distress can exceed what practical care can reliably address. Her response should be informed by experience and urgency, not modern obstetric certainty.

A birth at a wealthy home may involve more attendants, clean cloth, warm water, space, and paid assistance. A poor household may have less privacy, fewer supplies, and more pressure for the mother to return to work. These are differences in resources, not differences in the value of the people involved.

## 4. Herbal practice and prepared remedies

Fenna Wale's Physic Garden is a practical source of herbs and mundane preparations. Fenna is a trained healer and herbalist, not a spellcaster. Her established stock includes:
- healer's kits and clean bandages;
- herbal poultices for comfort and minor wound care;
- traditional fever tea with no guaranteed mechanical effect;
- dried yarrow and other labeled herbs;
- limited antitoxin and purchased alchemical supplies;
- scent-masking herbs for household use.

Her stock and listed prices are controlled by [Tallowmere Shops, Stock, and Prices](../locations/starting_city/Shopkeepers_and_Stock.md). Do not change them in this document.

Fenna refuses to claim that herbs cure curses, magical diseases, or grievous wounds. She may explain what a preparation is traditionally used for, what she has observed, and what she does not know. A preparation should only grant a reliable mechanical effect if its effect is defined by Pathfinder rules or separately approved campaign canon.

Miraq Ashwhisk's alchemical shop is distinct. She can supply alchemical goods and laboratory materials according to her shop record. Alchemy is not interchangeable with ordinary herbalism, and neither should be treated as a universal substitute for divine magic.

## 5. How a patient moves through the city

There is no single mandatory path. The following are useful common patterns, not official referral rules.

- **Minor cut, burn, or household complaint:** family or neighbor provides immediate care; the patient may seek Fenna's advice or supplies if needed.
- **Work injury without immediate life threat:** a coworker or household member helps; the patient may consult Edda, Fenna, or the hospice depending on the injury, means, and need for continued support.
- **Serious injury or a person without support:** urgent help is sought; the hospice may provide temporary care if it has capacity. It cannot promise specialist treatment or a bed.
- **Pregnancy and birth:** Edda is a known local midwife; family support and available supplies matter. The hospice may assist when needed, but it is not established as a dedicated maternity ward.
- **Persistent or unexplained illness:** a household may seek practical advice, herbal preparations, religious support, or a physician from outside the established local roster. No named Tallowmere physician is currently confirmed.
- **Need for magical healing:** only a specifically established spellcaster with appropriate Pathfinder First Edition capability can provide it. A temple's existence is not enough.

The patient may move between providers, but care can be delayed by cost, distance, work obligations, stigma, distrust, or uncertainty about whether the problem is serious.

## 6. Sanitation and the shape of illness

Tallowmere has public wells, pumps, cisterns, drains, privies, refuse arrangements, cellars, granaries, stables, and a working harbor. Their quality and maintenance vary by district and household.

Established local vulnerabilities include:
- crowded housing and shared facilities;
- lower waterfront and Rivergate streets that can become muddy or flood-prone;
- differences between private water access and shared wells or pumps;
- drainage and seepage problems;
- stored food exposed to damp or pests;
- animal waste and refuse in busy working districts;
- indoor smoke and poor ventilation;
- limited space to isolate a sick person in a crowded household.

These conditions create plausible risks, not proof of a current outbreak. A blocked drain, contaminated well, or spoiled food store should have a discoverable cause if it becomes important. Do not assign a disease or blame a community without evidence.

The city's ratfolk residents are not inherently diseased or contagious. Ordinary rats, sanitation problems, and ratfolk communities are separate matters. Prejudice may lead people to make false accusations, but the setting should not validate those accusations as medical fact.

## 8. Magic and the limits of healing

Pathfinder First Edition rules govern actual spells, potions, alchemical extracts, and supernatural effects. Do not create a free healing service because a character found a temple or hospice.

A magical service requires an actual practitioner with the class, level, spell access, preparation or spells known, and resources to provide it. It also requires that the practitioner is available and willing. The current local records do not establish a routine public spellcasting service at Lantern Hospice, the House of the Dawn, or any other Tallowmere institution.

Healing hit point damage does not automatically remove disease, poison, disability, infection, or every consequence of trauma. Use the actual spell description and Pathfinder rules. Ordinary care remains important even in a world where magic exists.

## 9. GM portrayal: care without melodrama

- A sick person is still a person with preferences, responsibilities, relationships, and opinions.
- A healer can be competent and still fail because the condition is beyond their skill.
- A household may delay care because of money or lost wages, not because its members are foolish.
- A charitable institution can do real good while making painful decisions about limited resources.
- A patient may distrust a practitioner for personal, religious, cultural, or prior-experience reasons.
- A physician or healer can disagree with a priest without either being malicious.
- Disability is not a moral lesson, and chronic illness does not erase a person's agency.
- Disease is not a convenient way to punish players for touching the wrong object.
- Do not make every injury fatal or every illness a mystery. Ordinary recovery, setbacks, and lasting consequences all have a place.

## 10. Unresolved details

- The exact physical layout, number of beds, resident staff, and daily capacity of Lantern Hospice.
- Whether the hospice has a dedicated treatment room, separate sleeping wards, or only adapted charitable accommodation.
- Sister Alwen's formal religious affiliation, if any, beyond her established role and charitable work.
- The exact training history and full capabilities of Edda Moss and Fenna Wale.
- Whether any physician visits Tallowmere regularly from another settlement.
- The identity and availability of any divine spellcaster capable of healing.
- Specific locally common diseases, endemic pathogens, outbreaks, and their prevalence.
- Detailed citywide rules for waste removal, well inspection, quarantine, and public-health authority.
- Exact prices for services not already listed in shop inventories.

Do not resolve these details just to make the document look complete. They can be established when a patient, institution, crisis, or player decision makes them relevant.
