# Tallowmere: Merchant Personalities, Obligations, and Disputes

**Status:** Initial private motives and commercial relationships established for the GM. These are independent local interests, not evidence of a single coordinated scheme. NPCs can change their positions through events and play.

This record adds motives and relationships to the businesses in `Shopkeepers_and_Stock.md` and `Merchant_Relationships_and_Supply_Lines.md`. It does not replace their listed stock, prices, or Pathfinder rules.

## Principal Merchant Personalities

### Beren Harlowe — Harlowe's General Stores
Beren is practical, careful with money, and more generous to familiar customers than his public manner suggests. His shop is profitable but not wealthy; most of his capital is tied up in stock. He dislikes customers who treat a small shop as an inexhaustible warehouse.

- **Current concern:** A regional wholesaler has shortened his payment terms after two late river deliveries. Beren is trying to preserve his usual prices without exhausting his cash reserve.
- **Useful connection:** He knows which ordinary goods are moving through the city and which suppliers are dependable.
- **Pressure point:** A large order paid promptly would help him, but he will not sell below replacement cost merely to win a customer.

### Mara Venn — The Salted Tack
Mara is a disciplined former caravan quartermaster who tests equipment and keeps careful repair records. She is blunt with inexperienced buyers but not unkind.

- **Current concern:** One of her two regular caravan suppliers has begun sending lower-quality climbing gear while charging the old price. Mara has not yet decided whether the fault lies with the maker or an intermediary.
- **Useful connection:** She can assess practical travel gear and knows caravan routes, schedules, and common hazards.
- **Pressure point:** She needs reliable replacement stock before the next large caravan season.

### Orsik Feld — Orsik Ironworks
Orsik is a skilled, unsentimental smith who prefers repeat contracts to showy commissions. He treats apprentices firmly but pays them on time.

- **Current concern:** Charcoal costs have risen after a poor burn season in the western uplands. He is considering a price increase on custom work while keeping common tools competitive.
- **Useful connection:** He knows local metalworkers, builders, and the Artificers' Hall's practical standards.
- **Pressure point:** He needs a more reliable charcoal supplier, not a patron who wants to control his shop.

### Miraq Ashwhisk — Tallowmere Alchemical Supply
Miraq is observant, technically curious, and exact about measurements. She is accustomed to surface customers underestimating her, but does not assume every awkward interaction is malicious. She prefers clear terms and documented orders.

- **Current concern:** A supplier recently delivered a batch of glass vessels with inconsistent wall thickness. Miraq rejected part of the shipment and is negotiating a replacement rather than quietly passing defective goods to customers.
- **Useful connection:** She knows local sources for ordinary alchemical supplies and can identify some specialist suppliers, though she does not know every rare component in the region.
- **Pressure point:** She wants dependable glassware and a larger workroom eventually, but will not take on debt that gives a lender control over her business.
- **Personal boundary:** She does not want to be treated as the ratfolk PC's representative, mentor, or automatic ally. A relationship must be earned.

### Ilyne Marris — The Brass Scale
Ilyne is discreet, analytical, and careful about what she promises. She is not a secret banker with unlimited funds; her business depends on reputation and the trust of several larger partners.

- **Current concern:** A merchant client has missed a repayment date on a secured loan. Ilyne holds collateral, but liquidating it would damage a relationship she may need later.
- **Useful connection:** She can arrange introductions, assess commercial credibility, and explain how a transaction is likely to be perceived by established merchants.
- **Pressure point:** She values clients who keep written agreements and dislikes being asked to guarantee another person's honesty.

### Nessa Tor — Gull & Anchor Chandlery
Nessa is direct, sociable when she chooses, and exceptionally good at remembering which captains pay their bills. She has no patience for romantic stories about the sea; she knows the cost of a damaged mast.

- **Current concern:** A shipwright's yard owes her for a large consignment of rope and canvas. The yard claims a delayed shipowner has not paid. Nessa believes the yard is solvent but wants a payment schedule.
- **Useful connection:** She knows ship movements, repair demand, and which vessels are likely to need supplies before departure.
- **Pressure point:** She needs the debt paid without driving a useful customer out of business.

### Hesta Pellor — Three Grain Bakery
Hesta is energetic, proud of her craft, and protective of her apprentices. She remembers who buys the last white loaf and who complains about the price after eating it.

- **Current concern:** One mill agent has offered a cheaper flour contract, but the grain quality is inconsistent. Hesta suspects the savings would be lost in spoiled batches.
- **Useful connection:** She hears neighborhood news early and supplies food to several businesses.
- **Pressure point:** She wants stable flour prices and predictable orders, not a monopoly over the city's bread trade.

### Ollo Venn — Salt & Root Provisioners
Ollo is courteous, calculating, and more comfortable with large ledgers than small talk. He is not related to Mara Venn; the shared surname is coincidental.

- **Current concern:** He has bid aggressively for a ship-provisioning contract and may have priced it too low. If he wins, he will need dependable deliveries and tight waste control.
- **Useful connection:** He can assemble provisions in bulk and knows the seasonal availability of preserved foods.
- **Pressure point:** He needs the contract to be profitable, but cannot easily withdraw his offer without harming his reputation.

### Brunna Keld — Hearth & Hammer Armoury
Brunna is a dwarf armorer whose work is precise and whose patience for missed deadlines is short. She takes pride in supplying Greywatch but dislikes being treated as a mere extension of the garrison.

- **Current concern:** Her contract requires a batch of repairs before the next formal inspection. A private client has offered a lucrative commission that would compete for the same skilled hours.
- **Useful connection:** She understands armor fitting, repair, and the practical maintenance of a garrison's equipment.
- **Pressure point:** She needs more trained hands, but will not put an apprentice on work beyond their competence.

### Jorren Vale — Redoubt Arms
Jorren is a patient weaponsmith with a craftsman's pride and a cautious approach to credit. His sister Tessa is more outgoing and often handles first contact with customers.

- **Current concern:** He and Brunna disagree over whether the garrison should standardize on a narrower range of replacement weapons. Their disagreement is about cost, maintenance, and readiness, not personal hatred.
- **Useful connection:** He can commission uncommon mundane weapons and advise on quality and balance.
- **Pressure point:** He wants a larger workshop, but expansion would require either a loan or a partner.

## Commercial Disputes and Obligations

These disputes are real but limited. None requires the PCs to intervene.

1. **The delayed shipyard account:** A local repair yard owes Nessa Tor 46 gp for rope, canvas, and fittings. The yard claims payment is due from a shipowner whose vessel remains laid up. Nessa has agreed to wait ten more days before seeking formal collection. The shipyard's solvency is not yet in question.
2. **The secured loan:** Ilyne Marris holds collateral against a 120 gp loan to a small merchant partnership. The payment is twelve days overdue. The collateral is worth enough to cover the principal if sold promptly, but a forced sale would likely damage the partnership and Ilyne's reputation among local traders.
3. **The flour contract:** Hesta Pellor and Ollo Venn are both negotiating with the same two mill agents for preferred access to a portion of the next flour shipment. Their needs differ: Hesta wants consistent baking flour; Ollo wants volume and price certainty for provision contracts.
4. **The garrison schedule:** Brunna's standing contract takes priority over private armor commissions. She has not breached any agreement, but private customers sometimes resent waiting. Jorren's shop receives some overflow weapon work, which has caused quiet grumbling among other smiths.
5. **The defective glassware:** Miraq rejected part of a recent shipment. The supplier, a glass factor named Pellis Arno, insists the goods were within ordinary tolerances. Miraq has retained samples and wants a neutral craftsperson to inspect them before deciding whether to end the relationship.
6. **The charcoal increase:** Orsik's current charcoal supplier, a western upland factor named Deren Holt, has raised prices. Deren cites a poor burn season and transport costs. Orsik suspects the increase is partly opportunistic but has no proof.
7. **The caravan equipment complaint:** Mara Venn is quietly collecting examples of inferior climbing gear from a wholesaler called Rusk & Fen. She has not accused them publicly because she cannot yet prove deliberate substitution.

## How These Can Reach the PCs

Possible approaches include:
- Offer a fair supply contract or provide a competing source.
- Help verify a disputed shipment or inspect goods.
- Purchase a debt, provide a loan, or negotiate a repayment arrangement.
- Take on a delivery, escort, or procurement job.
- Offer a workshop partnership, investment, or equipment commission.
- Use a merchant's dispute to build trust with one party, while accepting that the other party may remember the choice.

These are opportunities, not prewritten quests. The PCs may ignore every one of them. If they do become involved, apply the same standards of evidence, negotiation, and consequence to all sides.

## GM Consistency Notes

- A debt is not proof of criminality. A supplier dispute is not automatically fraud.
- Do not make all merchants members of one faction or secret cabal.
- Each party has a plausible account of its own position. Determine objective truth before running a scene where it matters.
- Do not change listed shop prices retroactively because the PCs have money or become influential.
- If PCs make a binding agreement, record the terms, dates, collateral, and consequences.
- Keep Pathfinder crafting rules intact. Commercial access to materials or services is a setting question; it does not create extra universal item-creation prerequisites.
