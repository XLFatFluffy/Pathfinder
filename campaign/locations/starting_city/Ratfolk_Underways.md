# Tallowmere: The Ratfolk and the Underways

**Status:** Established decentralized community framework; initial networks and named contacts are documented in [Ratfolk Community Networks and NPCs](Ratfolk_Community_Networks_and_NPCs.md). Neighborhood names, population, and detailed settlement geography remain open.

## Foundational Principle
The ratfolk beneath Tallowmere are a community, not a criminal trope, hidden army, or unified faction. They have families, elders, children, tradespeople, craftspeople, merchants, healers, religious practices, arguments, aspirations, and ordinary problems.

Ratfolk are naturally subterranean and maintain extensive but incomplete networks beneath the wider world. Tallowmere's Underways connect older foundations, cellars, drainage channels, disused storage spaces, natural cavities, and purpose-built passages. Some routes are familiar and maintained; others are unstable, flooded, sealed, occupied, or unknown.

## Settlement Pattern
The Underways are connected neighborhoods rather than one central kingdom. Clusters form around shared wells, workshops, market chambers, defensible halls, or access routes. Some ratfolk live entirely below ground; others maintain surface work, shops, and relationships. The surface term "Underways" is an umbrella label. Ratfolk use their own names for halls, routes, and neighborhoods.

## Livelihoods
Ratfolk residents may be tinkerers, repairers, toolmakers, salvage specialists, couriers, guides, dyers, leatherworkers, tailors, herbalists, cooks, brewers, traders, masons, diggers, maintenance workers, or craftspeople. These are opportunities shaped by environment and community networks, not racial destiny. No trade is exclusive to ratfolk.

## Leadership
There is no single ratfolk monarch beneath Tallowmere. Neighborhoods rely on respected elders, household heads, work associations, and practical leaders. Some matters are decided by consensus; others by whoever controls needed resources or has earned trust.

A loose gathering of neighborhood representatives may coordinate water access, route safety, surface negotiations, and defense. Its authority is limited and depends on cooperation. Not every resident accepts its decisions. This decentralized pattern reflects the wider ratfolk network: extensive, connected in places, fragmented in others, and difficult for surface authorities to understand.

## Relationship with the Surface
Prejudice against ratfolk is widespread but not universal. Discrimination may affect housing, employment, market access, testimony, public safety, and enforcement. The Denvor government has no comprehensive equality or exclusion policy; local practice varies by official, neighborhood, guild, and circumstance. Some ratfolk hold recognized leases or commercial arrangements; others operate informally because landlords or officials refuse to deal with them.

The city benefits from ratfolk labor, trade, repairs, and knowledge while often denying them equal standing. This contradiction creates tension and potential for political change.

## Internal Differences
Ratfolk disagree about how to respond to persecution. Some favor negotiation and durable relationships; others prioritize self-sufficiency and minimizing surface contact. Some younger residents are openly angry; some elders fear retaliation against vulnerable households. Merchants may prioritize customer access over political protest. Families disagree about whether children should work or study on the surface. These are personal and political differences, not moral categories. No position represents all ratfolk.

## Underground Environment
Ordinary hazards include unstable masonry, flooding, poor air, vermin, contaminated water, hidden drops, abandoned wells, and routes that become impassable. Some deeper passages connect to the wider subterranean network, but not all are known or safe. Dangerous creatures or supernatural threats may exist in neglected depths, but ratfolk are not defined by living under constant siege.

## Campaign Relevance
The ratfolk PC's goal of ending persecution can connect to local conditions without making the whole community depend on him. He may find allies, critics, competing strategies, old grievances, and residents who do not want to become symbols of his cause. The community also has goals beyond his mission: safe water, secure homes, trade access, family disputes, maintenance, religious observances, and relations with other settlements.

## Open Details
Neighborhood names, local representatives, population, shared meeting place, surface access points, customs, religious life, and connections to the wider network remain to be developed.

The initial Cistern Keepers, Open Stall Petition, Underway Couriers, and Quiet Hearths are informal networks rather than formal factions.
