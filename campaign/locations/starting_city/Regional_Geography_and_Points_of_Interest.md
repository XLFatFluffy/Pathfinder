# Tallowmere Regional Geography, Routes, and Points of Interest

**Status:** Working regional geography established; exact cartographic placement and surveyed distances remain provisional.  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-04

This document turns the surrounding settlements into a coherent travel region. It establishes the broad shape of the land, the relationship between river, shore, roads, and settlements, and a first set of recognizable natural and built landmarks. It is a regional play map in text form, not a final scale map.

## 1. The Shape of the Region

Tallowmere stands on the northwestern shore of the central inland sea. Its harbor occupies a sheltered inlet where the River Vale reaches the sea. Inland, the river drains the lower Western Uplands and the farm country between the uplands and the Northfields.

The immediate region is best understood as a broad, inhabited basin opening toward the inland sea:

- **North:** gently rolling Northfields, with farms, orchards, pasture, hedgerows, and scattered hamlets.
- **Northwest:** the River Vale, its mill settlements, flood meadows, and the road continuing inland toward Fordmere.
- **West:** wooded lower hills and managed woodland, becoming rougher toward the Western Uplands.
- **West-southwest:** exposed stone ridges and quarry country around Stonehollow.
- **East and southeast:** the inland-sea shore, with low bluffs, coves, fishing landings, and salt-working places.
- **South:** a gradually rising overland route toward Harthbridge and the fragmented southern jurisdictions.

The terrain near Tallowmere is not mountainous. The Western Uplands rise gradually from wooded hills into rougher ridges and broken high ground. The region has enough open farmland and navigable water to support regular trade, but travel becomes slower away from maintained roads and river landings.

## 2. River Vale and Its Watershed

The River Vale is the principal inland route serving Tallowmere. It descends from the western uplands, passes through the lower farm country, and bends toward the city before entering the harbor.

### Main course
- The upper river rises in several streams in the Western Uplands.
- These streams join in a wooded valley west of Brackenholt.
- The main river then runs generally east and southeast through the River Vale.
- Millhaven lies on a reliable mill reach northwest of Tallowmere.
- Fordmere lies farther upstream, where the channel narrows and the banks provide a dependable crossing.
- Below Millhaven the river broadens, slows, and divides around low islands and gravel bars before reaching Tallowmere's harbor.

The river is navigable by suitable barges between Tallowmere and the lower reaches near Millhaven in ordinary conditions. Upstream travel toward Fordmere is possible for smaller, lightly loaded craft but is slower and more dependent on water level, towpaths, and local pilots. It is not a deep-water ship channel.

### Tributaries and watercourses
- **Mill Brook:** a smaller stream entering the Vale near Millhaven. It supplies water to farmsteads and contributes to the mill's flow.
- **Ash Run:** a seasonal watercourse draining the Northfields toward the main river. It can flood low fields after prolonged rain.
- **Blackroot Burn:** a fast, rocky stream descending from the western woodland. It is useful for small workshops and woodland settlements but unreliable in dry weather.
- **Stonewash:** a runoff channel draining the Stonehollow quarry slopes. Quarry owners are expected to keep spoil and silt from blocking its lower course; enforcement is inconsistent.

### Flood behavior
Spring melt and prolonged autumn rain can raise the River Vale. Flooding usually affects low meadows, riverside stores, and the approaches to minor crossings before threatening Tallowmere's main defenses. The city has embankments and drainage works in its lower quarters, but these require maintenance. Floodwater can leave silt, damage roads, contaminate shallow wells, and interrupt barge schedules.

## 3. Woodland, Hills, and Open Country

### The Brackenwood
The Brackenwood is the managed woodland west of Tallowmere and around Brackenholt. It is a patchwork of coppice, mature stands, clearings, charcoal burns, and older unmanaged pockets. It supplies fuel, poles, rough timber, game, and gathered plants.

The wood is worked rather than pristine. Woodland rights are divided among households and customary users under Denvor oversight. Some older paths are poorly marked, especially where charcoal burners shift their work sites.

### The Greyback Ridges
The Greyback Ridges are the low, stony hills southwest of the city, rising toward the Western Uplands. Stonehollow's quarries occupy the accessible eastern faces. Beyond the worked slopes, the ridges become rougher and less regularly traveled.

The ridges contain small caves, abandoned prospecting cuts, and old charcoal clearings. These are ordinary features of a worked landscape; no particular ruin, monster lair, or treasure site is established by their existence.

### The Northfields
The Northfields are open agricultural country north and northeast of Tallowmere. Low rises separate fields and drainage hollows. Hedgerows, ditches, small copses, orchards, and farm lanes break up the view. Ashcombe Manor stands on a modest rise near the northern road, where it can observe traffic without dominating the whole region.

### The Eastern Shore
The coast southeast of Tallowmere is irregular but comparatively accessible. Low bluffs alternate with narrow beaches, shingle coves, and small landing places. Saltwick occupies one of the better working coves. Farther along the shore, isolated farmsteads and fishing huts are scattered between the larger settlements.

The shore is exposed to sudden storms crossing the inland sea. Wrecks and damaged boats are part of local memory, but no single famous wreck is established yet.

## 4. Roads and Established Routes

Road quality varies with maintenance, season, and traffic. The principal routes are passable to carts in ordinary weather, though some stretches become rutted or muddy. Side lanes and woodland tracks are less reliable.

### North Road
From Tallowmere's North Gate, the North Road crosses the outer farms and continues toward Ashcombe Manor and the Northfields. Branch lanes serve orchards, grazing farms, and scattered hamlets. The route eventually joins the wider Northern Road network.

### River Vale Road
The River Vale Road leaves Tallowmere by the western approach and follows the river upstream. It serves riverside farms and Millhaven before continuing toward Fordmere. It is the principal land route for grain, charcoal, and timber moving between the inland settlements and Tallowmere.

### Western Track
A rougher branch from the West Road leads toward Brackenholt and the managed woodland. It is suitable for carts in dry conditions, but heavy rain can make it difficult. Smaller tracks continue toward charcoal clearings and the lower uplands.

### Quarry Road
The Quarry Road branches southwest from the western approach and climbs toward Stonehollow and the Greyback Ridges. It is built to carry stone carts, but its steepest stretches require careful handling of heavy loads. Road damage is a recurring expense for the quarry partnerships.

### Eastern Shore Road
The Eastern Shore Road leaves the city by the coastal approach and runs southeast to Saltwick. It connects smaller landings and coastal farms. It is generally easier to travel in fair weather than during storms or high surf.

### King's Road / South Road
The King's Road leaves Tallowmere through the South Gate and continues south as the South Road toward Harthbridge. Beyond Harthbridge it enters a more fragmented landscape of minor lordships, toll rights, and independent jurisdictions. The King's Road name does not mean the Crown directly maintains every mile.

### River navigation
The river itself links Tallowmere to Millhaven and, with more difficulty, Fordmere. Barges generally carry grain, timber, stone, charcoal, and bulk supplies downstream. Upstream movement requires towing, poling, or favorable current and is slower. River pilots and ferrymen know local hazards that are not obvious to strangers.

## 5. Crossings and Route Control

The following crossings are established regional features:

- **Old Stone Bridge:** the principal permanent river crossing within Tallowmere.
- **Mill Bridge:** a smaller crossing upstream from the city, serving mills and local farms.
- **Fordmere Crossing:** a chartered crossing farther upriver, administered by House Merewick. Its ferry supplements the crossing when water level or repairs make the fixed crossing unsafe.
- **Millhaven Mill Weir:** a working mill structure, not a public road bridge. A narrow service crossing exists for mill workers and local traffic; it is not suitable for heavy wagons.
- **Ash Run culverts:** several small road culverts in the Northfields. They are vulnerable to blockage and seasonal washout.

There is no established permanent bridge across the River Vale between Tallowmere and Fordmere beyond the crossings listed above. Do not assume every farm lane has a safe ford.

## 6. Regional Landmarks and Places

These places provide orientation and local color. Their presence does not automatically imply a hidden adventure, treasure, or supernatural event.

### The Three Mile Stones
A line of old boundary and distance stones along the River Vale Road west of Tallowmere. Some have been moved or reused in field walls. The stones are useful for local directions, though their original measurements are no longer reliable.

### Bellwater Pool
A deep, slow pool on the River Vale upstream of Millhaven. Bargemen use it as a waiting and turning place when water conditions permit. Children fish there; local stories warn against swimming after heavy rain because the current below the surface can be deceptive.

### The Old Burn
An abandoned charcoal-burning clearing on the edge of the Brackenwood. It is occasionally reused by seasonal burners, but its old earth mounds and collapsed shelters make it a poor permanent camp.

### The Quarry Steps
A stone-cut track descending from the Three Faces at Stonehollow toward the Quarry Road. It is steep and worn smooth in places. Loaded carts use the lower road rather than the steps.

### Ashcombe Rise
The low hill on which Ashcombe Hall stands. The rise provides a view of the North Road and nearby fields, but not an unobstructed view of Tallowmere or the entire region.

### Merewick Yard
The open yard beside Fordmere's toll office, ferry landing, and inn. Carts queue here during busy periods, and merchants can exchange loads or arrange onward transport.

### Saltwick Beacon
A modest shore beacon maintained by Saltwick's boat-owning households. It is a navigational aid and storm warning point, not a fortified lighthouse. Its signal can be difficult to see in heavy rain or fog.

### The South Mile Cross
A weathered roadside cross on the South Road north of Harthbridge. Travelers leave small tokens or repair offerings there. Its religious dedication is not agreed upon locally and is not yet assigned to a specific deity.

## 7. Travel Conditions and Approximate Journey Times

Distances and times below are planning values. They assume ordinary weather, a maintained route, and no unusual delays. Carts, heavy loads, poor weather, injury, toll queues, and river conditions can increase travel time.

| Route | Approximate distance | Typical travel |
|---|---:|---|
| Tallowmere–Millhaven | 9 miles | 3–4 hours on foot; shorter downstream by barge |
| Tallowmere–Ashcombe Manor | 11 miles | 3–5 hours on foot |
| Tallowmere–Saltwick | 12 miles | 3–5 hours on foot |
| Tallowmere–Brackenholt | 14 miles | 4–6 hours on foot; slower with loaded carts |
| Tallowmere–Stonehollow | 17 miles | 5–7 hours on foot; most of a day with stone carts |
| Tallowmere–Fordmere | 19 river miles by route | Most of a day on foot; river travel varies substantially |
| Tallowmere–Harthbridge | 27 miles | A full day for a fit traveler; often longer with wagons |

A normal overland travel day is generally around 18–24 miles on a decent road. These shorter regional journeys may still consume much of a day once loading, gates, tolls, weather, and local business are included.

## 8. Political and Practical Geography

The map does not follow one continuous noble boundary:
- House Denvor's authority is strongest around Tallowmere, Millhaven, Brackenholt, Stonehollow, and Ashcombe Manor.
- Fordmere is governed through the Merewick crossing charter, recognized within the wider Kharadorn sphere.
- Saltwick's shore charter and local freeholders hold customary rights; its superior jurisdiction remains deliberately unresolved.
- Harthbridge is governed under House Velleran's town charter and is outside direct Denvor administration.
- House Kharadorn's strategic influence is expressed through charter recognition, regional defense, and wider political authority, not daily administration of every settlement.

These are overlapping jurisdictions rather than a clean set of modern borders. Roads, river access, toll rights, and customary land use can matter as much as a line on a map.

## 9. Regional Points of Interest: Status

**Established:** River Vale, Mill Brook, Ash Run, Blackroot Burn, Stonewash, Brackenwood, Greyback Ridges, Northfields, Eastern Shore, named roads, listed crossings, and named landmarks in this document.

**Not established:** exact coordinates, precise property boundaries, detailed stream flow rates, exact bridge construction dates, a complete network of minor tracks, specific ruins or monster lairs, and any hidden treasure or secret organization. Those should be added deliberately when they become useful to the campaign.

The illustration agrees with the established framework in the following respects:
- Tallowmere occupies the northwestern shore of the Inland Sea at the mouth of the River Vale.
- The River Vale approaches from the west and northwest, passing Millhaven before reaching Tallowmere.
- Fordmere lies farther upstream.
- Brackenwood and Brackenholt lie west of the city; the Western Uplands rise farther west.
- Stonehollow and the Greyback Ridges lie southwest.
- The Northfields and Ashcombe Manor lie north and northeast.
- Saltwick lies along the southeastern shore.
- Harthbridge lies south, along the route toward the southern jurisdictions.

The map is a **relative-layout illustration, not a surveyed scale map**. Its printed 0–100 mile scale bar is inconsistent with the established journey distances in Section 7. For example, the visual separation between Tallowmere and Millhaven is far greater than the documented 9-mile route. Do not use the printed scale bar to calculate distances, travel times, or settlement coordinates.

Until a properly scaled regional map is produced:

2. Use the written route distances and travel assumptions in Section 7 for play.
3. Do not infer exact borders, coordinates, road lengths, river widths, or property boundaries from the illustration.
