# Tallowmere: Surrounding Settlements and Landholders

**Status:** Initial regional settlements and local landholders established; exact map positions, boundaries, and travel times remain approximate.  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-09

This document gives Tallowmere a usable hinterland. The settlements are distinct communities with their own livelihoods and local concerns, not interchangeable dots placed around the city. Their populations are working estimates, not census figures.

Distances are approximate road or river-route distances from Tallowmere's principal market gate. They are intended for planning and can be adjusted when the regional map is finalized. Do not treat them as surveyed measurements.

## 1. Regional Arrangement

Tallowmere's immediate hinterland is organized around five practical directions:

- **North and northeast:** the Northfields, grain farms, orchards, grazing, and the main northern road.
- **West and northwest:** the River Vale, mills, river landings, and the road into the Western Uplands.
- **West and southwest:** woodland, charcoal burners, quarries, and smaller mining works.
- **East and southeast:** the inland-sea shore, fishing villages, salt works, and small coastal landings.
- **South:** the South Road, minor lordships, toll rights, and a more fragmented political landscape.

House Denvor's lordship includes Tallowmere and a modest surrounding territory. It does not own every nearby farm, village, or landing. Several settlements lie under tenant arrangements, neighboring charters, or independent local authority.

## 2. Settlements within the Denvor Lordship

### Millhaven
**Type:** Grain village and mill center  
**Approximate population:** 650–800  
**Approximate distance:** 9 miles northwest, following the River Vale road  
**Authority:** Denvor lordship; local reeve appointed under the lordship

Millhaven is one of the principal grain collection points supplying Tallowmere. Its watermill serves nearby farms, and its market day draws carters, smallholders, mill workers, and merchants from the river valley.

**Economy**
- Grain, flour, oats, and seasonal legumes.
- Milling and grain storage.
- Livestock, dairy, and small-scale brewing.
- Cart repair and basic river transport.

**Local concerns**
- Mill rights and water access are valuable and occasionally disputed.
- Upstream maintenance affects the mill's water supply.
- Harvests are uneven between farms; a good village total can conceal hardship among tenant households.
- Millers dislike being blamed for price rises caused by poor harvests or transport delays.

**Notable place:** The Long Mill, a large watermill with a public grain measure and adjoining storage sheds. Its current machinery is maintained by successive millwrights; it is not an ancient wonder.

**Local authority:** Reeve Harlan Tebb handles routine dues and disputes. He is practical, protective of the mill's privileges, and careful not to antagonize Greywatch.

### Brackenholt
**Type:** Woodland and charcoal settlement  
**Approximate population:** 300–400  
**Approximate distance:** 14 miles west of Tallowmere, beyond the lower river farms  
**Authority:** Denvor lordship, with customary woodland rights shared among several households

Brackenholt supplies charcoal, firewood, poles, and rough timber from the managed Brackenwood and its customary work areas. Its people work in scattered clearings and small clusters of homes rather than one dense village center. The Deep Bracken lies beyond the managed woodland: occasional charcoal camps and limited production are recorded there, but do not assume they provide the regular city supply or that Brackenholt's customary rights extend into the outer forest.

**Economy**
- Charcoal burning and firewood.
- Coppiced wood, poles, and rough timber.
- Small-scale hunting, trapping, and woodland gathering.
- Carting and seasonal labor in Tallowmere.

**Local concerns**
- Woodland rights and cutting limits are frequent sources of disagreement.
- Charcoal prices rise when weather disrupts burning or cart traffic.
- Some older woodland tracks are poorly maintained and can become dangerous after heavy rain.
- The settlement needs buyers but resents merchants who dictate prices from the city.

**Notable place:** The Burners' Green, a broad clearing used for seasonal charcoal stacking, exchange, and local gatherings.

**Local authority:** No single village reeve commands all the burners. A Denvor forester records cutting rights and reports serious disputes; household elders and work crews settle many ordinary matters themselves.

The **Brackenwood Charcoal Fellowship** represents several working families. Mara Bellwood is its elected spokesman. The Fellowship seeks to preserve customary woodland access, predictable cutting limits, fair charcoal prices, and protection against outside buyers monopolizing local production. It does not own the woodland as a unified body, replace Denvor oversight, or speak for every household with customary rights.

For a practical GM guide to charcoal production, woodland labor, customary access, the Burners' Green, and the Brackenwood/Deep Bracken distinction, see [Brackenholt: Woodland Work, Charcoal, and Customary Rights](Brackenholt_Woodland_Work_and_Charcoal.md).

### Stonehollow
**Type:** Quarry village  
**Approximate population:** 250–350  
**Approximate distance:** 17 miles west-southwest, on the lower edge of the Western Uplands  
**Authority:** Denvor-chartered quarry rights held by several local families

Stonehollow supplies building stone, paving blocks, rubble, and dressed stone for Tallowmere's repairs and construction. Its quarries are useful rather than extraordinary: no famous gemstone vein or legendary metal deposit is established.

**Economy**
- Limestone and common building stone.
- Quarrying, dressing, and loading.
- Masonry and cart repair.
- Seasonal work on city walls, roads, bridges, and private buildings.

**Local concerns**
- Quarry faces require careful management; accidents are a real occupational risk.
- Heavy carts damage road surfaces and compete with ordinary traffic.
- Quarry families argue over extraction rights and the cost of maintaining access roads.
- Demand from Tallowmere fluctuates with construction and defensive works.

**Notable place:** The Three Faces, three adjoining quarry cuts worked by different family partnerships.

**Local authority:** The quarry charter defines extraction rights and dues. Disputes over boundaries are heard by a Denvor-appointed clerk with the right of appeal to the lordship's court.

### Ashcombe Manor
**Type:** Small fortified manor and agricultural holding  
**Approximate population:** 90–130 within the manor hamlet and household lands  
**Approximate distance:** 11 miles north of Tallowmere  
**Authority:** House Avenholt, a minor house holding its land under House Denvor

Ashcombe Manor is held by House Avenholt, a minor human noble family with a small but productive estate. The manor includes a modest fortified residence, tenant farms, pasture, and a small watch post near the northern road.

**House Avenholt**
- **Arms:** A white ash tree above a black plough on a muted red field.
- **Words:** “Rooted Through Winter.”
- **Standing:** A locally established minor house. It is wealthy enough to maintain its manor and household retainers but has no continent-wide political weight and no automatic Imperial Council seat.
- **Current head:** Lady Ysabet Avenholt, a widowed landholder in her early fifties.
- **Heir:** Ser Corvin Avenholt, her adult son, capable but impatient with the limits of the family's resources.

The Avenholts owe customary dues and military service to House Denvor. They retain local authority over their tenants and estate affairs, subject to the terms of their tenure.

**Economy**
- Grain, sheep, and dairy.
- Timber rights on a small managed woodlot.
- Rental income from tenant holdings.
- Occasional horse breeding for local use.

**Local concerns**
- Corvin wants to improve the manor's defenses and increase the family's standing.
- Ysabet is cautious about new expenditure and values reliable relations with Greywatch.
- Tenant farmers have complained about drainage and boundary maintenance. The complaints are specific and unresolved, not proof of general cruelty.
- The manor's small garrison is adequate for local security, not a force capable of challenging Tallowmere.

**Notable place:** Ashcombe Hall, a stone manor house with a small curtain wall, granary, stable yard, and chapel room.

## 3. Settlements under Other Local Authorities

### Fordmere
**Type:** River crossing, ferry, inn, and toll point  
**Approximate population:** 180–250, excluding seasonal travelers  
**Approximate distance:** 19 miles upriver from Tallowmere  
**Authority:** A separate hereditary crossing charter held by the Merewick family; charter recognized within Kharadorn's wider sphere

Fordmere sits where the river narrows and a reliable crossing has long served inland traffic. A ferry operates when water levels or repairs make the old crossing unsafe. An inn, stables, a small market yard, and a toll office serve travelers.

**Economy**
- Crossing fees under the charter.
- Ferry service and cart handling.
- Inn, stabling, and repairs.
- Grain, livestock, and timber exchange.

**Local concerns**
- The Merewicks want the crossing to remain profitable and oppose any proposal to build a competing bridge nearby.
- River floods can suspend the crossing and damage the approach roads.
- Carters complain when tolls are collected during delays or when the ferry is unavailable.
- The crossing is useful enough that merchants tolerate the fees, but not without complaint.

**Local authority:** The Merewick family administers the crossing charter. The current holder, Sir Davren Merewick, is a careful toll-keeper with a reputation for strict accounting.

Fordmere is not a Denvor possession. The Denvors negotiate access and road coordination with the Merewicks when city traffic depends on the crossing.

### Saltwick
**Type:** Fishing and net-making village on the Eastern Shore  
**Approximate population:** 400–550  
**Approximate distance:** 12 miles southeast by coastal road  
**Authority:** Local freeholders and a small hereditary shore-charter; the precise superior jurisdiction remains to be confirmed

Saltwick is a working fishing community with small boats, net lofts, smokehouses, and seasonal salt handling. It supplies fresh and preserved fish to Tallowmere and other shore settlements.

**Economy**
- Fishing and fish preservation.
- Net making, rope repair, and small-boat maintenance.
- Salted and smoked fish.
- Limited salt gathering and exchange with larger producers.

**Local concerns**
- Fishing rights and seasonal grounds are disputed with crews from other ports.
- Storms and damaged boats can quickly reduce household income.
- Saltwick wants better road maintenance but resists city control over its local landing.
- Some families fish independently; others work under merchants who advance gear and take a share of the catch.

**Notable place:** The Net Hall, a long communal building used for net repair, gear storage, and meetings of boat-owning households.

**Local authority:** A shore reeve chosen by the charter-holding families handles routine disputes. The village has no large noble household or substantial garrison.

### Harthbridge
**Type:** Small market town and commercial rival  
**Approximate population:** 1,600–2,000  
**Approximate distance:** 27 miles south by the South Road  
**Authority:** House Velleran, a minor human noble house holding a town charter confirmed by a regional authority within Kharadorn's sphere

Harthbridge is a modest inland market town at a junction between the South Road and two local routes. It has no harbor comparable to Tallowmere, but its market and toll privileges make it a useful alternative for merchants moving goods by land.

**Economy**
- Weekly livestock and produce market.
- Warehousing for inland merchants.
- Leather, wool, grain, and cart trade.
- Inns, smiths, and wagon repair.
- Toll income from the road junction and market privileges.

**House Velleran**
- **Arms:** A gold key crossed with a silver sheaf on a dark blue field.
- **Words:** “The Road Remembers.”
- **Standing:** A minor house with a compact territory and useful charter rights. It is locally influential but does not rival a Great House.
- **Current head:** Lord Beric Velleran.
- **Heir:** Lady Maud Velleran, his eldest child, who manages much of the town's commercial correspondence.

The Vellerans are not enemies of House Denvor. They compete with Tallowmere for some inland trade and cooperate when road security, grain movement, or regional disputes require it.

**Local concerns**
- Harthbridge merchants want more cargo to be sold at its market rather than carried straight to Tallowmere.
- Tallowmere merchants accuse some Harthbridge factors of using toll privileges to disadvantage outside traders.
- The Vellerans are divided over whether to invest in better roads or preserve the town's existing toll income.
- Lord Beric is cautious; Maud is more commercially ambitious.

**Notable place:** The South Market, a broad market yard with a covered livestock ring, weigh house, and charter notice board.

Harthbridge gives the region a nearby rival without making it a major power. Its competition is commercial and jurisdictional, not an established feud.

## 5. Travel and Movement

These are initial planning estimates for ordinary conditions, not exact map measurements. A party on foot with normal gear generally covers 18–24 miles per day on decent roads, less in poor weather, difficult terrain, or while avoiding danger. Mounted travel may be faster but depends on road quality, mounts, and rest. River travel depends on current, vessel, cargo, and direction.

| Route | Approximate distance | Typical journey |
|---|---:|---|
| Tallowmere to Millhaven | 9 miles | Half-day walk; shorter by barge downstream |
| Tallowmere to Brackenholt | 14 miles | Half-day to most of a day |
| Tallowmere to Stonehollow | 17 miles | Most of a day with loaded carts |
| Tallowmere to Ashcombe Manor | 11 miles | Half-day |
| Tallowmere to Fordmere | 19 river miles | Most of a day by road; river travel varies |
| Tallowmere to Saltwick | 12 miles | Half-day by coastal road |
| Tallowmere to Harthbridge | 27 miles | One full day on foot, longer with wagons |

The regional map may alter these distances. The roads are not uniformly maintained, and a route that is short on a map may be slow because of river crossings, steep grades, tolls, or poor surfaces.

## 6. Trade and Supply Relationships

The settlements depend on one another:
- Millhaven and the Northfields supply grain and flour to Tallowmere, but also sell to nearby villages and Harthbridge.
- Brackenholt supplies charcoal and timber to Tallowmere's smiths, kitchens, builders, and ship repairers. Deren Holt is one charcoal factor active in this trade; his exact home and woodland contracts remain unspecified.
- Stonehollow supplies common building stone, including material used in repairs to roads, walls, quays, and private buildings.
- Ashcombe Manor supplies agricultural goods and modest mounted service when required by House Denvor.
- Fordmere handles inland traffic that does not need to reach the harbor directly.
- Saltwick supplies fish and maritime craft goods, while depending on Tallowmere for larger markets and some manufactured goods.
- Harthbridge competes for land-carried trade but also buys and sells goods that pass through Tallowmere.

No settlement is economically exclusive. A bad harvest, road closure, price dispute, or local feud can redirect trade, but merchants and households generally seek alternatives before accepting ruin.

## 7. Local Security and Disputes

Security is uneven:
- Denvor patrols and household retainers offer the strongest protection near Tallowmere and along the most important roads.
- Village watches can respond to local thefts and disturbances but are not professional field forces.
- The Merewicks maintain a small guard at Fordmere to protect the crossing and collect tolls.
- Saltwick relies on boat crews, household cooperation, and occasional help from passing patrols.
- Harthbridge has its own town watch and the Velleran household's retainers.

Common regional problems include livestock theft, disputed boundaries, damaged roads, illegal timber cutting, toll disagreements, cargo loss, and occasional predatory creatures in less settled areas. Serious supernatural incidents are possible but not routine.

No major bandit army, rebel force, or secret cult is established in the immediate region.

## 8. Adventure and Campaign Use

These places are available for the PCs to engage with, but none exists solely as a quest location.

- **Millhaven:** Grain contracts, mill disputes, harvest problems, water rights, and the price of flour.
- **Brackenholt:** Charcoal supply, woodland rights, missing workers, dangerous old tracks, and resource gathering.
- **Stonehollow:** Quarry commissions, worksite accidents, stone supply, and disputes over extraction boundaries.
- **Ashcombe Manor:** Denvor vassal politics, estate management, family ambition, and tenant grievances.
- **Fordmere:** Toll disputes, river crossings, missing cargo, and merchant route choices.
- **Saltwick:** Fishing rights, wreck salvage, boat repairs, and shore trade.
- **Harthbridge:** Commercial rivalry, market access, road tolls, and relationships with another minor house.

The PCs can trade with, work for, compete against, assist, or ignore any of these communities. Their actions should have proportionate consequences.

## 10. House Relland's Holding

**Status:** The house's existence, minor-house standing, general holding, and allegiance are established. The specific merchant incident is quarantined provisional PC-backstory material.

House Relland is a minor human noble family owing allegiance to House Velleran. Its modest manor and tenant lands lie along the South Road within the broader Velleran-chartered territory south of Tallowmere. The exact surveyed location and boundary remain provisional pending the finished regional map. The house is locally established but has no continent-wide influence and no Imperial Council seat.

That incident, its participants, concealment, evidence trail, and PC connections are **not current campaign facts unless the players confirm the relevant character concepts**.
