# Residential Life and Housing in Tallowmere

**Status: ESTABLISHED CITY FRAMEWORK — 4 November 2026**

Tallowmere's housing reflects a working port of approximately 18,000 people, not a grand capital. Wealth, crowding, sanitation, noise, safety, and access to services vary sharply by street. District descriptions are patterns, not rigid class or ancestry boundaries. Exact addresses, household counts, property deeds, and individual leases remain open unless stated here.

## General Housing Pattern

Most residents rent rooms, share family dwellings, live above shops or workshops, occupy small houses in narrow lanes, or reside in employer-provided rooms. A household may include relatives, apprentices, servants, and boarders who are not all related.

Buildings are commonly built in stages. Older stone or timber ground floors may support upper stories of timber framing, plaster, tile, or slate. Wealthier homes have enclosed courtyards, private wells or cisterns, storage, and servants' rooms. Poorer homes rely on shared wells, public pumps, communal privies, and neighborhood arrangements. The wider construction, materials, and maintenance framework is covered in [Building, Construction, and Maintenance](../../world/Building_Construction_and_Maintenance.md).

A room's price depends on size, condition, location, furnishings, water access, lease length, and whether meals or services are included. There is no single citywide rent schedule. Landlords and tenants negotiate within local custom and the realities of supply.

## Residential Character by District

### Greywatch Rise
The most exclusive residential ground lies near Greywatch. House Denvor's household occupies the fortified seat; senior officers, retainers, and a handful of wealthy families occupy nearby substantial houses. Some properties are held by old local families and rarely come onto the market. Security is visible, streets are better maintained, and access may be questioned near the castle precinct. Ordinary rooms are uncommon.

### High Ward
The High Ward contains the broadest range of comfortable housing: merchant townhouses, prosperous craftsmen's homes, legal and professional households, respectable lodging houses, and smaller dwellings tucked behind main streets. The best houses have courtyards and servants' quarters; modest homes may have two or three rooms over a shop. It is quiet by port standards, though carts and bells still carry.

### Old Quays
Housing is crowded and tied to maritime work. Sailors rent beds or rooms between voyages; chandlers, shipwrights, and warehouse workers live above or behind businesses. Some old merchant families retain substantial waterfront homes, but many ground floors have become commercial space. Noise, smoke, damp, and fire risk are persistent concerns.

### New Quays
The New Quays have newer buildings, workers' lodging houses, store-yard cottages, and larger merchant properties near better-served streets. Some housing is temporary or seasonal, especially for sailors and laborers. Development is uneven: a new counting-house may stand beside a rough lodging yard or unfinished row of dwellings.

### Market Ward
Housing is mixed and expensive relative to its size. Shopkeepers often live above their premises. Apprentices and servants sleep in attic rooms or shared quarters. Inns and lodging houses serve visiting merchants, carriers, and people between homes. The busiest streets are noisy from dawn; quieter residential lanes sit behind the market frontage.

### Rivergate
Rivergate houses many mill workers, bargemen, carters, smiths, laborers, and small tradespeople. Homes are practical and often shared by extended families. Lower streets can become muddy or flood-prone after heavy rain. Some workers live in rooms provided by mills or employers, but such arrangements can end when work ends.

### Lantern Ward
The Lantern Ward has modest family homes, religious hostels, charitable accommodation, healers' residences, and small properties owned by temple or hospice institutions. Some residents receive temporary shelter through charitable houses, but capacity is limited and assistance is not guaranteed. The district is not uniformly wealthy or safe from hardship.

### The Underways
The Underways are a patchwork of inhabited chambers, converted cellars, old foundations, service passages, natural cavities, water chambers, and connected routes beneath parts of Tallowmere. Ratfolk households occupy some spaces; others are used for storage, workshops, access, or remain uninhabited.

Some homes are dry, carefully maintained, and passed between generations. Others are cramped, damp, poorly ventilated, or vulnerable to seepage. Conditions vary with the age and structure of the space, household resources, and access to water and repair materials. The community maintains shared passages and water infrastructure where it can, but it does not control every tunnel beneath the city.

Ratfolk also live on the surface, including in rented rooms, shops, workshops, and mixed-ancestry households. Not every ratfolk person lives below, and no single representative speaks for all Underways residents. Discrimination can make leases harder to secure or more expensive, but individual landlords and neighbors vary.

## Rent, Lodging, and Household Costs

The figures below are local working estimates for ordinary long-term residential arrangements, not a mandatory city tariff. Condition and location can move a price substantially.

| Accommodation | Typical local rent |
|---|---:|
| Shared bunk or bed in a crowded lodging house, per month | 5 sp–1 gp |
| Small private room in a modest lodging house, per month | 1–2 gp |
| Basic room in a respectable house, per month | 2–4 gp |
| Small rented dwelling of two or three rooms, per month | 4–8 gp |
| Comfortable townhouse or large family dwelling, per month | 10–20 gp |
| Substantial High Ward residence, per month | 20–40 gp or more |
| Servant's or apprentice's room as part of employment | Often included in terms; otherwise negotiated |

Short-term inn rates are separate from monthly residential rent and should use Pathfinder 1e lodging and services prices as the mechanical baseline. Food, stabling, laundry, fuel, and private security are not automatically included in rent.

For broad household living standards, use Pathfinder 1e cost-of-living rules as the baseline rather than treating rent as the whole cost of living. Poor, common, good, wealthy, and extravagant lifestyles represent bundles of ordinary expenses and social conditions. Do not add lifestyle costs mechanically on top of rent if that double-counts housing and meals. State what a quoted price includes whenever the distinction matters.

Most ordinary landlords request rent monthly. A deposit or advance payment may be negotiated, especially for furnished rooms or tenants without local references, but it is not a universal legal requirement. Leases may be oral or written depending on landlord, property, and term. Disputes over deposits, repairs, and notice are handled through custom, contract, and local courts.

## Ownership and Property

Property ownership is concentrated among noble families, wealthy merchants, religious institutions, established craft families, and people who have accumulated savings over generations. Small houses and workshops can be owned by ordinary tradespeople, but many residents rent.

A property may carry obligations beyond its purchase price: repairs, roof and wall maintenance, drainage, fire precautions, access rights, and lawful local dues. Buying a building does not automatically grant a market stall, guild standing, harbor berth, or permission to obstruct a public lane. Commercial premises and rents are documented separately in Commercial_Property_and_Premises.md.

House Denvor does not own every building in Tallowmere. The lordship has rights and revenues established by charter and custom, but private, guild, religious, and communal property exists. The precise boundaries of manorial rights and property jurisdiction remain open.

## Sanitation, Water, and Fire

Public wells, pumps, cisterns, drains, privies, and waste collection are unevenly distributed. Wealthier streets can maintain private facilities; crowded lanes rely on shared arrangements. The city has drainage and old water infrastructure, but it is not uniformly clean or modern. Flooding and seepage are local problems, especially around Rivergate and lower waterfront streets.

Fire is a serious risk in dense districts. Kitchens, braziers, workshops, warehouses, and upper floors built close together create danger. Residents keep buckets, ladders, and basic tools where they can; civic officers enforce some fire rules, with varying consistency. Recent warehouse fires and near-misses have increased concern, but do not establish that residential fires are part of a conspiracy.

## Water, Waste, and Street Cleanliness

The city's established water and waste infrastructure, district differences, flood risks, and unresolved civic responsibilities are detailed in [Water, Waste, and Street Cleanliness in Tallowmere](Water_Waste_and_Street_Cleanliness.md). The city has public wells, pumps, the Old Cistern, drains, privies, and uneven refuse arrangements; do not assume a universal sewer or collection service.

## Household Pressures and Social Life

- Rent and wages: a missed week of work can threaten a laborer's ability to pay. Landlords may accept delay from reliable tenants, but not all can afford to.
- Seasonal work: sailors, carriers, and casual dockworkers may take short leases or share rooms while work is available.
- Apprentices and servants: some receive lodging as part of employment. Quality varies and can become a point of dispute.
- Extended families: multi-generational households are common, especially among craftspeople and long-established residents.
- New arrivals: migrants and sailors rely on inns, lodging houses, relatives, employers, or charitable institutions until they find stable work.
- Prejudice: ratfolk and other marginalized residents may face unequal rents, refusals, harassment, or pressure to use less desirable entrances. These are individual and institutional behaviors, not universal rules.
- Mutual aid: neighbors share childcare, tools, meals, repairs, and information. Such ties can be dependable, but they also create obligations and disagreements.

## Residential Households and Neighbors

These residents add ordinary lives and relationships to the city. They are not assigned quest-givers. Their circumstances can change as the campaign develops.

### The Rill Household — Rivergate
Jessa Rill, quay labor forewoman already recorded in Workforce_and_Daily_Life.md, lives with her mother Marta, younger brother Tavin, and Tavin's daughter Elsie in a narrow rented house off a cart lane. Jessa's income is steadier than most casual laborers', but supports several people. Marta keeps the accounts and is cautious about debt. Tavin works when his health permits and resents being treated as a dependent. They are close, but money and pride create regular arguments.

### The Pellor Household — Market Ward
Hesta Pellor, baker at Three Grain Bakery, shares a modest house with her husband Corren, an aging carter, and their adult niece Lysa, who helps at the bakery. Hesta is considering cheaper flour because costs are rising; Corren worries a bad batch will damage the family's reputation. Lysa wants to save enough to rent a room of her own. Their home is warm, crowded, and usually smells of bread.

### The Venn Household — Old Quays
Mara Venn, owner of the Salted Tack, rents the upper floor above a storehouse office. Her adult son Pate works aboard coastal vessels and is home irregularly. Mara's business and home share a narrow stair, and she keeps separate accounts for each. She has little patience for customers who confuse a fair price with a personal favor.

### The Moss Household — Lantern Ward
Edda Moss, midwife and practical healer already recorded in Workforce_and_Daily_Life.md, rents two rooms with a small rear garden. Her sister Nalia keeps the household and assists with laundry and deliveries. They sometimes provide a bed to a woman who needs to stay near the hospice before or after childbirth. This is informal help, not a hospital or guaranteed charitable service. Edda's work brings her into homes across several districts.

### The Low-Arch Household — The Underways
Kett-of-the-Low-Arch, masonry patcher and passage inspector already recorded in Workforce_and_Daily_Life.md, shares a dry chamber with partner Veska and two children, Pip and Rusk. Their home is near a maintained water channel and shared storage niche. Kett's work is valued, but payment for communal repairs can be irregular. Veska trades in repaired lamps and fittings. The family is proud of its home and wary of surface officials who assume underground residents have no recognized property or neighborhood customs.

### The Ashwhisk Rooms — Market Ward / Underways Access
Miraq Ashwhisk, proprietor of Tallowmere Alchemical Supply, rents two rooms above a narrow service lane and uses a small secured rear store for work materials. She has chosen a surface address for the shop and a practical connection to an Underways passage, rather than treating either location as her only home. Her living arrangements are private. She does not automatically host customers, employees, or acquaintances, and her professional relationship with the ratfolk PC must develop through play.

### The Reed Household — Rivergate
Pellan Reed, organizer in the River Carters' and Bargemen's Company, lives with his husband Orren and Orren's elderly aunt Sella in a well-kept rented dwelling near the river. Their income is respectable but tied to transport contracts. They sometimes host visiting boatmen for a meal, though rarely overnight. Pellan knows many people through work but does not consider every acquaintance a friend.

## GM Use: Residential Opportunities

The housing framework creates practical options without assigning the PCs a preferred path:
- Rent a room, take a townhouse, lease a workshop with living quarters, or eventually purchase property.
- House employees or apprentices through negotiated employment terms.
- Establish a public-facing business in a district where customers can find it.
- Build trust with neighbors through fair dealings, repairs, reliable payment, and local involvement.
- Encounter housing discrimination, rent disputes, overcrowding, fire hazards, or property claims as social circumstances rather than mandatory plot gates.
- Develop a property into a base, workshop, meeting place, or commercial concern if the PCs pursue it.

No residence grants automatic political status or guild membership. No landlord is required to accept a tenant, but refusal should arise from a plausible circumstance or preference, not a hidden rule designed to block the players.
