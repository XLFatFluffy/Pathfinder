# Tallowmere — Current Session One GM Packet

**Snapshot:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC  
**Visibility:** GM-ONLY  
**System:** Pathfinder First Edition  
**Status:** CURRENT GENERIC PREPARATION  
**Purpose:** Table-side preparation that does not assume party composition, backstory, route, or a required opening encounter.

## Locked

- Campaign begins in Tallowmere.
- Campaign date is 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC.
- Tallowmere is a walled port city on the northwestern shore of the central inland sea, where the River Vale reaches the sea.
- The city is a sandbox. No single quest or route is required.
- The world exists independently of the PCs.
- The Living World Engine v2 is GM-reviewed rather than autonomous.
- Six Great Houses, House Denvor, Tallowmere civic institutions, local factions, public destinations, current pressures, regional settlements, and established NPCs are available.

## Not locked until final sheets

Do not assume:

- party size;
- ancestry, class, level, alignment, or deity;
- prior PC relationships;
- arrival route;
- starting equipment, wealth, or special items;
- personal backstories;
- companion identity or statistics;
- a troll encounter;
- a Selwick wagon encounter;
- a required first quest;
- PC-specific secrets or political connections.

Earlier preparation containing these assumptions is legacy material.

## Session objective

The first session should establish:

1. who the PCs are;
2. what they want;
3. what they notice about Tallowmere;
4. one or two meaningful local relationships;
5. at least one player-driven problem or opportunity;
6. the first consequences that make the wider world feel responsive.

Do not force the campaign's larger mysteries into the opening.

## Opening options

### Approach to Tallowmere

Use established roads and surrounding region. A roadside complication can occur if appropriate. The prepared troll/Selwick encounter is an option, not a requirement.

### Harbor arrival

Use Tallowmere's port identity immediately. Shipping, harbor procedures, labor, merchants, travelers, and commercial interests provide immediate material without requiring a crisis.

### Start inside the city

Begin at an established public location and give the PCs freedom of movement. Useful locations include the Old Quays, Market Ward, Rivergate, Lantern Ward, or another established public place appropriate to the characters.

## Public destinations

- Blue Heron: respectable inn in the Old Quays.
- Crooked Oar: cheaper working tavern at the New Quays.
- Reeve's Hall: petitions, records, civic notices, and administration.
- Harbor Office: shipping declarations, berths, and harbor procedures.
- Bellmarket: public market, merchants, moneychanging, notices, and purchases.
- Warehouse Guild: major commercial institution.
- Lantern Ward: temples, hospice, healers, and burial grounds.
- Underways: decentralized ratfolk communities and routes; access depends on local knowledge and relationships.

## Current local pressures

- Harbor berth allocation.
- Warehouse fire precautions.
- Uneven but currently adequate Northfield harvest.
- Ratfolk market access.
- House Denvor priorities.
- Kharadorn harbor-defense and charter review.
- Skilled labor, credit, and supply pressure.
- Longmead boundary dispute between Houses Harrowden and Wrenford.

These are world conditions, not assignments.

## Information handling

Use the established Tallowmere rumor and information records.

For any player question:

1. decide whether the character would ordinarily know;
2. use local or specialist knowledge where appropriate;
3. call for a check only when the information is genuinely uncertain or specialized;
4. distinguish fact, rumor, inference, and GM-only truth;
5. never reveal GM-only truth merely because the players ask.

## Encounter rule

Do not finalize encounter difficulty until the final character sheets are available.

For each prepared encounter, check party size, level, action economy, healing, damage, defenses, consumables, unusual abilities, companion participation, terrain, retreat options, and consequences of failure.

The troll encounter remains a prepared option. It is not current canon and must be rebuilt against the final party before use.

## Player-choice response rules

- Help an actor: change the relationship only as far as the actual help justifies.
- Harm or expose an actor: determine what the actor can reasonably learn and do.
- Ignore an issue: let established actors continue their priorities. Do not automatically escalate to catastrophe.
- Investigate: provide evidence appropriate to witnesses, records, location, and successful checks.
- Travel: use established roads, rivers, settlements, and ecology. Do not invent settlements to fill map space.
- Create a new relationship: record it after the scene or session.
- Change the local balance of power: create a Living World review proposal rather than silently rewriting canon.
- Ask about an unresolved mystery: keep it unresolved unless the GM deliberately answers it.

## Session tracking

Capture short notes for:

1. date and approximate time;
2. location;
3. PCs present;
4. NPCs/factions involved;
5. what actually happened;
6. information learned or revealed;
7. promises, debts, injuries, losses, or gains;
8. relationship changes;
9. new quests, issues, or projects;
10. player-visible versus GM-only information.

Do not interrupt play to complete every field.

## End-of-session

1. Record the session summary.
2. Record meaningful events.
3. Update affected NPCs, factions, houses, quests, locations, and relationships.
4. Advance the campaign date if appropriate.
5. Open a Living World review if meaningful time passed or the PCs materially changed a situation.
6. Create only supported consequence proposals.
7. Approve, reject, defer, or record each proposal.
8. Leave unresolved questions unresolved.
