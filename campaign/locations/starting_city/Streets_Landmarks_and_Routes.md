# Streets, Landmarks, and Routes of Tallowmere

**Status: ESTABLISHED STREET-LEVEL FRAMEWORK — 4 November 2026**

This record gives Tallowmere recognizable streets, public spaces, bridges, and civic landmarks. It establishes a usable mental map without pretending that a scaled city survey already exists. Relative connections are canon; exact lengths, compass bearings within individual districts, property frontages, and final map geometry remain provisional.

The city occupies a rise above a sheltered harbor and lower river terraces. The older walled city forms an irregular crescent around the inner harbor. Streets in the older quarters follow the slope and older property lines; newer streets near the New Quays and outer defenses are broader and more regular.

## Principal Street Network

### The Harbor Road
The Harbor Road follows the inner waterfront from the Old Quays toward the newer wharves. It is busy with porters, carts, ship crews, chandlers, and harbor officials. It is not one uniformly broad road: older sections narrow around warehouses and slipways, while newer sections widen near cargo yards. Access can be restricted when cargo is being moved or harbor defenses are closed.

### Market Street
Market Street runs inland from the Old Quays through the heart of Market Ward, passing the principal market square and the guildhall quarter before dividing toward the North Gate and West Gate approaches. It is one of the city's busiest pedestrian routes. Shopfronts, stalls, inns, moneychangers, public notices, and food sellers line its central stretch.

### The King's Road
The King's Road enters through the South Gate and climbs toward Market Ward, where it joins the inland road network. It is the principal overland route toward the Central Crownlands and Valedorn. Within Tallowmere it is used by merchants, messengers, officials, and travelers. Its name is traditional and does not imply that the Crown directly administers the road inside the city.

### West Road
The West Road connects West Gate to Rivergate and the road toward the Western Uplands. Timber, stone, charcoal, grain, and livestock enter by this approach. Cart traffic is heavy in the morning and can make the lower Rivergate streets difficult to cross.

### North Road
The North Road leads from North Gate toward the Northfields and the Northern Road. Farmers, drovers, mill agents, and seasonal laborers use it. A small open space outside the gate serves as an informal meeting point for carts waiting to enter.

### Lantern Way
Lantern Way climbs from Market Ward toward the Lantern Ward, passing hospice grounds, shrines, and burial access. It is quieter than Market Street, though processions and funerals can make it busy. The street is maintained more carefully near the principal religious institutions.

### Greywatch Stair and Rise Road
Two principal approaches climb toward Greywatch Rise. Greywatch Stair is the older, steep pedestrian route, with broad stone steps, landings, and narrow side passages. Rise Road is the wider route used by mounted visitors, carts serving the noble precinct, and official processions. Both are overlooked from the fortified heights.

### Quay Lane
Quay Lane runs behind the older waterfront frontage, linking warehouses, chandlers, shipwright yards, and taverns in Old Quays. It is crowded with handcarts and workers. During loading hours, some stretches are effectively impassable to ordinary traffic.

### New Wharf Road
New Wharf Road serves the expanded harbor frontage and bulk-cargo yards. It is wider and straighter than the old quay lanes, with space for wagons to turn. It connects to the outer defensive works and the South Gate approaches.

### River Road
River Road follows the city side of the navigable river through Rivergate. Mills, ferry landings, store yards, and barge offices face onto it. Its lower sections are muddy and vulnerable to high water; sections may be closed after heavy rain or flooding.

## Squares, Markets, and Gathering Places

### The Bellmarket
The principal public market square in Market Ward. Produce, household goods, cloth, tools, and imported wares are sold here on ordinary market days. Licensed stalls occupy marked spaces; temporary sellers gather along the approaches when space permits. Public proclamations are sometimes read from the market steps. It is a place of commerce, gossip, arguments, and civic announcements.

### Weighers' Court
A paved commercial court off Market Street, near the principal market weighing facilities and cargo clerks. Merchants settle disputes over measures, damaged goods, and declared weights here before matters reach the magistrates. It is not a court of law despite the name.

### Ropewalk Green
A long, narrow open strip near the Old Quays, used for rope work when weather permits and as a place for crews to gather. It is not a manicured park. At busy times, coils of rope, drying lines, carts, and temporary work benches occupy much of the space.

### River Steps
A stepped landing on the Rivergate waterfront where people can reach small boats and ferries. Bargemen, children, fish sellers, and laborers use it throughout the day. The steps become slippery in wet weather and may be partly submerged when the river rises. Its exact spatial relationship to the managed Ferry Steps crossing is not established; do not assume they are the same landing.

### Lantern Close
A modest open court near the hospice and principal temple grounds. It provides space for public alms distribution, religious observances, and gatherings connected with the charitable institutions. It is not exclusively religious property; residents also use it as a quiet meeting place.

### Heron Court
A small paved forecourt below the approach to Greywatch, marked by a weathered stone carving of a heron standing in reeds. It is used for formal arrivals, petitions, and household announcements. The heraldic image is associated with House Denvor, but the court remains a public approach rather than a private garden.

## Bridges, Crossings, and Harbor Controls

### The Old Stone Bridge
The principal permanent crossing over the river within the city approaches. It connects Rivergate's two banks and carries the West Road traffic toward the older market streets. Its central span is old and narrow; heavy wagons may be regulated or required to cross in sequence. The bridge is a strategic point during flood, fire, and defense emergencies.

### Mill Bridge
A smaller crossing upstream from the main bridge, near the working mills. It is used by mill workers, local residents, and carts serving the western riverbank. Its capacity is limited and it is not a preferred route for large commercial caravans.

### The Ferry Steps
A managed ferry crossing serves people and small loads where the river channel widens. The Ferryman's Steps is the family business operating the crossing and small-cargo punt services; 'The Ferry Steps' names the crossing, while 'The Ferryman's Steps' names the business. Schedules depend on weather, river level, and demand. This is a service, not a second permanent bridge.

### Harbor Chain and Boom
The harbor entrance can be restricted by a chain, boom, or other barrier operated from fortified waterfront positions. Its use is an emergency measure, not a routine toll gate. Closing the harbor disrupts trade and requires coordination between harbor officials and the garrison.

### Quay Gates
Controlled openings separate parts of the waterfront and cargo yards from the city streets. They allow officials to regulate access to bonded cargo, ship repair areas, and defensive positions. Their hours and procedures vary by location and security conditions; do not assume every quay is locked at night.

## Civic and Notable Landmarks

### Greywatch
The fortified ancestral seat of House Denvor dominates the city's highest ground. Its towers and walls overlook the harbor and much of the old city. It contains the lord's household, administrative rooms, guard quarters, stores, and defensive works. Greywatch is both a residence and a working seat of local government.

### The Reeve's Hall
A civic building near Market Ward where the Town Reeve's clerks maintain records, receive petitions, post notices, and handle routine administrative business. It is not the chief court; formal judicial matters fall under the magistrate's jurisdiction.

### The Harbor Office
A fortified administrative building close to the Old Quays, housing the Harbor Master's clerks, vessel declarations, berth records, and port notices. Captains and merchants visit to settle harbor business. Its proximity to the waterfront makes it a practical place to encounter shipping disputes and official announcements.

### The Weigh House
A stone building beside Weighers' Court containing public scales and measure standards. Official weighing can be witnessed by merchants and clerks. The building is a point of friction when cargo owners dispute a tally or claim that goods were damaged during handling.

### The Watch House
The principal city-watch station is located near the junction of Market Street and the road toward North Gate. Smaller watch posts and gate rooms operate elsewhere. The Watch House contains holding cells for short-term detention, duty rooms, and a small records office; it is not a prison for long sentences.

### The Garrison Yard
A secured yard within Greywatch Rise used for drills, equipment storage, and mustering. It is distinct from the city watch's ordinary patrol stations. Its access is controlled by the Castellan and household command.

### The Lantern Hospice
A charitable institution in Lantern Ward providing care and temporary assistance to the sick, injured, poor, and travelers in need, within its means. It is associated with Sister Alwen's administration. Its capacity is finite and it is not a substitute for a large hospital.

### The Sailors' Shrine
A modest but well-used shrine near the Old Quays, maintained by Brother Caldus. Sailors leave tokens, seek blessings, and remember those lost on the water. It serves many people regardless of their home district.

### The Old Cistern
A covered public water cistern in the older city, maintained through civic labor and neighborhood arrangements. It is a useful landmark and an important resource during dry periods or fires. It is not connected to every Underways water chamber; subterranean water systems are locally varied.

### The Denvor Granary
A substantial storehouse near the Rivergate approach, maintained under the lordship's oversight for public provisioning and obligations under the local charter. Its existence does not mean the city has unlimited emergency reserves. Stock levels, grain quality, and release authority are matters of practical politics.

## District Connections at a Glance

- Greywatch Rise overlooks the harbor; Greywatch Stair and Rise Road connect it to the lower city.
- High Ward lies below and southeast of Greywatch and connects toward Lantern Ward by Lantern Way.
- Old Quays follow the older inner waterfront and connect inland through Quay Lane and Market Street.
- New Quays extend along the outer harbor frontage, served by New Wharf Road.
- Market Ward links the waterfront to the North Gate, West Gate, and South Gate approaches.
- Rivergate follows the lower river and connects to the West Road, River Road, Old Stone Bridge, Mill Bridge, and River Steps.
- Lantern Ward sits on the landward side of the High Ward and is reached from Market Ward by Lantern Way.
- The Underways extend beneath parts of several districts; no surface street gives a complete picture of their routes.

## Navigation and Table Use

For play at the table, use the following broad travel assumptions until a scaled map exists:
- Crossing one compact district on foot usually takes 5–10 minutes.
- Walking between adjacent districts usually takes 5–15 minutes depending on slope, crowding, and route.
- Crossing from one side of the city to the other generally takes 25–40 minutes on foot in ordinary conditions.
- A cart takes longer on crowded quay lanes, steep approaches, and market days.
- A mounted traveler gains little time inside the crowded old city and may be required to dismount in narrow streets or at busy crossings.
- Travel through the Underways depends on local route knowledge, access, water levels, obstructions, and permission; it is not automatically faster or safer than surface travel.

These are practical table estimates, not measured distances. Change them when the final map establishes actual geometry or when a scene introduces unusual crowding, weather, injury, pursuit, or obstruction.

**Naming clarification:** Rivergate is the district; River Gate is the controlled river-access point. The River Steps public landing and the managed Ferry Steps crossing are separately named features, but their precise relative positions remain unestablished. Do not merge them or place them on a map without supporting artwork or a later GM decision.
