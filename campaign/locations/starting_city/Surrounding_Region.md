# Tallowmere: Surrounding Region

**Status:** Regional framework established; settlement names, distances, and precise borders remain provisional.

## The City's Hinterland
Tallowmere depends on a mixed hinterland of farms, villages, river communities, woodland, upland quarries, and smaller market settlements. The city is not self-sufficient. Its prosperity depends on keeping roads and river traffic open and maintaining workable relations with the surrounding landholders.

House Denvor's lordship includes a modest rural territory around the city. Beyond it lie neighboring holdings and jurisdictions connected to House Kharadorn's wider sphere. The exact boundaries and vassal relationships remain to be mapped.

## Immediate Surroundings

### The River Vale
The river west of Tallowmere carries grain, timber, stone, livestock, and people from inland settlements. Its banks support mills, ferries, fishing hamlets, and small landing places. Seasonal flooding can enrich fields but also damage bridges, roads, and riverside stores.

### The Northfields
A patchwork of farms, orchards, grazing land, and villages supplies grain, vegetables, dairy, wool, and livestock. The harvest is important to Tallowmere's stability. Poor weather, disease, banditry, or disputes over dues can quickly affect city prices.

### The Western Uplands
Woodland, quarries, hills, and smaller mining operations supply timber, building stone, charcoal, and metals in varying quantities. The uplands are less densely settled. Old tracks, abandoned workings, isolated shrines, and stretches of difficult terrain create opportunities and risks.

### The Eastern Shore
Fishing villages, small ports, salt and net-making communities, and coastal farms connect Tallowmere to the wider inland-sea economy. Some settlements are within Denvor influence; others answer to different lords or chartered authorities.

### The South Road
The road south enters a more fragmented political landscape of minor lordships, principalities, independent towns, and competing toll rights. Merchants value the route but must account for local law, fees, and security.

## Local Settlements
The surrounding region should include several different kinds of settlement rather than a ring of interchangeable villages:
- A grain-producing village with a market and mill.
- A timber and charcoal settlement near the western woodland.
- A quarry village supplying stone to Tallowmere.
- A river crossing with a ferry, inn, and toll rights.
- A fishing and net-making village on the eastern shore.
- A small fortified manor belonging to a Denvor vassal or tenant.
- A market town outside direct Denvor jurisdiction, creating a nearby commercial rival.

Names, exact populations, ownership, and distances are deliberately unassigned until the regional map is developed.

## Roads and Security
The major roads are maintained unevenly. Near Tallowmere, patrols and local obligations offer some protection. Farther away, travelers depend on village watches, household retainers, merchant escorts, and local agreements.

Threats should vary by location and season: robbery, disputed tolls, predatory creatures, dangerous weather, damaged bridges, old ruins, political violence, and occasional supernatural dangers. The wilderness is dangerous, but not every mile outside the walls is a monster-infested death trap.

## Regional Power
House Denvor is locally important but not a Great House. Its influence is strongest in the immediate hinterland and weakest where neighboring charters, Kharadorn rights, or independent authorities intervene. House Kharadorn's strategic influence is real, but it does not mean every nearby village is directly administered by dwarves.

## Adventure and Sandbox Use
The hinterland offers supply routes, contracts, local disputes, ruins, isolated communities, escort work, resource gathering, and relationships with smaller landholders. It also gives the city a reason to care about events beyond its walls. A harvest failure, bridge collapse, road attack, or change of lord can affect Tallowmere's prices and politics.
