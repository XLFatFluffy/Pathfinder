> This is the operational guide to the campaign's starting city and immediate hinterland. It is deliberately separate from the continental Campaign Bible. Use it at the table; consult the linked source files for full shop inventories, legal text, NPC biographies, maps, and encounter mechanics.

## 1. Tallowmere at a glance

Tallowmere is a walled port of approximately 18,000 people on the northwestern shore of the central inland sea, where the navigable River Vale reaches the sea through a sheltered estuary. It is a working harbor and regional trade center, not the continental capital and not the largest port on the inland sea.

The old city forms an irregular crescent around the natural harbor. It occupies a defensible rise above the water and lower river terraces. Greywatch stands on the highest ground, overlooking the harbor mouth and principal quays. Older streets follow the slope and property lines; newer commercial streets are broader and more regular.

Tallowmere should feel prosperous but strained, busy but not cosmopolitan beyond recognition, politically layered, and full of people with ordinary work and competing interests. It is a sandbox, not a quest hub that waits for the PCs.

## 2. Authority, charter, and government

### House Denvor

House Denvor is an established human minor house, locally powerful but with little continental influence. Its seat is Greywatch. Its arms are a black heron standing in shallow silver water upon a deep green field; its words are *Where Waters Gather*.

Lord Oswin Denvor governs Tallowmere and a modest surrounding lordship under a hereditary charter confirmed by House Kharadorn after the Great Rebellion. The charter requires annual tribute, maintenance of harbor defenses, and provision of ships, supplies, or transport when lawfully called upon. Kharadorn retains strategic rights concerning the harbor and inland-sea defense but does not administer ordinary city life.

Denvor powers include appointing civic officers, enforcing customary law, collecting established dues, calling the local levy, granting or confirming certain privileges, and presiding over local courts or appointing judges. These powers are constrained by the charter, custom, Kharadorn obligations, and the need for civic cooperation.

### The Chartered Council

The Chartered Council is an advisory and negotiating body for principal commercial, property, and civic interests. It is not an elected parliament, independent legislature, or sovereign city government. Lord Oswin retains final local authority under the charter. Its influence is substantial because the city depends on cooperation: ignoring every recommendation can delay ships, repairs, investment, labor, and credit.

Do not treat a Council recommendation as automatically binding or a Council disagreement as a constitutional crisis.

### Principal civic officers

- **Reeve Marrec Holt:** Human town reeve. Methodical, restrained, protective of records; handles petitions, notices, dues, market and road administration. Overworked and wary of recordkeeping errors becoming scandal.
- **Harbor Master Ysella Vane:** Human; direct, experienced, hard to intimidate. Wants safe traffic, fair quay allocation, reliable fees, and a strong harbor reputation. Knows the difference between a legitimate delay and a manufactured excuse.
- **Chief Magistrate Odran Pell:** Half-elf; patient, observant, exacting about testimony. Values orderly hearings but recognizes that wealthy litigants can afford delay.
- **Captain Brinna Salk:** Human captain of the Watch; practical and blunt, protective of patrols, concerned with coverage, discipline, staffing, and preventable violence.
- **Castellan Hadrik Voss:** Dwarf veteran professional contracted to serve the Denvors. Values preparation, stores, clear command, and maintained defenses. His ancestry does not explain his loyalty.

## 3. House Denvor household

- **Lord Oswin Denvor:** In his early fifties. Measured, observant, cautious, and committed to negotiated solutions and predictable obligations. Believes a lord's first duty is to leave the city more secure than he found it. Caution can preserve stability but delay necessary action.
- **Lady Catrin Denvor:** Politically capable; maintains relationships with charitable institutions, established families, and visiting dignitaries. Less patient than Oswin with officials who mistake courtesy for weakness.
- **Ser Edwyn Denvor:** Eldest child and recognized heir, mid-twenties. Has inspected harbor, garrison, and surrounding estates. Favors greater investment in defense and infrastructure and is more willing than Oswin to test boundaries. Not a reckless warmonger.
- **Lady Anwen Denvor:** Younger child, early twenties. Perceptive and socially skilled; interested in commercial and civic networks beyond noble ceremony. Has genuine relationships outside the nobility. Informal influence; ambitions not publicly understood.

The family is not a unified bloc. Oswin and Edwyn differ over pace and spending; Catrin mediates but has her own priorities; Anwen sees opportunities and problems others miss. There is no established succession crisis.

## 4. Districts and city character

- **Greywatch Rise:** Castle, Denvor household, garrison, and local authority on the high ground.
- **The High Ward:** Wealthier homes, legal offices, established temples, and professional services.
- **The Old Quays:** Historic waterfront, shipwrights, chandlers, inns, older warehouses, and maritime housing. Irregular, crowded, damp, and exposed to fire and flood.
- **The New Quays:** Expanded docks, bulk cargo, larger warehouses, provisioning yards, and newer merchant concerns.
- **Market Ward:** Bellmarket, guildhalls, shops, moneychangers, scribes, and public notices. Higher rents and established traders influence stall allocation.
- **Rivergate:** Bargemen, mills, smiths, workshops, cart yards, laborers, and river commerce. Noise, smoke, and heavy traffic are normal.
- **Lantern Ward:** Temples, hospice, healers, burial grounds, and quiet residential streets.
- **The Underways:** Varied subterranean spaces: cellars, storage, wells, drainage, ratfolk homes, workshops, and routes. Not a single city beneath the city, not a unified faction, and not automatically accessible. A surface lease does not grant rights to underground passages.

## 6. Factions, guilds, and local power

### Political and civic actors
- **House Denvor:** Local ruling house; cautious, negotiated, preserves autonomy and city stability.
- **House Avenholt:** Denvor vassal; Lady Ysabet Avenholt, heir Ser Corvin; agricultural estate at Ashcombe Hall. Ysabet is cautious with expenditure; Corvin favors defense and status.
- **House Velleran:** Lord Beric Velleran, heir Lady Maud; seat Harthbridge. Protects market and toll privileges; commercial rival and practical counterpart to Denvor, not an established enemy.
- **House Merewick:** Holds separate Fordmere crossing/ferry/toll charter. Its precise charter boundaries and relationship details remain in the regional holdings register.
- **House Relland:** Minor human house under Velleran; Lady Mereth Relland, heir Ser Corren; South Road manor. Connected to the ratfolk PC's family tragedy. Do not expand the family's culpability beyond established facts.
- **Kharadorn Charter House:** Strategic charter presence and oversight; not daily city administration.
- **Chartered Council:** Advisory and negotiating body, not sovereign government.

## 8. Temples and religion

No faith is Tallowmere's official religion; no temple governs the city or automatically holds a Council seat.

- **The Open Ledger — Abadar:** High Ward, near legal/commercial offices; most prominent formal temple. Prelate Damas Orrel. Contracts, honest measures, civic order, numeracy, voluntary mediation.
- **The Quiet Gate — Pharasma:** Lantern Ward, adjoining older burial ground. Keeper Lethia Sorn. Funerary rites, burial records, care for dying, remembrance.
- **The Tideward Shrine — Gozreh:** Old Quays above waterfront. Brother Caldus. Sailors' vows, river blessings, weather observances, memorials.
- **The House of the Dawn — Sarenrae:** Lantern Ward, near Lantern Hospice. Charity, refuge, reconciliation, healing support.
- Household and neighborhood shrines exist for other Pathfinder gods. Desna, Erastil, Torag, Calistria, Nethys, and others may be represented in smaller or private observances.

A temple does not automatically provide magical healing. Any spellcasting service needs an actual Pathfinder First Edition NPC build, appropriate level, spells, components/foci, availability, and price.

## 11. Rumor truth register — GM-only

Use `world_engine/Tallowmere_Rumor_Truth_Register.md` for full source and knowledge boundaries. Key established truths:

1. **Cargo tally alteration:** True but limited. A junior clerk at one private warehouse altered entries to conceal breakage/spoilage and avoid a wage deduction. Not a theft ring; no established link to fires, Grey Net, or Bent Seal. Clerk and warehouse not yet identified.
2. **Riverside property buying:** Distorted. Two separate partnerships inquired about leases/storage; no consortium or large-block purchase.
3. **Sealed passages beneath old quay:** True but misunderstood. A dockworker used a limited unsafe service access to retrieve tools/items. Not a smuggling tunnel or Underways route.
4. **Kharadorn envoy with expanded demands:** False as of opening. Rumor exaggerates the legitimate charter review.
5. **Lost ship sighting on eastern shore:** Unresolved. Similar vessel, delayed ship, false report, or missing vessel; do not decide without evidence.
6. **Minor landholder in debt:** True in general, identity unresolved. Do not assign to a named house without evidence.
7. **Fine ratfolk alchemical apparatus:** True, modestly. Miraq and another craftworker made a precision retort/condenser for a private customer. Not a magic item or new formula.
8. **Old inland-sea / Dragonstone charts:** True. A factor working for Seralyth Vaelorian's Western Pass commercial review purchased/commissioned old incomplete charts for commercial/historical research. Not a plan to seize Dragonstone and not evidence of the underground route.
9. **Warehouse fires deliberate:** Speculative; cause unknown.
10. **Noble magical services / other circulating claims:** Preserve the exact status in the register; do not convert rumor into fact without evidence.

A rumor's source, travel path, distortion, objective truth, and who has learned it are separate things.

## 12. Criminal and informal operators

Tallowmere has no unified thieves' guild, citywide criminal boss, or secret council. Illicit activity is fragmented and opportunistic.

- **The Grey Net:** Noll Tarrow, human, former tally clerk. Loose cargo-diversion and dockside theft arrangement; paperwork and misdirection preferred to violence. Limited scale; members may steal from one another.
- **The Bent Seal:** Edrik Vale, human copyist/contract clerk. Forged cargo marks, altered receipts, false ownership papers, commercial fraud. Not a master forger; careful examination can expose work.
- **The Quiet Account:** Hessa Quill, halfling former household steward. Debt collection, coercive lending, intimidation, private settlements. Not a bank and has no monopoly on lending.
- **Sella Vane:** Independent boat owner smuggling small quantities through minor quays and river landings; small crew, not a fleet.
- **Othric Pell:** Half-elf information broker; trades information but does not automatically know every secret.

Crime is specific. The Watch has honest officers, opportunists, and overwhelmed people. Corruption may occur, but it is not universal. Ratfolk are not collectively criminal and do not control all tunnels.

## 13. Player-character connections and protected local secrets

### House Relland and the ratfolk PC

House Relland is a minor human house under House Velleran, holding a modest manor and tenant holdings along the South Road. Current head: Lady Mereth Relland. Heir: Ser Corren Relland. Former head at the time of the incident: Lord Edvar Relland, deceased.

Approximately ten years before Session One, armed retainers wearing Relland colors stopped the ratfolk PC's travelling-merchant family, falsely claimed a road levy, and demanded the family's stock. The demand was unlawful extortion. The confrontation turned violent; the parents were murdered and the child was about to be killed when the vampire intervened. The attackers were led by Ser Deylan Marrick, an armsman supervising Relland road patrols who abused his authority.

**Continuity limits:**
- The record does not establish that Lady Mereth or the wider family personally ordered the murders.
- It does not absolve the household of responsibility for its retainers.
- House Relland does not know the child survived.
- Ser Corren's knowledge is incomplete.
- Do not make the incident a continent-wide conspiracy or implicate a Great House.
- The exact roadside location remains approximate.

### Vampire PC's identity

The vampire PC is the concealed surviving Crown Prince of the fallen dragon-riding dynasty. No local or continental actor is automatically aware of the identity or survival. House Relland, the Crown, Kharadorn, and intelligence actors do not receive knowledge without a plausible source, discovery, and causal chain. The PC's personal name remains the player's choice.

### Other secrets and unknowns

- The dwarven underground route to Dragonstone is GM-only and unknown to ordinary actors.
- The Underways are not a single map-complete tunnel system. Exact passages, household routes, and access rights are private and unresolved.
- No actor automatically knows a PC's identity, personal history, or secret.
- Do not assign every rumor to the PCs or connect unrelated local pressures.

## 14. Opening session and sandbox

### Opening setup

The planned first encounter is a troll attack on a Selwick & Company wagon on a suitable approach road within roughly half a day's travel of Tallowmere. Place it to match the PCs' actual route and the calibrated regional geography.

Cargo: finished wool cloth, sealed lamp oil, household metal fittings, and smaller crates of ordinary trade goods.

Crew:
- **Hobb Tern:** Senior carter; practical and cautious; knows the cargo account.
- **Lysa Fen:** Assistant carter and teamster; young but experienced; likely first survivor the PCs encounter.
- Two hired guards; names and exact fates are not fixed until encounter preparation.

The troll is a standard Pathfinder 1e CR 5 threat whose regeneration is overcome by fire or acid. It can be dangerous for two level-5 PCs. The companion's provisional CR 8 estimate changes the party's capability, but CR values are not simply added. Do not finalize balance until both character sheets and companion statistics are checked. Do not inflate monster statistics to counter a strong companion. Retreat, concealment, negotiation, or bypassing the creature remain valid.

### Outcomes and reward

If crew and cargo are largely saved, Tavren Selwick pays the full **30 gp** reward, provides a meal and one night's lodging at the **Blue Heron**, and gives a signed introduction to **Meret Sorn**. If cargo is damaged or lost, adjust the reward proportionally. If crew are lost, Selwick's response depends on what the PCs could reasonably have saved. If the PCs bypass or retreat, there is no automatic punishment; record the wagon's fate only after it is known.

The 30 gp is a conditional reward, not starting money. No shared stipend or special starting item has been assigned. Character sheets govern actual starting wealth and possessions.

### First-day destinations

- Blue Heron: rest, food, local conversation, low-pressure introduction.
- Reeve's Hall: petitions, notices, records, civic procedure.
- Harbor Office: shipping declarations, berths, harbor rules, commercial disputes.
- Bellmarket: purchases, public notices, moneychanging, ratfolk market issue.
- Warehouse Guild: Meret Sorn and commercial environment.
- Lantern Ward: religious, charitable, healing, and burial questions.
- Underways: access requires introductions, local relationships, and consent.

The opening encounter is not a mandatory quest chain. Once the PCs reach Tallowmere, they can seek work, build relationships, pursue personal ambitions, explore, leave, ignore local politics, or create their own projects.

## 15. Table-side response principles

- Let players choose where to go and whom to approach.
- Do not present all ten rumors at once. Let them surface through people and places.
- An NPC is not a quest dispenser. They have work, obligations, opinions, and limits.
- Use the NPC information network to decide who could plausibly know a fact.
- Shops have finite stock. Prices, credit, supply, and quality vary.
- Do not make every local issue a conspiracy or connect it to a PC.
- The PCs can ignore an issue. It changes only if time and actor capability create a plausible consequence.
- Record what the PCs actually learn, change, buy, damage, promise, or reveal.
- Keep GM truth separate from rumor and player knowledge.
- If PCs acquire property, a minor house, guild, or political office, model the obligations and reactions proportionately rather than instantly escalating to continental war.
