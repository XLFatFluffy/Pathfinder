# Tallowmere: Workforce and Daily Life

**Status:** Initial labor, district-population, and everyday-life framework established. Named workers are supporting NPCs, not an exhaustive census.

Tallowmere has an approximate population of 18,000. Its workforce reflects a port city whose fortunes depend on cargo movement, river traffic, food supply, ship repair, crafts, administration, and household services. The city is busy, socially unequal, and dependent on people whose names rarely appear in noble records.

## The Shape of the Population

Tallowmere's population is mixed by ancestry, wealth, occupation, and place of origin. Humans form the largest single population group, but dwarves, halflings, half-elves, half-orcs, elves, and ratfolk are visible parts of civic life. The Underways contain a substantial ratfolk community, while surface-dwelling ratfolk also work, trade, rent rooms, and maintain family connections across districts.

The city has no reliable modern census. Tax rolls, guild lists, household registers, harbor records, temple accounts, and the watch's rolls each count different people for different purposes. Seasonal sailors, itinerant laborers, servants, apprentices, and residents of informal housing are easily missed. Avoid treating any precise ancestry or occupational percentage as established fact.

## Work by District

### Greywatch Rise
Household servants, guards, grooms, cooks, clerks, armorers, messengers, stable hands, and contractors serving the Denvor household and garrison. Work is comparatively steady but access is controlled by household reputation and contracts. Military employment comes with discipline and obligations.

### High Ward
Household servants, tutors, scribes, legal clerks, shop assistants, laundresses, cooks, gardeners, private guards, and skilled domestic craftspeople. Employment can be stable, but wealthy households may demand discretion and personal references.

### Old Quays
Sailors between voyages, riggers, caulkers, chandlers' assistants, fishmongers, porters, net-menders, ship repair crews, tavern workers, and warehouse hands. Employment rises and falls with ship arrivals and repair schedules. Casual workers may wait at dawn for a foreman's call.

### New Quays
Bulk porters, tally clerks, weighers, cart drivers, coopers, warehouse guards, provisioners, rope handlers, and cargo gangs. Work is physically demanding. Injuries, disputed tallies, delayed ships, and uneven access to shifts are recurring problems.

### Market Ward
Shop assistants, stallholders, peddlers, moneychangers' clerks, scribes, apprentices, bakers, tailors, cobblers, carriers, cleaners, and food sellers. This district offers more small-business opportunities than any other, but competition for good pitches and foot traffic is constant.

### Rivergate
Smiths, millers, carpenters, masons, carters, ferrymen, boat repairers, wheelwrights, charcoal handlers, and laborers. Workshops often employ family members and apprentices. Smoke, noise, water access, and cart congestion are ordinary disputes.

### Lantern Ward
Temple servants, hospice workers, healers, gravediggers, gardeners, cleaners, candle-makers, copyists, and people who provide food or care to the poor. Some work is paid; some is supported by donations, temple funds, or charitable obligations. Religious service does not automatically mean spellcasting.

### The Underways
Ratfolk and some other residents work as repairers, masons, water-channel maintainers, couriers, salvage sorters, dyers, leatherworkers, toolmakers, cooks, traders, guides, and craftspeople. Some earn wages from surface employers; others work through family firms, neighborhood agreements, or independent trade. Surface prejudice can make formal employment and housing harder to secure, but there is no single Underways labor authority.

## Employment and Wages

For ordinary hireling services, use Pathfinder First Edition's published baseline:
- **Untrained hireling:** 1 sp per day.
- **Trained hireling:** 3 sp per day.
- **Expert hireling:** 1 gp per day.

These are rules baselines, not a complete local wage survey. A skilled artisan, bookkeeper, foreman, alchemist, bodyguard, ship's officer, or business manager may command a negotiated wage reflecting responsibility, scarcity, reputation, danger, and the length of the contract. Do not automatically treat every employee as an expert hireling or add invented guild fees to their wage.

A day laborer is generally hired for a defined shift or task, not guaranteed permanent employment. Meals, lodging, tools, injury arrangements, and payment timing should be stated when a contract is made. Employers who want reliable workers usually offer predictable pay and safe conditions; those who do not may find workers taking better offers elsewhere.

## Common Hiring Practices

- **Casual labor:** Foremen gather workers at dawn near the quays, market approaches, and cart yards. A worker may be hired for a single unloading, hauling, cleaning, or repair task.
- **Regular employment:** Shops and workshops hire through personal introductions, public notices, family contacts, or guild referrals.
- **Apprenticeships:** A master and household negotiate training, term, duties, lodging, and any stipend. An apprentice is not simply free labor; poor treatment can damage a master's reputation.
- **Household service:** Positions are often filled through recommendations. Discretion and reliability can matter as much as skill.
- **Specialist work:** Employers seek references, examples of prior work, and clear written terms. Scarce specialists may negotiate advances or retainers.
- **Seasonal work:** Harvest, fishing, ship repair, and major cargo arrivals create temporary demand. Workers may move between town and countryside.

## Labor Conditions and Pressure Points

1. **Irregular quay work:** A late ship can leave a cargo gang idle; several arrivals together can create a shortage of hands.
2. **Injury and debt:** Porters and riggers risk serious injury. A worker unable to work may quickly fall behind on rent or food.
3. **Apprentice disputes:** Some masters train carefully; others use apprentices for repetitive labor and neglect instruction.
4. **Housing costs:** Workers near the quays pay for proximity to employment, while cheaper rooms farther away cost time and transport.
5. **Guild access:** Guild referrals can help workers find steady employment, but independent workers also compete for jobs.
6. **Ratfolk discrimination:** Some employers refuse ratfolk applicants or offer worse terms. Others hire them for skill and reliability while avoiding public association. This is inconsistent by employer, not a universal rule.
7. **Food prices:** Dockworkers and casual laborers are especially exposed to grain prices and interrupted deliveries.
8. **Corruption and favoritism:** A foreman may favor relatives or accept payment for shifts. Such conduct is possible, not universal.

## Supporting Working-People NPCs

These people give the city a human scale. Their lives continue whether or not the PCs employ them.

### Jessa Rill
**Ancestry:** Human | **Work:** Quay labor forewoman, Old Quays  
Organizes small cargo gangs and knows which ships routinely arrive short-handed. Fair but unsentimental; she will not promise a crew she cannot assemble. She wants safer lifting gear and payment disputes settled before workers lose a day's wages.

### Dorrik Slatehand
**Ancestry:** Dwarf | **Work:** Mason and repair contractor, Rivergate  
A patient stoneworker who takes contracts from householders, warehouse owners, and the city. He is currently short one reliable apprentice. He refuses jobs where the client expects sound work at the price of patchwork.

### Lella Fen
**Ancestry:** Halfling | **Work:** Market porter and delivery organizer, Market Ward  
Coordinates small deliveries for stallholders who cannot afford their own cart. Quick with names and debts, she knows which shops are struggling and which merchants quietly pay on time.

### Tomas Wren
**Ancestry:** Human | **Work:** Cooper's journeyman, New Quays  
Makes and repairs barrels for provisioners, shipowners, and breweries. He hopes to open his own shop but lacks capital and a dependable supply of seasoned timber.

### Sella Marr's apprentice, Neri
**Ancestry:** Half-elf | **Work:** Tailoring apprentice, Thread & Thimble  
Neri is diligent, ambitious, and frustrated by being trusted with repairs but not customer fittings. Sella insists that judgment takes time. Neri is saving to buy better shears and a measuring set.

### Varo Kett
**Ancestry:** Human | **Work:** River barge pilot  
Knows the river's seasonal moods and shallow reaches. He is reliable but refuses night passages in poor weather unless the cargo owner pays for additional hands and accepts the risk.

### Ansel Pike
**Ancestry:** Human | **Work:** Watch sergeant, North Gate patrol  
A steady officer who knows the difference between a suspicious stranger and someone simply trying to get home after a long shift. He wants more patrol coverage and fewer orders from merchants who mistake influence for authority.

### Rutha Bell
**Ancestry:** Half-orc | **Work:** Laundress and lodging-house keeper, Old Quays  
Runs a clean but crowded lodging house used by sailors, laborers, and travelers. She extends small amounts of credit to regulars but keeps strict records. She has little patience for people who damage rooms and leave others to pay.

### Pellis Arno
**Ancestry:** Human | **Work:** Glass factor and supplier  
Already known to Miraq Ashwhisk through a disputed glassware delivery. Pellis claims inconsistent wall thickness came from a bad batch rather than careless handling. The matter remains unresolved; he is neither proven dishonest nor automatically trustworthy.

### Edda Moss
**Ancestry:** Human | **Work:** Midwife and practical healer, Lantern Ward  
Works with the hospice when needed but maintains an independent practice among poorer households. She is respected for competence and discretion. She is not a divine spellcaster.

### Kett-of-the-Low-Arch
**Ancestry:** Ratfolk | **Work:** Masonry patcher and passage inspector, Underways  
Checks older supports, seals leaks, and warns residents away from unsafe passages. Kett wants surface builders to stop dumping rubble into old access shafts. Kett is respected for practical knowledge but has no authority over all Underways neighborhoods.

### Rillik Venn
**Ancestry:** Ratfolk | **Work:** Courier and message runner, surface and Underways  
Fast, cautious, and good at finding alternate routes. Rillik carries commercial messages for clients who value discretion, but refuses to transport stolen goods or threats. Knows Serren Coil by reputation; their working relationship is cordial but competitive.

### Mara Doss
**Ancestry:** Human | **Work:** Grain tally clerk, Rivergate  
Records mill deliveries and notices discrepancies between declared loads and actual weights. She has recently been pressured to approve a ledger before checking the figures. She has not decided whether to report the pressure.

### Brother Halen
**Ancestry:** Human | **Work:** Hospice stores clerk, Lantern Ward  
Tracks donations, food stores, blankets, and medicines for Sister Alwen. Meticulous and overworked. He wants the hospice accounts to be transparent, partly because rumors of favoritism threaten donations.

## Daily Rhythm

**Before sunrise:** Bakers light ovens; fishers and river crews prepare; quay foremen gather laborers; market porters move goods before the streets fill.

**Morning:** Shops open, carts enter through the gates, clerks and officials begin their rounds, workshops start noisy work, and the market reaches its busiest period.

**Midday:** The market and quays remain active. Laborers eat at stalls and taverns. Magistrates hear scheduled matters; guild representatives meet when business requires it.

**Afternoon:** Cargo handling continues, workshops complete deliveries, household errands peak, and merchants settle accounts before closing.

**Evening:** Shops shutter, taverns and inns fill, sailors seek food and company, and watch patrols become more visible near gates and waterfront streets.

**Night:** Harbor work does not stop entirely. Night crews, guards, ferrymen, inn staff, healers, and people with reasons to avoid attention remain active. The city is quieter, not asleep.

## What the PCs Can Do With This

The workforce creates practical routes into city life:
- Hire temporary labor for a move, expedition, warehouse, or workshop.
- Recruit a permanent employee or apprentice.
- Find a craftsperson for repairs, commissions, or specialist advice.
- Learn about cargo schedules, local employers, and labor disputes.
- Establish fair employment practices that may earn trust among workers.
- Support or exploit labor networks, with consequences for reputation and relationships.
- Build commercial ties with ratfolk workers without assuming they all share the same political goals.

No worker is automatically loyal because the PCs pay them once. Reliability grows through fair terms, safe work, regular payment, and how the employer behaves when something goes wrong.
