# Obsydian and Dragonglass

**Status:** ESTABLISHED MATERIAL DISTINCTION; specific magical applications remain unresolved.  
**Visibility:** GM reference; reveal material knowledge according to a character's background and expertise.  
**Ruleset:** Pathfinder First Edition

This record distinguishes ordinary black onyx, called **obsydian** in the campaign, from **dragonglass**, a separate material made by dragons. Both possess necromantic properties. They are not the same substance and must not be treated as interchangeable merely because both are dark or associated with necromancy.

## 1. Obsydian — ordinary black onyx

Obsydian is ordinary, naturally occurring black onyx. It is a gemstone and can be obtained through normal mineral and gem trade where local supply permits. It is not made by dragons, is not a fragment of a dragon, and is not itself dragonglass.

Its necromantic properties are established. The precise nature, strength, and practical applications of those properties have not yet been defined. A piece of obsydian does not automatically cast a spell, animate the dead, contain a soul, or grant a bearer a class ability.

Obsydian is an ordinary gemstone rather than a rare dragon relic and can be replenished through normal gemstone trade. Its value depends on quality, size, workmanship, provenance, local supply, and the buyer. No universal campaign price or fixed magical premium is established.

## 2. Dragonglass — dragon-made material

Dragonglass is a distinct substance made by dragons. Its dragon-made origin is the defining distinction from ordinary obsydian. It also possesses necromantic properties.

The exact process by which dragons make dragonglass, the conditions required, its physical characteristics, the quantity that can be produced, and whether any living dragons can still make it are unresolved. Do not assume that ordinary obsydian becomes dragonglass through exposure to dragonfire or that a smith, alchemist, or wizard can reproduce it through ordinary craft.

Dragonglass is not simply another name for obsydian. It should not be substituted for ordinary black onyx in a description, trade record, ritual, or item recipe unless a specific effect or procedure is separately established.

## 3. Shared necromantic affinity

Both materials have necromantic properties, but that shared affinity does not establish identical effects.

Until a particular spell, ritual, crafted item, or other use is defined:
- Do not assign either material an automatic spell effect.
- Do not assume either can create, control, or destroy undead by itself.
- Do not assume either can hold a soul, preserve a corpse, or communicate with the dead without a defined effect.
- Do not add a new Pathfinder crafting prerequisite, component cost, or mechanical bonus solely because a material is necromantically resonant.
- Treat a claimed use as a matter of specialist knowledge or a specific established procedure, not universal common knowledge.

A future ritual or item may use one material, the other, or neither. The fact that both are necromantic does not make them interchangeable ingredients.

## 4. Knowledge and portrayal

**Ordinary trade knowledge:** Obsydian is recognized as a form of black onyx and can be bought or sold as a gemstone. Its availability and price vary by quality and market.

**Specialist knowledge:** The necromantic properties of either material may be known to scholars, alchemists, necromancers, collectors, or people with relevant experience. The extent of that knowledge is not universal.

**Dragonglass knowledge:** Its dragon-made origin is established, but the amount of reliable surviving knowledge about its production and use is unresolved. Stories about it may be incomplete or exaggerated.

**Player-character knowledge:** A character's familiarity depends on upbringing, profession, training, prior study, and the specific information the GM has made available. Possessing obsydian does not automatically mean a character understands dragonglass or the deeper properties of either material.

## 5. Player-facing summary

Use this only for a character whose background, training, or relevant experience justifies knowing the distinction:

> Obsydian is ordinary black onyx, a gemstone that can be obtained through normal trade. It is not made by dragons. Dragonglass is a separate material created by dragons. Both have necromantic properties, but they are not the same substance and cannot be assumed to have the same effects. Possessing either material does not, by itself, grant magical abilities or cause a spell to occur. The precise uses of each depend on specialist knowledge and specific established rituals or magic items.

This is a concise statement of the established distinction, not proof that every commoner or merchant knows the materials' necromantic properties.

## 6. Boundaries and unresolved details

Established:
- Obsydian is ordinary black onyx.
- Dragonglass is a separate material made by dragons.
- Both materials possess necromantic properties.
- They are not interchangeable by default.

Unresolved:
- The precise magical effects of either material.
- Whether their necromantic properties are identical, related, or merely share a broad affinity.
- The dragon-making process and the historical circumstances in which dragonglass was produced.
- Whether dragonglass can still be made.
- Known deposits, sources, trade routes, specialist buyers, and reliable market values.
- Specific spells, rituals, or magic items that use either material.

The value or quantity of a particular character's obsydian is a character-specific equipment decision. It does not establish a universal market price, starting allocation, or general rule for all characters.
