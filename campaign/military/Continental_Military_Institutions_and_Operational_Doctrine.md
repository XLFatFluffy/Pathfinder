# Continental Military Institutions and Operational Doctrine

**Snapshot:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC  
**Status:** GM operational framework proposal; no force totals or new wars established  
**Purpose:** Give the Living World Engine a consistent way to reason about military authority, readiness, mobilization, logistics, and consequences without inventing troop counts or turning every disagreement into a war.

This document consolidates established military facts and proposes conservative operating rules for simulation. Statements are marked **Established** when directly supported by current canon and **Working model** when they are a proposed way to adjudicate future events. A working model is not a dated event, secret plan, or claim that a force is currently mobilized.

## 1. Core principles

1. **Military power is layered.** A ruler may command household troops, a Great House may command its own forces, a vassal may owe a specific contingent or service, and a town may maintain a watch. These are not interchangeable.
2. **Lawful obligation matters.** Military service, armed retainers, local levies, transport, ships, animals, supplies, and fortification maintenance depend on a charter, grant, treaty, custom, or explicit GM decision. There is no universal service quota.
3. **Command is not the same as ownership.** A liege may lawfully call a vassal's service under the relevant obligation, but the exact command relationship and limits depend on the recorded arrangement.
4. **Readiness is not mobilization.** A maintained garrison, seasonal provisioning effort, inspection, or exercise does not establish a war footing.
5. **Logistics constrain every force.** Food, fodder, roads, bridges, weather, shipping, repair materials, pay, and time can limit operations before an army reaches an enemy.
6. **No unsupported numbers.** Do not assign troop totals, fleet sizes, casualty figures, readiness percentages, or campaign budgets without an authoritative source or explicit GM decision.
7. **No automatic escalation.** A border concern, legal petition, supply review, or political insult does not automatically produce armed conflict.

## 2. Military force categories

Use these categories when recording an actor's military capacity:

| Category | Meaning | Important limit |
|---|---|---|
| Household guard | Forces maintained for a ruler, household, seat, or immediate protection | Not automatically the full field army of the house |
| Professional force | Personnel maintained through continuing service and specialized training | Exact size, funding, and readiness require evidence |
| Garrison | Troops assigned to a fort, pass, port, wall, or strategic site | Cannot be counted as freely deployable without leaving the post exposed |
| Vassal contingent | Forces or armed retainers owed under a specific feudal obligation | Terms vary by charter and custom; no universal quota |
| Local levy | Locally raised defensive or service force | Training, equipment, duration, and legal authority vary |
| Watch or town guard | Local security, patrol, gate, and public-order force | Not equivalent to a field army |
| Clan force | Military capacity provided through the Kharadorn clan structure | Clan autonomy and individual obligations must be respected |
| Naval force | Warships, escorts, coastal craft, pilots, crews, and harbor defenses | Commercial shipping is not automatically a war fleet |
| Auxiliary support | Wagons, engineers, scouts, guides, labor, animals, stores, and transport | Support capacity can be more decisive than nominal combat strength |

A single actor may control or call upon several categories, but the records must keep them distinct.

## 3. Established Great House and Crown military profiles

### The Crown and House Calderan

**Established**
- The Crown maintains a permanent household guard and a distinct royal military force.
- The forces can protect Valedorn and uphold ordinary royal authority.
- They are not presumed capable of defeating all six Great Houses acting together.
- The Master of War, Marshal Garran Holt, coordinates the royal army, readiness, fortifications, military logistics, and defense planning.
- The Crown commands its own forces. Great Houses retain their own forces and hereditary military obligations under the post-Rebellion settlement.

**Working model**
- Separate palace/household security from forces available for field operations.
- Crown officers may coordinate defense of Crown lands and royal routes, but should not be assumed to command Great House forces.
- Any major levy, extraordinary taxation, or realm-wide war engages the established Imperial Council political process.
- A royal military reform can create political pressure even before it changes actual force strength.

**Unresolved**
- Royal army composition, recruitment, funding, command layers below the Master of War, permanent garrisons, regional reach, and mobilization capacity.
- The balance between household guard, field force, city defense, and Crown-land garrisons.

### House Veyr — Northern Marches

**Established**
- The Northern Watch is a permanent frontier force responsible for the surviving portions of the Ancient Barrier.
- Mobile Marcher Companies conduct patrols, escorts, and rapid response.
- House Veyr maintains a separate Household Guard.
- Lord Stellan Veyr is a veteran commander; Ser Halvar Veyr is a Northern Watch officer and is popular among frontier soldiers.
- Sparse settlement and dependence on southern supplies constrain the house.
- The Northern Watch is supported by seasonal provisioning and the wider Veyr political and landed network.

**Working model**
- Treat Barrier garrisons and fixed frontier posts as committed forces, not a freely available field army.
- Marcher Companies are the principal mobile response element for patrols, escorts, and urgent local threats.
- Veyr's most consequential military vulnerability is the supply chain from the south, not an assumed lack of courage or competence.
- The house may prioritize frontier security over southern court politics, but it still requires information and a credible reason to act.

**Unresolved**
- Exact number and condition of Barrier posts, force totals, command hierarchy, reserve depth, and service obligations of each banner house.

### House Vaelorian — Western Highlands

**Established**
- Vaelorian maintains a comparatively compact, professional force.
- Its established military character includes well-equipped infantry, skilled archers, household troops, and mountain garrisons.
- It controls some strategically important mountain passes, but not every route through the western ranges.
- Its financial and commercial power supports military capability, but exact wealth and military totals are unknown.

**Working model**
- Prioritize professional readiness, selected fortified passes, and protection of mines, commercial centers, and high-value routes.
- Avoid treating every older elven estate or commercial partner as a military vassal.
- Vaelorian may use contracts, finance, and commercial pressure before committing troops.

**Unresolved**
- Force totals, formal command structure, exact garrison locations, and the service expected from individual banner houses.

### House Edarth — Western Heartlands

**Established**
- Edarth's power rests partly on agricultural population, landed nobility, traditional military strength, experienced commanders, lesser noble alliances, strategic roads, and fortifications.
- Its political culture emphasizes duty, loyalty, oaths, preparation, supply, and discipline.
- Edarth is decentralized relative to Vaelorian, with meaningful lesser-house relationships.
- Houses Harrowden and Wrenford are Edarth banner houses; their Longmead dispute remains low-intensity and is not an armed conflict.

**Working model**
- Treat Edarth's military strength as a network of the ruling house, household forces, fortified holdings, and vassal obligations rather than one permanently assembled army.
- The banner-roll and service review may clarify records, but does not automatically increase levies or change customary obligations.
- Garrick Edarth is likely to prefer lawful arbitration and controlled mobilization over allowing a lesser-house dispute to become a private war.

**Unresolved**
- Number of sworn houses, exact service obligations, command hierarchy, levy customs, force totals, and the relationship between household troops and regional contingents.

### House Kharadorn — Dwarven Empire, Dragonstone, and Khar-Dum

**Established**
- The dwarven empire consists of independent clans united under the main branch.
- Kharadorn military strengths include heavy infantry, elite armored troops, engineers, siege specialists, clan contingents, underground fortifications, and specialized engineering traditions.
- Dwarven naval forces maintain military control of the Central Inland Sea. Established forces also include naval garrisons, coastal fortifications, marines, and vessels that may be requisitioned in wartime.
- Khar-Dum and Dragonstone remain distinct military and political centers.
- Dragonstone is a conquered stronghold and strategic possession; Khar-Dum is the separate ancestral homeland in the northeastern mountains.
- Individual clan tribute, military, labor, and resource obligations are not quantified.

**Working model**
- Treat the main branch as the coordinating imperial authority while preserving the clans' internal governance and customary rights.
- Do not assume every clan can be mobilized by a simple royal order; identify the relevant agreement, obligation, and decision-maker.
- Distinguish Dragonstone's garrison and sea-control duties from forces available for distant land campaigns.
- Engineering and defensive preparation may be more important than numerical superiority in a fortified or subterranean operation.

**Unresolved**
- Imperial command structure, clan muster rules, fleet composition, exact garrison commitments, military service agreements, and the limits of the main branch's direct authority over individual clans.

### House Merrowen — Eastern Maritime Territory

**Established**
- Merrowen's power rests on ports, shipping, naval tradition, commercial relationships, and eastern coastal routes.
- Its territory includes the Northern Coast, Eastern Shore, Great Delta, and Southern Coast, but does not imply control of every eastern port.
- House Ternholt is a banner house with local fishing, pilotage, and harbor interests.
- Lady Selene Merrowen is gathering information about eastern coast pilotage and harbor practice; this is a proposed review, not a naval mobilization.

**Working model**
- Distinguish warships, escorts, coastal craft, merchant vessels, pilots, and harbor defenses.
- Treat smaller ports' local customs as meaningful constraints on centralized naval policy.
- Merrowen's naval and commercial power can create leverage through access, escort, insurance, pilotage, and port practice without requiring a blockade.

**Unresolved**
- Fleet size, warship classes, command structure, port garrisons, naval readiness, and the legal relationship between the Great House and independent or chartered ports.

## 5. Minor houses, local authorities, and military obligations

- A minor house's household guard is not automatically a field contingent.
- A banner house owes only the service established by its charter, custom, grant, or explicit GM decision.
- Direct Crown vassalage remains distinct from service to a neighboring Great House.
- A crossing charter, harbor right, woodland custom, or toll privilege does not create a general military obligation unless the record says so.
- Clan Emberdelve's membership in the Kharadorn imperial union does not establish its precise troop contribution.
- Independent southern powers may maintain local retainers, city watches, port defenses, or limited coastal craft. These do not create a unified Southern army.

When a specific vassal is called, record the authority issuing the summons, the legal basis, the requested service, the response, the cost, and the political consequence. Do not infer the response from allegiance alone.

## 6. Mobilization and command: Living World Engine procedure

The following is a **working simulation procedure**, not a claim that every realm uses identical formal terminology.

1. **Trigger:** Identify the concrete threat, obligation, request, or political decision that could justify military action.
2. **Authority:** Determine who may lawfully order the force to act. Separate Crown, Great House, clan, vassal, city, and charter authority.
3. **Information:** Determine what the decision-maker knows, what is rumor, and how quickly reliable information can arrive.
4. **Readiness:** Identify which forces are already maintained, which are committed to garrisons or patrols, and which require assembly.
5. **Obligation:** Identify the specific charter, custom, treaty, or decision governing any vassal or clan contribution.
6. **Logistics:** Check food, fodder, transport, roads, weather, stores, repair, pay, shipping, and time.
7. **Political cost:** Check Council scrutiny, noble resistance, local rights, merchant disruption, and the risk of provoking other powers.
8. **Action:** Record the actual order, response, delay, refusal, negotiation, or deployment. A proposed action is not an accomplished event.
9. **Outcome:** Resolve only what follows from the available forces, terrain, information, logistics, and opposition. Record losses and consequences only when supported by the event.

### Escalation ladder

Use this as a guide, not an automatic sequence:

**warning or request → intelligence and reconnaissance → local readiness → lawful summons or requisition → limited muster → concentrated deployment → engagement → occupation or settlement**

An actor may stop, negotiate, or withdraw at any stage. Skipping stages requires a credible urgent cause and a record of the political and logistical consequences.

## 7. Logistics and campaign constraints

Before a force moves, evaluate:

- **Food and water:** stores, local supply, purchase, requisition, spoilage, and seasonal availability.
- **Fodder and animals:** grazing, feed, remounts, pack animals, wagon teams, and veterinary care.
- **Transport:** roads, bridges, ferries, ships, ports, carts, and the availability of drivers and crews.
- **Weather and terrain:** snow, rain, river levels, mountain passes, heat, marsh, and coastal conditions.
- **Fortifications:** garrison commitments, repair materials, siege equipment, engineering skill, and access.
- **Pay and obligation:** wages, customary service, compensation, debts, and the risk of unpaid or prolonged service.
- **Information:** messengers, scouts, guides, local cooperation, and the time required to verify reports.
- **Civilian impact:** harvest labor, market disruption, tenant flight, damage to roads and fields, and loss of trade.

Do not assign exact marching speeds, supply consumption, or campaign ranges until the map scale, route distances, and relevant Pathfinder rules have been calibrated for the specific situation.
