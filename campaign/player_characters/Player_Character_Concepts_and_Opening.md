# Player Character Concepts and Opening Adventure

**Status:** PROVISIONAL LEGACY PLAYER CONCEPTS / GM PREPARATION — review required 2026-11-09  
**Ruleset:** Pathfinder First Edition  
**Starting location:** Tallowmere  
**Important:** The players have described character concepts, not completed character sheets. Do not treat unchosen names, ages, classes, feats, equipment, alignments, or mechanical details as established.

This document preserves an earlier shared-backstory draft and a proposed opening encounter. The players are revising their concepts; treat every PC-specific element below as provisional legacy material, not a current campaign assumption, until the players confirm what they are keeping. It is not a substitute for final character backgrounds or character sheets.

## 1. Shared Backstory: The Vampire and the Ratfolk

The two PCs already know one another and have a history together before Session One.

### The Ratfolk's Family

The ratfolk PC was born into a family of travelling merchants. His family moved between settlements and trading communities, earning their living through buying, selling, and carrying goods.

Approximately ten years before Session One, while travelling through lands held under House Velleran, they were intercepted and extorted by armed men wearing the colors of House Relland, a minor noble family. The men used their position and force to compel the family to surrender all of their wares for a fraction of their actual value: pennies on the gold piece. The family protested the extortion.

The confrontation turned violent. The men murdered the ratfolk's mother and father and were in the process of killing the child when the vampire intervened.

### The Rescue

The vampire PC arrived during the attack, killed the assailants, and saved the ratfolk child.

The responsible house is House Relland, a minor family owing allegiance to House Velleran. The attack occurred approximately ten years before Session One. The attackers were led by Ser Deylan Marrick, an armsman supervising Relland road patrols who abused that authority to extort travellers. See House_Relland_and_the_Merchant_Incident.md for the household's cover-up and surviving avenues of evidence. The exact roadside location remains approximate.

The ratfolk remembers the vampire as the person who saved his life. Their relationship is not merely one of obligation: the ratfolk regards the vampire as a friend as well as a mentor.

### The Mentorship

The vampire taught the ratfolk the fundamentals of alchemy. He recognized that the younger ratfolk possessed an unusual aptitude and that, with instruction and experience, the pupil could surpass the teacher.

This creates a useful relationship dynamic:
- The vampire has experience, knowledge, and the history of having saved the ratfolk's life.
- The ratfolk has his own intelligence, talent, judgment, and developing expertise.
- The vampire is a mentor, but not the owner of the ratfolk's choices.
- The ratfolk is grateful and loyal, but should remain an independent character with his own ambitions and opinions.
- The vampire has promised that helping him restore the fallen Targaryen dynasty could create substantial opportunities for the ratfolk to advance his goal of ending persecution and securing ratfolk acceptance and standing. The promise does not predetermine what reforms will occur, what position the ratfolk will hold, or whether the restoration will succeed.
- Their friendship predates the campaign and should be portrayed as an established relationship, not repeatedly tested through artificial misunderstandings.

The ratfolk PC's desire to improve the treatment and standing of ratfolk can be informed by the loss of his family and the injustice they suffered. Do not reduce his entire identity to that trauma. The vampire has also promised that, if the ratfolk helps him reclaim the mantle of the fallen Targaryen dynasty and restore its power, that project will offer substantial potential and opportunities for advancing the ratfolk's own cause. This is an offer of potential, not a guarantee of equality, legal reform, political office, or any particular outcome.

### Unresolved Backstory Details

Keep the following open until the players complete their characters or wish to define them:
- Both PCs' names, ages, and exact ancestries/heritages.
- The vampire's identity and circumstances at the time of the rescue.
- The location and approximate date of the attack.
- The minor house responsible and whether it still exists.
- The attackers' names, rank, and whether they acted under direct orders or abused their association with the house.
- Whether the ratfolk remembers the attackers clearly or has evidence identifying them.
- What happened to the family's caravan, remaining property, and trade contacts.
- How long the vampire and ratfolk have travelled together.
- The precise alchemical instruction already received and what the ratfolk has learned independently.

House Relland is a minor house in Velleran-chartered territory south of Tallowmere, not a Tallowmere faction. The house believes the travelling family died and does not know the ratfolk child survived. Any later confrontation or political consequences should arise through the PCs' choices and evidence.

## 2. Proposed Session One Opening: The Roadside Troll

The players want the PCs to kill a troll on their way to Tallowmere. This is a suitable opening because it gives them an immediate shared objective, demonstrates their existing teamwork, and creates a natural reason for local people to notice them when they reach the city.

### Core Situation

On the final approach to Tallowmere, the PCs encounter a damaged wagon belonging to Selwick & Company, the trading concern of wealthy Tallowmere merchant Master Tavren Selwick. A troll has attacked the wagon and its crew. Its exact hunting ground is selected to fit the PCs' approach route.

The PCs can track, confront, avoid, or otherwise deal with the threat. Their chosen approach should determine how the scene develops. The intended player-facing opportunity is to kill the troll, not to force a particular sequence of actions.

The precise road and encounter site remain flexible until the PCs' approach direction is known. Place the wagon within a half-day of Tallowmere on a suitable established route. An abandoned site may be used only if its geography fits; do not invent a new history for the site merely to stage the fight. The wagon carries ordinary commercial goods bound for Selwick's Old Quays warehouses.

### Encounter Design and Rules

The default creature is the standard Pathfinder First Edition troll (CR 5), using its published statistics and abilities. Do not alter its statistics to force a desired result.

The party is expected to consist of two PCs, but their exact builds and level are not yet known. A standard troll can be extremely dangerous to a small party, especially if the PCs do not understand regeneration. Before finalizing the encounter:
- Review the completed character sheets and actual party level.
- Apply Pathfinder encounter-building guidance for a two-character party.
- Decide whether a standard troll is appropriate or whether the scene should use a lower-CR creature or a different threat if the party is not mechanically ready.
- Preserve the troll's published regeneration and vulnerability rules if using the standard creature.
- Provide fair clues that ordinary wounds are not stopping it: an animal carcass with wounds that have begun closing, a trail of blood that ends where the creature recovered, or a witness who saw it rise after being badly injured.
- Make fire or acid a discoverable and usable tactical answer. Do not assume the ratfolk has a particular formula, extract, bomb, or item until the character sheet confirms it.
- Do not secretly waive regeneration or change its vulnerability to make the encounter easier.
- Make retreat, concealment, negotiation, or bypassing the creature possible if the players choose not to fight.

The encounter should reward preparation and teamwork rather than depend on the players guessing a hidden GM rule.

### Possible Scene Elements

Use only what is needed for the final encounter:
- A damaged merchant cart or overturned wagon.
- A frightened driver, guard, or traveller hiding nearby.
- Signs that the troll has been feeding and returning to the same hunting ground.
- A narrow road, ditch, stream, ruined wall, or other ordinary terrain that can create tactical choices.
- A small amount of recoverable cargo or equipment, with ownership made clear.
- A witness who can later identify the PCs as the people who dealt with the threat.

These are optional encounter components, not all mandatory features.

## 3. Natural Introduction to Tallowmere

The troll encounter should lead into the city without making the PCs feel that the entire world has been waiting for them.

A simple connection is that the attacked travellers or cargo are bound for Tallowmere. If the PCs rescue a survivor, recover identifiable goods, or bring credible proof of the troll's death, they have a practical reason to enter the city and speak with someone.

Possible introductions:
- A surviving carter asks them to escort the wagon and remaining cargo to Tallowmere.
- Master Tavren Selwick, owner of the cargo, offers 30 gp if the PCs meaningfully protect his crew or help recover the consignment, a meal and one night's lodging at the Blue Heron, and a signed introduction to Meret Sorn of the Warehouse Guild.
- If the PCs bring only proof of the troll's death without helping the crew or cargo, Selwick may offer a smaller payment after verifying the report.
- The surviving crew can corroborate the PCs' actions. The Town Watch may record the incident, but no separate civic bounty is established.
- A local trader recognizes the recovered goods and introduces the PCs to a relevant merchant.
- If no one survives, the PCs can still arrive with evidence and decide whether to report the threat.

Selwick's payment and introduction are established in Selwick_Merchant_Contact.md. They are conditional on meaningful help to his people or cargo, not merely a claim that the troll was killed. No separate public bounty or legal entitlement is established.

The introduction should expose the PCs to Tallowmere's ordinary civic and commercial life. It need not immediately involve Lord Denvor, the Kharadorn Charter Warden, or a Great House.

## 4. How the Backstory and Opening Relate

The troll incident does not need to be connected to the murder of the ratfolk's family. Keeping them separate at first gives the PCs a personal history without turning every event into part of one conspiracy.

The backstory can become relevant in Tallowmere through ordinary interactions:
- The ratfolk may encounter prejudice or barriers to trade.
- A merchant may recognize the practical value of his alchemical skill.
- The vampire may be asked about his unusual past or abilities, but his identity is not automatically known.
- The ratfolk may react strongly to extortion, abuse of authority, or threats against travelling merchants.
- A later discovery may connect the minor house to a wider political or commercial network, but only if the GM deliberately establishes evidence.

The PCs should decide how much of their history they disclose. NPCs do not know it merely because it appears in this GM document.

## 5. Preparation Checklist Before Session One

1. Obtain both completed character sheets.
2. Confirm the PCs' level, hit points, defenses, attack options, skills, and relevant abilities.
3. Confirm the vampire's actual class features and how his vampirism works mechanically.
4. Confirm the ratfolk's alchemist class, discoveries, formulae, extracts, bombs, equipment, and crafting choices.
5. Reassess the troll encounter against the actual two-character party.
6. Select the approach road and encounter site based on the PCs' travel direction.
7. Confirm which crew members survive and what condition the wagon and cargo are in.
8. Select the road and troll site to match the PCs' approach.
9. Prepare Selwick's letter and decide whether he is in the city to meet them personally or communicates through his factor.
10. Leave the PCs free to choose where they go and whom they approach.

## GM Principles

- The PCs already know and trust one another; do not stage a first meeting.
- The vampire saved the ratfolk and taught him alchemy; the ratfolk is a friend and pupil, not a dependent NPC.
- The ratfolk's aptitude is real, but do not pre-award mechanical abilities or crafting feats before character creation.
- House Relland is responsible for the retainers' extortion and subsequent cover-up; the wider family did not formally order the murders.
- The troll uses published Pathfinder rules. Its encounter suitability must be checked against the completed party.
- The city introduction should arise from the encounter's consequences, not a forced cutscene.
- Selwick's conditional payment and written introduction are established; no separate public bounty exists.
- Selwick and House Relland are not connected to one another or to the ratfolk family's tragedy.


## 6. Companion NPC and Campaign Structure

The PCs are expected to travel with a companion NPC. The current working assumption is that this companion may be approximately **CR 8**, but the exact creature/NPC statistics, class levels, equipment, and abilities are not yet finalized.

The likely concept is **Alaric Thorne**, a human huntsman, tracker, and scout proposed in a separate player-submitted concept. He remains provisional and subject to GM refinement and approval. His final mechanics and role must fit the campaign's Pathfinder First Edition rules and should not overshadow the PCs.

### Encounter-Balance Implications

- The opening party is expected to consist of two PCs, currently only concepts, plus the companion NPC.
- Do not treat the companion's CR as if it were a PC level or simply add CR values to calculate party level.
- A CR 8 combat-capable ally is a major force beside two level-five PCs. Encounter difficulty must account for his actual offensive, defensive, mobility, scouting, and support abilities.
- Reassess the proposed standard CR 5 troll after the PCs' character sheets and the companion's final build are available.
- Do not increase the troll's statistics or add extra enemies merely to counterbalance the companion. Build a fair encounter for the actual group and the intended opening tone.
- The companion should contribute meaningfully without solving every problem or displacing player decisions.

### Campaign Structure After Arrival

The troll and Selwick wagon are intended as an opening introduction to Tallowmere, not the start of a mandatory quest chain. Once the PCs reach the city and have had a chance to establish their first contacts, the campaign opens into a **sandbox**.

The PCs may pursue commercial opportunities, personal goals, relationships, faction interests, exploration, political developments, or entirely unexpected plans. Tallowmere and the surrounding region continue to function independently of them. The GM should present opportunities, consequences, and information without assuming a required order of adventures or a single central plot.

The opening establishes a foothold; it does not prescribe what the PCs must do next.


## 7. Vampire PC — Royal Origin and Captivity

**Status:** PLAYER-PROVIDED BACKSTORY; character name and exact dates not yet supplied. GM-facing.

The vampire PC was the heir to the throne of the fallen dragon-riding dynasty. During the Great Rebellion, the revolt destroyed his family and overthrew the dynasty. He escaped the castle during the collapse of the royal household.

After escaping, he was captured by a powerful vampire and held captive for approximately 100–150 years. He eventually found a way to kill the vampire who held him, thereby securing his freedom. The identity of this captor, the circumstances of the imprisonment, and the means by which the PC killed the captor remain undefined.

After gaining his freedom, the vampire eventually met **Aldric Voss**. They became friends. At a later point, the vampire turned Aldric into a vampire so that Aldric could survive a conflict they faced together. The nature, date, and consequences of that conflict remain to be established.

The vampire later encountered the ratfolk PC and his family during the attack by House Relland's retainers. He intervened, killed the attackers, and saved the ratfolk child. He subsequently became the ratfolk's mentor and friend, teaching him the fundamentals of alchemy as described above.

### Chronology and continuity

The campaign begins on **1 Harvestfall, 243 AC**. The Great Rebellion is currently placed approximately 200–300 years before the campaign, with its exact date unresolved. A captivity period of approximately 100–150 years can fit within that broad historical window. Do not assign exact years to the escape, capture, release, meeting with Aldric Voss, turning, or ratfolk rescue until the historical chronology and both characters' ages are reviewed together.

### Terminology and canon safeguards

- The player described the fallen dynasty as the **Targaryen** dynasty / throne. In current campaign records, “House Targaryen” is a legacy source label only; the dynasty's final in-world name remains undecided. Preserve the player's intended royal lineage, but do not silently promote the legacy label to the official setting name.
- The vampire PC is the surviving Crown Prince and direct heir of the fallen dynasty. His claim was never formally surrendered. His explicit long-term ambition is to restore the dynasty to its former glory and power, reclaim the throne, and retake Dragonstone from House Kharadorn. His survival and identity remain concealed; the wider world and the six Great Houses believe the old dynasty extinct. No surviving loyalists are established.
- His royal identity, survival, captivity, and actions are GM-facing until the player decides what the character has disclosed and the GM determines who could know.
- **Aldric Voss** is a distinct individual from **King Aldric Calderan**. Do not conflate them.
- The vampire who held him captive is not automatically the progenitor of all vampires. The scope of that vampire's lineage or influence is not yet established.
- The vampire PC's approved mechanical template is recorded separately in the character-creation decisions; this narrative record does not add or alter mechanics.

### Details still to establish with the player

- Character's name, age at the Great Rebellion, and apparent/current age.
- Whether he was formally crowned, designated heir, or simply next in the line of succession.
- Which castle or royal seat he escaped from; do not assume the current provisional name “Dragonstone” was its original name.
- The exact circumstances of the family's deaths and whether any relatives might have survived.
- The identity, motives, lineage, and lair of the vampire captor.
- How he killed the captor and whether the act had wider consequences.
- Aldric Voss's identity, background, the conflict that led to his turning, and his current whereabouts/status.
- How much of the vampire's royal history Aldric Voss and the ratfolk PC know.
- The precise political and military methods by which he intends to restore the dynasty; his long-term goal itself is established. 
- Whether any surviving loyalists, allies, or supporters exist; none are currently established.



### Established long-term ambition, present strategy, and shared promise

The vampire PC intends to take up the mantle of the fallen Targaryen dynasty and rebuild it into its former glory and power. He seeks to restore his family's rule, reclaim the throne, and retake Dragonstone, now held by House Kharadorn. The Great Rebellion was led by the dwarven power that is now represented in current canon by House Kharadorn; older source references to House Baratheon are superseded.

His immediate strategy is to accumulate wealth and power. Wealth, political influence, allies, and eventual military strength are resources for the restoration project. This is an active long-term ambition, not merely a possible consequence of his ancestry. The exact methods, timetable, supporters, and sequence of political or military moves remain the player's decisions.

### Promise to the Ratfolk PC

The vampire has promised the ratfolk that helping him take up the mantle of the fallen Targaryen dynasty and restore its power offers substantial potential for advancing the ratfolk's own ambition: ending persecution and securing acceptance, safety, and standing for ratfolk. The restored dynasty's wealth, influence, and political reach could create opportunities for meaningful social change. This is a promise of potential, not a guarantee that ratfolk will achieve equality, that specific laws will change, or that the ratfolk will receive a particular title, office, or reward. The precise terms of their understanding and how the ratfolk intends to use those opportunities remain with the players.

The player has previously established that the PC was nine years old when the Great Rebellion began, escaped the siege of Dragonstone, and spent centuries hiding and abandoning his name. Coordinate this with the current account of his 100–150 years of captivity and the broader chronology before assigning exact dates. Do not turn the provisional historical label “Dragonstone” into the confirmed original name of the old dynasty's seat.
