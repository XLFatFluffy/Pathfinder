# Player Starting Handout — The Realm and Tallowmere

**For:** Session One  
**Campaign date:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC  
**Ruleset:** Pathfinder First Edition  
**Visibility:** PLAYER_SHARED  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-09

This guide contains broad public information that a reasonably informed person could know. It is not an exhaustive gazetteer, and it does not mean every character knows every local detail. Your character's upbringing, education, travels, ancestry, class, and personal history may give them more specific knowledge. Major settlements and fortresses are widely known; small villages, minor estates, precise routes, local disputes, and detailed political arrangements may not be. This guide does not include private motives, hidden histories, or secrets.

## The Setting

The campaign takes place in a high-medieval fantasy world based on the Westeros and *Game of Thrones* setting framework: noble houses, hereditary claims, feudal obligations, warfare, trade, and political intrigue. The campaign uses Pathfinder First Edition rules and its established names, geography, and history.

Humans are the largest population across much of the continent. Elves and dwarves hold major political power, while many other ancestries live throughout the settled lands. Ratfolk communities are often subterranean. Prejudice against ratfolk is widespread in surface society, but it is not universal and reactions vary by place and individual.

Magic exists, but it is uncommon in everyday life. Many ordinary people will live their entire lives without seeing a spell cast. Arcane practitioners are exceptionally rare; most recognized wizards serve Great Houses or other wealthy patrons. Enchanted items are scarce, expensive, and not ordinary shop stock. A magic weapon or protective item is a notable possession, not routine equipment for soldiers or merchants. The gods of the Pathfinder pantheon are real and widely recognized, although worship and religious customs vary between communities. Divine spellcasters are uncommon; a temple or shrine does not necessarily have someone capable of magical healing.

### Faith in everyday life

There is no single church or supreme religious authority across the continent. Different communities honor different gods, and worship may be public, private, or tied to particular trades and local customs. A priest may lead ceremonies, teach, keep records, provide counsel, or conduct funerals without being able to cast spells.

In Tallowmere, several faiths have visible public institutions:
- **Abadar:** The Open Ledger, a temple associated with lawful trade, contracts, honest measures, civic order, and commerce.
- **Pharasma:** The Quiet Gate, serving funerary rites, burial, remembrance, and care for the dying.
- **Gozreh:** The Tideward Shrine, associated with sailors, riverfolk, weather observances, and safe voyages.
- **Sarenrae:** The House of the Dawn, known for charity, refuge, reconciliation, and aid to the poor.

Smaller or household observances include Desna among some travelers and couriers, Erastil among families with rural ties, and Torag among some dwarven residents. These are visible local traditions, not an exhaustive list of the gods worshipped in the city. No one faith is Tallowmere's official religion, and these institutions do not replace secular government.


## The Crown and the Six Great Houses

The current monarch is **King Aldric Calderan**, of House Calderan. The capital is **Valedorn**, where the Imperial Seat stands. The monarch presides over a six-seat Imperial Council, with one seat appointed by each Great House. The Council is an aristocratic negotiating body concerned with matters affecting the realm and the privileges of the Great Houses; it is not a modern elected parliament.

| **Vaelorian** | Elven commercial and financial power in the western highlands | Aurelith | Lord Theren Vaelorian |
| **Kharadorn** | Dwarven imperial dynasty; holds Dragonstone and exercises major strategic influence over the central inland sea | Dragonstone; ancestral homeland at Khar-Dum | High King Durnak Kharadorn |

The six houses united in the Great Rebellion, which overthrew the old dragon-riding dynasty roughly two to three centuries ago. The rebellion ended that dynasty's rule from the Imperial Seat. Most dragons were killed; those that survived the war disappeared from public view, and their ultimate fate is unknown. House Kharadorn was the rebellion's principal military and political architect. The victorious houses established the present post-Rebellion order, with House Calderan occupying the throne.

## A Short History of the Realm

The present political order was established after the **Great Rebellion**, fought roughly two to three centuries ago. Before the rebellion, a dragon-riding dynasty ruled from the Imperial Seat for approximately four centuries. At the height of its power, it possessed twelve dragons. Dragon-riding was tied to an exclusive royal bloodline, and the dragons gave the dynasty a military advantage no ordinary army could easily match.

Over generations, the old dynasty increasingly sought to centralize authority and reduce the independence of the great houses. Six Great Houses eventually united against it. Their rebellion had two connected aims: to preserve their own political independence and to destroy dragon-riding as the foundation of the dynasty's supremacy.

The war lasted several years. The dwarven Great House now known as **Kharadorn** was the principal architect of the rebellion and developed specialized weapons to kill dragons. Most dragons died during the conflict. Those that survived after losing their riders fled or disappeared; their fate is unknown.

The old ruling family was nearly exterminated, and the dynasty lost the Imperial Seat. The victorious houses established the current post-Rebellion order. House Calderan now holds the throne. House Kharadorn took the old dynasty's stronghold of Dragonstone by conquest. Dragonstone is now a Kharadorn possession, while Khar-Dum remains the Kharadorns' ancestral homeland.

The broad outline is established, but the exact dates, original name of the fallen dynasty, and many details of the war remain uncertain or unresolved. No living claimant to the old dynasty is publicly recognized.

## How Power Is Organized

The realm is built around hereditary rule, noble houses, landholding, sworn obligations, and negotiated privileges. The six Great Houses are the principal continental powers, but they are not the only political authorities. Minor houses hold local estates, castles, and territories; independent towns, smaller principalities, and other local powers also exist.

The Imperial Council gives each Great House one seat. It is an aristocratic body for negotiation among the Crown and the major houses, not an elected assembly representing the population. A ruler's authority, local charters, noble obligations, and commercial privileges can all matter in daily life.

Tallowmere illustrates how these layers work together: House Denvor governs the city under a hereditary charter confirmed by House Kharadorn. Denvor handles ordinary local administration, while Kharadorn retains strategic rights and harbor interests.

## Tallowmere

Tallowmere is a walled port city on the northwestern shore of the central inland sea, where the navigable River Vale reaches the sea. Its population is estimated at about 18,000. It is an important working harbor and trade center, but it is neither the continental capital nor the largest port on the inland sea.

**House Denvor** governs Tallowmere from **Greywatch**, its fortified seat on the high ground above the harbor. Lord Oswin Denvor is the city's lord. House Denvor is a locally important minor house, not a seventh Great House. Its hereditary charter was confirmed by House Kharadorn. The Denvors govern ordinary city life; Kharadorn retains strategic rights and harbor interests but does not administer the city's daily affairs.

### City districts

- **Greywatch Rise:** House Denvor's fortified seat, garrison, and local authority.
- **The High Ward:** Wealthier homes, legal offices, and established temples.
- **The Old Quays:** Historic waterfront, shipwrights, chandlers, inns, and older warehouses.
- **The New Quays:** Expanded docks, bulk cargo, and newer merchant concerns.
- **Market Ward:** Bellmarket, guildhalls, shops, moneychangers, and public notices.
- **Rivergate:** Bargemen, mills, laborers, and river commerce.
- **Lantern Ward:** Temples, hospice, healers, and burial grounds.
- **The Underways:** A network of subterranean spaces containing decentralized ratfolk neighborhoods, homes, workshops, and routes. It is not one unified settlement or political faction.

### Useful public destinations

- **The Blue Heron:** A respectable, comparatively quiet inn in the Old Quays.
- **The Crooked Oar:** A cheap, noisy working tavern at the New Quays.
- **The Copper Ladle:** An inexpensive cooked-food stall near Bellmarket.
- **Reeve's Hall:** Local administration, petitions, records, and public Council agenda notices.
- **The Harbor Office:** Shipping declarations, berths, and harbor procedures.
- **Bellmarket:** The principal public market in Market Ward and a major meeting place.
- **The Warehouse Guild:** A major commercial institution in the city.
- **Lantern Hospice:** A charitable house providing limited care and assistance; it is not a large hospital or a guaranteed source of magical healing.
- **The House of the Dawn:** A Sarenite religious house providing meals, basic care, limited shelter, and charitable assistance. It is distinct from the hospice.
- **The Underways:** Ratfolk communities and routes; access and introductions depend on local knowledge.

## The Calendar and the Present Date

The campaign uses the **After the Crowning (AC)** dating system. The current year is **243 AC**, and the campaign begins on **1 Harvestfall, 243 AC**. The Crowning marks the establishment of the post-Rebellion order and current monarchy.

A standard year has twelve months of thirty days each, followed by five additional Yearturn days. Every fourth year includes one extra leap day. The months are Frostwane, Thawrise, Greenwake, Bloomtide, Suncrest, Highsun, Goldleaf, Harvestfall, Emberwane, Mistfall, Duskreach, and Longnight.

The seven-day week runs Firstday, Secondday, Thirdday, Fourthday, Fifthday, Sixthday, and Restday. Seasons are not fixed to particular months and can vary by region or last for extended periods. Month names are traditional and do not guarantee particular weather.

Widely recognized observances include:
- **Winter's End (Thawrise):** Preparation for renewed travel, planting, trade, and outdoor work after winter.
- **First Planting (Greenwake):** The beginning of the agricultural year where soil and weather permit; seed blessings and communal planting are common.
- **Day of the Crown (Suncrest):** A civil observance of the monarchy and post-Rebellion settlement. People celebrate it for different reasons, including stability, tradition, and royal authority.
- **Highsummer Feast (Highsun):** A celebration of abundance, work, food, music, games, and the growing season.
- **Harvest Home (Harvestfall):** A major agricultural observance tied to the harvest, seasonal accounts, food stores, and preparation for winter.
- **Night of Remembrance (Duskreach):** A time to honor the dead through family stories, memorials, lamps, and shared meals.
- **Yearturn:** Five days between years, traditionally associated with remembrance, settling debts, renewing oaths, honoring ancestors, and welcoming the new year.

Local customs differ by region, faith, class, and household. The same observance may have different meanings in different communities.
