# The Imperial Council and Royal Court

**Status:** ESTABLISHED — Stage 9, 2026-11-04  
**Scope:** Current Imperial Council representatives, Crown officers, court faction leadership, and principal political relationships.

The six Council representatives are not automatically the rulers of their Great Houses. Each house appoints its own representative. The Crown's officers serve the monarch and are not representatives of the Great Houses. Religious authorities may advise the Crown but have no automatic Council seat.

## 1. The Imperial Council

The Imperial Council has six permanent seats, one appointed by each Great House. The monarch presides but does not hold an additional Great House seat. The Council remains an aristocratic institution of negotiation and privilege, not a modern parliament. Its exact voting rules, quorum, vetoes, formal powers, and customary procedures remain unresolved.

### House Calderan — Lord Chancellor Othmar Rusk
A senior Crown noble from an old Calderan vassal family, appointed as the Crown's representative to the Council. Rusk is an experienced administrator and negotiator, loyal to the dynasty but not blindly loyal to King Aldric's every decision. He believes the monarchy must preserve its legitimacy by observing the settlement's customs even while gradually expanding its practical authority.

**Council priorities:** Protect the Crown's prerogatives, avoid a united Great House opposition, and keep negotiations alive when compromise is possible.  
**Political character:** Measured, procedural, and difficult to provoke. He is often mistaken for a weak negotiator because he rarely raises his voice.

### House Vaelorian — Lady Ilyra Vaelorian
A senior cousin of Lord Theren and a member of an established commercial branch of the family. Ilyra is a formidable financial negotiator with extensive knowledge of noble debts, trade agreements, and commercial obligations. She is loyal to House Vaelorian's long-term interests, but she does not always agree with Theren or Seralyth on how those interests should be pursued.

**Council priorities:** Protect commercial privileges, oppose arbitrary Crown taxation, preserve trade access, and prevent any single power from controlling the continent's major routes.  
**Political character:** Courteous, exacting, and quietly intimidating. She remembers every concession and every unpaid obligation.

### House Kharadorn — Thane Brokk Ironvein
A senior clan leader appointed by High King Durnak to represent the dwarven empire's interests in Valedorn. Brokk is not a royal family member and takes pride in that independence. He understands surface politics well enough to negotiate, but he has little patience for assumptions that the dwarves are merely another vassal house.

**Council priorities:** Protect dwarven sovereignty, preserve control of Dragonstone and the inland sea, defend clan privileges, and prevent Crown jurisdiction from expanding into dwarven affairs.  
**Political character:** Blunt, patient, and difficult to intimidate. He regards an agreement as meaningful only when its obligations are clear.

### House Edarth — Lady Ysabet Edarth
A cousin of Lord Garrick and a respected representative of several Edarth landed families. Ysabet is more politically flexible than Garrick and believes Edarth's independence is best preserved through alliances and careful negotiation, not reflexive opposition to every royal initiative.

**Council priorities:** Preserve traditional noble rights, protect landed families from extraordinary levies, and ensure that Crown reforms do not weaken local authority.  
**Political character:** Practical and observant. She can cooperate with the Crown without being mistaken for a royal loyalist.

### House Veyr — Ser Merek Veyr
A veteran frontier officer and member of a collateral Veyr branch. Merek has served in the northern territories and understands the cost of maintaining the Ancient Barrier. He is less interested in court ceremony than in securing resources for the north.

**Council priorities:** Secure reliable Crown and Great House support for the Northern Watch, protect Veyr's authority over frontier defense, and prevent southern political disputes from diverting essential supplies.  
**Political character:** Direct, severe, and impatient with ornamental promises. He is respected by military-minded nobles but can alienate negotiators.

### House Merrowen — Lady Cassia Merrowen
A member of a powerful Merrowen maritime branch with close ties to shipowners and merchant families. Cassia is a skilled diplomat who believes Merrowen's influence rests on its ability to keep trade moving. She is close to Lady Maris's commercial circle but is not simply her mouthpiece.

**Council priorities:** Protect maritime privileges, resist excessive customs restrictions, preserve access to ports and markets, and prevent Kharadorn from turning inland-sea control into a monopoly over commerce.  
**Political character:** Charming, adaptable, and relentlessly transactional. She rarely makes an argument without knowing who profits from it.

## 2. The Royal Household and Crown Officers

These officers serve King Aldric and the Crown administration. Their offices are established; the following individuals are the current officeholders.

### Royal Chancellor — Lord Chancellor Othmar Rusk
Rusk also serves as the Crown's appointed representative on the Imperial Council. This dual role gives him unusual access to both royal administration and inter-house negotiation. It also means other representatives sometimes question whether he is acting as a neutral negotiator or the king's agent.

**Responsibilities:** Royal administration, decrees, correspondence, coordination of government business, and management of the Crown's formal dealings with the Council.

### Master of Coin — Lady Meredyth Sorn
A precise, unsentimental administrator from a wealthy but minor Crown-vassal family. She is not a member of House Vaelorian, though she has worked with its lenders and commercial agents for years. Meredyth believes the Crown's finances are dangerously dependent on short-term borrowing and privileges granted to powerful houses.

**Responsibilities:** Royal revenue, taxation, debts, treasury, and financial reporting.  
**Political pressure:** Vaelorian creditors want repayment and influence; the king wants funds without surrendering authority; the Great Houses resist extraordinary taxation.

### Master of Laws — Lord Bastian Wycliffe
A learned jurist from an established Crown family, known for his command of royal precedent and the complicated privileges of the Great Houses. Bastian is cautious about changing law by decree. He believes that a monarch who disregards precedent may win a dispute and lose the legitimacy needed to govern.

**Responsibilities:** Royal justice, major legal disputes, interpretation of Crown law, and advice on the boundaries between royal jurisdiction and noble privilege.  
**Political pressure:** Both royal expansionists and traditionalists attempt to recruit his authority for opposing interpretations of the law.

### Master of War — Marshal Garran Holt
A career soldier of modest noble birth who rose through royal service. Garran is disciplined and competent, with a realistic understanding of the Crown's military limitations. He does not believe the royal forces can defeat all six Great Houses acting together.

**Responsibilities:** Coordination of the royal army, readiness, fortifications, military logistics, and defense planning.  
**Political pressure:** The king wants a more reliable royal force; the Great Houses resist any army that could become an instrument of centralization.

### Royal Spymaster — Mistress Nerys Vale
A discreet, highly connected intelligence officer whose public duties are vague and whose actual network is deliberately compartmentalized. Nerys relies on informants, servants, merchants, messengers, and personal intermediaries rather than assuming that every secret can be bought. Her loyalty is to the Crown as an institution, though she believes the monarch must be protected from dangerous information as well as supplied with it.

**Responsibilities:** Intelligence gathering, informant networks, assessment of threats to the Crown, and coordination of covert information.  
**Political pressure:** Every faction wants information from her; several believe they have sources inside her network. Whether those beliefs are correct is not established.

### Lord Chamberlain — Lord Perrin Ashcombe
An exacting courtier responsible for the royal household's daily operation. Perrin controls schedules, ceremonies, access to the monarch, palace staff, and the practical machinery through which people gain or lose proximity to the Crown. He is not a great strategist, but he understands that access is a form of power.

**Responsibilities:** Royal household, court ceremony, palace administration, and access to the monarch.  
**Political pressure:** Noble families compete for appointments, invitations, private audiences, and positions within the royal household.

## 3. The Five Court Factions

These are political movements and overlapping networks, not formal parties with membership cards. A person may cooperate with one faction on a particular issue while opposing it on another. House affiliation does not determine faction membership.

### The Royal Court
**Principal figure:** Queen Elowen Calderan  
**Key associate:** Lord Chancellor Othmar Rusk

The Royal Court seeks to preserve the monarchy, maintain political stability, and prevent disputes among the Great Houses from becoming another continental war. Its members favor compromise where possible and incremental strengthening of royal institutions where necessary.

The Queen is particularly effective at maintaining relationships with noble families and religious authorities. Rusk handles the administrative and constitutional side of the same project.

**Internal disagreement:** How far the Crown can expand its authority without provoking a coalition of the Great Houses.

### The Traditionalists
**Principal figure:** Lord Garrick Edarth  
**Key associate:** Lady Ysabet Edarth

Traditionalists defend the ancient rights and privileges of the Great Houses and lesser landed nobility. They are not necessarily hostile to the monarchy; they oppose royal actions that threaten hereditary authority, local jurisdiction, and established obligations.

Garrick is an influential voice because of Edarth's military and agricultural strength. Ysabet is more willing to negotiate than he is.

**Internal disagreement:** Whether preserving the settlement requires active resistance to royal reform or selective compromise.

### The Royal Expansionists
**Principal figure:** Princess Maerelle Calderan  
**Key associate:** Marshal Garran Holt

Royal Expansionists believe the Crown must gradually reclaim authority lost after the Great Rebellion. They favor stronger royal administration, more dependable Crown forces, improved taxation, and reduced dependence on Great House cooperation.

Maerelle gives the movement dynastic ambition and court access. Holt is not a declared political partisan, but his arguments for a capable royal army often support expansionist aims. His professional concern is military readiness, not Maerelle's personal ambitions.

**Internal disagreement:** Some favor gradual institutional reform; others believe the Crown must eventually confront the Great Houses directly.

### Merchant Interests
**Principal figure:** Lady Cassia Merrowen  
**Key associate:** Lady Ilyra Vaelorian

Merchant Interests seek stable trade, predictable taxation, secure roads and ports, enforceable contracts, and protection from arbitrary tolls or political blockades. They include merchants, lenders, shipowners, commercial nobles, and officials whose fortunes depend on trade.

They are not a unified merchant party. Vaelorian financiers and Merrowen maritime interests often cooperate, but they compete over routes, fees, lending, and influence.

**Internal disagreement:** Whether commercial stability is best protected by a stronger Crown or by preserving the independence of powerful regional houses.

### The Religious Establishment
**Principal figure:** High Prelate Aveline Marr  
**Key associate:** Prior-General Odran Thrice-Blessed

The Religious Establishment seeks to preserve the independence and influence of organized religious institutions and to strengthen their role in public life. Its leaders maintain relationships with the Crown, Great Houses, temples, monasteries, and religious orders.

Aveline is a senior religious authority with recognized access to the royal court. She is a skilled institutional politician, but she does not command all faiths or speak for every priest. Odran leads a prominent religious order whose members are active in charity, education, and the protection of religious sites.

**Internal disagreement:** Whether religious institutions should remain chiefly advisers and moral authorities or seek a stronger role in secular law and public governance. Different faiths also disagree over doctrine, wealth, and institutional privilege.

## 4. Religious Presence at Court

High Prelate Aveline Marr is the most visible religious adviser in Valedorn's royal court. Her influence derives from institutional relationships, public reputation, and the Crown's desire for religious legitimacy, not from a Council seat or universal authority over the faiths.

Prior-General Odran Thrice-Blessed represents one influential religious order, not the entire Religious Establishment. Other faiths maintain their own senior clergy and may disagree with Aveline's positions.

Neither Aveline nor Odran is assumed to possess divine spellcasting ability. Their offices and influence do not depend on magical power.

## 5. Principal Political Fault Lines in Valedorn

1. **The Crown and the Great Houses:** Aldric wants a stronger monarchy; the Great Houses want the settlement's limits preserved.
2. **Royal administration and noble privilege:** Rusk, Meredyth, and Bastian must navigate the conflict between royal authority, financial dependence, and inherited legal rights.
3. **The army and the balance of power:** Maerelle wants stronger Crown forces; the Great Houses fear a royal army capable of coercion.
4. **Trade and maritime control:** Ilyra and Cassia cooperate on commercial stability but disagree over who should control routes, fees, and access.
5. **The northern burden:** Merek wants resources for Veyr and the Northern Watch; southern representatives resist demands that may become open-ended.
6. **Religious authority:** Aveline seeks institutional independence and influence, while secular rulers want religious legitimacy without surrendering political authority.
7. **Information and access:** Nerys's intelligence network and Perrin's control of court access make both offices valuable to every faction.

## 6. Unresolved Details

- Exact Council voting rules, quorum, vetoes, formal powers, and customary procedures.
- How each Great House appoints or removes its Council representative.
- The size, funding, recruitment, and actual reach of Nerys Vale's intelligence network.
- The exact composition and command structure of the royal military.
- The relative strength, membership, and wider networks of the five court factions.
- The names, doctrines, and institutional structures of the faiths represented by the Religious Establishment.
- The identity and organization of additional religious advisers and scholarly officials.
- The precise legal boundaries between Crown courts, noble jurisdictions, and clerical courts.

These details remain open for future development and play.
