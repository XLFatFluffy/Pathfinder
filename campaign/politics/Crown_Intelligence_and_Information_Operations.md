# Crown Intelligence and Information Operations

**Snapshot:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC  
**Status:** GM operating framework proposal; network size and specific operations remain unresolved  
**Authority:** Established Royal Spymaster role and campaign knowledge-boundary standards.

## 1. Purpose

This document defines a cautious GM reference for how the Crown may gather, evaluate, protect, and act on information without turning the Royal Spymaster into an omniscient observer.

The framework does not establish a secret conspiracy, a current investigation, named agents, a fixed number of informants, or a particular operation.

## 2. Established office and limits

**Royal Spymaster:** Mistress Nerys Vale.

Established description:
- Nerys is discreet and highly connected.
- Her public duties are deliberately vague.
- Her actual network is compartmentalized.
- She relies on informants, servants, merchants, messengers, and personal intermediaries.
- She does not assume every secret can be bought.
- Her loyalty is to the Crown as an institution.
- She believes the monarch must be protected from dangerous information as well as supplied with it.

The Royal Spymaster serves the Crown. The office is not automatically an instrument of the Queen, Princess Maerelle, any court faction, or a Great House.

**Unresolved:** The network's size, funding, recruitment, geographic reach, internal structure, methods, individual agents, and current operations remain unknown.

## 3. Information source categories

The following are possible source categories consistent with the established description. Their existence as categories does not establish a specific informant or report.

| Source category | Potential information | Typical limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Royal household and court staff | Access, schedules, appointments, private disputes, unusual visitors | Personal loyalty, factional pressure, incomplete access, gossip |
| Servants and household intermediaries | Movement, routines, overheard remarks, changes in a household | Limited context, fear of discovery, hearsay |
| Merchants and lenders | Trade disruption, debts, contracts, cargo, price pressure, political spending | Commercial bias, confidentiality, self-interest |
| Messengers and couriers | Route conditions, official correspondence, delays, unusual traffic | They may know delivery, not contents or wider context |
| Ports, pilots, and shipmasters | Vessel movement, harbor conditions, cargo, navigation, coastal rumors | Weather, port jurisdiction, false manifests, commercial rivalry |
| Local officials and reeves | Petitions, disputes, taxes, public order, local decisions | Local bias, limited reach, fear of offending superiors |
| Religious and scholarly contacts | Public sentiment, institutional disputes, travel, records, education | Institutional caution, doctrinal disagreement, confidentiality |
| Military officers and patrols | Readiness, garrisons, road security, frontier conditions | Command restrictions, incomplete reconnaissance, security concerns |
| Paid or voluntary informants | Specific local observations or access | Reliability varies; payment can encourage exaggeration or deception |

No source category grants automatic access to every location, house, council, or private conversation.

## 4. Evaluating information

Use this sequence when the Crown receives potentially important information:

1. **Record the claim.** Preserve the source's actual statement, date received, location, and any known intermediary.
2. **Identify source access.** Could the source directly observe the matter, hear it from someone else, or only infer it?
3. **Separate observation from interpretation.** "A ship arrived after dark" is not the same as "the ship carried spies."
4. **Assess reliability.** Consider track record, motive, proximity, detail, and whether the source risks punishment or expects reward.
5. **Seek corroboration.** Look for independent confirmation where the matter justifies the cost and delay.
6. **Assess urgency.** Distinguish immediate danger from a matter that can wait for further evidence.
7. **Disseminate by need.** Share only with officials who need the information to perform their duties.
8. **Record the decision.** The Crown may act, wait, investigate, protect a source, or deliberately withhold the report.

A report may remain uncertain even after review. Corroboration improves confidence; it does not make an uncertain claim automatically true.

## 5. Compartmentalization

Compartmentalization is an established feature of Nerys's network. Apply it conservatively:

- A source knows what they directly observe and what they are told.
- An intermediary may know who requested information without knowing the ultimate purpose.
- A recipient may receive a report without knowing the original source.
- Separate reports may reach Nerys without their sources knowing of one another.
- Nerys may withhold information from the monarch when she judges immediate disclosure harmful, but this does not establish a specific current disagreement.
- A compromised source does not automatically expose an entire network.

Do not create an elaborate hierarchy, code system, secret headquarters, or universal agent network unless later established.

## 6. Relationship to other Crown offices

| Office | Proper responsibility | Intelligence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Royal Spymaster, Nerys Vale | Gather and assess information for the Crown; coordinate covert information-gathering | Does not automatically command the royal army, courts, or Great House agents |
| Royal Chancellor, Othmar Rusk | Administration, decrees, correspondence, government business, Council dealings | May receive reports needed for administration, but is not the intelligence service |
| Master of Laws, Bastian Wycliffe | Crown law, major legal disputes, precedent, jurisdiction | Evidence used in legal proceedings must be handled through appropriate legal authority |
| Master of War, Marshal Garran Holt | Royal army, readiness, fortifications, logistics, defense planning | Military reconnaissance and readiness reporting remain within military command |
| Lord Chamberlain, Perrin Ashcombe | Household operations, schedules, ceremony, access to the monarch | Controls court access and routine household information, not the whole intelligence network |
| Master of Coin, Meredyth Sorn | Revenue, taxation, debts, treasury, financial reporting | Financial records may inform investigations but do not establish criminal intent by themselves |

The Crown's offices may cooperate, but their responsibilities remain distinct.

## 7. Great House and foreign information networks

- Each Great House has its own household, vassals, officials, merchants, and sources of information.
- The Crown does not automatically receive Great House private reports.
- A Council representative knows what their house has officially communicated and what they personally learn; they are not omniscient.
- Kharadorn clan information and internal affairs are not automatically available to the Crown.
- Merchant and maritime sources may carry information across political boundaries, but commercial contact does not establish loyalty to the Crown.
- Religious institutions and scholarly networks have their own interests, hierarchies, and confidentiality.
- A source may serve multiple interests, knowingly or unknowingly, but do not assume double agents without evidence.

## 8. Rumors, misinformation, and deliberate deception

The Crown may receive:
- accurate reports;
- incomplete but honest observations;
- mistaken interpretations;
- outdated information;
- deliberate lies;
- rumors repeated as fact;
- true information presented with a false explanation;
- fabricated reports designed to waste attention or implicate a rival.

A false report does not automatically mean the source is a traitor. Mistakes, poor access, translation, memory, delay, and local bias are ordinary explanations.

When a report is wrong, preserve what the recipient believed at the time and record when the error was discovered. Do not retroactively make an actor omniscient.

## 9. GM reference: who knows what

When deciding whether an actor plausibly knows something, distinguish:

- **Claim:** What is alleged or observed?
- **Source:** Who or what provided it?
- **Access:** Direct observation, secondhand report, inference, or rumor?
- **Date observed:** When did the source learn it?
- **Date received:** When could the recipient plausibly know it?
- **Reliability:** Unverified, plausible, corroborated, disputed, or disproven.
- **Recipient belief:** What does the actor actually think?
- **Possible response:** What could the actor credibly do with this information, given their authority and resources?
- **Exposure risk:** Could acting reveal the source or method?

Information travel time depends on route, weather, vessel availability, messenger access, secrecy, and institutional delay. Do not assign exact delivery times without a relevant map route and established travel basis.

## 10. Campaign-start baseline

At 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC:

- Nerys Vale holds the established office of Royal Spymaster.
- Her network is described as discreet, connected, and compartmentalized.
- No specific Crown intelligence operation, assassination investigation, infiltration, or secret report is established by this framework.
- No Great House is assumed to be penetrated by Crown agents.
- No actor automatically knows the vampire PC's identity or survival.
- House Relland does not know the ratfolk PC survived the historical incident.
- The dwarven underground route to Dragonstone remains GM-only and unknown to ordinary actors.
- Existing Tallowmere rumors retain their own documented sources and truth status; they are not automatically Crown intelligence.

## 11. Open decisions

- Network size, funding, recruitment, reach, and reporting structure.
- Named agents, informants, handlers, and intermediaries, if the campaign requires them.
- The extent of Crown intelligence activity outside Valedorn.
- Legal and customary limits on covert information gathering.
- How the Crown handles intercepted correspondence, source protection, and information that may implicate powerful nobles.
- The relationship between Nerys's office and local watches, noble retainers, port authorities, and military scouts.

These details remain open until campaign events or GM decisions establish them.
