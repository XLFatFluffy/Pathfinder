# House Relland and the Merchant Incident

**Status:** ESTABLISHED CAMPAIGN BACKGROUND — 2026-11-04  
**Classification:** GM-facing; the PCs' knowledge is limited to what they remember and what they may later discover.  
**Approximate date of incident:** Ten years before Session One.

This record establishes the minor house responsible for the ratfolk PC's family tragedy and the house's response. It does not establish that the wider Relland family personally ordered the murders, nor does it absolve the household of responsibility for what its retainers did.

## 1. House Relland

**House:** Relland  
**Standing:** Minor human noble house  
**Territorial allegiance:** House Velleran  
**Holding:** A modest manor and several tenant holdings along the South Road, within the broader Velleran-chartered lands south of Tallowmere. The precise boundary and surveyed location remain provisional pending regional map integration.  
**Political scale:** Locally established, with limited wealth and retainers; no continent-wide influence and no Imperial Council seat.

House Relland is an old but unremarkable landed family. Its income comes from rents, grazing rights, grain, and control of a small stretch of road frontage. The family has enough retainers to protect its estate and assert its local privileges, but it is not a military power.

### Current family

- **Lady Mereth Relland:** Current head of the house. Careful, proud, and concerned with preserving the family's standing. She inherited the estate after her father's death and maintains the household's official account of the old incident.
- **Ser Corren Relland:** Her adult heir. More interested in restoring the family's income and local reputation than revisiting old household scandals. His knowledge of the incident is incomplete.
- **Lord Edvar Relland:** Mereth's late father and head of House Relland at the time of the incident. He is deceased.

House Relland is not a secret power in Tallowmere. Its influence is local to its own estate and its Velleran connections.

## 2. The Extortion and Murder

Approximately ten years before the campaign, the ratfolk PC was travelling with his parents, a family of itinerant merchants. Their small caravan crossed land claimed by House Relland.

A group of armed men wearing Relland household colors stopped the family. They claimed the merchants owed a road levy and demanded that they surrender their entire stock. The demand was not a lawful toll under any charter known to the regional authorities. It was an extortion scheme carried out by men using the house's name and livery.

The merchants protested. The men forced them to sell or surrender all their wares for a tiny fraction of their actual value, effectively pennies on the gold piece. When the parents continued to resist, the confrontation became violent. The attackers killed both parents and turned on the child.

The vampire PC arrived during the attack, killed the assailants, and rescued the ratfolk child. The child survived because of the vampire's intervention. The two PCs' friendship and mentor-pupil relationship grew from that rescue.

### What the attackers were doing

The group was led by **Ser Deylan Marrick**, a household armsman who had been given authority to supervise road patrols and collect legitimate estate dues. He and several men under his command had been abusing that authority to extort passing traders.

Marrick had no lawful authority to seize an entire merchant family's stock. His men used the threat of detention, violence, and the apparent authority of the Relland livery to make merchants comply.

The extortion was not an official Relland policy or a lawful levy. However, it was made possible by the house's retainers, its symbols, and weak oversight. House Relland bears institutional responsibility even if Lord Edvar did not personally order the attack.

## 3. House Relland's Response

The vampire killed the attackers at the scene. No member of the Relland household witnessed the rescue, and the family received no reliable report that the ratfolk child survived. House Relland therefore believes the entire travelling family died and that the attackers also perished in an encounter whose full details were never publicly established.

After the incident, the household faced an awkward problem: its men had been operating an illegal toll racket, and a dead merchant family could draw questions from other traders.

Lord Edvar's response was a controlled cover-up rather than a public investigation.

- The household described the event as a violent roadside dispute involving suspicious itinerant traders.
- The seized goods were entered in a private account as abandoned or forfeited merchandise, not as property taken by the retainers.
- Ser Deylan Marrick's immediate command was quietly dissolved. The household claimed he had been dismissed for misconduct, but no public charge or trial followed.
- The remaining retainers were reassigned or discharged in ones and twos. No public inquiry was opened into the murders.
- No meaningful compensation was paid to the dead merchants' family, and no apology was issued.
- House Relland preserved its official position that the merchants had been involved in an unlawful dispute and that the house had already dealt with the matter internally.

This response limited immediate scandal but did not erase the truth. A few old household accounts and the memories of former retainers may contradict the official version.

### What House Relland knows now

Lady Mereth knows that her father's retainers were accused privately of abusing road authority and that the household quietly removed several men. She does not know the full circumstances of the murders and has accepted her father's explanation that the matter was an ugly but contained disciplinary problem.

Ser Corren knows only that his grandfather dismissed several retainers after an incident on the South Road. He has not been told that a travelling family was murdered.

Neither has reason to know that the ratfolk child survived or that the vampire PC was present. The PCs' identities and the rescue are not common knowledge.

## 4. Evidence and Possible Future Discovery

No single piece of evidence automatically proves the whole event. Potential sources include:

- **Old household accounts:** A ledger entry records goods taken from a travelling family as forfeited stock, though the quantities and valuation do not match a lawful levy.
- **A former retainer:** One discharged man remembers Marrick's racket and knows that the household chose quiet dismissal over public prosecution. He may be reluctant to speak.
- **The ratfolk PC's memory:** He remembers the attack, his parents, the men, and the vampire's rescue. His account is genuine testimony, but the passage of time and the absence of surviving witnesses may make formal proof difficult.
- **The vampire PC's testimony:** The vampire can describe what he saw and did. Whether he wishes to reveal himself or his involvement is entirely his choice.
- **A surviving merchant contact:** The family's former trade partners may remember that the family disappeared on the South Road, but they may not know what happened.

Do not place all this evidence in one convenient location. Discovery should require investigation, relationships, or credible leads.

## 5. Current House Relland Position

House Relland is not currently hunting the PCs, because it does not know they survived or were involved. It is not automatically an enemy of Tallowmere, House Denvor, or the ratfolk community.

If the incident becomes public, likely responses are:
- Lady Mereth initially protects the family's reputation and repeats the official account.
- Ser Corren may be more concerned with the practical damage to the house's standing and may be willing to investigate if shown credible evidence.
- Former retainers may deny, minimize, or confirm parts of the story depending on their own culpability and loyalties.
- House Velleran may prefer the matter remain local unless it becomes a public scandal or threatens the road's commercial reputation.

House Relland can be held accountable through evidence, political pressure, legal proceedings, negotiation, or other player-chosen action. No particular outcome is predetermined.

## 6. Boundaries and Continuity

- The incident occurred approximately ten years before Session One.
- The ratfolk PC was a child; his exact age is not established until character creation.
- His parents were travelling merchants and were murdered by the extortionists.
- The vampire PC killed the attackers and saved the child.
- The responsible men were associated with House Relland, a minor house under House Velleran.
- The extortion was not a lawful tax or toll.
- House Relland concealed the misconduct rather than making public amends.
- The house does not know the ratfolk child survived.
- The event does not implicate a Great House and does not create a continent-wide conspiracy.
- The PCs decide what they disclose and whether they pursue the matter.
