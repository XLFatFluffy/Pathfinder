# Hideout and Base Development System

**Status:** APPROVED FRAMEWORK  
**Visibility:** GM reference  
**Applies to:** Current campaign  
**Ruleset:** Pathfinder First Edition

## Purpose

The player characters begin the campaign with a shared hideout rather than being assigned a finished fortress or estate. The hideout is a player-created campaign asset. Its design establishes what the characters value, what capabilities they prioritize, and what kind of organization they may eventually build.

The hideout is not a complete power base at the start. It is the first stage of a longer progression:

**Hideout → Headquarters → Estate/Compound → Stronghold → Seat of Power → Territory → Regional Power → Continental/World Power**

The PCs' ambition to accumulate wealth, influence, territory, followers, military strength, intelligence, and magical capability can therefore be represented through the gradual development of the base.

## Starting Development

The PCs receive **20 Base Development Points** at campaign creation.

These points represent the hideout's unusual capabilities, specialized construction, security, facilities, and infrastructure. Ordinary necessities such as floors, ordinary walls, basic doors, beds, a simple cooking area, and mundane household storage are assumed unless the chosen location makes them unavailable.

The PCs also receive **10 Starting Resource Points** for initial supplies, furnishings, tools, and other non-character resources. Character starting equipment remains governed by Pathfinder character creation and the final approved character sheets.

The starting hideout should remain appropriate to the PCs' actual starting wealth, level, location, and circumstances. The 20 points do not automatically mean they own a castle.

## Base Development Points

Base Development Points are a measure of the hideout's development capacity.

They are **not the same thing as gold**.

A player may spend a Base Development Point to establish a facility, improvement, security feature, or other approved base capability. The GM determines the actual physical requirements, availability, construction time, and gold cost when those details matter.

### Core rule

**Development Points unlock the capability. Gold pays for the actual work, equipment, materials, labor, or acquisition.**

This distinction prevents the base from becoming a free collection of buildings simply because the players have accumulated points.

A facility may also be acquired through play rather than constructed. Purchasing, inheriting, capturing, repairing, discovering, or otherwise obtaining an existing property can change its point requirements at the GM's discretion.

## Functional and Mechanical Benefits

The current point costs describe the base's structure, not its final mechanics.

The GM will determine which facilities:

- provide bonuses to relevant activities;
- reduce penalties or risks;
- improve research, crafting, healing, training, or information gathering;
- unlock access to higher-quality equipment or services;
- reduce the cost or time of certain activities;
- permit activities that could not reasonably be performed without the facility;
- attract or support specialists;
- improve the base's security or resistance to intrusion;
- create additional campaign opportunities.

These mechanics are deliberately not exposed in the player creation document.

A facility should not automatically grant a broad numerical bonus when a narrower, more meaningful benefit would better fit the campaign.

## How the PCs Earn More Development Points

Development Points should not simply arrive because a character leveled up. The base is part of the campaign world, not a second character sheet wearing a tiny hat.

The recommended progression is a **milestone system**:

### Normal advancement

The party gains **1 Base Development Point at major character-development milestones**, normally tied to gaining a character level or an equivalent campaign advancement.

The GM may delay or combine this point when the characters have not had meaningful opportunity to develop the base.

### Major accomplishments

The party can earn additional Base Development Points for accomplishments that materially increase their ability to develop or sustain the base.

Examples include:

- acquiring a significant property;
- establishing a valuable trade connection;
- securing a new source of materials;
- gaining a powerful patron or ally;
- bringing a specialist into their organization;
- completing a major construction project;
- claiming or securing territory;
- establishing a meaningful intelligence network;
- recovering a significant magical resource;
- defeating or removing a threat that materially affected the base;
- completing a major campaign objective.

These should generally award **1–3 points**, depending on significance.

### Gold

Gold does **not** directly purchase Base Development Points.

Gold pays for:

- construction;
- renovation;
- materials;
- labor;
- furnishings;
- equipment;
- security;
- specialist work;
- maintenance;
- staffing;
- supplies;
- acquisition of existing property.

This means the PCs can have available Development Points but still lack the money or opportunity to use them.

Conversely, they may discover or purchase an existing facility and avoid some construction requirements.

### Acquired Facilities

If the PCs acquire an existing building, estate, ruin, workshop, tower, tunnel, or other useful property during play, the GM may award some of its development value without requiring the PCs to spend their own points.

The condition, ownership, security, legal status, location, and usefulness of the property still matter.

## Base Growth and Campaign Scale

The base should grow as the PCs' power grows.

Early game:
- hideout;
- personal quarters;
- secure storage;
- specialized workspaces;
- limited contacts.

Middle game:
- headquarters;
- staff;
- workshops;
- intelligence network;
- guards;
- larger stores;
- multiple buildings.

Later game:
- estate;
- fortified compound;
- military infrastructure;
- political offices;
- larger intelligence apparatus;
- magical facilities;
- territory.

Endgame:
- stronghold;
- regional administration;
- military headquarters;
- major magical center;
- political seat;
- capital or conquered center of power.

The base therefore becomes one of the campaign's primary records alongside character progression, wealth, allies, enemies, territory, and influence.
