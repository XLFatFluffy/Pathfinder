# Character–World Boundary Model

**Status:** Phase 5 operational model established  
**Campaign date:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC

The World Engine and Character Engine are separate systems that exchange only meaningful state changes.

## World Engine owns

- macro political conditions;
- Great House and faction objectives;
- economic and military conditions;
- regional projects;
- diplomacy;
- seasonal and environmental pressure;
- world events occurring independently of the PCs;
- actor knowledge and public information;
- long-term consequences that do not depend on a PC's immediate scene.

## Character Engine owns

- PC statistics and character-sheet state;
- player decisions;
- conversations and scene actions;
- exploration and immediate discoveries;
- combat resolution;
- personal inventory and equipment;
- personal relationships;
- character goals;
- downtime choices;
- consequences that originate directly from PC actions.

## Boundary event

A boundary event is created when information or consequences cross between systems.

Examples:

- A PC exposes a smuggling operation → World Engine receives a confirmed criminal-network disruption.
- A Great House changes a harbor policy → Character Engine receives a public or discoverable change affecting available trade.
- A PC earns a political obligation → World Engine receives the new relationship or obligation.
- A regional shortage affects the town → Character Engine receives the practical local consequence.

## Required fields

Every boundary event should identify:

- event ID;
- originating system;
- date;
- actors;
- objective event;
- affected world-state records;
- affected character-state records;
- information visibility;
- source record;
- GM disposition.

## Information rule

Crossing the boundary does not mean universal knowledge.

A PC action may be objectively recorded while remaining unknown to:

- other PCs;
- NPCs;
- Great Houses;
- the public;
- or the World Engine's other actors.

Likewise, a world event may affect the PCs without them immediately knowing its cause.

## Pre-campaign protection

Events dated before the PCs entered play must never be attributed to them retroactively.

## GM control

The World Engine may identify plausible consequences, but it does not automatically decide dramatic PC consequences. The GM reviews material changes before they become established campaign history.
