# Continental Strategic Pressure Register

**Snapshot:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC  
**Status:** GM reference; synthesis of established Great House, Crown, and political canon.  
**Purpose:** Give the Living World Engine a wider political context without manufacturing a current crisis.

This register captures structural tensions that already exist in the setting. They are not dated events, active wars, secret conspiracies, or guaranteed future plots. A pressure becomes an active issue only when a specific actor takes action, conditions materially change, or evidence establishes a concrete dispute.

## Status key

- **STRUCTURAL:** A continuing political or economic condition.
- **WATCH:** Worth reviewing when relevant time passes or new evidence appears.
- **ACTIVE ISSUE:** A concrete, current dispute or organized effort. None of the entries below is automatically an active issue.
- **UNKNOWN:** Deliberately unresolved. Do not fill it by simulation.

## Continental pressures

### CSP-001 — Crown authority and Great House autonomy

**Status:** STRUCTURAL  
**Principal actors:** House Calderan / Crown; all five other Great Houses; Imperial Council; overlapping court movements.

**Established condition:** King Aldric Calderan seeks to strengthen royal authority through appointments, administration, taxation, and strategic alliances without provoking a united Great House response. The Great Houses publicly uphold the post-Rebellion settlement while protecting their own rights and interests. The Imperial Council provides a negotiating forum, but its detailed voting and enforcement procedures remain unresolved.

**Why it matters:** A royal appointment, tax proposal, legal reform, military levy, or intervention in a house's local jurisdiction may draw different responses from different houses. Support may be conditional, delayed, or traded for concessions.

**Plausible mechanisms if circumstances arise:** Petition, Council negotiation, court lobbying, refusal or delay of cooperation, legal challenge, appointment contest, or negotiated compromise.

**Unknown:** Current private bargains, exact Council procedures, relative faction influence, and any specific proposal not recorded in the campaign documents.

**Boundary:** No current constitutional crisis, coordinated rebellion, or secret plan to depose the king is established.

### CSP-002 — Kharadorn control of the Inland Sea and Merrowen maritime interests

**Status:** STRUCTURAL  
**Principal actors:** House Kharadorn; House Merrowen; Crown; merchants and shipping interests where documented.

**Established condition:** Kharadorn controls Dragonstone and the central inland sea and retains strategic defense and charter rights. Merrowen seeks to protect eastern ports and shipping and preserve commercial independence. Maris Merrowen wants freer navigation than Kharadorn is inclined to allow.

**Why it matters:** Navigation rules, harbor access, inspections, convoy arrangements, tolls, naval deployments, or the treatment of merchant vessels could create friction. The local Tallowmere harbor review is legitimate charter oversight and is not itself evidence of a wider confrontation.

**Plausible mechanisms if circumstances arise:** Diplomatic protest, charter interpretation, commercial pressure, negotiated navigation terms, inspection disputes, or defensive demonstrations.

**Unknown:** Exact naval strengths, fleet deployments, current navigation proposals, and the detailed legal scope of sea-control rights.

**Boundary:** No active naval blockade, declared maritime dispute, or imminent war is established.

### CSP-003 — Vaelorian finance and the political price of credit

**Status:** STRUCTURAL  
**Principal actors:** House Vaelorian; Crown; Great Houses and commercial borrowers where established.

**Established condition:** Vaelorian possesses major financial and commercial leverage. The Crown needs Vaelorian financial support, and Theren expects influence in return. Vaelorian influence is substantial but does not amount to control over the Crown, every lender, or all continental commerce.

**Why it matters:** Credit, refinancing, investment, trade access, and financial guarantees can influence political choices without requiring armies. A borrower under pressure may seek alternate lenders, accept conditions, delay a project, or incur reputational costs.

**Plausible mechanisms if circumstances arise:** Negotiated credit, changed lending terms, competing commercial arrangements, public sponsorship, or refusal to finance a project.

**Unknown:** Specific Crown debts, lending agreements, amounts, maturities, default risks, and private financial arrangements.

**Boundary:** No particular debt crisis, blackmail arrangement, or Vaelorian plan to control the monarchy is established.

### CSP-004 — Northern frontier needs and southern support

**Status:** STRUCTURAL  
**Principal actors:** House Veyr; Crown; House Edarth; House Kharadorn where relevant to established relationships.

**Established condition:** Veyr controls most of the northern frontier and maintains the Northern Watch and surviving portions of the Ancient Barrier. The house needs reliable resources and support from the south. Sparse settlement and dependence on southern supplies constrain it. The Barrier's builders, original purpose, and any supernatural threat remain unresolved.

**Why it matters:** Requests for grain, materials, troops, repairs, or political attention may compete with southern priorities. Delayed support can affect Veyr's readiness or willingness to cooperate, but the specific consequence depends on what was requested and what is actually available.

**Plausible mechanisms if circumstances arise:** Formal petition, supply negotiation, resource allocation, local substitution, political pressure, or a change in frontier policy.

**Unknown:** Current reserve levels, exact needs, supply contracts, Barrier condition beyond established records, and the nature of any threat beyond it.

**Boundary:** No new invasion, Barrier failure, or imminent northern catastrophe is established.

### CSP-005 — Edarth and the defense of traditional noble rights

**Status:** STRUCTURAL  
**Principal actors:** House Edarth; Crown; other Great Houses; lesser landed houses where relevant.

**Established condition:** Edarth seeks to preserve traditional noble rights, resist royal centralization, and maintain its agricultural and military strength. Garrick Edarth is suspicious of Crown centralization, but the house may negotiate or cooperate when its rights and interests are protected.

**Why it matters:** Crown law, taxation, military obligations, appointments, or intervention in lesser-house relationships may draw Edarth scrutiny. Its position may overlap with other houses on one issue and diverge on another.

**Plausible mechanisms if circumstances arise:** Legal argument, Council opposition, coalition-building, negotiated exemptions, or support conditioned on protections.

**Unknown:** Any current legal challenge, coordinated opposition, specific lesser-house petition, or private agreement.

**Boundary:** Traditionalism is not automatic rebellion. No current Edarth-led uprising or organized anti-Crown conspiracy is established.

### CSP-006 — Fragmented Southern Reaches

**Status:** STRUCTURAL GEOPOLITICAL CONDITION  
**Principal actors:** Southern principalities, independent cities, island powers, minor houses, and the Crown where it has a documented interest.

**Established condition:** The Southern Reaches are politically fragmented. They contain principalities, independent towns and cities, island powers, and lesser noble territories rather than one unified southern kingdom.

**Why it matters:** A local succession, trade agreement, port dispute, marriage, or change of allegiance could matter greatly to neighboring powers while remaining marginal to the Great Houses. Different southern actors may have unrelated interests and should not be treated as a unified bloc.

**Plausible mechanisms if circumstances arise:** Local treaty, marriage alliance, commercial agreement, succession claim, changing allegiance, or a conflict between identifiable neighboring powers.

**Unknown:** The full roster of southern rulers, precise borders, treaty networks, military capabilities, and current local disputes.

**Boundary:** Fragmentation alone does not establish a war, succession crisis, or Crown campaign.

## Review rules

1. Review a structural pressure when a relevant actor gains new information, resources, opportunity, or a reason to act.
2. Do not advance it merely because a session ended or a world-turn report needs more entries.
3. Before creating an active issue, identify the specific actor, objective, action or contested condition, location, evidence, and likely opposition.
4. Keep separate the public position, private belief, and actual decision of each actor.
5. Record proposed developments as proposals until the GM approves them.
6. A structural pressure may remain stable for years, change gradually, or become irrelevant after player actions.

## Campaign-start guardrail

At 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC, these pressures provide context for future simulation. They do **not** establish a current Great House war, active continental border dispute, assassination plot, succession emergency, naval blockade, or northern invasion. The Living World Engine should generate such developments only from a credible chain of causes and a GM-approved world-state change.
