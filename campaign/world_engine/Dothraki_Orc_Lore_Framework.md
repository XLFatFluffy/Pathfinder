# Dothraki / Orc Lore Framework

**Status:** Phase 4 framework established from existing campaign canon  
**Campaign date:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC

This record establishes the current Dothraki/orc canon boundary for the Living World Engine. It does **not** fill unresolved cultural, geographic, political, military, religious, economic, or historical details merely to make the setting appear complete.

## Established canon

The following statements are supported by the current campaign records:

- The Dothraki are an established orc people.
- The wider world knows of the Dothraki.
- The Dothraki are feared by the wider world.
- Detailed Dothraki lore has not yet been established in the campaign records.
- The Dothraki therefore exist as a real world actor/culture even though many details about them remain unresolved.

## Lore domains still unresolved

The following areas remain open unless a dedicated source establishes them:

1. **Self-identification and terminology**
   - What the Dothraki call themselves.
   - Whether "Dothraki" is their own name, an external name, or both.
   - Internal terms for people, groups, leaders, outsiders, territory, and institutions.

2. **Culture and social organization**
   - Family and kinship structures.
   - Marriage and inheritance customs.
   - Status and social hierarchy.
   - Hospitality, honor, obligation, punishment, and dispute resolution.
   - Gender roles and expectations.
   - Childhood, adulthood, elder status, and death customs.

3. **Political organization**
   - Whether the Dothraki are unified, divided, federated, tribal, clan-based, or organized through another structure.
   - Leadership titles and succession.
   - Major groups or factions.
   - Relationships among Dothraki groups.
   - Whether any central authority exists.

4. **Territory and geography**
   - Exact homeland or homelands.
   - Borders.
   - Settlements, camps, strongholds, or seasonal movement patterns.
   - Routes and travel distances.
   - Relationship to established continental geography.
   
   No new Dothraki territory or map geometry should be added until supported by authoritative campaign material or an explicit GM decision.

5. **Military organization**
   - Force organization.
   - Command structure.
   - Recruitment and obligations.
   - Weapons, armor, mounts, logistics, and battlefield doctrine.
   - Strategic objectives.
   - Historical military conflicts.
   
   The existing fear associated with the Dothraki does not by itself establish any particular troop count, doctrine, or capability.

6. **Religion and belief**
   - Gods, spirits, ancestors, sacred places, religious specialists, rituals, and taboos.
   - Whether religious authority is centralized or local.
   - Relationship between religion and political leadership.
   
   Existing campaign rules concerning the Pathfinder pantheon do not automatically establish Dothraki religious practice.

7. **Economy and material life**
   - Food production and acquisition.
   - Livestock and other resources.
   - Trade.
   - Currency or exchange practices.
   - Craft production.
   - Relations with settled markets.
   
   No production totals, wealth figures, prices, or trade routes are established here.

8. **Relations with the wider world**
   - Which powers have direct contact with the Dothraki.
   - Diplomatic relationships.
   - Trade or raiding relationships.
   - Historical wars and agreements.
   - What outsiders believe about them versus what is actually true.

9. **History**
   - Origin stories.
   - Major migrations.
   - Major wars.
   - Relationships with the dragon-riding dynasty and Great Houses.
   - Historical changes in Dothraki organization.
   
   No historical event should be inferred solely from the fact that the Dothraki are feared.

10. **Language and knowledge**
    - Language family and dialects.
    - Literacy.
    - Written traditions.
    - Oral history.
    - What Dothraki know about the settled continent.
    - What settled peoples know accurately about the Dothraki.

## World Engine treatment

The Dothraki should be represented as a real cultural/world actor, but the engine must not manufacture missing details.

The engine may track:

- established relationships;
- known external perceptions;
- verified contact;
- discovered information;
- actor goals once those goals are established;
- resources once established;
- military conditions once established;
- projects once established;
- consequences of future events.

The engine must not infer a complete Dothraki society from the word "orc," from fear of the Dothraki, or from external fantasy conventions.

## Information boundaries

Different actors may possess different information about the Dothraki.

The following distinctions should remain available:

- **Objective truth:** what is actually established as true in the campaign.
- **Dothraki knowledge:** what Dothraki actors know.
- **Settled-world knowledge:** what outsiders know.
- **Public belief:** widely repeated claims that may be incomplete or wrong.
- **Rumor:** claims lacking sufficient verification.
- **GM unresolved:** information not yet established for the campaign.

A discovery about the Dothraki should change the knowledge state of the discoverer and only the appropriate downstream actors. It must not automatically reveal the information to the entire world.

## Development rule

Future Dothraki lore should be added in discrete, source-backed records. Each addition should identify whether it is:

- **ESTABLISHED** — approved campaign canon.
- **SIMULATED** — a result of World Engine play.
- **PROPOSED** — a candidate awaiting GM approval.
- **RUMOR** — an in-world claim not established as objective truth.
- **UNRESOLVED** — intentionally left open.

This framework exists to prevent the Phase 4 work from quietly turning assumptions into canon. Humanity has invented enough accidental history already.
