# Dragon and Wider-World Integration Framework

**Status:** Phase 4 integration established  
**Campaign date:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC

This record integrates the established dragon canon with the World Engine without selecting a future outcome.

## Established canon

- Dragons are real.
- A dragon-riding dynasty ruled from the Imperial Seat for approximately 400 years.
- The dragon-riding dynasty possessed exactly twelve dragons at its historical peak.
- The Great Rebellion was fought in part to end dragon-riding supremacy.
- Most dragons were killed during the rebellion.
- The dragon-riding dynasty fell.
- The dwarven victors claimed Dragonstone.
- The present fate of surviving dragons is unresolved.
- The future relevance of surviving dragons is unresolved.

## Unresolved

The World Engine must not silently resolve:

- the exact number or identity of surviving dragons;
- their present locations;
- their condition;
- whether they remain active in the world;
- who, if anyone, can interact with them;
- whether dragon-riding can return;
- the precise nature of any future dragon-related event;
- the relationship between dragons and the eventual global transformation.

## Historical versus current knowledge

Historical facts about the dragon-riding dynasty are distinct from present-day knowledge about surviving dragons.

A character may know that dragons once existed without knowing whether any survive.

A historical record, rumor, artifact, or witness account may provide evidence without automatically establishing objective truth.

The Discovery Model governs how new evidence changes actor knowledge.

## World Engine rule

Dragons should remain available as a long-term world condition and discovery domain, not as a predetermined plot trigger.

A dragon-related world fork may be created only when accumulated evidence and world state make materially different futures plausible.

The engine must not create a dragon event merely because the campaign has not used dragons recently.

## Material distinctions

The campaign distinguishes ordinary Obsydian black onyx from dragon-made dragonglass. Both have established necromantic properties, but they are not the same substance and their precise uses remain unresolved.

See campaign/magic/Obsydian_and_Dragonglass.md for the material record.
