# Living World Engine — Information Model

**Status:** Phase 1 core model
**Campaign start:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC

## Purpose

The information model prevents the World Engine from treating the setting as omniscient.

The same event may be objectively true while unknown to one actor, misunderstood by another, rumored publicly, or intentionally hidden from players.

## Information layers

### Objective truth
What the campaign record establishes as actually true. Objective truth is not automatically public knowledge.

### Actor knowledge
What a specific actor plausibly knows. Each entry should identify actor, information, source, acquisition context where useful, confidence, and limitations.

### Actor belief
What an actor believes to be true. Beliefs may be wrong and must not be converted silently into objective truth.

### Public knowledge
Information broadly available in the relevant community or region. Public knowledge is geographically and socially scoped.

### Player knowledge
Information intentionally available to the PCs. It may include public facts, discoveries, rumors, or GM-provided information, but it must not automatically include GM-only material.

## Rumors

A rumor is an information object, not a truth value.

Track claim, source, audience, spread, confidence, objective status if known, and competing versions.

A rumor may be true, partly true, false, or unresolved. Repetition is not verification.

## Information propagation

When information moves between actors, evaluate:

**Source → channel → audience → delay → distortion → resulting knowledge**

Established channels may include direct conversation, official records, civic notices, merchants, travelers, religious institutions, letters, informal networks, ratfolk community networks, and Crown intelligence where specifically established.

The existence of a channel does not imply universal access.

## Information constraints

An actor cannot know an event that has not reached them, know a secret merely because the GM knows it, infer exact numbers without a credible source, identify a hidden actor without evidence, or react to a player action before it could plausibly become known.

## Confidence labels

Useful qualitative labels are certain, strong, probable, uncertain, rumor, disputed, and unknown.

These describe the actor's information state, not objective truth.

## Visibility

Every world-engine result should be assignable to:

- GM-ONLY
- ACTOR-PRIVATE
- LIMITED / LOCAL
- PUBLIC
- PLAYER-SHARED
- DISCOVERABLE

Visibility is not the same thing as truth.

## Discovery

Discovery changes knowledge, not necessarily the underlying world.

A PC discovering a hidden fact should create a discovery event, a knowledge update for the relevant PC or party, and any consequences caused by the discovery itself.

Do not rewrite the original hidden condition simply because it was discovered.

## Safeguards

- Knowledge is actor-specific.
- Belief is not truth.
- Rumor is not fact.
- Public information is scoped.
- GM knowledge is not automatically player knowledge.
- Player knowledge is not automatically world knowledge.
- Unknown remains unknown until evidence or approved development establishes otherwise.
