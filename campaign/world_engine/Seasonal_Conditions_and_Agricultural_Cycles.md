# Seasonal Conditions and Agricultural Cycles

**Snapshot:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC  
**Status:** GM operating framework proposal; regional climate tendencies require GM review  
**Authority:** Campaign Calendar, authoritative continental artwork, Continental Economic Network, and WTR-001 opening baseline.

## 1. Purpose and limits

This framework gives the Living World Engine a consistent way to account for seasons, agriculture, travel, trade, and military logistics. It does not assign fixed weather to calendar months or create a current storm, drought, frost, failed harvest, or other event.

The calendar explicitly states that month names do not determine weather or season. Seasons may arrive early or late, last months or years, and vary by region. The GM records actual seasonal conditions when they become relevant.

**Campaign-start safeguard:** WTR-001 records the Northern Watch autumn provisioning project and Northfield harvest monitoring, but does not record a specific temperature, rainfall pattern, storm, frost, or other weather condition on 1 Harvestfall. Do not invent one.

## 3. Agricultural cycle

Agricultural timing is regional and weather-dependent. Use the following sequence as a general model, not a rigid calendar:

1. **Land preparation:** repair tools, prepare fields, maintain drainage, arrange seed and labor.
2. **Planting:** sow crops when soil, moisture, and local conditions permit.
3. **Growing:** monitor rain, temperature, pests, labor, livestock, and water.
4. **Harvest:** gather crops at local maturity; weather and labor determine pace and losses.
5. **Storage and transport:** dry, thresh, store, sell, and move produce; protect stores from damp, pests, fire, and theft.
6. **Winter preparation:** secure fodder, fuel, repairs, clothing, stores, and transport before conditions worsen.

The timing of these stages may differ between the north, highlands, central plains, riverlands, and southern zones. A month name is never sufficient evidence that a stage has begun or ended.

## 4. Regional economic and logistical effects

### Grain and livestock
- Edarth, the Crownlands, and fertile southern lowlands are important agricultural sources.
- Veyr, Kharadorn, Merrowen, and major cities depend on reliable supply from productive regions.
- Poor local yields first affect the farms and markets directly connected to them. Wider effects require transport links, reserves, and time.
- Livestock may buffer some food pressure but requires pasture, fodder, water, and winter shelter.

### Timber and fuel
- Northern and western woodlands support timber, charcoal, pitch, construction, shipbuilding, mining, and heating.
- Wet ground, snow, and local access rights can delay cutting and transport.
- Increased cutting can create future shortages or customary-right disputes, but does not automatically cause a crisis.

### Rivers and roads
- Rainfall and snowmelt may affect crossings, ferries, bridges, mills, and road conditions.
- A river crossing can become difficult without being completely impassable.
- Road disruption should identify the specific stretch, cause, duration, available alternatives, and affected traffic.

### Maritime systems
- The Central Inland Sea and eastern coast are separate maritime systems with distinct authorities and trade relationships.
- Storms, fog, winds, pilotage, harbor capacity, and vessel availability can delay shipping.
- A delay is not a blockade. A storm is not an attack. A change in navigation conditions does not automatically alter sovereignty.

### Arid southern territories
- Wells, cisterns, oases, and caravan stops are strategic local resources.
- Dry periods can increase the value of water rights and transport access.
- A drought becomes a political issue only when evidence shows a specific shortfall, affected communities, and competing claims or responses.

## 5. Seasonal conditions for the Living World Engine

When a world turn advances, record only the level of detail needed by active issues and projects:

| Field | Values / guidance |
|---|---|
| Regional season | GM-described condition; do not infer from month name |
| Temperature | Broad description such as cold, mild, warm, hot, or variable |
| Precipitation | Dry, normal, wet, or unusually wet; identify region and duration |
| Ground / water | Dry, firm, muddy, flooded, frozen, or thawing where relevant |
| Agricultural stage | Preparation, planting, growing, harvest, storage, or winter preparation |
| Travel effect | None, slower, restricted, or impassable for a specific route |
| Trade effect | None, delayed, rerouted, cost pressure, or supply interruption |
| Military effect | None, readiness burden, supply delay, restricted movement, or defensive advantage |

Do not fill every field for every region every turn. Record only meaningful conditions that affect an actor, issue, project, or player-facing event.

## 6. Event and consequence rules

Before creating a seasonal event, answer:

1. Which region is affected?
2. What actual weather or environmental condition occurred?
3. How long has it lasted?
4. What evidence would local actors have?
5. Which crops, roads, crossings, ports, stores, or communities are exposed?
6. What reserves or alternative routes exist?
7. Which actors have the means and incentive to respond?
8. What is the smallest credible consequence?

Use gradual propagation. For example:

**poor local harvest → lower local market supply → reserve use or imports → price pressure → merchant and lordly decisions → possible wider effects if transport or reserves fail.**

Do not jump directly from one poor harvest to continent-wide famine. Do not create a severe winter, drought, flood, or storm merely to make a world-turn report interesting.

## 7. Campaign-start baseline

At 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC:

- The calendar month is Harvestfall, but the month name alone does not establish weather.
- WTR-001 records Northern Watch autumn provisioning as underway / seasonal.
- Northfield harvest monitoring remains WATCH; the harvest is expected to be adequate but uneven, with reported farm damage and labor shortages.
- No famine, continent-wide trade collapse, major road closure, port blockade, drought, flood, or severe weather event is established.
- Normal seasonal trade is assumed in the Continental Economic Network.
- No additional weather condition should be added to the opening baseline without GM approval or an observed event.

## 8. Open decisions

- Whether the continent's broad climate tendencies should be further defined beyond the visible terrain and current regional descriptions.
- Local planting and harvest calendars for specific crops and regions.
- Typical flood, snow, storm, and drought patterns where they become relevant.
- Seasonal fishing, shipping, and caravan windows.
- Historical extreme seasons and their political/economic consequences.

These details may be developed region by region as play or world simulation requires. They do not need to be fixed before Session One.
