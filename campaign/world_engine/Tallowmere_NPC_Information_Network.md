# Tallowmere NPC and Information Network

**Snapshot:** 1 Harvestfall, 243 AC  
**Visibility:** GM-ONLY  
**Status:** Supporting reference built from established NPCs, factions, issues, and rumor records.

This record describes plausible information channels without creating a secret master network. Knowing a person does not mean knowing everything they know.

## 1. Civic and noble channels

### House Denvor
Primary channels:
- Reeve's Hall and civic petitions.
- Harbor administration.
- Household servants and officers.
- Noble and merchant social contacts.

Likely information:
- Formal petitions.
- Harbor and infrastructure concerns.
- Public disputes.
- Matters that reach the household through official channels.

Limits:
- Private household decisions remain private unless leaked or publicly discussed.

### Reeve Marrec Holt
Primary channels:
- Petitions.
- Notices.
- Civic records.
- Ordinary residents and merchants.

Likely information:
- Public complaints.
- Requests for leases and market access.
- Routine administrative disputes.

Limits:
- Does not automatically know private criminal activity, Underways matters, or noble secrets.

### Harbor Master Ysella Vane
Primary channels:
- Harbor Office.
- Captains and ship crews.
- Wharf labor.
- Cargo and berth paperwork.

Likely information:
- Shipping activity.
- Berth disputes.
- Cargo declarations.
- Practical harbor disruptions.

Limits:
- Cargo records are not equivalent to proof of criminal intent.

## 3. Ratfolk channels

### Tikka Vell
Public-facing market-access advocate. Likely to know:
- Ratfolk traders seeking stalls.
- Market reactions to open sales.
- Public petition activity.

### Miraq Ashwhisk
Commercially oriented. Likely to know:
- Customers.
- Workshops.
- Trade opportunities.
- Practical reactions from merchants.

### Nix-of-the-Cistern
Safety-focused. Likely to know:
- Household concerns.
- Retaliation fears.
- Safe routes and trusted contacts within her network.

### Vessa Reedtail
Community safety and consent. Likely to know:
- Household-level concerns.
- Whether particular proposals are trusted.

No single ratfolk NPC represents all Underways communities.

## 4. Criminal and informal channels

The existing criminal-operator records remain authoritative.

Informal actors may know:
- Private debts.
- Smuggling opportunities.
- Vulnerable traders.
- Rumors not recorded in official notices.

They do not automatically know:
- Every crime in the city.
- Every Underways route.
- Noble secrets.
- A player character's private identity or history without a plausible source.

## 5. Rumor movement

Rumors should move through plausible channels.

| Information | Strongest likely channel |
|---|---|
| Harbor berth dispute | Captains, harbor workers, merchants |
| Warehouse fires | Warehouse workers, guild members, nearby residents |
| Harvest concerns | Farmers, millers, grain merchants |
| Ratfolk market petition | Market traders, ratfolk advocates, Reeve's Hall |
| Kharadorn review | Harbor Office, Denvor administration, merchants |
| Labor and credit disputes | Guilds, lenders, merchants |
| Longmead dispute | Edarth-aligned nobles, affected tenants, regional officials |
| Lost-ship sighting | Sailors, harbor workers, taverns |
| Noble magical-services rumor | Social circles; reliability uncertain |

A rumor's source, travel path, and distortion should be tracked separately from its objective truth.

## 6. Information rules

1. NPCs know what they have reason and opportunity to know.
2. Public information can still be misunderstood.
3. Private information requires a plausible source.
4. Rumors lose or gain detail as they travel.
5. A PC's social status, ancestry, class, actions, and reputation can change access.
6. Intimidation, bribery, friendship, service, or evidence can open channels, but results depend on the target.
7. GM-only facts do not become player knowledge merely because they are written in campaign records.
8. Do not create a hidden organization to explain ordinary information flow.

## 7. First-session practical use

When a player asks, "Who would know this?", choose the nearest plausible information channel first.

When a player asks, "Can I find out?", identify:
- who knows,
- why they know,
- whether they are accessible,
- whether they have reason to share,
- and what evidence or check would change the answer.

This keeps the city responsive without making every NPC an omniscient quest board.
